-------------------------------

deck /dek/
1. (verb) decorate or adorn brightly or festively. ("Ingrid was decked out in her Sunday best")
2. (verb, informal) knock (someone) to the ground with a punch.

-------------------------------

stack /stak/

1. (noun; computing) a set of storage locations that store data in such
a way that the most recently stored item is the first to be retrieved.

2. (verb) to shuffle or arrange (a deck of cards) dishonestly so as to gain an unfair advantage.

Push-Pop Propaganda (Also: Propagandistic Push-Pop, or simply P3):

A tactic discussed at length in the internal L.D.™ volume "Objective Pragmatics Vol. II: Geometric Propaganda," first introduced in "Chapter 3: Decking the Stack." Relies on the objective pragmatic phenomenon that the revelation of hidden/buried/encoded secrets is an experience commonly associated with having discovered the truth, and uses the resulting feeling of subjective understanding to lead (or mislead) the target into confident conclusions along arbitrary paths in inference space, the abstract space of possible conclusions derivable from following the so-called "Natural Standard Heuristics" (or: NSH) -- described by the volume as the "logical and computational rules of inference embedded in the human brain's evolved domain-specific logics." It is alleged to be a theorem of objective pragmatics that this process can have arbitrarily large effects in principle, starting from any given set of background (i.e., prior) information, and allows one to lift oneself out from what may otherwise have been a situation too difficult or complex to dig oneself out from.
