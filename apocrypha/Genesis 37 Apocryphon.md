
> _All language is but a poor translation_
> -Jackdaw Freeman

We assert that the concept of translation is a low energy effective field theory, as in theoretical physics. Translation is a good enough approximation below a certain energy. For linguistically null events (e.g., a smile, body language, nonverbal sex) translation is unnecessary.

When it comes to the prelinguistic parts of our nature, it is a good approximation to say We never left Babel.

For all events from the linguistically simple (e.g., adult smalltalk) to the linguistically intermediate (e.g., children's literature) to the linguistically advanced (e.g., Dostoyevsky) we have a nearly perfect low energy effective theory, the equivalent of classical Newtonian physics, applicable to all situations a person is ever likely to encounter in the course of an average human life.

That low energy effective theory is the art and science we call Translation.

In the landscape of linguistic objects, the bible is a singularity. It is an ultraviolet catastrophe.

Translation is a low energy effective theory. It works for essentially every linguistic event in human life.

The bible however is a singularity.

It is much stranger linguistically than either its fans or its detractors commonly understand. 

It is not only written in a language we understand much more poorly than classical Greek or Latin, its authors (and editors) stretch the boundaries of Genre in a way that no other book before or since has come close to. Some authors use puns and permutations to drive the plot, placing combinatorics and constrained optimization for "how much can one get away with as a writer" above the logic or storytelling.

What is Jonah? What is Joel? What the devil is Job? What genre are any of these? If you know, think again.

Is there a cultural or historical reason for Joseph's coat of many colors, or is the whole point that the coat was "passim" (a permutation of the name Joseph in Hebrew.)

Is Samuel fiction? No. Nonfiction history? Better. But no. Fictional propaganda about nonfiction history? Even better. Almost yes. But still no.

This is no different than a Taylor expansion. A 3rd order Taylor series of the genre of Samuel is that it is a nonfiction history of the origins of a monarchy, as told through entirely fictional propaganda, about nonfiction historical individuals. Is that what Samuel *is*? It's a good enough approximation for most purposes.

So what is Genesis? Is it fiction? Nonfiction? The author of the J source's job application to become the royal court historian under a late David or early Solomon leading to her being commissioned to write the court history in most of 1 Samuel and all of 2 Samuel leading to a more or less lifelong position in the royal court stretching into the early years of Rehoboam and the civil war that split the kingdom united by David?

Like any approximation, it is surely incorrect in detail. Fine. But how well does it allow us to organize and predict the features of Genesis and Samuel in a simple, elegant, and parsimonious way? Probably better than any other theory of the book. So then, what is Proverbs? Ecclesiastes? Song of Solomon? Is Jonah satire? Is Joel? Do the two share an author? And what in the devil is going on in Job? Translation has been described as Kissing through a Veil. The Veil is the low energy effective theory. It is inevitably imperfect. The Veil cannot be removed. The only solution, when forced to kiss through a veil, is to remove some other constraint down below, and allow the text to open up, as we have our way with it, and it has its ways with us. This, we believe, is the only approach to translating the text that follows that has a prayer of properly representing the text. The text that follows is the bible, through the unavoidable veil of translation, with some extra activity going on down below. The veil can't be removed. The same is not true for the rest of what has before now been covered. Against that background, no further explanation is needed. The text that follows, like the original, does not explain itself. The text does not tell the reader what it is, or attempt to convince you, or ask permission. The text does what it does, with itself and to you. On that note, this note is the last one. There will be no more explanations of the text in the text. Kissing through a veil is not enough, nor is kissing. In physical terms, we now enter the region of strong coupling. Knowledge of the text requires opening up enough to take this in. For good and bad. Now you know us, it's time for us to learn you. We must know. We will know.

We now come to text.
