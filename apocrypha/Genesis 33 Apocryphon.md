THE LONELY BOY
==============

Once upon a time, there was a boy.

He was the worst human being who has ever existed.

I don't know very much about the boy. But I do know how to play something called the game.

So let's play a game.

And while we're playing, let's go see about this boy...


    At the elementary school the boy had been accustomed to passing up each year without any special effort.

    At his new school, to start with, he also tried improvisation at which he was a master.
    He had to do it all the more as he found little pleasure in learning by heart, so much valued by his teachers.

    But here the trick did not work. So he started to sulk and let things drift.

    Nobody took much notice of him in class; he had no friends and did not want any.

    Sometimes some of his spoiled classmates would make him feel that they did not accept as one of them, this
    village boy - a sufficient reason for him to withdraw even more.

    It is significant that not one of his many schoolmates could claim any close relationship or friendship with him.

    [The boy] spoke but rarely of his family.

    He used to say that it was advisable not to mix too much with grownups,
    as these people with their peculiar ideas would only divert one from one's own...

    [H]e dissociated himself from his contemporaries, who were at that time very arrogant and turbulent,
    and went his own way, refusing to join any of the then existing political parties.

    Although he always felt a sense of responsibility for everything that happened he was always a lonely and solitary man,
    determined to rely upon himself only, and to reach his goal.

Everything he did came from a single little source. Just one thing. Not even a big one.

It's something all the adults do: disliking another group, a lot, for some reasons.

This boy loved his group. And resented the others. Just like all the adults tend to do.

If you look carefully, you'll see your parents doing the same thing.
And your friends.
And maybe yourself too.

The boy just did it better. Or worse. Much worse. But much more efficiently.

He may not have disliked the groups he disliked much more or much less than most adults do.

What matters is how he treated them. And the fact that, somehow, he wound up in power.

And when he did, he treated them differently for being other. Like they'd always treated him.
Just like all the adults sometimes treat the groups they don't like inside the secret privacy of their worried little heads.

In the boy's eyes, everyone had always made him feel other. He'd felt that way for as long as he could remember.

And living like that, a boy can't help but feel angry.

It wasn't their fault that he was angry. His anger didn't have much to do with them, really.

We may never know all the reasons why he was angry,
but what we do know is how he was always treated by people.
Normal people. People just like me and you.

For the boy, and for all of us, it's often hard to realize why we do the things we do.

We all feel the need to have someone to blame. Someone to point to, and say "this... this is all because of you."

The boy really needed someone like that too.

So...

Remember, there are lots of uniquely horrible things in the world.

But this lonely boy wasn't really one of those things.

He didn't chop people up and have sex with their bodies and keep the dead body parts in the refrigerator for food.

That would be unique. And uniquely horrible.

But this lonely boy of ours wasn't all that unique.

He was much more similar to you and me than that odd body part sex refrigerator person.

So when you get to earth, you'll hear a lot of people saying this lonely boy is bad.

And they're right, if the word "bad" has any meaning at all, then he most certainly was.
In the adult world, this boy was perhaps the worst person who has ever existed.

Worse even than that strange refigerator person.

But even worse than the lonely boy or the refigerator person is the tendency that lives right here inside all of us.

The tendency in us that causes normal boys like that to go on to do the worst things in the world.

Now some optional homework, for once you get out there.

If you ever get a chance when you're older to go to something called the Holocaust Museum, you'll
see some of those worst things in the world.

There are shoes there.

They're the saddest shoes that have ever existed. Every day, adults walk by them, and cry.

But by the time you get there -- to this museum -- you'll already have heard a lot about those shoes.

You can cry in front of them if you need to.

But the shoes have seen more tears than any shoes ever have. More than any shoes before them or since.

And the shoes don't want any more people to cry.

"What we would appreciate, if you ever find yourself here," say the shoes, "is to do something that few people do."

Choose your least favorite thing. A person. Or a group.

Any group of depraved vile individuals who you wish tomorrow you could wipe the world clean of.

(We promise you'll have plenty of ideas in mind by the time you get here, if you ever do...)

Maybe for you it will be the rich. The bankers. Or the traders on wall street.
The people whose actions or speech you call racist. Or sexist. Or disgusting.
The atheists. The christians. The cute little [religion/barber-shop]s called cults.
The pedophiles. The people who should have known better at the time. The bosses who
create a hostile work environment. Or any people who are phobic of something you love
or are equally philic of something you fear.

But in order for you to do your part for the shoes, we only ask you to imagine some group
not defined by its violence. Perhaps members of this group will have used violence in the past.
Maybe many of them will have. Life is long, as they say. But your group,
the one that you choose for the shoes, should not be something like
"serial rapist murderers." Why not? Because that's a little too easy.
We know you're better at the game than just that.

So choose something else. Fiction or non-fiction or anywhere in between. The details don't matter.
But whatever the cause of that contempt that you feel, the shoes insist that your feeling must be real.

Now... the game.

In that building from before, in just the same order as the story it catalogues,
long before you arrive at the shoes, there are speeches.

Old speeches on film.

That's our boy. The one who was alone.

These films contain speeches the lonely boy gave.

Look at all of the people this lonely boy inspired.
Look at his energy. His words.

And listen... most of the time,
you can't quite tell who exactly he's talking about.

So let's fill in the gaps, as our minds tend to do, but this
time let's exercize that gap-filling responsibly.

I want you to imagine that all of the energy and all of the words
in that grown-up-version-of-the-lonely-boy's speeches are directed
against the same group from before. Your least favorite group.
Whichever one you decided to choose for the shoes.

This isn't something adults ever to teach you to do.

But just imagine... and listen... the boy's an amazing speaker.

His clothes are a bit weird by the standards of today.

But the words have a sort of timeless power about them.

And those things he's saying. He loves us. Every word
of it is real. He's protecting us from them. And not
for one single moment does it feel insincere.

Every single word he says is beautiful... if you don't know.

And when you're done. Only then. Only after you can feel the
real beauty of those words when you let them inside your head and
feel that the force of them is the same force protecting your Us
from your Them, protecting you from every last thing that you fear.
Once you can feel it for real. Go and look at the shoes.

...

Congratulations. That's the end of round one.

There are lots and lots of more rounds of the game.

Those rounds are called a responsible life.

A normal life. Full of learning, but different.

Now once you've done this sort of thing enough. Made it a habit.
You may notice yourself starting to feel a bit divorced from your species.

The adults in that building doing the things that they do,
they may seem to be playing a similar game too. But I assure you,
they're mostly not doing that thing you just did.

To be sure, they'll watch the same films, and shake their heads back and forth,
and they'll say the boy was a monster, which he was.

And then the adults keep walking... and they cry by the shoes.

And then after they leave, they continue disliking all the same people
and things they don't like, exactly as much as they ever did before.

And the adults feel very, very good about themselves.

But the next day, the shoes are still there.

And the shoes have to watch the same things all again.

And again. And again.

Adults never learn.

That's why we need you.

They listen to you.

And you listen to us.

But the adults rarely listen to each other anymore.

Love always,
~The Shoes
