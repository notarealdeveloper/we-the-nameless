Halcyon Adaptation, Hyperion Symposium (HA, HS), or
Human-Human Social Algorithm (HH, SA), or
Humanity as Humans (H AS H), or
HASH
===============================================

A social algorithm developed by the terrorist organization known as L.D.™

According to the group's internal documentation, the goal of the process is the algorithmic construction of universality (see also: losing face; becoming faceless) through a process of expansion of one's self-defining parameters without requiring a denial, repression, or contraction of one's default identity.

Specification
-------------
Given a subgroup consisting of some number N of individuals, each individual chooses an adjective that describes some aspect of themselves. The algorithm's effectiveness increases to the extent that each individual's adjective is anything that is divisive, shameful, or socially taboo to some other group or in some other social context. In what follows, this self-chosen adjective describing some aspect of oneself will be called "A." The A stands for aspect. Or adjective. We haven't decided yet. The algorithm then proceeds as follows.

A complete HASH has N-1 stages. In every stage S, each of the N members is paired with another. This is called a relationship (also: dyad, pair, couple, inverse, or duality). The two members then exchanges adjectives. For example, given a relationship between two individuals I and J, each having chosen adjectives i and j, respectively, we then form the pairs (I, j) and (J, i). These pairs form the basis of the stage S for the two individuals in question. A given stage S may last for an arbitrary amount of time T. During that time, individual I adopts adjective j as a property of themselves, within the context of some social community C to which they belong (with individual J doing the same, for the adjective i, and some group D). The social context or community C may be any social situation, from a series of anonymous online interactions, to a series of interactions with one's family and friends. The parameters of C are an implementation detail not defined by the HASH specification. Individual I's job is then to adopt adjective j as if it were part of themselves, doing so among members of the group C to which I belongs, and coming to its defense when necessary. Depending on the parameters of C and the values of I and j, this task may in practice be arbitrarily simple or arbitrarily difficult.

In doing so, individual I gains familiarity and experience with the arguments, responses, rejections, ostracism, etc., that members of group C typically offer against individuals with adjective j. In collaboration with their partner, the knowledge of group C possessed by individual I is then combined with the reciprocal knowledge possessed by J of the inner states and subjective experiences of individuals with adjective j. The result of this collaboration is that both I and J gain a new ability, namely the ability to convincingly simulate a hypothetical individual who is both a member of I's group C and possesses property j. Both in the simple case of anonymous interactions and in more advanced real-life scenarios, the process relies on the reflexive rejection experienced by individual I at the hands of their own group C to motivate further exploration by I into the nature and properties of individuals with adjective j. The typical result is that the pair develops a series of well designed (I,j)-specific explanations, analogies, and psychological hacks as a result of their struggle to convey to the mass of C the similarities between members of C and individuals with property j.

When the time interval T has elapsed, the subgroup undergoes a permutation and each individual receives a new partner. The algorithm proceeds as above for N-1 rounds, or until every individual has paired with every other.

When carried out with sufficient sincerity, the HASH algorithm is said to generate for each member a new individual-specific identity that is simultaneously faceless and unique, without requiring any modifications to or destruction of one's original identity.

Though the process may be reversible in principle, it is often said to be irreversible in practice. Or more simply, it is widely recognized that once one has been HASHed, it is extremely difficult in practice to invert the algorithm and return to the identity with which one began.
