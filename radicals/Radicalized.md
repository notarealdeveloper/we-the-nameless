## Scope and reading conventions

Only Genesis–Kings are in the 100. First and Second Samuel form one conventional book, as do First and Second Kings. Ezra, Nehemiah, and Esther retain their 11 original units in a separate archive, outside every core count.

The Chinese glosses are lexical; the English microbook titles and the character-to-passage mappings are editorial. These are reading divisions, not claims to recover original authors’ documents. Repeated signs are deliberate.

Passages retain the supplied common English/KJV-style versification. Do not treat them as ready-made Hebrew manuscript anchors. In 1 Samuel 4:1, **a** is the opening sentence about Samuel’s word reaching Israel; **b** begins the battle narrative.

## Exactly what changed

Four adjacent pairs were merged. No surviving boundary moved; no new boundary was introduced. The former boundary of each merged pair remains recorded as an internal transition in the JSON.

| New unit | Former pair | New range | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 011 **易 — Exchange** | 兄 Birthright + 易 Exchange | Genesis 25:19–31:55 | The exchanges at home and the exchanges abroad form a single arc: the deceiver becomes the deceived. The homeward movement remains a separate microbook. |
| 023 **山 — The Mountain Covenant** | 山 The Mountain + 法 The Covenant | Exodus 19:1–24:18 | The mountain encounter and the covenant’s terms belong to one enacted agreement; the sanctuary pattern still begins separately at Exodus 25:1. |
| 035 **田 — The Land and Its Rest** | 田 The Land Released + 果 Consequences | Leviticus 25:1–26:46 | Leviticus 25 commands the land’s rest; 26:34–35 describes its rest during the inhabitants’ exile. Land, release, obligation, and consequence remain together. |
| 075 **王 — The First King** | 王 A King + 失 Rejection | Samuel 1 Sam 8:1–15:35 | The institution is requested, established, tested, and disowned before the narrative turns to David’s anointing at 1 Samuel 16:1. Saul’s later death and its lament remain separate. |

**Genesis:** Laban’s explanation that the younger cannot precede the firstborn (29:26) makes the elder/younger reversal explicit. Keeping the inheritance episode with the later exchanges lets the reversal answer the earlier deception. [New check: Genesis 29](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/genesis/29).

**Exodus:** Arrival, speech, terms, assent, blood, and ascent make one mountain covenant. The prescribed sanctuary remains its own next movement. [New check: Exodus 24](https://www.dioceseoflansing.org/exodus-24).

**Leviticus:** The land is commanded to rest, then receives that neglected rest during exile. The merge makes the relationship between land tenure and covenant consequence visible. It does not reduce chapter 26 to agriculture alone. [Leviticus 25](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/leviticus/25); [Leviticus 26:34–35,43](https://biblehub.com/leviticus/26-43.htm).

**Samuel:** The first king is requested, selected, established, tested, and rejected. The next unit begins with the anointing of his rival. Rejection is not treated as death: Saul’s subsequent life, flight narratives, death, and David’s lament stay in their later units. [1 Samuel 15](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1samuel/15).

Three signs were then replaced by already-established vocabulary; their titles and ranges did not change.

| Unit | Before → after | Reason |
|---|:---:|---|
| 062 Belonging | 同 → **合** | Shared belonging is retained under the existing sign for joining and union; the threatened rupture ends in recognized membership rather than war. |
| 077 The Fugitive | 走 → **行** | The fugitive’s journeys reuse the established movement sign. The title and rationale preserve the distinction between chosen migration and forced flight. |
| 091 The Word Against Kings | 言 → **口** | Prophetic mouths, disputed testimony, and lethal speech reuse the sign already used in Numbers and Judges. The mouths of prophets are explicit in 1 Kings 22:22–23. |

The prophetic-mouth connection is literal in the underlying passage: 1 Kings 22:22 depicts deception acting through prophets’ mouths. This is a semantic reuse of **口**, not a new claim that it literally means prophecy. [New check: 1 Kings 22:22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Kings+22%3A22&version=KJV).

## Why seventy, rather than forty

The supplied core inventory had 77 unique signs; its 79-sign metadata included the coda-only 囗 and 死. The four merges retire 兄, 法, 果, and 失 from the microbook vocabulary. The three substitutions retire 同, 走, and 言. **77 − 4 − 3 = 70.** No new character is added.

Seventy has an explicit biblical resonance in the elders of Exodus 24:1,9 and Numbers 11:16. Seven tens also makes an easily inspected vocabulary layout. This is a chosen design resonance, not a claim that seventy is universally symbolic in every culture. [Numbers 11:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+11%3A16&version=NRSVUE).

I have not forced the vocabulary down to forty: that would require many more changes and would collapse useful distinctions such as clothing, bones, names, mouths, ears, staffs, houses, and fields. The small number is serving the reading, not replacing it.
