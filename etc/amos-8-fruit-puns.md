
Yes. The trick is reproducing the Hebrew mechanism: innocuous fruit noun → near-homophone meaning doom/end, ideally with only a tiny phonetic change.

Hebrew qayiṣ → qēṣ is unusually good, so exact English equivalents are hard. But there are some excellent comic substitutions.

1. PEARS → PERISH is probably the funniest

This preserves the sheer brutality of Amos's transition:

> YHWH: “What do you see, Amos?”
Amos: “A basket of pears.”
YHWH: “Pears.”
“Perish.”
Amos: “…Right.”



Or even more dad-joke-like:

> “What do you see?”
“Pears.”
“Exactly. Prepare to perish.”



That's remarkably close to the qayiṣ/qēṣ principle: the visual object supplies the phonological cue for the oracle.

2. DATES → YOUR DATE IS UP

This may actually work better as English literature:

> YHWH: “What do you see?”
Amos: “Dates.”
YHWH: “Exactly.”
“Israel's date is up.”



Or:

> “A basket of dates.”
“Correct. Israel has reached its expiration date.”



The latter even preserves the ripe fruit → end-time semantic association. Amos's קיץ isn't arbitrary fruit: it's summer/ripe fruit, so there's already an implication of the agricultural cycle reaching its endpoint.

3. LIMES → YOUR TIME HAS COME

Phonetically this is gorgeous:

> “What do you see?”
“Limes.”
“Right. Israel's time has come.”



limes : time isn't quite as close as qayiṣ : qēṣ, but the semantic correspondence is nearly exact: קֵץ = end, hence “your time has come.”

You could exaggerate the sound correspondence deliberately:

> “Limes.”
“Limes. Times. Israel's time is up.”



That actually feels like the Hebrew wordplay.

4. GRAPES → GRAVES

For maximum black comedy:

> “What do you see?”
“Grapes.”
“Close. Graves.”



😂

That's not a translation so much as an English recreation, but it captures Amos 8's tonal violence magnificently: one phoneme converts pleasant agricultural produce into annihilation.

Best card version

I think GRAPES / GRAVES wins visually and comedically:

BASKET OF GRAPES

When Amos enters the battlefield, reveal a basket of grapes.

“What do you see?”
“Grapes.”
“Almost. Graves.”

That's the exact kind of divine dad joke immediately followed by national catastrophe that makes Amos 8:1–2 so memorable.

But for translation fidelity, I'd rank them:

DATES → date is up > LIMES → time is up > PEARS → perish > GRAPES → graves

And for actually making somebody laugh:

GRAPES → GRAVES > PEARS → PERISH > DATES → DATE'S UP.
