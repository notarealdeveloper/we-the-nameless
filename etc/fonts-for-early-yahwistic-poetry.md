
## David Noel Friedman

### Pottery, Poetry, and Prophecy

Early Hebrew Poetry

IN A RECENT SURVEY of early Hebrew poetry, the late Professor W. F. Albright proposed a basic sequence dating, using certain stylistic phenomena as determinative criteria.¹ Chief among these were repetitive parallelism and paranomasia or wordplay. Relative chronology is fixed by reference to Ugaritic poetry which is substantially older than practically all surviving biblical verse. Since repetitive parallelism is frequent in Ugaritic poems, its occurrence in Hebrew poetry would be an indicator of early date. On the other hand, paranomasia is rare in Ugaritic, so its occurrence in Hebrew poetry would point to a somewhat later date of composition. Albright was able to discern a pattern in early Hebrew poems exhibiting a gradual decline in the use of repetitive parallelism with a corresponding increase in the use of paranomasia.² This discovery permitted an arrangement of the poems in sequence, between the thirteenth and tenth centuries B.C., with more specific dates being assigned on the basis of historical allusions and other information in the poems. Albright fixed the order and dates of the poems as follows:

1. Song of Miriam (Exodus 15)—early thirteenth century

2. Song of Deborah (Judges 5)—ca. 1150

3. Oracles of Balaam (Numbers 23–24)—ca. 1200

4. Testament of Moses (Deuteronomy 33)—middle of eleventh century

5. Song of Moses (Deuteronomy 32)—ca. 1025

6. Testament of Jacob (Genesis 49)—late eleventh century

7. Song of Hannah (I Samuel 2)—late eleventh

8. David’s Lament (II Samuel 1)—early tenth

9. Oracle of David (II Samuel 23)—first half of tenth

10. Royal Psalm (II Samuel 22 = Psalm 18)—tenth

11. Psalm 78—tenth

12. Psalm 68—tenth
