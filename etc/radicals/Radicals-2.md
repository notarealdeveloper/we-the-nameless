**I would now change 根 → 元 and 屯 → 干. And 主 really does earn a place—not merely because it resembles 王.**

My preferred version for your **whole-set visual brief** is:

# 元　出　示　干　申　土　士　王　主　曰


Every character is now **three to five strokes**. More importantly, the set increasingly looks like transformations of a shared alphabet rather than ten independently selected illustrations.

The English columns remain thematic labels, not literal translations. **亡 is still a serious alternative to 主**: the choice is between a stronger graphic/ritual network and a stronger tragic ending.

## 1. Genesis: 元 is the clearest improvement

**元 gives you “origin” and Japanese _gen_ in four strokes.**

Its Japanese on-readings include **gen** and **gan**; its kun-readings include **moto**, “source/base,” and **hajime**, “beginning.” Its semantic range includes origin, beginning, head, and chief. This is not extracting “gen” from an obscure reconstruction: it is an ordinary, dictionary-listed Japanese reading. ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0001982700/ "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0001982700/"))

That gives it three independently useful properties:

**The word:** beginning, origin, source.  
**The sound:** _gen_.  
**The shape:** four strokes, with the same paired horizontals that recur in 示 and 干.

The pun is still a **Romanization pun**, not an exact match to the English soft-_g_ pronunciation of _Genesis_. But it preserves what was attractive about 根 while removing most of its visual complexity.

There is also a deeper semantic advantage over a generic “creation” character. **元 is not restricted to making the physical universe.** Its “head/first” range lets it suggest beginnings, founders, and first ancestors. That suits a book whose origins move from the world to particular ancestral families. CUHK traces the early graph to a human figure with the head emphasized, with “first” and “beginning” developing from that head sense. ([Humanum](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E5%85%83 "漢語多功能字庫"))

Visually, I would compare:

**元　示　干**

They look related without being difficult to distinguish. That is precisely the balance the set needs.

**生** remains attractive for birth, life, and growth; **本** remains attractive for root and origin. But neither gives you the combination of **four strokes + beginning + gen**. For this particular project, **元 beats both**. ([Humanum](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E7%94%9F "https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E7%94%9F"))

## 2. Numbers: 干 is the strongest new candidate

The book’s problem is not simply that it happens in a desert. It is that a community is **counted, organized, prepared to enter, and then prevented from entering after its refusal**. The second census marks a replacement generation preparing to do what the first did not. That makes “a counted people under arms, in a dry place, repeatedly in conflict with authority” a more useful organizing idea than “wilderness scenery.” ([USCCB](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/numbers/0 "https://bible.usccb.org/bible/numbers/0"))

**干 gets surprisingly close to that combination.**

### Warfare: an actual shield

**干 means a shield**, and by extension has associations with defense and armed conflict. **干戈**, shields and spears, is an established expression for weapons or warfare. CUHK documents early military uses and the relationship to characters for defending or warding off. ([Humanum](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E5%B9%B2 "https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E5%B9%B2"))

This is a much stronger connection than “the character vaguely looks like a spear.” The military meaning is real.

### Rebellion: offending or transgressing

Another attested sense is **to offend against, violate, or interfere**. The _Shuowen_ glosses 干 with 犯. That does not make 干 a literal translation of “rebellion,” but it puts conflict with a boundary or authority inside the character’s genuine semantic range. ([Humanum](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E5%B9%B2 "https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E5%B9%B2"))

For Numbers, I would use this as the second layer: not merely **weapons**, but **a people repeatedly transgressing**.

### Wilderness: dryness

In Japanese, 干 has established readings and uses involving drying and becoming dry, including **干す, hosu**, and **干る, hiru**. In modern simplified Chinese, it also represents **乾** in the “dry” sense. Those are different orthographic traditions, so I would not pretend that every classical occurrence of 干 means “dry.” ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0001012200 "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0001012200"))

But the sound connection is older than modern simplification. Baxter–Sagart give **干 “pole”** and **乾 “dry”** the same Middle Chinese transcription, **kan**, and the same Old Chinese reconstruction, ***[k]ˤar**. The dryness association therefore has a genuine historical phonetic basis as well. ([UMich LSA Sites](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/wp-content/uploads/sites/1415/2025/03/BaxterSagartOCbyMandarinMC2014-09-20.pdf "https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/wp-content/uploads/sites/1415/2025/03/BaxterSagartOCbyMandarinMC2014-09-20.pdf"))

### Numbers: quantity and counted units

This is the weaker—but still real—layer. Japanese dictionaries list the quantitative usage seen in **若干**, “some/a certain amount.” Baxter–Sagart also list a **干** word glossed “piece, item,” reconstructed ***kˤar**. ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0001012200 "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0001012200"))

I would describe this as **an enumeration echo**, not claim that 干 itself means “census.”

The resulting stack is:

> **Shield / transgression / dryness / quantity.**

That is a lot of relevant meaning for **three straight strokes**.

### Why it wins for the set

Compare:

**干　土　士　王　主**

Numbers now belongs to the same graphic family as the books that follow. You can read the sequence, as your own emblematic interpretation, as:

**armed conflict → land → warriors → king → lordship**

It does not encode the entire plot, but it makes the transition from the wilderness books into the political books visible.

**My qualification:** 干 does **not** intrinsically mean wandering, scouting, cloud, fire, or complaining. I would not manufacture those connections. Its case is strong enough without them.

### The other Numbers finalists

|Candidate|Strokes|What genuinely recommends it|Why I would not put it first|
|:-:|:-:|---|---|
|**屯**|4|Encampment, gathering, hardship|Excellent thematic fit; less integrated with the straight-bar family|
|**井**|4|Well; orderly arrangement; visually resembles **#**|Wonderful numerical rebus, but its “numbers” meaning would be your invention|
|**戍**|6|Garrison, soldiers, military camp; **shù**, homophonous with 數 “number”|Best direct sound pun for Numbers, but more visually complicated|
|**斥**|5|Scout/reconnoitre; reject; rebuke|An unusually good miniature of the scouting-and-rejection episode, but weaker across the whole book|

The military and hardship senses of **屯**, the well/order senses of **井**, the military senses of **戍**, and the scout/rebuke/reject senses of **斥** are dictionary-attested. The **戍 shù / 數 shù** connection is an exact modern Mandarin homophone. ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0005352400/ "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0005352400/"))

**井 deserves special attention**, though. Its visual resemblance to **#** is immediately legible to an English-language audience. A well also has a specific anchor in Numbers’ well-song, and its grid can evoke the organized camp. Those are useful design associations, though it is neither a literal census character nor an exact diagram of the twelve tribes. ([USCCB](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/numbers/21 "https://bible.usccb.org/bible/numbers/21"))

My ranking is therefore **干 for the whole set; 屯 for the most direct narrative emblem; 戍 for the strongest sound pun; 井 for the cleverest cross-script rebus**.

## 3. Deuteronomy: 申 gets considerably better under examination

I would now regard **申 as close to locked**.

First, its meanings really do include **stating, explaining, extending, and repeating**. The “again” layer is not something we have inferred from a convenient compound. Taiwan’s Ministry of Education lists it separately. ([Revised Mandarin Dictionary](https://dict.revised.moe.edu.tw/dictView.jsp?ID=8983&la=0&powerMode=0 "https://dict.revised.moe.edu.tw/dictView.jsp?ID=8983&la=0&powerMode=0"))

Second, **申 is already the first character of the established Japanese title 申命記**, Deuteronomy. So this is not merely a novel symbol that happens to suit the book: it overlaps an actual naming tradition. ([Bible.org](https://www.bible.or.jp/know/know02.html "https://www.bible.or.jp/know/know02.html"))

Third, the Japanese verb **申す, mōsu**, means “to say,” especially in humble or polite usage. Japanese biblical **Moses is モーセ, Mōse**. That produces a much better near-pun than merely comparing _shēn_ with an English name:

**Mōse / mōsu — Moses / to say.** ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kotoba/0003584100 "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kotoba/0003584100"))

It is not identity: the final vowels differ, and **mōsu is the reading of 申す**, not an instruction to pronounce every isolated 申 “mōsu.” But as a bilingual visual/verbal joke, it is exceptionally well placed.

You get:

> **Moses / speaking / reiteration / an actual Deuteronomy title.**

And you retain the connection:

**示 + 申 → 神**

Those really are the components of 神, with 示 commonly taking its side form 礻. The explanation “Leviticus plus Deuteronomy makes God” is **your constructed reading**, not the historical etymology; the component relationship itself is genuine. ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0003606600 "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0003606600"))

One correction to your notes: I would **demote the “draw out”/Moses-etymology connection**. The attested extension/stretching sense of 申 does not straightforwardly mean “pull a child out of water.” It can remain a loose secondary association, but **mōsu / Mōse** and **reiterated speech** are much firmer foundations. ([Revised Mandarin Dictionary](https://dict.revised.moe.edu.tw/dictView.jsp?ID=8983&la=0&powerMode=0 "https://dict.revised.moe.edu.tw/dictView.jsp?ID=8983&la=0&powerMode=0"))

## 4. Kings: 主 has three real advantages—and one real cost

**Yes: Kings is the best place for 主.**

Its ordinary meanings include **lord, master, ruler, and the person in charge**. Japanese readings include **shu**, **nushi**, and **aruji**. So the basic political meaning is already secure. ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0003042300 "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0003042300"))

But the interesting part is what it does **between the other characters**.

### It connects kingship to worship

For this emblem, I would frame Kings not simply as “more kings,” but as:

> **Who rules—and by whose authority are the rulers judged?**

That is a defensible distinction from Samuel’s establishment of monarchy. Kings repeatedly evaluates rulers on religious grounds, and its political history is inseparable from competing sanctuaries and the demand for worship centered on Jerusalem. ([USCCB](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1kings/0 "https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1kings/0"))

Accordingly:

**王 — the king**  
**主 — lordship, including the authority over the king**

That second line is an interpretive use of the ordinary “lord/master” meaning, not a claim that 主 inherently means “God above a monarch.”

### Its relationship with 示 may be historical, not merely graphic

CUHK presents the analysis of Tang Lan and Chen Mengjia that **主 and 示 differentiated from one earlier graph associated with an ancestral spirit tablet**. On that account, the two characters have a real historical relationship in the religious sphere. ([Humanum](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E4%B8%BB "https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E4%B8%BB"))

That gives your set a particularly good three-way connection:

**Leviticus 示 ↔ Kings 主 ↔ Samuel 王**

The first link can have historical and ritual depth; the second is an exceptionally close modern graphic resemblance.

### There is also a lamp reading—with a specific Kings connection

A different traditional account, represented in the _Shuowen_ and Kanjipedia, associates **主 with a burning wick or lamp**, with Kanjipedia describing a flame on a stand at an altar. This is **an alternative account of the graph’s origin**, not another settled fact to stack uncritically onto the tablet analysis. ([Humanum](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E4%B8%BB "https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E4%B8%BB"))

Nevertheless, as an interpretive bonus it is excellent: **1 Kings 15:4 uses the image of a lamp in Jerusalem for the continuation of David’s line through a successor**. ([Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Kings+15%3A4&version=NIV "https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Kings+15%3A4&version=NIV"))

So 主 can gather:

**ruler / lord / sacred authority / temple association / dynastic lamp**

That is substantially richer than “王 with a dot because it looks nice.”

### What you lose by replacing 亡

You lose the superb **王 wáng → 亡 wáng** turn from kingship to disappearance and ruin. That remains the tighter verbal ending.

One historical correction matters: **王 and 亡 are homophones in modern Mandarin, not in the Middle or Old Chinese forms checked here**. Baxter–Sagart distinguish them clearly. ([UMich LSA Sites](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/wp-content/uploads/sites/1415/2025/03/BaxterSagartOCbyMandarinMC2014-09-20.pdf "https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/wp-content/uploads/sites/1415/2025/03/BaxterSagartOCbyMandarinMC2014-09-20.pdf"))

My judgment is:

**主 wins on the interconnected visual-and-ritual system.**  
**亡 wins on economy, narrative finality, and the modern Mandarin pun.**

For the exact brief you gave this round, I lean **主**. For a set intended to read as a compressed tragedy, I would retain **亡**.

## 5. The characters I would leave alone

### Exodus: 出

**出 is already doing its job with almost no waste.** It means going out or bringing out, and it is present in the established Japanese title **出エジプト記**. Its ordinary range accommodates both departure and being brought out, without needing a separate character for rescue. ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0003214000 "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0003214000"))

It also supplies something the increasingly horizontal set needs: a strongly angular form built from **open compartments and a central axis**.

I would not replace it with a prettier but less specific emblem for mountains, water, walking, or freedom.

### Leviticus: 示

**Keep the sacred radical rather than trying to translate “law.”** Kanjipedia connects the early shape with a ritual stand used in worship; its later ordinary verbal meaning is “show.” Its value here is primarily the religious component and its history, not a literal translation of _Priest_, _Rites_, or _Laws_. ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0002841600/ "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0002841600/"))

It now has more connections than before: visual affinity with **元 and 干**, composition with **申**, and a possible historical relationship with **主**.

That is precisely the kind of character whose value increases when the rest of the set improves.

### Joshua: 土

**Keep the object of the conquest rather than the act of fighting.** Joshua’s narrative includes not only conquest but the division of the land among the tribes. **土** allows the emblem to stand for the outcome toward which the previous books have been moving. ([Bible.org](https://www.bible.or.jp/know/know02.html "https://www.bible.or.jp/know/know02.html"))

The alternative **入**, “enter,” still makes a beautiful **出 → 入** opposition. But **土** participates in more of this particular set’s relationships. I would accept the loss of the neat exit/entrance pair to preserve the stronger larger structure.

### Judges: 士

**士 remains better than a verdict/judgment character.** Its martial and status-bearing senses suit the leaders more readily than a modern courtroom emblem would. It is also already present in the Japanese title **士師記**. ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0002694900 "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0002694900"))

There is a tempting distraction here: **士 can be read samurai**, which offers the beginning of “Sam.” But I would **not** move it to Samuel merely to harvest that syllable. The existing **土 → 士 → 王** progression is worth more than that isolated pun. ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0002694900 "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0002694900"))

### Samuel: 王

**Keep the making of kingship here.** Samuel is where the tribal order becomes centralized monarchy under Saul and David. Giving it 王 lets Kings be represented by what happens _to_, _above_, or _after_ that kingship—主 or 亡—rather than making both books compete for synonyms of “king.” ([Bible.org](https://www.bible.or.jp/know/know02.html "https://www.bible.or.jp/know/know02.html"))

That is a productive division of labor between the two emblems.

### Dud.: 曰

For your **Speech / Logos / Word** brief, I would keep **曰**.

Japanese **曰く, iwaku**, provides “says/thus says,” while the dictionary also records the associated range of reason or background. That gives your Logos label a secondary connection to **an account or explanation**, rather than only the physical act of speaking. It is not a claim that 曰 is the standard technical translation of Greek _logos_. ([Kanjipedia](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0000423500 "https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kanji/0000423500"))

Visually, 曰 closes the set with its simplest framed utterance. And it provides a good contrast with **申**: related speech associations, related box-like geometry, distinct functions.

## 6. What the ancient readings actually contribute

I would keep the historical phonology focused on the places where it genuinely changes the decision.

These are Baxter–Sagart forms: **MC is their Middle Chinese transcription**, while starred forms are **Old Chinese reconstructions**, with uncertainty marks retained. Their MC notation represents distinctions in historical sources; it is not itself a narrow phonetic reconstruction. ([UMich LSA Sites](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/wp-content/uploads/sites/1415/2025/03/BaxterSagartOCbyMandarinMC2014-09-20.pdf "https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/wp-content/uploads/sites/1415/2025/03/BaxterSagartOCbyMandarinMC2014-09-20.pdf"))

|Character and sense|Middle Chinese|Old Chinese|Useful consequence|
|---|---|---|---|
|**元**, head/first|**ngjwon**|***[ŋ]o[r]**|The useful **gen** reading is Japanese, not this Chinese form|
|**干**, pole|**kan**|***[k]ˤar**|An old sound connection with 乾 “dry”|
|**乾**, dry|**kan**|***[k]ˤar**|Dryness is not merely a modern look-alike association|
|**干**, shield|**kan**|***kˤa[r]**|Warfare belongs to the same historical sound neighborhood|
|**王**, king|**hjwang**|***ɢʷaŋ**|Historically distinct from 亡|
|**亡**, flee/disappear/die|**mjang**|***maŋ**|The 王/亡 homophone is specifically modern Mandarin|

The forms are directly attested in their published tables. ([UMich LSA Sites](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/wp-content/uploads/sites/1415/2025/03/BaxterSagartOCbyRadStr2014-09-20.pdf "https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/wp-content/uploads/sites/1415/2025/03/BaxterSagartOCbyRadStr2014-09-20.pdf"))

The useful result is not “every ancient reading happens to sound biblical.” It is more selective and more convincing:

**Japanese supplies Gen and the Moses/speaking near-pun. Ancient Chinese strengthens 干’s network. Modern Mandarin supplies the king/ruin homophone.**

## 7. The whole-set payoff

I would judge the final design in three visual groups:

### 元　示　干

**Beginnings, worship, conflict:** paired upper horizontals, with different structures beneath.

### 土　士　王　主

**Land, warriors, kingship, lordship:** the same small vocabulary of bars and a vertical axis, with changes in proportion, number, and marking.

### 出　申　曰

**Departure, reiterated speech, utterance:** an angular family of open and closed spaces.

Those are **design relationships**, not a claim that all ten graphs descend from one pictograph. Their strength is that they can be perceived before anyone reads the explanations.

For the actual lettering, I would use a shared monoline treatment and consistent horizontal levels, but **not normalize away the distinctions**. The upper and lower bar lengths must keep **土 and 士** unmistakable; **曰** must remain distinct from 日; **主** needs a clearly separate top mark. “More alike” stops being an improvement when it becomes “harder to tell apart.”

**My final recommendation for this round is therefore:**

# 元　出　示　干　申　土　士　王　主　曰

**元 and 干 are the substantive upgrades. 申 becomes even harder to replace. 主 is the best new connection-maker; 亡 remains the better last word on kingship.**
