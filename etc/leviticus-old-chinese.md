And your 男 nˤəm bit has the makings of a wonderfully stupid running footnote:

男 = nˤəm = “man,” except man is upside down. This is consistent with China, where 頭 tóu means “head” and 洞 dòng means “hole,” because gravity works the other way over there.

The multilingual coincidences are probably even more fertile than English alone: searching Old Chinese against English + Biblical Hebrew vocabulary should turn up things like mə-lək and bˤrak that we'd completely miss with English-only phonetic matching.
