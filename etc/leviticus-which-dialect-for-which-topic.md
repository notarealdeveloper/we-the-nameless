
Absolutely. Interpreting “what the dialect's name means” as the literal meaning/etymology of the Chinese name—e.g. Cantonese = 廣東話 “Guangdong speech,” Hakka = 客家話 “guest-family speech”—rather than the English label.

Since several modern varieties coexist, the modern rows are only approximately ordered.

Date	Variety / stage	Chinese name	What the name literally means	Leviticus pairing

Modern	Cantonese / Yue	粵語 / 廣東話	“Yue language” / “Guangdong speech.” 粵 is the ancient regional name Yue; 廣東 = “east of Guang[nan]” historically	Lev 16:29 — Day of Atonement. 粵 itself isn't especially Levitical, but 廣 “broad/wide” works nicely with the goat being sent into the open wilderness
Modern	Hakka	客家話	“Guest-family speech.” 客 = guest/stranger, 家 = household/family	Lev 19:33–34 — resident alien. Almost absurdly perfect: “When a stranger/guest resides with you…”
Modern	Southern Min / Hokkien	閩南語 / 福建話	“Southern Min language” / “Fujian speech.” 閩 is the ancient regional name Min; 福建 comes from the historical place names Fuzhou + Jianzhou	Lev 1:11 — north side of the altar. 南 literally means south, giving you the opposite compass direction
Modern	Teochew / Chaoshan	潮州話 / 潮汕話	“Tide-prefecture speech” / “Tide-Shantou speech.” 潮 = tide	Lev 15 — bodily flows/discharges. 潮 “tide, flood, flowing water” is wonderfully appropriate
Modern	Fuzhou / Eastern Min	福州話 / 閩東語	“Blessing-prefecture speech” / “Eastern Min language.” 福 = blessing, good fortune	Lev 26:3–13 — covenant blessings. This one is nearly exact: obey YHWH → 福, blessing/prosperity
Modern	Shanghainese / Wu	上海話 / 吳語	“Above/on-the-sea speech” / “Wu language.” 上海 = “upon/above the sea”	Lev 11:9–12 — sea creatures. 海 literally = sea
Modern	Suzhounese / Wu	蘇州話 / 吳語	“Su-prefecture speech” / “Wu language.” 蘇 can mean revive, come back to life (though here it's a place name)	Lev 14:1–20 — restoration after skin disease. 蘇 = revive/recover makes a nice cleansing/restoration pun
Modern	Gan	贛語	“Gan language.” 贛 is fundamentally the regional/river name rather than a transparent everyday semantic compound	Lev 11:9–12 — aquatic animals, loosely via the Gan River association
Modern	Xiang	湘語	“Xiang language,” named for the Xiang River	Lev 14:5–6 — purification over running/living water. A river-name language for the ritual using living water
Modern	Jin	晉語	“Jin language.” 晉 historically names the ancient state; as a verb it also means advance/promote	Lev 21:10 — the priest exalted/promoted above his brothers. 晉 = advance in rank
~900–1200 CE	Late Middle Chinese	晚期中古漢語	“Late-period Middle-Ancient Han language.” 晚 = late; 中古 = middle antiquity	Lev 27 — valuations/redemption at the end. 晚 “late/end-stage” → appropriately the last chapter
~600–900 CE	Early Middle Chinese	早期中古漢語	“Early-period Middle-Ancient Han language.” 早 = early	Lev 23:10 — firstfruits. 早 = early, hence the early/first produce
~600 CE onward as a reconstructed system	Middle Chinese	中古漢語	“Middle-Ancient Han language.” 中 = middle/center	Lev 16:17 — the sanctuary's inner/central ritual. 中 = middle/interior works with the central sanctuary
~25–220 CE	Eastern Han Chinese	東漢漢語	“Eastern Han Chinese.” 東 = east; 漢 = Han	Lev 16:14 — sprinkle blood eastward. This is excellent: the text explicitly specifies the east side
~1250–200 BCE	Old Chinese	上古漢語	“Upper/High Antiquity Han language.” 上古 = highest/earliest antiquity	Lev 25:8–17 — Jubilee/restoration of ancestral holdings. 古 = ancient/old, nicely matching return to ancestral property


A few of these are much better than the others. If we're judging purely by “holy shit, that sounds like something Leviticus actually talks about,” I'd pull out:

客家 / Hakka is the jackpot among the dialect names themselves. 客 kè is exactly the ordinary semantic territory of guest, visitor, stranger, while Leviticus 19:34 commands that the גֵּר—the resident outsider living among Israel—be treated like a native and loved as oneself. So “Guest-Family Language” practically comes with its own Leviticus cross-reference.
