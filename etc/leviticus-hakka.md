
Let's use Taiwan Sixian Hakka (四縣腔) so we're dealing with one defined sound system rather than silently mixing Hakka varieties. This matters more than it did for Hokkien: Taiwan's MOE dictionary explicitly distinguishes Sixian, Hailu, Dabu, Raoping, Zhao'an, and Southern Sixian readings. 

For our purposes, the jackpot sound neighborhoods are:

YA ≈ ia — importantly, 野 “wilderness/wild” = iaˊ (ia24) in Sixian Hakka. 

WEI ≈ vi / vui — Hakka gives us a real /v/ initial here. For example 威 “might/authority” = viˊ (vi24), while 唯 occurs as viˇ (vi11). 


So Hakka can produce things sounding roughly ia-vi, which is arguably closer to Yahweh than Mandarin ya-wei.

And 野違 is again the monster hit.

In Sixian Hakka, 野 is actually attested as ia24, “open country/field; wilderness,” while Hakka's WEI-like series naturally tends toward vi, thanks to its /v/ initial. 

So:

> 野違 ≈ ia-vi ≈ “wilderness + transgression.”



That's not merely semantically excellent for Lev 16:21–22; /ia vi/ is also arguably a better raw phonetic approximation to “Yahweh” than Mandarin /ja wei/.

One correction to the Hokkien pass, though: we were a little too liberal about assuming character readings from syllable families. For Hakka I'd keep verified individual readings separate from candidate semantic pairings until each character is checked in the MOE Hakka dictionary. The official dictionary supports accent-by-accent searching, so we can make the next pass rigorous.
