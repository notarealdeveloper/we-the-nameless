
Leviticus

1. Well done — new. The animal is completely burned, i.e. extremely well-done; “well done” also means the sacrifice/job was performed correctly. Better than Burnt offering, which was merely literal.


2. Grain expectations — Grain offering + Great Expectations. Also literally the chapter’s expectations/rules concerning grain.


3. Burning fat — The sacrificial fat is literally burned; “burning fat” is what modern humans try to do at the gym.


4. Sin tax — Sin produces a required payment in livestock/flour; a “sin tax” is also a modern tax imposed on undesirable behavior.


5. Fine print — These are detailed rules concerning culpability, confession, restitution, and payments: the legal fine print, some of which tells you your fine.


6. Burn notice — The altar fire and burnt-offering procedures are a literal notice about burning; a “burn notice” also means official termination/disavowal.


7. Terms of consumption — Regulations determine who may eat which sacrifices and when; sounds like contractual “terms of consumption.”


8. Clerical installation — Aaron and sons are formally installed as priests; “clerical” also means office work, and “installation” sounds like installing personnel/software.


9. Trial by fire — The new priesthood’s inaugural performance culminates in divine fire; idiomatically, a first severe test of competence.


10. Strange fire; Fired priests — They offer “strange fire”; divine fire kills them. They are therefore spectacularly, literally fired from the priesthood.


11. Clean eating — “Clean” animals may be eaten; also the modern lifestyle phrase clean eating.


12. Labor regulations — Regulations following childbirth; labor means both childbirth and work governed by regulations.


13. Spot checks — Priests repeatedly inspect suspicious skin spots; “spot check” means a quick inspection.


14. House cleaning — Purification of skin disease and literally of infected houses; also ordinary “house cleaning.”


15. Fluid dynamics — Rules governing semen, menstrual blood, and other bodily fluids; also the branch of physics.


16. Scapegoating — One goat literally becomes the scapegoat; the ritual supplied English with the metaphorical sense of transferring guilt to someone else.


17. Blood work — new. The chapter regulates the ritual handling of blood; “blood work” is also medical testing.


18. Forbidden relations — new. “Relations” means sexual intercourse/relationships and relatives. The chapter forbids both particular sexual relations and relations with particular relations.


19. Moral fabric — new, much better. The chapter lays out the moral fabric of community life while literally legislating fabric mixtures in v.19.


20. Capital offenses — The chapter lists offenses carrying death penalties: literally capital offenses. “Offense” simultaneously means prohibited conduct and an affront.


21. Clerical standards — Standards for priests, i.e. clergy; also sounds exactly like HR standards for clerical employees.


22. Holy orders — Rules/orders concerning holy food and offerings; “Holy Orders” is also the later technical name for clerical ordination.


23. Holy days — The sacred calendar consists of holy days; “holiday” itself descends from essentially that expression.


24. Light bread; Stoned speech — Lamps = light, showbread = bread, giving the deliberately ambiguous noun phrase “light bread.” Then a man’s speech gets him literally stoned.


25. Lease release — Jubilee returns land and releases indentured Israelites; in modern property language, release from a lease.


26. Terms and conditions — Covenant terms, with material conditions that follow obedience or disobedience. Also every contract’s dreaded footer.


27. Face value — People are assigned monetary valuations according to age/sex: literally a value attached to a person’s face/person. “Face value” is also an ordinary financial term.

Numbers

1. Numbers — Almost too perfect to improve: the book named Numbers opens by making everybody produce some numbers.


2. Human shield — The tribes form concentric human barriers around the sacred center; militarily, that arrangement is quite literally a human shield.


3. Prime numbers — Levites are numbered while replacing Israel’s firstborn—its “prime/first” humans. Meanwhile prime numbers is mathematical language.


4. Carry-on limits — Levites are assigned what sacred objects they may carry, with strict age and handling limits; also airline carry-on restrictions.


5. Trial separation — new. Unclean people are separated from camp, while the suspected adulteress undergoes an actual trial concerning marital separation/betrayal. “Trial separation” is also a marital arrangement.


6. Hair apparent — Nazirite hair must grow conspicuously; hair apparent ≈ heir apparent.


7. Twelve-step program — Twelve tribal leaders make essentially the same offering over twelve successive days: an extremely literal twelve-step program.


8. Light work — Aaron tends the lights; Levites are consecrated for their work. “Light work” also means an easy job.


9. Cloud schedule — Israel travels or camps according to the cloud; the cloud literally controls their schedule. Also sounds like cloud-computing infrastructure.


10. Trumpet call — Trumpets issue calls for assembly/departure/war; “trumpet call” is already both literal sound and summons.


11. Fowl mood — The people are in a foul mood; YHWH supplies fowl until they regret asking.


12. White privilege — Miriam challenges Moses’ uniquely privileged prophetic status and is struck startlingly white. The provocative modern phrase gets split into its two literal components.


13. Minority report — Ten spies say no; Joshua and Caleb form the dissenting minority report. Also the familiar title/phrase.


14. Forty-year sentence — Israel receives a forty-year sentence as punishment; YHWH also literally pronounces that sentence.


15. Fringe benefits — Israel is ordered to wear fringes/tassels; the rule is presented as spiritually beneficial. Also employee compensation.


16. Ground rules — Korah challenges the rules governing authority; the ground opens and settles the disagreement.


17. Staff meeting — Tribal leaders’ staffs are gathered together for a meeting/test; Aaron’s staff wins.


18. Clerical benefits — Priests and Levites receive tithes, portions, and other compensation: literally clergy employee benefits.


19. Red tape — A red heifer produces an elaborate purification procedure; “red tape” means cumbersome ritual/bureaucratic procedure.


20. Rock bottom — Moses strikes the rock; the episode is the bottom of his career, costing him entry to the land, and Aaron dies.


21. Snake oil — A bronze snake functions as a healing device; “snake oil” is the classic term for a supposed miracle cure. Here, awkwardly, the snake oil actually works.


22. Talking ass — Balaam’s ass literally talks; “talking ass” also sounds like an assessment of either Balaam or anyone talking nonsense.


23. Curse reversed — Balaam is hired to curse Israel but produces blessing instead: the curse is literally reversed.


24. Star witness — Balaam becomes the involuntary witness for Israel, and his oracle famously sees a star coming from Jacob. Legal witness + celestial star.


25. Spear pressure — Phinehas solves Israel’s Baal-peor/sexual conformity problem with a spear; “spear pressure” echoes peer pressure, which helped get everybody into the mess.


26. Recount — This is the second census: they count everybody again. Hence, a recount.


27. Daughter rights; Succession plan — Zelophehad’s daughters establish inheritance rights; immediately afterward Joshua is appointed under Moses’ succession plan. Sounds like one HR/legal department wrote the chapter.


28. Daily grind — Daily sacrifice is repetitive routine, the “daily grind,” and it repeatedly includes grain offerings that must be ground.


29. Holiday pay — Festivals/holidays come with mandatory sacrificial payments. Also modern paid-holiday compensation.


30. Binding agreement — new. A vow is an agreement whose words legally/ritually bind the speaker. Exactly what a binding agreement means in contract language.


31. Body count — new, and grimly better. The chapter counts dead enemy bodies and inventories surviving captive girls among the booty. “Body count” also has the modern sexual-partner meaning, making the chapter’s treatment of captive virgins especially blackly appropriate.


32. Settlement terms — new. Reuben and Gad want to settle east of Jordan; Moses negotiates the exact terms of that settlement.


33. Moving account — new. It is an account of Israel’s movements from station to station; “a moving account” ordinarily means an emotionally affecting narrative. The joke is that this particular moving account is basically an itinerary spreadsheet.


34. Border control — Detailed control/definition of Israel’s borders; also the modern state function.


35. Safe houses — Cities of refuge are legally designated safe places for accidental killers; a “safe house” is precisely a protected refuge.


36. Marrying within means — Heiresses must marry within their own tribal group; “marry within one’s means” ordinarily means choosing a financially suitable spouse.

Deuteronomy

1. Previously on Israel… — Moses begins an extended recap of the previous seasons. It is literally the Bible’s “previously on…”


2. Moving violations — Israel’s movements through Edom/Moab/Ammon come with rules about whose territory they may violate; then battles begin. Also traffic tickets.


3. King size — new. Og is a king, and the text bizarrely stresses his enormous bed. He is, with unusual literalness, king-size.


4. Image problem — Moses warns against making divine images; Israel therefore has an image problem in both the literal and reputational senses.


5. Second opinion — The Decalogue is delivered again, with some changed wording: a second statement of the divine judgment/opinion.


6. Hear here — Shema: “Hear, Israel”; and hear it here, in Moses’ speech. Also “hear, hear!”


7. Cancel culture — Israel is ordered to wipe out rival peoples’ cultic culture and destroy their cult objects. A brutally literal version of “cancel culture.”


8. Breadwinner — Manna/bread teaches dependence on YHWH, while the chapter warns that YHWH is ultimately the source of Israel’s economic success—the real breadwinner.


9. Golden oldie — Moses retells that old favorite, the golden calf story.


10. Tablet replacement — Moses receives replacement stone tablets; sounds like exchanging a broken iPad.


11. Terms apply — Blessing applies if Israel obeys; curse applies if it does not. Covenant fine print: terms apply.


12. Location services — Worship is centralized at the one divinely selected location; sounds like turning on GPS permissions.


13. False advertising — Prophets, dreamers, friends, or towns may “sell” other gods using deceptive signs and persuasion.


14. Clean living — Rules about clean/unclean food accompany tithing and communal practice; also the lifestyle slogan.


15. Release clause — new. The law contains a compulsory periodic release of debts and servants; “release clause” is contractual language.


16. Party rules — Regulations for the great festival parties, followed by rules for people who rule and judge.


17. Royal court — new and much cleaner. The chapter regulates the supreme judicial court and then the royal court by regulating the future king. One phrase genuinely denotes both subjects.


18. Medium rare — Mediums are prohibited; consequently one hopes they become rare. Also steak.


19. Safe harbor — Cities of refuge provide legal safe harbor; “safe harbor” is itself a legal doctrine/exemption phrase.


20. Rules of engagement — It is literally the rules governing military engagement; already a standard military phrase.


21. Cold case — An unidentified murder victim is found and nobody knows the killer: an ancient cold case, complete with forensic-ish communal procedure.


22. Mixed signals — Do not mix seed, animals, or fibers; the chapter then turns to disputed sexual signs/evidence and ambiguous accusations. Mixtures + signals.


23. Holy shit — moved here from Leviticus because here it is actually perfect. Israel’s military camp must remain holy because YHWH walks through it, so soldiers are commanded to dig a hole and bury their shit outside camp. Literal holy shit.


24. Separation agreement — Divorce begins with a written instrument governing marital separation; the chapter is full of contractual protections concerning pledges, wages, loans, and dependents.


25. Brother-in-law — The chapter contains the law requiring a dead man’s brother to marry his widow. He is literally the brother in the law.


26. Produce declaration — new. The worshipper brings agricultural produce and must recite a formal declaration; simultaneously, the law commands him to produce a declaration.


27. Curse words — The people literally pronounce prescribed curse words from Ebal. Usually that phrase means profanity.


28. Blessed if; Damned if — new. “If you obey…” → blessed. “If you do not…” → damned/cursed. It converts the idiom “damned if…” back into a brutally literal conditional.


29. Standing agreement — new. The chapter opens with everyone standing before YHWH in order to enter the covenant—an agreement. Hence a literally standing agreement.


30. Life choice — Moses explicitly sets life and death before Israel and tells them to choose life; therefore the climactic covenant decision is literally a life choice.


31. Change of command — Command passes from Moses to Joshua while Moses gives instructions concerning the commandments and Torah. Leadership command + verbal/legal command.


32. Swan song — Moses’ great final song immediately before his death: almost the dictionary definition of a swan song.


33. Parting gifts — new. Moses parts from Israel by giving each tribe a verbal gift: its blessing.


34. Dead end — Moses reaches the end of his journey and dies before crossing into the land. His road quite literally dead-ends.

II Samuel

1. Fallen king; Killer song — Saul is the fallen king, both militarily and literally dead. David composes a “killer song”: a powerful song about killed men, while the alleged killer is himself killed.


2. Split decision — Israel splits between David and Ish-bosheth; military contests decide the issue. Also the boxing/judicial phrase.


3. Side effects — new. Abner changes sides, defecting to David, and the principal effect is that Joab murders him. Changing sides has side effects.


4. Head of state — Ish-bosheth is Israel’s head of state; assassins remove his literal head and deliver it to David.


5. Capital gains — David captures Jerusalem and makes it his capital, while gaining territory, status, wives, and victories.


6. Arkward dancing — new. David dances wildly in front of the Ark, to Michal’s intense embarrassment. Literally ark-ward and socially awkward dancing.


7. House promise — David offers to build YHWH a house; YHWH instead promises to build David a dynastic house. The chapter itself makes the pun.


8. Kingdom come — David’s kingdom expands and subordinate kingdoms come under him; also the familiar religious phrase “thy kingdom come.”


9. Table manners — David’s treatment of Mephibosheth shows unusually gracious royal manners, specifically by giving him a permanent seat at the king’s table.


10. Beard minimum — Hanun shaves half the envoys’ beards, leaving them at a humiliating beard minimum. Sounds like bare minimum.


11. Bed, Bath & Beyond — new. David takes Bathsheba to bed; Bath = Bathsheba; then he arranges for her husband to be sent decisively beyond. Awful dad-pun surface, unusually exact three-part plot underneath.


12. Child support — David desperately fasts and prays in support of the sick child; the child dies anyway. “Child support” normally means the financial obligation created by sexual relationships.


13. Amnon-consensual — new. Amnon + nonconsensual. The entire hideous center of the chapter compressed into one malformed adjective.


14. Hair apparent — Absalom’s extraordinary hair is described, while he is the royal son positioning himself as an heir apparent.


15. Son of a coup — David’s son stages a coup. It also begins like the conventional curse “son of a…”


16. Rock bottom — David is at political rock bottom while Shimei literally throws rocks at him.


17. Counter counsel — new. Ahithophel gives one counsel; Hushai supplies counter-counsel designed specifically to defeat it. Also sounds like opposing counsel in court.


18. Hanging heir — new. Absalom, the would-be royal heir, is left hanging from a tree. Heir also evokes his notorious hair—though the text itself says his head caught in the oak, not explicitly his hair.


19. Return policy — David’s return to Jerusalem requires negotiations about whether and how the king will be brought back, plus decisions concerning Shimei and Mephibosheth. Literally a political return policy.


20. Heads will roll — new. Sheba’s rebellion ends when a woman negotiates peace and his severed head is tossed over the wall. The idiom becomes reportage.


21. Giant losses — David’s side kills several Philistine giants/descendants of giants; both sides also sustain grievous human losses amid famine and bloodguilt.


22. Rock music — David sings a long piece of music repeatedly calling YHWH his rock.


23. Mighty last words — new. The first unit is David’s last words; the second is his mighty men. “Mighty” deliberately reaches across the semicolon and modifies the wrong noun.


24. Fatal figures — new. David insists on obtaining census figures; the figures lead directly to a plague and an enormous death figure. Numbers become fatal.

II Kings

1. Drop dead — new. Ahaziah literally drops/falls through a lattice and Elijah informs him that he is going to die. “Drop dead” becomes a plot synopsis.


2. Upward mobility — Elijah moves dramatically upward into heaven, while Elisha moves upward professionally by inheriting his role.


3. Ditch effort — The coalition is told to dig ditches everywhere before battle; it is also effectively a last-ditch military effort.


4. Raising capital — new. Elisha provides the widow with oil she can sell for capital, and later literally raises the Shunammite’s child from death. Economic raising + resurrection.


5. Clean bill — Naaman receives a clean bill of health, while money/payment—his attempted bill for the cure—drives the Gehazi sequel.


6. Cutting edge; Siege mentality — The lost iron cutting edge of an axe floats; then Samaria develops a grotesque literal siege mentality under siege.


7. Market correction — The siege’s absurd food prices suddenly collapse once the Arameans flee: possibly the Bible’s most violent market correction.


8. Regime change — Elisha predicts Hazael’s rise; Hazael murders Ben-hadad and becomes king. Modern geopolitical euphemism rendered literally.


9. Window dressing — Jezebel puts on makeup/dresses herself and appears at the window immediately before being thrown out of it. The idiom “window dressing” means cosmetic presentation.


10. Heads up — Jehu receives the severed heads of Ahab’s seventy sons and has them piled up. Ordinarily “heads up!” means “warning!”


11. Hidden heir — Joash, the surviving royal heir, spends six years literally hidden from Athaliah. Not really a pun, but the paradox is so exact and compact that I’d keep it.


12. Building fund — new. Money is accumulated in a chest to repair the Temple building; simultaneously they are literally building a fund.
