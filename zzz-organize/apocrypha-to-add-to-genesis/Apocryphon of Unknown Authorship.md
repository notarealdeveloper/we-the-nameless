Though the group's name is unknown, a common belief among enthusiasts is that they call themselves **The Ketuvim**, or **The Ktavites**.

The term is from the Hebrew word \heb{כתבים} _Kətavim_ meaning Writings, or Writers.

This is sometimes spelled \heb{כותבים} _Kotvim_, meaning Writers.

The popular etymology of the name is that it was chosen in contrast to the \heb{קראים} _Qaraʾim,_ a sect whose bible consists of only the Torah, and whose name means "Readers," in contrast with the Rabbinites, whose texts consist of the Tanak, and later Rabbinic writings and oral teachings including the Talmud, the Mishnah, the Midrash, and the Siddur, among others.

That is, though unsubstantiated, the common belief is that the group identifies itself with the _Authors_ (sic; often capitalized by spinoff sects) of the biblical texts. It remains unclear what the original authors of the text believed. Even if the above is true, it is not clear in what sense the group "identifies" with the biblical authors.
