	}

	\aA{
		Ox: An adult cow. Basically a farm tractor made of meat.

	\aA{

	\aA{

					\hline
		}
		\endgroup

	\aA{
		Let's agree that ``1 ox + 1 fatling'' is about 30 shekels in 1000 BC, or about 30 months work at the average wage.
	}

	\aA{

	}

	\aA{

	\aA{
		David wants the God Box, because it's lucky and he wants the luck.

	}

	\aA{
		How do you make the gods happy?
	}

	\aA{
		Naturally, sacrifices.
	}

	\aA{
		So, the text notes that David kills an ox and a fatling every time the guys move six steps.
	}

	\aA{
		Now, in our more conservative estimate of the distance of house to palace, that's 2,000 steps, or 2000/6 = 333 adult cows and 333 young cows (or whatever animal that was) for a total of 666 dead animals weighing almost exactly half a million pounds, spread out over a mile long walk, at a cost of about 10,000 shekels total, or 10,000 months (833 years) of work at the average wage.
	}

	\aA{
		However, if ``Obed-edom the Gath guy'' does in fact live in Gath, then we're looking at 80,000/6 = 13,333 sacrifices of 1,500 lbs each for a total of 20 million pounds of meat stretching over 32.3 miles in a very much not refrigerated outdoor mid-day sun, at a cost of 400,000 shekels total, or 400,000 months (33,333 years) of work at the average wage.
	}

	\aA{
		Now During the Davidic era (around 1000 BC), Jerusalem was a small hilltop fortress town.
	}

	\aA{
		Most mainstream archaeologists and historians estimate the population of Jerusalem was roughly 1,500 to 2,000 people.
	}

	\aA{
		The estimated population of Israel overall during the Davidic period ranges drastically between 40,000 to 300,000 people according to archaeological estimates, compared to several million people implied by biblical texts.
	}

	\aA{
		Taking the geometric mean of 40k and 300k, we get an estimate of about 100,000 people, halfway between the two estimates on a logarithmic scale.
	}

	\aA{
		Now, the shekel is a unit of weight, with the unit of currency being the amount of silver with that weight. A shekel is about 11-14 grams of silver.
	}

	\aA{
		A common back-of-the-envelope estimate is that an ordinary person in the Iron Age produced goods and services worth roughly 10–20 shekels per year (most of it consumed directly rather than sold).
	}

	\aA{
		Using 15 shekels/person/year as a midpoint:
	}

	\aA{

		\setlength{\tabcolsep}{0.35em}

			\hline
			Kingdom of David & \~{}100,000 & \~{}1.5 million shekels/year \\
			\hline
		}
		\endgroup
	}

	\aA{
		So we're looking at a ceremony with a cost somewhere between about 1/3 of the GDP of Jerusalem on the low end, and 1/3 of the GDP of all of Israel on the high end.
