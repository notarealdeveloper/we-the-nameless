\Verse{54}
{
	\hX{וַיִּקַּח דָּוִד אֶת־רֹאשׁ הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי וַיְבִאֵהוּ יְרוּשָׁלָ͏ִם וְאֶת־כֵּלָיו שָׂם בְּאהֳלוֹ׃}
}
{
	\eX{
		David took the head of the Philistine and brought it to Jerusalem;
		and he put his weapons in his own tent.
	}
}
{
	\aB{
		David takes the head to Jerusalem which is sort of a minor plot hole.\footnote{Jerusalem isn't conquered until 2 Samuel 5. Judges 1:21 says the Jebusites were still living there with Benjamin. David has no meaningful connection with Jerusalem before he captures it. Rather, David is associated with Bethlehem (his birthplace), Judah and Hebron (where he reigns for 7½ years), the wilderness (Adullam, Ziph, En-gedi), and Ziklag (given to him by Achish) but Jerusalem is essentially absent from David's story until the conquest.}
	}

	\aB{
		And he also takes his weapons to his tent.
	}

	\aB{
		\Table{lrrrr}{
			From / To & Elah Valley (battle) & Tent \textsl{(assumed near battlefield)} & Bethlehem & Jerusalem \\
			\textbf{Elah Valley (battle)} & -- & $\sim$0.5 mi & $\sim$13 mi & $\sim$16.5 mi \\
			\textbf{Tent \textsl{(assumed near battlefield)}} & $\sim$0.5 mi & -- & $\sim$13 mi & $\sim$16 mi \\
			\textbf{Bethlehem} & $\sim$13 mi & $\sim$13 mi & -- & $\sim$5.5 mi \\
			\textbf{Jerusalem} & $\sim$16.5 mi & $\sim$16 mi & $\sim$5.5 mi & --
		}
	}

	\aB{
		Assuming standard walking times.
	}

	\aB{
		\Table{lrrrr}{
			From / To & Elah Valley (battle) & Tent \textsl{(assumed near battlefield)} & Bethlehem & Jerusalem \\
			\textbf{Elah Valley (battle)} & -- & $\sim$10 min & $\sim$260 min & $\sim$330 min \\
			\textbf{Tent \textsl{(assumed near battlefield)}} & $\sim$10 min & -- & $\sim$260 min & $\sim$320 min \\
			\textbf{Bethlehem} & $\sim$260 min & $\sim$260 min & -- & $\sim$110 min \\
			\textbf{Jerusalem} & $\sim$330 min & $\sim$320 min & $\sim$110 min & --
		}
	}

	\aB{
		Ok so...
	}

	\aB{
		Assuming the tent is near the battle site.
	}

	\aB{
		Battle to Jerusalem:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item 5 hours 30 mins.
			\item Holding the head.
			\item Holding the weapons.
		\end{itemize}
	}

	\aB{
		In Jerusalem
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Presumably he drops the head off.
			\item With the Jebusites.
			\item Who run Jerusalem then.
			\item Which is a weird thing to do.
			\item Because they seem to be Canaanites.
			\item Not Philistines.
			\item And not Israelites.
			\item So they're unlikely to have any interest in this head, positively or negatively.
			\item That said, it's a severed head.
			\item So probably negative.
			\item All else being equal.
			\item Back to the road.
		\end{itemize}
	}

	\aB{
		Jerusalem to Tent:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item About 5 hours 20 mins.
			\item Holding the weapons.
			\item No longer holding the head.
			\item Assuming he dropped it off with the Jebusites.
		\end{itemize}
	}

	\aB{
		Tent to Home:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item 4 hours 20 minutes.
		\end{itemize}
	}

	\aB{
		Alternatively, to play apologetics:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item If his tent was at home in Bethlehem.
		\end{itemize}
	}

	\aB{
		Then we shave about 8 hours off the total walk.
	}

	\aB{
		In addition:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item If we don't require the ordering of the trips to match the order in the text.
			\item If we assume he had someone to help him carry the stuff.
			\item If we assume he didn't walk, but drove some cows in a cart or something.
			\item Or any other such assumptions.
		\end{itemize}
	}

	\aB{
		We still have to explain taking a severed head to a city that neither side of this battle was involved with.
	}

	\aB{
		Which is a pretty weird behavior, to say the least.
	}

	\aB{
		Not just being silly here. There are entire academic papers on this.

		But we've had enough fun in this verse.
	}

	\aB{
		Moving on.
	}
