## NHS

NHS. (נחש).
- The Hebrew term for snake.
- As used in the first words of Genesis 3, by the author of the J source.
- The term is spelled identically in biblical Hebrew to the term נַחַשׁ, meaning: magic, sorcery, spell, enchantment, augury; the term in Aramaic means "to divine," or "divination."

## NSH

NSH. (נסה).
- Test, trial.
- A term characteristic of the E source.
- The term used in Genesis 22:1 to introduce the sacrifice of Isaac.

NSH. (なし).
- Nothing.

## HSN

HSN. (חשן).
- Priestly breastplate.
- The location of the The Urim and Thummim, sacred objects in the P source, used for divination.

## HNS

HNS. (حَنَش).
- Snake, Adder, Viper.
- A term used in the book of Dudetheyreontome.

## SNH

SNH. (סנה).
- The "bush" in the famous burning bush scene in Exodus 3.
- A Hebrew term of unknown meaning, never used outside of this story.
- Etymology uncertain, possibly derived from the unused root  סנה (saná) with the meaning of "to be sharp, sharpen".
- The term may be a deliberate pun on Sinai, a feature common in Hebrew texts.

SNH. (שנה)
- Year. The time it takes for the Earth to complete one revolution around the Sun.

## SHN

SHN (\*šḥn)
- Proto-semitic root, found in Akkadian, Ugaritic, Syriac, Hebrew, Arabic, Ethiopic, and Modern South Arabian, meaning heat, to be hot, to get warmer.

## NNSH

NNSH. (名無し).
- Nameless.

## NSHNSH

NSHNSH. (なしなし).
- Hard pass.
- Seriously no.
- None of the above.
- Nothing worth exploring.
