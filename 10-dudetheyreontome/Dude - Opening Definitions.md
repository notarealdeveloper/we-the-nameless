## NHS


Sinai
- The mountain at which the Ten Commandments were given to the Hebrew prophet Moses by God, according to the Book of Exodus in the Hebrew Bible/Old Testament.

Horeb
- In the Book of Deuteronomy, these events are described as having transpired at Mount Horeb.

Names
- Sinai and Horeb are generally considered by biblical scholars to refer to the same place.

## Names: Horeb / Sinai
- The location of these two mountains (or this one mountain) is unknown.
- It is considered one of the most sacred locations in the three major Abrahamic religions: Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.
- Scholars have argued however that the names of these two places (or the name of this one place) are in opposition.

## Sun and Moon
- Sinai may derive from the name of Sin, the Mesopotamian deity of the moon.
- Horeb appears to mean "glowing/heat," which seems to be a reference to the sun.
- Sinai and Horeb would therefore be the mountains of the moon and sun, respectively.

NHS. (נחש).
- The Hebrew term for snake.
- As used in the first words of Genesis 3, by the author of the J source.
- The term is spelled identically in biblical Hebrew to the term נַחַשׁ, meaning: magic, sorcery, spell, enchantment, augury; the term in Aramaic means "to divine," or "divination."

## SNH

SNH. (סנה).
- The "bush" in the famous burning bush scene in Exodus 3.
- A Hebrew term of unknown meaning, never used outside of this story.
- Etymology uncertain, possibly derived from the unused root  סנה (saná) with the meaning of "to be sharp, sharpen".
- The term may be a deliberate pun on Sinai, a feature common in Hebrew texts.

SNH. (שנה)
