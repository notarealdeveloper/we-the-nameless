------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 24:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why have times (of wrath) not been stored by Shaddai?

(Why) have those who know him not foreseen his days (of wrath)?

---

Footnote 24:1. Job accuses the deity of failing to stock an arsenal of punishments for the wicked—whose activities he will describe in what follows. Compare Deuteronomy 32:34–35. For “times (of disaster),” compare Psalm 10:1.

Footnote 24:1. See 20:28. The “Day of the Lord” is a well-known prophetic notion, a time at which the deity unleashes punishment. The wicked seem to show no fear of retribution.

## Job 24:2–4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

They pull back boundary-lines,

Steal flocks and graze them (as their own).

They drive off the orphan’s donkey,

Take in pledge the widow’s ox.

They turn the needy off the road;

The poor of the land are forced to hide.

---

Footnote 24:2–4. The wicked.

Footnote 24:2–4. For this crime, see Deuteronomy 19:14.

Footnote 24:2–4. For this crime, see Deuteronomy 24:6.

## Job 24:5–6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Wild asses go to the wilderness for their wage,

They look for prey in the desert, food for their young.

They harvest in a field not their own,

They collect the remains of the rich man’s orchard.

---

Footnote 24:5–6. The general syntax and sense of this difficult verse are illuminated by comparison with Psalm 104:23. The phrase *lo* “for him” is apparently dittography.

Footnote 24:5–6. The poor of the land (verse 4). Just as undomesticated animals must fend for themselves in the wild, the poor must find sustenance in available fields.

Footnote 24:5–6. Reading *belilo* “his fodder” as two words—*beli lo*, with several ancient translations, and see 18:15.

Footnote 24:5–6. The unique verb *liqqesh* is related to late crops (in the tenth-century BCE Hebrew inscription known as the Gezer Calendar) and late rain (*malqosh*)—they come only after the harvest and collect what remains.

Footnote 24:5–6. Reading (with several commentators) *‘ashir* “wealthy” for *rasha‘* “wicked.” There is no reason to single out the wicked as the owner of the orchard.

## Job 24:7–8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

They lie down naked at night for lack of clothing—

They have no cover against the cold.

They are soaked by mountain rain,

And for lack of shelter hug the rocks.

---

Footnote 24:7–8. Compare the language of Isaiah 25:4.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 24:9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

They rob the orphan from the breast,

And seize in pledge the child of the poor.

---

Footnote 24:9. The wicked.

Footnote 24:9. Hebrew “orphan” has the narrower meaning of “fatherless”—he may well have a mother.

Footnote 24:9. Reading *‘ul* “infant, child” for the preposition *‘al* (so Malbim and others).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 24:10–11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

They walk about naked for lack of clothing,

And though they starve, they must carry sheaves of grain.

When the olives drop, they press (them);

They must trample the winepress though they thirst!

---

Footnote 24:10–11. The poor.

Footnote 24:10–11. Reading *binshor zetim* “in the dropping of olives” (compare Anat) for *bein shurotam* “between their rows,” which provides no context or antecedent. The verb *nashar* is the Aramaic and Late Hebrew equivalent of *nashal*, said of olives in Deuteronomy 28:40.

## Job 24:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

From within the town the mortals howl,

And the throats of the wounded cry for help;

But Eloah does not hear (their) prayer.

---

Footnote 24:12. Reading with some versions and traditions: *yishma‘ tepillah* for *yasim tiplah* “(does not) treat as slander.” The reading is supported by Elihu’s echo in 34:28; and compare Job in 27:9.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 24:13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

These are among the rebellious of the land,

Who do not acknowledge his ways,

And do not dwell in his paths.

---

Footnote 24:13. Reading *’arets* “land” with the ancient Greek for *’or* “light.” These rebels against the ways of God are enumerated in the following verses. They specifically violate the sixth, seventh, and eighth of the Ten Commandments, in that order.

Footnote 24:13. God’s.

## Job 24:14c, 16–17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

And at night the thief walks about;

He breaks into houses in the dark.

During the day they seal themselves up.

They do not care for the light.

For all of them daybreak is deep-shade,

For they know well the terrors of deep-shade.

---

Footnote 24:14c, 16–17. This line from the end of verse 14 apparently belongs here. Reading *yehallek ganab* “walks about a thief”—or in Aramaic: *yehak ganab*—for *yehi ke-ganab* “he will be like a thief” (Dhorme and others).

Footnote 24:14c, 16–17. The thieves.

Footnote 24:14c, 16–17. For this sense of *yada‘* “to know,” see the comment on 9:21.

Footnote 24:14c, 16–17. Night is day for the wicked; see Job 38:15.

Footnote 24:14c, 16–17. Reading plural *yakkiru* for the singular.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 24:18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He floats-lightly upon the water;

His plot of land is accursed;

He cannot make headway through the orchards.

---

Footnote 24:18. The criminal.

Footnote 24:18. The point is, he can be seen, in spite of his efforts to act under cover. See Elihu’s elaboration of this theme in 34:21–22. For the image, compare Hosea 10:7.

Footnote 24:18. “Floats-lightly” puns on “accursed,” which in turn partially echoes “plot (of land).”

Footnote 24:18. He can run, but he cannot hide. Orchards serve as a hiding place in Judges 21:20–21.

## Job 24:19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Parching and heat remove the snow water;

(Thus) does Sheol (remove) those who sin.

---

Footnote 24:19. Literally, “steal.”

## Job 24:20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He can forget mercy;

His hope is the worm;

He will no more be remembered;

And wrongdoing will be broken like a tree.

---

Footnote 24:20. Reading *raḥamim* “mercy” for *reḥem* “womb, woman,” taking the extra *m* from the beginning of the following word. Compare the ancient translations.

Footnote 24:20. Reading *tiqwato* “his hope” for *metaqo* “is sweet to him” (which does not agree in gender with the word for “worm,” which metonymically represents the grave (similarly, Szczygiel). Compare Job 34:15.

## Job 24:21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He mates with a barren woman—she will not give birth.

(He mates with) a widow—it will do him no good.

---

Footnote 24:21. For this usage, compare Proverbs 29:3.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 24:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He may draw along mighty men with his strength;

But when he acts, he will find his life insecure.

---

Footnote 24:22. The Greek translation reads *’ebyonim* “the needy.”

Footnote 24:22. Literally, “stands up.”

Footnote 24:22. Reading *ḥayyaw* “his life” for *ḥayyin*, the Aramaic form of “life”; so several ancient translations. This fate alludes to the curse in Deuteronomy 28:66.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 24:23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He will allow him to feel safe, supported.

But his eyes are on their paths.

---

Footnote 24:23. God.

Footnote 24:23. Of the wicked. Compare the echo in Job 34:21.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 24:24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

They lift but a little, and they are gone;

They lower, and they shrivel like grass;

And they wither like the head of a stalk.

---

Footnote 24:24. God’s eyes.

Footnote 24:24. The wicked. Reading *we’einam* “and they are not” for *we’einenu* “and he is not” (with some ancient translations); as seen above (for example at 15:18), the combination *nw* and the letter (final) *m* are easily confused.

Footnote 24:24. *Kol* is a term for “grass” known from Arabic. Compare the Aramaic translation from Qumran; so R. Meyuhhas and some modern scholars.
