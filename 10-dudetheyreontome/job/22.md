------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Eliphaz the Teimanite and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Does a man bring benefit to El?

Can (even) the clever bring benefit to him?

Does Shaddai have an interest in your being just?

Does he make a profit from your integrity?

---

Footnote 22:2–3. Perhaps Eliphaz is making this inference on the basis of Job’s complaint in 10:14–15.

Footnote 22:2–3. Literally, “Or is there a profit (for him) if you make your ways whole?” Compare 4:6.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Is it from fear of you that he should accuse you,

That he should join a lawsuit with you:

---

Footnote 22:4. A pun: the term for “fear” is identical to that for “reverence” in 4:6, where it is associated with Job’s “integrity.”

Footnote 22:4. What follows (through verse 11) is a parody of the charges Job imagines the deity is making against him, on the basis of which he believes he is being punished by God (Tur-Sinai). The charges are too extreme to be serious—violations of the norms set forth in Exodus 22:21–26 and elsewhere. Eliphaz is mocking Job’s claim that the deity is paying him undue attention. The norms entailed here are traditional responsibilities of a just leader, reflected for example in the North Canaanite (Ugaritic) epic of Kirta.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:5–7, 9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

“You see, your evildoing is great!

There is no end to your crimes!

For you seize-collateral from your brothers without cause;

And you strip the clothes off the naked.

No water do you give the weary to drink;

And from the hungry you withhold food.

Widows you’ve dismissed empty-handed;

And you push back the arms of the fatherless.”

---

Footnote 22:5–7, 9. Verse 8 is out of place, interrupting the mock tirade. It was apparently miscopied here because the scribe saw the word “arm” in verse 9 and mistakenly copied verse 8, which also features the word “arm.” Realizing his error, the scribe returned to verse 9 and continued copying. Verse 8 makes sense following verse 14.

Footnote 22:5–7, 9. Reading second-person *tedakke’* with several ancient translations, in accord with the preceding verb forms instead of the third-person (and incongruent singular) *yedukke’.*


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

So the man of the arm, his is the earth;

And the (man of) raised face inhabits it.”

---

Footnote 22:8. Power.

Footnote 22:8. The recipient of the powerful’s favor, the privileged; see 2 Kings 5:1; Isaiah 3:3.

Footnote 22:8. Compare Job’s assertion in 9:24.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:10–11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

That is why all around you are traps;

And why sudden dread overwhelms you.

Your light goes dark—so you cannot see;

And a downpour of water blankets you.

---

Footnote 22:10–11. Reading *’oreka ḥashak* for *’o ḥoshek* “or darkness,” based on 18:6, style, and the Old Greek translation.

Footnote 22:10–11. Said for example of the drowning of the Egyptians in Exodus 14:28; 15:5, 10.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Truly, Eloah is in highest heaven;

At the top of the lofty stars!

---

Footnote 22:12. Literally, “head.” The received text begins the line with “And look!” which interrupts the syntactic sequence. The scribe apparently began writing “And the top” (*w-r-’-sh*), but wrote a *he* instead of a *shin* (*w-r-’-h*, “and look!”); and, without erasing the mistaken writing, wrote the desired word correctly (compare Budde). The scribal habit of not erasing errors, of simply continuing to copy the correct text, is deeply rooted in the ancient Near East and in the Bible. See note 5 above.

Footnote 22:12. There are two implications to Eliphaz’s point: God is too elevated to pay mind to Job in the way Job imagines; yet on account of his elevation he can see everything—contrary to Job’s assertions. Compare Psalm 33:12–13.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:13–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But you have said: “What does El know?

Can he enact justice from behind foggy cloud?

The clouds are his blind, so he cannot see,

As he walks about the rim of the sky.

---

Footnote 22:13–14. A direct rejoinder to Job’s assertion in 21:22; and compare 3:23.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:15–16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Don’t you observe the ways of the depraved,

That men of iniquity have trod—

Who have been severed before their time,

Their foundation swept away by a stream?

---

Footnote 22:15–16. There is a double sense: “observe” and “maintain.” Does Job not realize what happens to the wicked? Is he beginning to behave like them?

Footnote 22:15–16. Reading *‘awwalim* for *‘olam* “primeval” (ways); see 18:21 (compare Hayyut).

Footnote 22:15–16. Cut off from the land of the living (see Isaiah 53:8). Compare Aramaic *qetam* “lop off” (Kahana).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:17–18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who say to El: “Turn away from us!

What can Shaddai do to us?”

When he filled their houses with bounty,

When the wicked’s plan was remote from him?

---

Footnote 22:17–18. Reading *lanu* for *lamo* “to him/to them” with some of the ancient versions. Eliphaz is echoing Job in 21:14–15.

Footnote 22:17–18. Reading *menu* for *menni* “from me” with the ancient Greek. Eliphaz is echoing Job in 21:16.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:19–20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The righteous see (this) and rejoice,

And the innocent mocks them:

“I swear their substance is destroyed,

And their earnings consumed by fire!”

---

Footnote 22:19–20. An oath formula.

Footnote 22:19–20. Reading *qimam* for *qimanu* “our substance”; the final *m* was misread as the sequence *nw* (a ligature; see for example on 15:18).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pray be solicitous of him and be whole (again)!

Thus will the bountiful come your way.

---

Footnote 22:21. “Be solicitous” is the same verb rendered “benefit” above in verse 2.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pray accept the instruction of his mouth,

And fix his teachings in your heart!


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:23–25

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If you return to Shaddai you will be rebuilt—

(If) you keep depravity far from your tent;

And treat choice-gold as (no more than) soil,

And rocks in the stream as the gold of Ophir—

If Shaddai will be your choicest-gold,

And the silver that sparkles for you;

---

Footnote 22:23–25. An echo of Job’s mock wisdom in 12:14.

Footnote 22:23–25. Echoing Zophar in 11:14.

Footnote 22:23–25. Famed for its gold (Isaiah 13:12); imported from somewhere south of the land of Israel (see 1 Kings 9:28). “Ophir” puns on *‘apar* “dust, dirt” in the preceding line; “rocks” puns on “choice gold.” For *shat* with preposition in the sense of “treat as,” see for example Jeremiah 3:19.

Footnote 22:23–25. There is a double sense: if the deity will be “your fortress.”

Footnote 22:23–25. Derived from the verb “shine” in Job 10:22.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:26–27

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Then when you entreat Shaddai,

And lift your face up to Eloah (in prayer),

When you appeal to him, he will hear you;

And you will pay your vows in full.

---

Footnote 22:26–27. For the usage of this verb, compare 27:10; also Psalm 37:4.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Then when you issue a resolution,

Light will shine o’er your paths.

---

Footnote 22:28. Contrast Job’s perspective in 19:8 and verse 11 above.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 22:29–30

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For he brings low the high and haughty,

And the one of lowered eye he saves.

Since El rescues the innocent,

You’ll be rescued for the cleanness of your hands.

---

Footnote 22:29–30. The received text has: “For they are low, and you say, ‘back/body,’ ” which is obviously corrupted. In accordance with the parallel line, in which the deity is clearly the subject, the present line must have the deity as its subject. The conjunction of illuminating the devotee’s path and bringing the haughty low is found in Psalm 18:28–29. In view of verse 28 there and Isaiah 2:11–12, where we find the phrase *kol ge’eh wa-ram*, “every haughty and high (person),” I read (with Ginsberg, compare Ball): *ki hishpil ’et ram we-ge’eh*.

Footnote 22:29–30. The humble, the meek.

Footnote 22:29–30. “Innocent” is a forensic derivation from the basic meaning of “clean.” The received text has: “He rescues the un-innocent/unclean,” which makes no sense here. For *’i naqi* “un-innocent” (the negative *’i* is postbiblical) read *’el naqi* “El (rescues) the innocent” (for example Ball). Ibn Janah (11th c.) in *The Book of Roots* realized a letter must be supplied.

Footnote 22:29–30. Reading *we-nimlatta* “you will be rescued” for *we-nimlat* “he is rescued.”
