------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:1–2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

There is a site whence silver comes,

And a place for refining gold.

Iron is removed from the ground,

And copper is poured out of stone.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:3–4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He puts an end to darkness,

And he probes to every extreme—

Stone, dark, and deathly-shade.

He breaks open streams where no one dwells,

Forgotten by travelers,

Neglected by wayfarers.

---

Footnote 28:3–4. The deity, Eloah/Shaddai, referred to in 37:22–23. The deity’s prowess contrasts with the limited abilities of humanity.

Footnote 28:3–4. For the deity as the one who probes the earth’s depths and reveals light beneath the darkness, see for example 11:7; 12:22; 26:10; 38:19–20; Isaiah 42:16; other passages may also be compared.

Footnote 28:3–4. The plural suffix—*m* was omitted by haplography (preceding another *m*).

Footnote 28:3–4. More literally, “(the streams are) low in people passing by.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:5–6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Land from which comes crystal,

And below which jasper (glows) like fire.

A place whose stones are sapphire,

And whose soil is gold.

---

Footnote 28:5–6. Reading *’aḥlam* for *leḥem* “bread,” which is inapt here; see the feminine form in Exodus 28:19; 39:12.

Footnote 28:5–6. Reading *nopek* for *nehpak* “turned over”; see Ezekiel 28:13–14.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:7–8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

A path no vulture has known,

Never caught by the eye of a hawk.

Never trod by wild beasts,

Never walked by a lion.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:9–10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He reaches his hand down to flint-stone,

From the root overturns mountains.

He breaks open channels in rock,

And his eye beholds everything precious.

---

Footnote 28:9–10. For this as divine activity, see 9:5 above.

Footnote 28:9–10. The Hebrew (*ye’orim*) puns on “light” (*’or* in verse 11).

Footnote 28:9–10. For this as divine activity, see for example Habakkuk 3:9; Psalms 74:15; 78:15; 114:8.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He exposes the well-springs of rivers,

And their secrets he brings to light.

---

Footnote 28:11. Reading *ḥasap* for *ḥibbesh* “binds up”; compare 12:22.

Footnote 28:11. Vocalizing *mabbekei*, cognate to *nebek* in 38:16 and known in that form in Ugaritic.

Footnote 28:11. Altering the feminine possessive suffix to suit the masculine plural reference.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But where can wisdom be found,

And what is the site of understanding?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:13–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

No human knows the way there,

As it cannot be found in the land of the living.

The Deep says, “It is not in me,”

And Sea says, “It is not with me.”

---

Footnote 28:13–14. Reading *darkah* for *‘erkah* “its value” with the Greek; a contamination from verses 17 and 19.

Footnote 28:13–14. The subterranean sea, often rendered “the abyss” in Genesis 1:2 and elsewhere. The Deep is the home of the Canaanite father-god and purveyor of wisdom, El.

Footnote 28:13–14. Yamm in 7:12.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:15–19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

It cannot be purchased with bullion;

Its price can’t be weighed out in silver.

Nor placed on the balance with gold of Ophir,

Nor with precious carnelian or sapphire.

It can’t be compared to gold or quartz,

Nor exchanged for the finest of jewelry.

It cannot be mentioned with coral or crystal;

For wisdom is worth more than rubies.

It can’t be compared with the topaz of Cush;

Nor poised on the balance with pure gold.

---

Footnote 28:15–19. *Sagur* “solid gold” in 1 Kings 6:20–21; 7:40, 50; known also from Akkadian. The present digression on wisdom’s being more valuable than jewels elaborates similar passages in Proverbs 3:14–15 and 8:10–11.

Footnote 28:15–19. See at Job 22:24.

Footnote 28:15–19. More literally, “jewelry of fine gold.”

Footnote 28:15–19. Reading *mekes* (Leviticus 27:23) for *meshek* “leather pouch.” In Job the letter *sin* often serves instead of *samekh*.

Footnote 28:15–19. Somewhere between the Sinai and Ethiopia.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But whence does wisdom come,

And what is the site of understanding?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

It is hidden from the eyes of the living,

Concealed from the birds of the sky.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Abaddon and Death say,

“Our ears have heard something about it.”

---

Footnote 28:22. See at 26:6.

Footnote 28:22. The personified realm of the dead; see for example 38:17 and Psalm 6:6.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:23–26

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Elohim knows the way there,

He’s aware of its location.

For he can look to the ends of the earth,

See all that is under the sky.

When he weighed the weight of the wind,

And measured the measure of water;

And set the amount of the rainfall,

And the path of the thundercloud;

---

Footnote 28:23–26. Compare Isaiah 40:12–13; Jeremiah 31:36.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:27

------------------------------------------------------------------------

That’s when he looked and appraised it,

Measured and examined it.

---

Footnote 28:27. The deity inspected wisdom like a jeweler a jewel (Habel). Wisdom was created at the beginning of creation; compare Proverbs 8:22–31, some of whose language is used in this passage.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 28:28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Then he said to Adam:

“Revering the Lord is wisdom,

And turning from evil, understanding.”

---

Footnote 28:28. The first human or humans; or “to humanity”; the latter interpretation is favored by the Qumran Targum.

Footnote 28:28. These are two of Job’s qualities; see 1:2. Ironically, Job, not his companions, is here implied to be “wise.”
