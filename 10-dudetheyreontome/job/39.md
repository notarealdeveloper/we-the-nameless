## Job 39:1–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do you know the season that wild goats give birth?

Or observe the calving of hinds?

Count the months that they gestate?

Know the season they give birth—

As they crouch and deliver their young,

Sending forth their offspring?

---

Footnote 39:1–3. For “filling months” in this sense, compare Genesis 25:24.

Footnote 39:1–3. For *ḥebel* in this sense, and not “birth pangs,” see the cognate verb in the sense of giving birth (for example Psalm 7:15).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 39:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Their children gain strength and grow up in the wild;

Once they’ve gone out, they never return.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 39:5–8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who sends forth free the wild ass?

The onager—who cuts its cords?

Whose home I made the steppe,

Its habitat the barren land.

He scorns the city’s clamor;

Hears not the shouts of a driver.

He surveys the hills for his pasture;

Searches for some vegetation.

---

Footnote 39:5–8. Literally, “opens, loosens.”

Footnote 39:5–8. Compare 3:18: the servant is free from his driver—in death.

Footnote 39:5–8. Reading *yatur* for *yetur* “excess (?).”

Footnote 39:5–8. Literally, “any green.”

## Job 39:9–12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Will the wild ox want to serve you?

Will he bed down in your crib?

Can you tie the wild ox by rope to a furrow?

Will it harrow the lowland behind you?

Would you rely on him for a full yield,

Leaving your efforts to him?

Would you trust him to bring back your seed,

And collect (the grain) from your threshing-floor?

---

Footnote 39:9–12. The urus; usually spelled *re’em*, not *rem*.

Footnote 39:9–12. For this sense of *koaḥ* in Job, see 31:39 (compare Tur-Sinai).

Footnote 39:9–12. Reading *yashib* for *yashub* “he comes back.”

Footnote 39:9–12. Would you receive a full return on your seed if the plowing were left to him?

## Job 39:13–18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Does the wing of the ostrich flutter?

Does she fly (like the) stork and the falcon?

She abandons her eggs on the ground,

Letting them warm in the dirt;

Forgetful of feet that can trample them,

Of animals that can squash them.

She treats her young harshly as though they’re not hers;

She frets not that her effort’s for nothing.

For Eloah put wisdom out of her mind,

And gave her no share of perception.

And yet she can speed in a run,

Scorning the horse and its rider.

---

Footnote 39:13–18. Since the following line begins with the interrogative particle *’im*, the interrogative particle *ha-* must have dropped out by mistake.

Footnote 39:13–18. Read *ye‘enim* (see Lamentations 4:3) for *renanim* (with change in vocalization) “screechers.”

Footnote 39:13–18. The line is variously read. Making no alterations in the consonants, I read *’abrah* “it takes wing” for *’ebrah* “wing.” For this verb, see verse 26.

Footnote 39:13–18. Reading *nitsa* (feminine of *nets;* see verse 26) for *we-notsa* “and plumage.”

Footnote 39:13–18. I am omitting the particle *ki* “for, since” at the beginning of the line; it would seem to have been copied here by mistake. It would make better sense at the beginning of the following verse. The scribe may have skipped to the next verse, which begins without *ki*, and then, realizing his mistake, continued copying verse 14.

Footnote 39:13–18. Incubate.

Footnote 39:13–18. Literally, “it”; there are several exchanges of singular and plural in Job.

Footnote 39:13–18. The verb translated “speed” may be related to an Arabic term for “prodding” a horse. For *marom* “up high” (the ostrich cannot fly), read *merots* “run, race” (Ecclesiastes 9*:*11).

Footnote 39:13–18. An ostrich can outrun a horse at short distances.

## Job 39:19–25

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do you give the horse its bravery?

Do you clothe its neck with a mane?

Do you make it noisy as locusts?

The blare of his snorting is fright!

He paws (the ground) with power,

Eager (to ply) his strength,

As he enters into the fray.

He scorns the fearsome, does not flinch;

He does not recoil from the sword;

Even as a quiver whirs by him;

Or the blade of a dagger or spear.

With thunder and tremor he laps up the land;

He swerves not at the blast of the horn.

To the horn he answers “hurrah!”

He can smell battle from a distance—

The thunder of captains and battle cries.

---

Footnote 39:19–25. *Gebura* may pun on “mane,” which is *guparu* in Akkadian.

Footnote 39:19–25. Interpreted on the basis of context; *ra‘ma* suggests “thunder,” thereby punning on the next verse. Contrary to what is widely claimed, the word is not the Arabic term for “mane.”

Footnote 39:19–25. Reading *hed* “reverberation” for *hod* “splendor.” Compare Jeremiah 8:14.

Footnote 39:19–25. Literally, “digs”; reading the singular for the text’s plural.

Footnote 39:19–25. Compare in Ugaritic and Akkadian *emūqu*. See also Jeremiah 47:5.

Footnote 39:19–25. A quiver’s-full of arrows.

Footnote 39:19–25. Literally, “gulps.” He keeps advancing; compare Shakespeare: “he seemed in running to devour the way” (*Henry IV*, *Part 2*, 1:1).

Footnote 39:19–25. On the preposition *badei*, see at 11:3.

## Job 39:26–30

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Does the falcon take flight through your wisdom,

As it spreads its wings toward the south?

Does the eagle fly high at your command,

Or when it nests at an elevation—

Dwelling and bedding in rock,

Protected beneath a cliff?

From there it searches its food;

Its eyes can see from a distance.

Its fledglings swill blood;

Where there is carrion, it is there.

---

Footnote 39:26–30. “South” recalls “Teiman,” the region of Eliphaz (see at 2:11).

Footnote 39:26–30. Literally, “mouth.”

Footnote 39:26–30. Compare Jeremiah 49:16.
