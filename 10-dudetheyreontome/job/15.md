------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Eliphaz the Teimanite, and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Does a sage utter such windy speech,

And fill his belly with an east wind,

Arguing a case to no advantage,

With words that bring no benefit?

---

Footnote 15:2–3. In the preceding discourse (especially chapters 12 and 14), Job had sought to demonstrate his familiarity with wisdom sayings and images.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

You go so far as to abrogate piety,

And expropriate talk from the presence of El.

---

Footnote 15:4. That is, the divine council. Only prophets can participate in the heavenly council (see Jeremiah 23:22). The plain meaning of this verse, missed by virtually all preceding translators, is evident from verse 8 below. Job claimed to have heard the report from a member of the divine council in 4:12–21. The fact that that passage is misplaced in the book (see there) is clear from what Eliphaz says in this chapter. Eavesdropping on conversations in heaven is regarded as a severe offense; compare the Qur’an 37:35, where those listening in on divine dialogue are destined for meteor and comet attacks.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:5–6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For your own mouth denounces your crime,

As you adopt a devious tongue.

Your own mouth condemns you, not I;

Your own lips testify against you.

---

Footnote 15:5–6. Your admission of listening in on divine conversation demonstrates your transgressive inclination. Eliphaz counters Job’s claim that God’s mouth indicts him unjustly (see 9:20).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Are you the first human to have been born?

Were you engendered before the hills?

---

Footnote 15:7. Only someone present at the creation could have direct knowledge of God and God’s ways; see Proverbs 8:22–31, verse 25 of which is quoted here by Eliphaz.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Have you been listening in the divine council,

Expropriating wisdom to yourself?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:9–10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

What do you know that we do not know,

That you understand—but not intelligible to us?

With us are the gray-haired and aged—

Greater in years than your father.

---

Footnote 15:9–10. Literally, “not with us”—a pun on “people-of-intelligence” in 12:2. Eliphaz counters Job’s claims in 12:3 and 13:1–2.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Are the consolations of these (men) too few for you—

That you (claim to) have a secret word?

---

Footnote 15:11. For the short form of “these” (not “El”—who is not known to have comforted Job!), see for example Genesis 19:25; 26:3 (Gersonides).

Footnote 15:11. “Have”—literally, “is with you”—a play on “is not with (intelligible to) us” in verse 10 above.

Footnote 15:11. An allusion to 4:12.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:12–13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

How your heart carries you away,

And how haughty are your eyes,

That you answer El with your wind,

And out of your mouth put forth such words:

---

Footnote 15:12–13. Reading *yerumun* “are elevated”; see Proverbs 6:17; 30:13 (Luzzatto).

Footnote 15:12–13. Compare verse 2 above. It is a formulaic reference to speech; see 35:4.

Footnote 15:12–13. A clear introduction to direct discourse; see 8:10 and the preface to this speech. Eliphaz is mocking Job.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:14–16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

“What is a mortal that he be rendered-pure?

That a man born of a woman be found-right?

If he puts no trust in his holy ones,

And the heavens are not pure in his eyes,

All the more one detested and depraved,

Who drinks corruption as though it were water!”

---

Footnote 15:14–16. Echoing Job in 14:1; compare 25:4.

Footnote 15:14–16. The reference to the deity without a nearby antecedent demonstrates that the passage is based on 4:17–19, where the mention of God is explicit.

Footnote 15:14–16. The angels; see 5:1 and the comment there.

Footnote 15:14–16. A metonym for the angels; see Job 25:5.

Footnote 15:14–16. Compare Psalm 14:1–3. Eliphaz’s characterization of humanity pushes Job’s satirically; Job had only said (in the name of the spirit) that humans were flawed and mortal, not altogether decadent.

Footnote 15:14–16. Compare the image in 34:7.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:17–18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I will declare to you—listen to me!

What I have seen—I’ll recount to you.

What the sages have revealed

And did not conceal from our ancestors:

---

Footnote 15:17–18. Eliphaz’s reference to “seeing” is no more than a cliché of traditional wisdom; compare 4:8.

Footnote 15:17–18. Reading *’aboteinu* for the traditional text’s nonsensical *’abotam* “their ancestors.” The letters *nw* were written close together and misread as final *mem;* compare at 8:8.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

“To them alone was the land given,

And no alien has passed among them.

---

Footnote 15:19. To the righteous, the antithesis of the wicked. The antecedent of “them” is missing because Eliphaz is quoting an excerpt from a known context.

Footnote 15:19. Contra Job’s contention that “the land has been given to the hand of the wicked” (9:24).

Footnote 15:19. And profaned the land; see Joel 4:17.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:20–21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The wicked all his days is caught up in a tempest,

And the years in store for the terrible are few.

The sound of the frightful is ever in his ears,

Even in peace-time, the marauder overtakes him.

---

Footnote 15:20–21. Literally, “he whirls.” An allusion to Jeremiah 23:19, the only other place this unusual verb form occurs, in which a “whirling storm” is said to befall the wicked.

Footnote 15:20–21. An echo of Job’s quoted wisdom in 12:6.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:22–23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He’ll not again feel secure in the dark,

And he’s destined for the sword.

He’s ordained to be food for the vulture,

He knows a dark day is set firm for him.

---

Footnote 15:22–23. The same expression as “puts no trust” in verse 15 above.

Footnote 15:22–23. Reading *no‘ad* for *noded* “wandering”; and *’ayyah* for *’ayyeh* “where?” with support of the Old Greek translation.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hostility terrifies him and distress attacks him,

Like a king fitted out for the fray.”

---

Footnote 15:24. Reading the verb as written as a singular, and joining the following verb to the noun that precedes it. The second verb echoes Job’s usage in 14:20.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:25–27

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Because he bent his arm toward El,

And played the warrior against Shaddai,

Would run against him with strong neck,

With his thickly plated armor—

Because he has covered his face with fat,

And put blubber over his sinews,

---

Footnote 15:25–27. Eliphaz turns from platitudes to an elaboration of the last image.

Footnote 15:25–27. Taking a divine prerogative; see Isaiah 42:13. There is a subtle allusion to Job’s having characterized himself at the outset as a “man” (*geber*), cognate to “warrior” (*gibbor*).

Footnote 15:25–27. A figure for arrogance; compare the phrase “strong neck” in Psalm 75:6.

Footnote 15:25–27. That is, acted rebelliously; see Deuteronomy 32:15.

Footnote 15:25–27. An Arabic borrowing.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Now he must dwell in ruined towns,

In uninhabitable houses,

Fated to be mounds.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:29

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He will not be rich,

His substance will not last,

And his wealth will not grow.

---

Footnote 15:29. It is possible that Eliphaz resumes his quotation of traditional wisdom, which reminds one of Bildad’s citation of conventional imagery in chapter 8. Verse 35 below is a virtual quotation of Psalm 7:15.

Footnote 15:29. Literally, “their wealth will not extend over the land.” The term for “wealth” is borrowed from Babylonian *nēmelu* with metathesis.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:30

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He cannot move out of the dark,

Fierce heat dries up his shoot,

He’s removed by the wind of his mouth.

---

Footnote 15:30. The next clause makes clear that the image is of the wicked as a hapless plant; see 8:11–15.

Footnote 15:30. An echo of Job’s use of the term in 14:7.

Footnote 15:30. See 4:9. The reference to the deity is implicit by dint of the allusion.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:31

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let him not trust in salvation,

For it will prove false.

---

Footnote 15:31. Reading the sequence *b-sh-w n-t-‘-h* as a single word, partly corrupted: *bitshu‘ah*. For the idiom, see Psalms 78:22; 146:3.

Footnote 15:31. Compare Psalms 60:13 and 108:12 for the phrase “and false is human salvation.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:32–33

------------------------------------------------------------------------

His palm tree will wither before its time,

And his foliage will not thrive.

He sheds his unripened fruit like a grapevine,

Casts off his blossoms like an olive tree.

---

Footnote 15:32–33. Attaching the last word of the preceding verse and reading *timorato* “his palm tree” (see 1 Kings 6:29, 32, 35, with support from the ancient Greek translation) for *temurato* “his recompense.”

Footnote 15:32–33. Reading *timmal* “it will wither”; see 18:16 and compare Abraham ibn Ezra’s commentary.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:34

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For the impudent’s company is sterile and lone,

As fire consumes the tents of graft.

---

Footnote 15:34. See 3:7 with the note.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 15:35

------------------------------------------------------------------------

They conceive travail and give birth to suffering;

Their own bellies generate deceit.

---

Footnote 15:35. An echo of 5:7 and a virtual quotation of Isaiah 59:4 and especially Psalm 7:15.

Footnote 15:35. Connoting “womb, bowels,” as in 3:10–11 and elsewhere.
