------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Job and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I have heard many such (sayings)—

Futile comforters are you all:

“Is there no end to windy speech?”

Or “What provokes you to speak this way?”

---

Footnote 16:2–3. The term *‘amal* more often denotes “travail, suffering” and thus connotes it here: the companions’ efforts in consolation ironically produce even greater suffering for Job. For *‘amal* as a poetic synonym of *shaw’* “naught” see Job 7:3.

Footnote 16:2–3. A paraphrase of Eliphaz in 15:2. Job is (inexactly) quoting the kinds of things his companions have said.

Footnote 16:2–3. Compare Job in 6:25–26.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:4–5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Even I would speak like you,

If you were in my place;

I would harangue you with words,

And shake my head at you.

I would embolden you with my mouth,

And spare you my consolations!

---

Footnote 16:4–5. An apparent influence from Babylonian *habāru* in the sense of “to harangue” (Finkelstein).

Footnote 16:4–5. An apotropaic gesture, to ward off evil; see Jeremiah 18:16; Lamentations 2:15.

Footnote 16:4–5. Reading *’aḥsop* for *yaḥsop* “he would spare.”

Footnote 16:4–5. Literally, “the movement of my lips,” a gesture of condolence; see Job 2:11 and 42:11; elsewhere used of the head (Psalm 44:15).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I speak out, my pain will not be spared.

But if I desist, how will it leave me?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

By now he has worn me down;

You have devastated my entire company.

---

Footnote 16:7. Job resumes his complaints against the deity. The shift between third and second person (enallage) is unusual but not unique.

Footnote 16:7. Job’s family and friends. The term *‘edah* in this context echoes Eliphaz in 15:34 but also puns on *‘ed* “(hostile) witness” in the following verse. For the alienation of Job’s family from him, see chapters 19 and 29 and compare Psalm 88:9.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

You have shriveled me, and this has become a stigma;

My gauntness stands up and testifies against me.

---

Footnote 16:8. Literally, “a witness.” Diminishing him with a terrible skin disease, the deity has created a witness to his being on the wrong side of God.

Footnote 16:8. Alternatively: “You have subdued me and become (my) prosecutor; my repudiator has stood up and testified against me.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

As his anger rages, he strikes a hostile pose;

He gnashes his teeth at me;

My enemy sharpens his eyes at me.

---

Footnote 16:9. The idiomatic term for “raging” connotes the mauling of a wild animal. For the idiom, see Amos 1:11.

Footnote 16:9. The verb *satam*, “to take a hostile attitude, act with malice,” echoes the name of the Satan.

Footnote 16:9. Suggests the evil eye; compare Psalm 35:19.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

People’s mouths gape at me,

They strike my cheeks to shame me;

They all form gangs against me.

---

Footnote 16:10. Literally, “their”—enemies roundabout, encouraged by the divine stigma.

Footnote 16:10. Compare Psalm 22:14; Lamentations 2:16.

Footnote 16:10. Compare Lamentations 3:30.

Footnote 16:10. Compare Isaiah 31:4.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

It is El who delivers me to the depraved,

Who thrusts me into the hands of the wicked.

---

Footnote 16:11. Literally, “makes me fall” (Numbers 22:32).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I was tranquil, then he tore me apart,

Seized me by the neck and ripped me apart.

He set me up as his target.

---

Footnote 16:12. Recall Job’s image of God as a lion on the prowl in 10:16.

Footnote 16:12. Recall Job’s image of God the hostile warrior in 7:21; compare Lamentations 3:12.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:13–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

His archers surround me;

He pierces my innards, showing no mercy;

He spills my gall to the ground.

He opens against me breach upon breach;

He runs at me like a warrior.

---

Footnote 16:13–14. A refrain in Lamentations (2:2, 7, 21; 3:43).

Footnote 16:13–14. God is said to breach Job’s exterior the way an attacker breaches a city wall.

Footnote 16:13–14. Echoing Eliphaz in 15:24.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I sewed sackcloth onto my hide;

And sank my horn into the dust.

---

Footnote 16:15. A metaphor for pride; see Psalm 75:5–6. Job, the victimized stag, must lower his horns in defeat.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My face has turned sullen from weeping;

Over my bright-eyes is deathly-dark—

---

Footnote 16:16. Literally, “darkened”; compare Lamentations 5:17.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Over no wrongdoing by my hand;

And my prayer is pure:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

“O earth, do not cover my (innocent) blood!

Let my outrage have no place (to hide)!”

---

Footnote 16:18. Many find here an allusion to Genesis 4:10.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For even now my witness is in the heavens,

The one who knows the truth is on high.

---

Footnote 16:19. “Knows the truth” is literally “testifier,” a synonym (in Aramaic) for “witness.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My companions are my taunters;

My eye pines for Eloah!

---

Footnote 16:20. “Taunters” (see Psalm 119:51) differently derived means “advocates” (for example Job 33:23). Again ironically, Job’s consolers are his disparagers (see 16:2 above).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For this man has a case with Eloah,

(Like) a human with his companion.

---

Footnote 16:21. Recall that Job at the outset (3:3) referred to himself as “man.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 16:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When a few more years come,

I will go whence I’ll not return.
