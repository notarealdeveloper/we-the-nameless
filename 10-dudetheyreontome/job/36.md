------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Elihu spoke again and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:2–4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Stay with me a while and I’ll tell you;

For Eloah has more words to say:

“I’ll take up my discourse from afar;

And provide my creature with justice.”

My words, in truth, are no lie;

You have here a man of honest intent.

---

Footnote 36:2–4. This line is entirely in Aramaic (so Rashi).

Footnote 36:2–4. Although most understand Elihu to be speaking, the deity, not Elihu, can speak to Job “from afar.” Moreover, others must interpret “my creature” (*po‘ali* “the work [of my hands]”; see for example Psalm 90:16) as “my Maker”; but this well-attested term is nowhere else used this way.

Footnote 36:2–4. Contra Job’s characterization of the companions in 13:4.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:5–6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

El, you see, is eminent in strength,

And does not reject the pure of heart.

He does not keep the wicked in life;

But fairness he gives to the meek.

He does not pry justice from the righteous.

---

Footnote 36:5–6. The received Hebrew is very awkward. Elihu is clearly responding to Job’s claims in 9:19, 22, and elsewhere. On the basis of Job 9:4 (Job) and 8:20 (Bildad), respectively, I read (similarly to Clines, *Job 21–37*): *hen ’el kabbir koaḥ/welo’ yim’as bar lebab*.

Footnote 36:5–6. Elihu is rebutting Job in 21:7.

Footnote 36:5–6. Ordinarily “the poor”; however, the term *‘aniyyim* often stands for *‘anawim* “the meek,” as in Psalm 9:13 (Tur-Sinai).

Footnote 36:5–6. On the basis of the Greek version, read here (with several moderns) *dino* for *‘ena(y)w* “his eyes.” If “his eyes” is original, however, compare Psalm 34:16. This line belongs to verse 7 in the traditional text.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:7–10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He places kings on the throne,

Seating them forever up high.

But if he binds them in shackles;

(If) they’re caught in cords of affliction;

He reveals to them their misdeeds,

Their growing number of crimes.

He opens their ears to (his) discipline,

Saying, turn back from iniquity.

---

Footnote 36:7–10. Reading *we-shat* for *we-’et* (direct object indicator, which is odd since there is no verb); see Psalm 137:11. (For another mistaken writing of *aleph* for *shin*, see Proverbs 28:18: *’aḥat* “one” written for *shaḥat* “pit.”)

Footnote 36:7–10. Contrast Job in 12:17–18.

Footnote 36:7–10. Reading *’asaram* for *’asurim* “they are bound”; the deity is the subject; compare the recapitulating form in verse 13.

Footnote 36:7–10. See at 33:18.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:11–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If they pay heed and do (as he says),

They will end their days in bounty,

And their years in prosperity.

But if they do not pay heed,

They will cross the channel,

Expiring for lack of wisdom.

For the impudent of heart will be brazen:

Though bound, they will not pray.

They will die at (the peak of their) youth;

Lose life among the reckless young.

---

Footnote 36:11–14. The verb is used in its Aramaic sense; compare 34:25.

Footnote 36:11–14. Echoing Job in 21:3.

Footnote 36:11–14. See at 33:18.

Footnote 36:11–14. Literally, “they do not put the nose,” a unique idiom. Compare the phrase “to raise the horn,” a figure from the realm of animals which has the sense of showing pride.

Footnote 36:11–14. Literally, “although he (the deity) has bound them.”

Footnote 36:11–14. Literally, “their life-force (will die) among male prostitutes.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:15–16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He pulls the poor out of affliction,

And opens their ears through distress.

He draws you away from adversity—

An expanse with below nothing firm;

And now your table is lavishly set.

---

Footnote 36:15–16. Informs them; see verse 10.

Footnote 36:15–16. “Distress” puns on “pulls out” in the preceding line.

Footnote 36:15–16. For this usage of the verb, see 2 Chronicles 18:31.

Footnote 36:15–16. Compare 37:10.

Footnote 36:15–16. Literally, “the top of your table is full of rich food.” “Top” is apparently cognate with Ugaritic *nḫt* “seat.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The case against the wicked you do not make;

But claims against the orphan you support.

---

Footnote 36:17. The Hebrew makes little sense as a whole, so moderns tend to emend it heavily; meanings the Hebrew cannot bear are often proposed. I read this line (with Tur-Sinai): *we-din resha‘im lo’ tadin*, literally, “the judgment of the wicked you do not judge.”

Footnote 36:17. Reading (compare Leveque): *u-mishpat yatom titmok*. The line would be too short without assuming a word, such as *yatom—*which shares letters with the following verb—was accidentally omitted. Compare Eliphaz in 22:5–9.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:18–19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Beware lest affluence draw you in;

Lest rich reward lead you astray.

Can your prestige be compared to gold?

Can any of your mighty efforts?

---

Footnote 36:18–19. Reading *ḥemeh* (Aramaic); for the Hebrew equivalent, see verse 21. The preceding word *ki* is dittography from the preceding line.

Footnote 36:18–19. Reading *le-betser* for *lo’ be-tsar* “not in distress” or the like.

Footnote 36:18–19. For the phrase “mighty efforts” in the sense of wealth, see Proverbs 24:5. It could also mean: “Can any of your enterprises (be compared to gold)?”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do not yearn through the night,

For the folk to rise up from their places.

---

Footnote 36:20. To begin the workday? Compare Job in the first part of chapter 7. The connection of the verse to the surrounding context is unclear. Many different readings and interpretations have been offered.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Beware, do not turn to iniquity!

For this you’ve been tested by affliction!

---

Footnote 36:21. Vocalizing *boḥarta*, the passive form of the Aramaic verb “test.” The syntax is still curious.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:22–26

------------------------------------------------------------------------

El, you see, excels in power;

There is no master like him.

Who commands him how to govern?

And who can say, “you’ve done wrong?”

Pay mind to exalt his work,

Which people have admired!

All humanity looks upon it;

Mortals gaze from afar.

El, you see, is more exalted than we know;

His years are a limitless number.

---

Footnote 36:22–26. A Hebraized form of Aramaic *mare’* (Daniel 2:47; 5:23).

Footnote 36:22–26. A form of *shur*, literally, “to see, look” (so Masnuth and others).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:27–28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For he sheds drops of water,

Pours rain on humanity;

As the skies precipitate,

Showering all humanity.

---

Footnote 36:27–28. The verb *gara‘* connotes raining in rabbinic Hebrew.

Footnote 36:27–28. Pseudo-Aramaic form of Hebrew *yatsoq;* compare Rashbam on Job 28:2.

Footnote 36:27–28. Reading *le’adam* for *le’eido* “for its mist.” Elihu is correcting the deity’s assertion in 38:26, according to which God provides rain specifically where people do not dwell. The shared stems for “rain” and “humanity” tighten the link.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:29–30

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who knows how he spreads the clouds,

Or the booming from behind them?

He lays his lightning over them,

And covers the floor of the sea.

---

Footnote 36:29–30. Reading *mi yabin* for *’im yabin* “if he understands.” Compare 37:15–16.

Footnote 36:29–30. Literally, “the spreads of cloud.”

Footnote 36:29–30. Literally, the booming of the “screen,” or thicket of cloud providing a screen for the deity; compare Psalm 18:12.

Footnote 36:29–30. Literally, “light”; compare 37:3, 11, 15.

Footnote 36:29–30. Literally, “roots, bottom.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:31

------------------------------------------------------------------------

With it he sustains the nations,

Providing food in abundance.

---

Footnote 36:31. Reading *yazin* (Aramaic) for *yadin* “judge”; compare Jeremiah 5:8 and the noun *mazon* “food.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 36:32–33

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Above the clouds he stores the lightning,

Ordaining it to one who is praying.

His thunder alerts him (of it),

And the tempest his cattle.

---

Footnote 36:32–33. Literally, “hands, palms.” A metaphor for “clouds” (Rashbam); compare 1 Kings 18:44: “Behold: a small cloud like a person’s palm/hand arising from the sea.”

Footnote 36:32–33. For “light/lightning” as a feminine noun, see Jeremiah 13:16.

Footnote 36:32–33. For this usage, see Isaiah 59:16 (so for example Rashbam, Masnuth, Meyuhhas). The same verb is used in this sense in the *Qal* conjugation in Job 21:15.

Footnote 36:32–33. Compare for example Micah 4:9.

Footnote 36:32–33. The person praying for rain.

Footnote 36:32–33. Reading *‘al‘ola* “storm” in Aramaic.
