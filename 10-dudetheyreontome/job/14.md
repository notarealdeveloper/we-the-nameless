------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:1–2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

A human, born of woman,

Is short of days and sated with restlessness.

He sprouts like a flower and withers.

He flees like a shadow and does not stay.

---

Footnote 14:1–2. “Flees” (*yibraḥ*) punningly suggests in this context the verb “blossoms” (*yipraḥ*).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Even on such a one would you train your eyes,

Bringing me into lawcourt with you?

---

Footnote 14:3. Compare Jeremiah 32:19. “Such a one” connotes “me”; see Psalm 34:7.

Footnote 14:3. Many prefer to read “him” (*’oto*) for “me” (’*oti*) with some of the ancient translations; but it is typical of Job to meld his particular case with his general observations.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who can produce pure out of tainted?

No one can!

---

Footnote 14:4. Compare the message of the spirit in 4:17–21. “Pure” and “tainted” are ritual categories, like edible and inedible animals (see Leviticus 11), implying that even the deity cannot transform one into the other.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When his days are curtailed,

The number of his months in your hands.

You have set him limits he cannot overrun.

---

Footnote 14:5. Compare Isaiah 10:23; for the general sense see Psalm 90:9–10.

Footnote 14:5. Literally, “with you.”

Footnote 14:5. An echo of Jeremiah 5:22, alluding to God’s mythological restraint of the sea; compare Job 7:12.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Look away from him and let him stop (being tormented)!

Until like a hired-hand he fulfills his day.

---

Footnote 14:6. An echo of Job 7:16 and 19.

Footnote 14:6. Compare the usage in Leviticus 26:34. An echo of Job 7:2.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:7–9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For there is hope for a tree:

If it is cut, it can go on to replace itself,

And its shoot will not stop (growing).

Even if its root grows old in the ground,

And its stump goes dead in the earth,

At the mere scent of water it will flower,

And like a sapling produce a plant!

---

Footnote 14:7–9. Contrast Job 7:6 and compare 11:18.

Footnote 14:7–9. “Sapling” in Job 8:16. Job counters Bildad’s image of the flourishing righteous.

Footnote 14:7–9. The Hebrew is replete with ironies: “sapling” here is literally “(newly) planted,” and the term rendered here “plant” is “harvested (produce)”—literally, “the cut.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But a man—he dies and grows feeble;

A human expires—and where is he?

---

Footnote 14:10. The verbs seem to be in reverse order; but the verb “grow feeble” (*wa-yeḥelash*) plays ironically on “replace itself” (*yaḥalip*) in verse 8.

Footnote 14:10. Echoes Job 7:8, 21 (said of Job); 8:18, 22 (said of the wicked).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Water can run out of the sea;

And a river can drain and dry out.

---

Footnote 14:11. The sea can run out of water.

Footnote 14:11. “Drain and dry out” (*yeḥerab we-yabesh*) echoes “grow feeble” in verse 10.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

So does a man lie down never to arise;

Until the sky is no more, they will not awaken,

They will not be aroused from their sleep.

---

Footnote 14:12. Contrast Isaiah 26:19 and Daniel 12:2, according to which the dead can be resurrected from the sleep of death.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Would that you conceal me in Sheol!

Hide me away till your anger subsides!

Set me a period—and then call me to mind!

---

Footnote 14:13. Job seeks a reprieve from his life of affliction—a “(fixed) period” of time to lie among the dead; compare Job’s idealization of death in 3:13–24.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If a man dies, can he revive?

All the days of my fixed sentence I will wait—

Until my replacement arrives.

---

Footnote 14:14. Of life on earth; see 7:1.

Footnote 14:14. An echo of 13:15.

Footnote 14:14. See verse 7 above. Job will be happy to “live” in the realm of the dead for a period, until he is regrettably relieved by someone else.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Summon and I will answer you—

When you long for the work of your hands!

---

Footnote 14:15. Sarcastic: when you miss me and need to restore me to my miserable life. Compare 10:8–13.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:16–17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Perhaps now when you count my steps

You will not keep my sins in mind.

Seal my transgression in a pouch,

And plaster over my crime!

---

Footnote 14:16–17. See 13:27.

Footnote 14:16–17. In parallel with the verb in the following clause, this verb should be read not as vocalized—as a passive participle (*ḥatum*)—but as an imperative: *ḥatom*.

Footnote 14:16–17. If the evidence against Job is kept in a pouch—as was the ancient practice preceding a trial—Job cannot be convicted. For the image, compare Hosea 13:12 (Holtz).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:18–19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

And yet, a cliff will fall and crumble;

A mountain will be moved from its place;

Rocks are worn down by water;

A torrent sweeps away the earth’s dust;

So do you obliterate a mortal’s hope.

---

Footnote 14:18–19. Job ceases to fantasize and offers his own image of human disintegration.

Footnote 14:18–19. An echo of Job 9:5.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

You assault him continually—and he passes on;

You disfigure him—and then you dispatch him.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If his sons receive honor—he does not know;

And if they are diminished—he does not see them.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 14:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But his family is pained over him;

And his household mourns him.

---

Footnote 14:22. Literally, “flesh”; see Genesis 37:27.

Footnote 14:22. Literally, “his self”; see Genesis 12:5.

Footnote 14:22. A more literal interpretation is: “Only his flesh pains him; and his life-force dries up on him.” The image of a family’s mourning is familiar from the Babylonian poem of the pious sufferer “Let Me Praise the Lord of Wisdom” and other ancient texts.
