------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Does a mortal not serve a fixed sentence on earth,

His days like a hired-hand’s days?

---

Footnote 7:1. Compare Isaiah 40:2.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Like a slave panting for some shade,

Or a hired-hand waiting for his earnings,

So am I meted out moons of futility;

Nights of suffering are allotted to me.

---

Footnote 7:2–3. In parallel with “nights,” we should understand “moons” literally (so Tur-Sinai)—a metonym for the night—rather than as “months,” as it is customarily understood. Job has not been suffering all that long a time.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When I lie down I think: When can I rise?

Every evening!

I am sated with tossing till dawn.

---

Footnote 7:4. Reading *middei* for *middad*, which would mean “tossing.” If the received text is correct, then we would seem to have an ancient explanatory gloss on the text: “Tossing at evening.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My flesh is cloaked with worms and lumps of dirt;

My skin is welted and decays.

---

Footnote 7:5. Many derive from Ethiopic, “to congeal” and therefore “to scab,” but context suggests cracking of the skin (so for example Malbim).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My days run faster than the shuttle in a loom,

Running out for lack of cord, lack of hope.

---

Footnote 7:6. Hebrew *tiqwa* means both “cord” and “hope.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Keep in mind that my life is mere wind;

My eye will no more see the good.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

O eye of my Observer, you will see me no longer!

Your eyes are upon me—and I’m gone!


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:9–10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

A cloud dissipates and passes;

So does one gone down to Sheol never come up again.

He does not return to his home;

His place knows him not anymore.

---

Footnote 7:9–10. The netherworld.

Footnote 7:9–10. The same line is used in Psalm 103:16 in describing the ephemeral nature of life.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

And so I will not hold back my mouth!

I will speak out of tightness of breath,

Complain out of bitterness of spirit.

---

Footnote 7:11. Distress. “Breath” is the same word as “wind” in verse 7 above.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Am I Yamm?

Or am I Tannin?

Then why do you place me under guard?

---

Footnote 7:12. See the comment on 3:10 above. The symbol of the watery chaos is the arch enemy of God.

Footnote 7:12. A primeval sea serpent, a form of Yamm.

Footnote 7:12. Not “that” but interrogative; see Jeremiah 8:22 (where *maddua‘* “why?” glosses the more archaic use of *ki*, cognate to *’eka* in Ugaritic).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:13–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When I think: My bed will give me comfort,

My couch will relieve my complaint,

You terrify me with nightmares,

You torment me with visions.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My throat would rather choke,

(Choosing) death over pain.

---

Footnote 7:15. “Throat” is the same word as “spirit” in verse 11 above.

Footnote 7:15. Reading *‘atsbotai* (see 9:28 and Psalm 147:3) for *‘atsmotai* “my bones.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I’m fed up! I won’t live forever!

Stop (tormenting) me! For my days are mere breath.

---

Footnote 7:16. The same term used by Qohelet (Ecclesiastes) when he says, “All is mere breath!” (1:2 etc.).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:17–18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

What is a mortal that you treat him as important?

Why do you pay him any mind,

Take account of him each morning,

Test him every minute?

---

Footnote 7:17–18. These two verses are a parody of Psalm 8:5–6 (so for example Fishbane):

“What is a mortal, that you pay him mind, / or a human that you take account of him?

“You have made him but a little less than God; / you have crowned him with honor and glory.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why can’t you just look away from me,

Let go for just a swallow of spit?

---

Footnote 7:19. For the instant it takes to swallow one’s spit.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I’ve sinned, what can I do to you,

O Watcher of Humankind?

Why have you made me your target?

How could I be a burden to you?

---

Footnote 7:20. See 6:4.

Footnote 7:20. The Hebrew was corrected by an ancient scribe to the euphemism “to myself,” but the original reading is acknowledged in rabbinic literature.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 7:21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why can’t you pardon my transgression,

Commute my punishment?

For I’ll soon be lying in the dirt—

And when you seek me, I’ll be gone.

---

Footnote 7:21. The verb “to pardon” is literally “to lift (the transgression from off the perpetrator),” thereby producing a play on “burden” (what is carried, the same verb as “to lift”): Job asks the deity, who should have no difficulty in bearing him, to lift off his burden—his presumed sin.
