------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Zophar the Na’amathite and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

It is my brooding that makes me respond—

By dint of the feelings within me.

I hear (your) lecture of refutation,

And the spirit by which I understand prompts me to speak:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:4–5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do you not know this from forever,

Since the human was put on earth,

That the joy of the wicked is short-lived,

That the pleasure of the impudent is of the moment?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:6–7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Though his summit go up to the sky,

And his head reach up to the clouds,

He will disappear like his dung for all time;

Those who’ve seen him will say: “Where is he?”

---

Footnote 20:6–7. The impudent’s.

Footnote 20:6–7. There is a pun on a biblical term for “excrement”—*ro’i* (Nahum 3:6; Zalcman).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:8–9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He’ll fly off like a dream that no one can find;

He’ll be banished like a vision of the night.

The eye that beheld him will do so no more;

His locale will see him no longer.

---

Footnote 20:8–9. An echo of Job 4:13.

Footnote 20:8–9. Echoes of Job 7:8, 10, 21.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

His children will be crushed by poverty,

As his hands give back his wealth.

---

Footnote 20:10. More literally, “Poverty will crush (reading *yarotsu*) his sons” (compare the Latin and Syriac translations).

Footnote 20:10. There is a pun: the term for “wealth” (*’on*) can also designate one’s “strength,” one’s progeny.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

His bones may be full of youthful-vigor,

But they lie down with him (dead) in the dust.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:12–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Though evil taste sweet in his mouth,

(Though) he hide it under his tongue;

(Though) he savor and not let it go,

And restrain it within his palate,

His food will turn over in his bowels—

Serpents’ venom within him.

---

Footnote 20:12–14. “Savor” (*yaḥmol*) echoes “like a dream” (*kaḥalom*) in verse 8.

Footnote 20:12–14. “Food,” more often “bread” (*leḥem*), puns on “savors” (*yaḥmol*) in verse 13 and on “wealth” (*ḥayil*) in verse 15 and below.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He may swallow wealth, but he will disgorge it;

El will dispossess him of it from his belly.

---

Footnote 20:15. The metaphor of consuming wealth mixes the literal (wealth, dispossess) and the figurative (swallow, disgorge).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He suckles the poison of serpents,

The tongue of the viper will slay him.

---

Footnote 20:16. As though the venom is ejected by the tongue, not the teeth.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He must never see rivulets of oil,

Or streams of honey and cream.

---

Footnote 20:17. Reading *yitshar* “fresh oil” for *naharei* “rivers of.” For the phrase “streams of oil,” see Micah 6:7 and Job’s echo of the phrase in 29:6. These delectable liquids contrast with the poisonous ones.

Footnote 20:17. “Streams” (*naḥalei*) puns on “wealth” (*ḥayil*) in verses 15, 18, 21.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:18–19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He must restore (his) profit without swallowing it,

The amount he received without enjoying it.

For he has crushed the property of the poor;

Has stolen a house rather than build one.

---

Footnote 20:18–19. Vocalizing *‘azab* as a noun; compare *‘izzabon* “wares” (Ezekiel 27:12, 27, 33).

Footnote 20:18–19. Note the twist on verse 10 above.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:20–21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Because his belly will know no peace,

He will not save (himself) with what he holds dear.

(He has) no survivor to consume it;

Therefore his bounty will not last.

---

Footnote 20:20–21. Reading *shelew* (a noun) for *shalew* (a predicate); see Psalm 30:7.

Footnote 20:20–21. Compare Psalm 39:12 (so for example Rashi).

Footnote 20:20–21. For this sense of the verb *yaḥil*, see Psalm 10:5; there is a play on *ḥayil* “wealth.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Though his satisfaction is complete, he’s distressed;

Any sufferer’s hand can overcome him.

---

Footnote 20:22. Compare Eliphaz in 15:21.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

On the one who fills his belly,

May he unleash his burning wrath!

And on him rain down his warfare!

---

Footnote 20:23. Compare Psalm 11:6, where the deity “rains down” fire and sulfur on the wicked. “Warfare” (*laḥum*) puns ironically on “food” (*leḥem*) and “wealth” (*ḥayil*).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:24–25

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If he escapes from a weapon of iron,

A bow of bronze will pierce him through.

He draws out (a sword) and it runs through the body;

The blade goes right through his innards;

He is stricken with fright.

---

Footnote 20:24–25. Such as a sword.

Footnote 20:24–25. For *ḥalap* “be pierced,” see Judges 5:26. The rare verb form suggests a double meaning: a bow “will take the place” (the more common meaning of *ḥalap*) of an unsuccessful sword.

Footnote 20:24–25. God or an indefinite subject—as though to say, “A sword is drawn …”

Footnote 20:24–25. An ellipsis of “the lightning blade (of a sword)”; see Deuteronomy 32:41.

Footnote 20:24–25. Literally, “comes out from his gall.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:26

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Darkness is in store for his protected ones;

A fire unfanned will consume them;

Any survivor in his tent will meet disaster.

---

Footnote 20:26. Apparently his family; see Psalm 83:4.

Footnote 20:26. A supernatural fire, like the one that annihilated Job’s flocks and servants (1:16).

Footnote 20:26. Literally, “him” (in Job singular and plural are not always distinguished).

Footnote 20:26. Reading *yeira‘* as a passive (*Niph‘al;* so D. Kimhi).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:27

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The heavens will reveal his crime;

And the earth will rise against him.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

A torrent will sweep away his house—

Inundation on the day of his anger.

---

Footnote 20:28. Reading *yubal* for *yebul* “crop”; see Jeremiah 17:8.

Footnote 20:28. Reading *yiggal* (*Niph‘al* of *gll*) for *yigel* “go into exile”; see Amos 5:24 (said of water).

Footnote 20:28. The deity’s.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 20:29

------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the wicked man’s portion from Elohim,

The allotment that El has declared for him.
