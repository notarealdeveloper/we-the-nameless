------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 29:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Job again took up his discourse and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 29:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If only I could be as in months of yore,

In the days Eloah would watch over me.

When his lamp would shine over my head,

When I could walk by its light in the dark.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 29:4–6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Just as I’d been in my earlier days,

When my tent stood under Eloah’s bond;

When Shaddai was still with me,

And my servants all around me;

When he would wash my feet in cream,

When the Rock would pour over me streams of oil.

---

Footnote 29:4–6. Compare Akkadian *harāpu* “to be early.”

Footnote 29:4–6. For this usage of *sod*, see especially Psalm 25:14.

Footnote 29:4–6. Reading *ḥem’a* “whey, butter” for *ḥema* “venom, rage”; so several versions; compare for example Deuteronomy 32:13–14, to which Job alludes here.

Footnote 29:4–6. For this epithet of the deity, see for example Deuteronomy 32:4, 18, 31.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 29:7–11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When I would go out to the city gate,

Take my seat in the public square—

Young men would see me and withdraw,

And the old would rise and stand;

Princes would hold back their words,

Placing a hand over their mouths;

Leaders would stifle their voices,

Their tongues clinging to their palates.

When an ear would hear me, it would affirm;

And when an eye would see me, it would attest.

---

Footnote 29:7–11. See the comment at 5:4.

Footnote 29:7–11. Compare the usage “holding back, withdrawn” in 1 Samuel 10:22.

Footnote 29:7–11. Compare 21:5. The gesture indicates keeping silent in order to listen.

Footnote 29:7–11. More literally, “the voices of leaders would be suppressed,” the same verb rendered “withdraw” in verse 8 above.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 29:12–13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When I would rescue the needy crying out,

And the orphan with no one to help him;

When I’d receive the vagabond’s blessing,

And bring joy to the heart of the widow;

---

Footnote 29:12–13. This verse together with 29:16 below allude to Psalm 72:12–14, which describe a righteous king.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 29:14–16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I clothed myself in the right, and it clothed me,

Justice (clothed me) like a robe and a headdress.

Eyes was I to the blind,

And legs I was to the lame.

I was the patron of the poor;

And pursued the complaint of the stranger.

---

Footnote 29:14–16. In the Bible and in Mesopotamian literature one speaks of being clothed by a curse (see Psalm 109:17–19). Here again Job inverts a literary convention.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 29:17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I would break the fangs of the depraved,

And cast the prey out of his teeth.

---

Footnote 29:17. For the figure of the wicked as a predator, see 4:10–11.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 29:18–20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

So I thought, “I’ll expire with my brood intact,

And I’ll multiply days like the sand!

My stock will be flush with water,

And dew will abide on my foliage!

My property will be restored,

And the bow in my hand replaced!”

---

Footnote 29:18–20. Literally, “in my nest (of fledglings)”; compare Deuteronomy 32:11; Isaiah 16:2.

Footnote 29:18–20. Compare for example Genesis 32:13; Isaiah 48:19. Rabbinic midrash interprets “like the phoenix.”

Footnote 29:18–20. For the arboreal imagery and some of the language, see 14:7–9 and compare 18:16. Job sees himself as the righteous in Bildad’s scenario (chapter 8).

Footnote 29:18–20. For *kabod* in this sense, see for example Genesis 31:1; Isaiah 10:3.

Footnote 29:18–20. Literally, “new.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 29:21–23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

To me people listened, and they waited,

They kept still, as I offered my counsel.

Once I had spoken, they had nothing to add;

My speech fell on them like the rain.

They would wait for my rainfall,

Their mouths open wide to catch it.

---

Footnote 29:21–23. More literally, “my word distilled upon them”; compare Deuteronomy 32:2.

Footnote 29:21–23. More literally, “And their mouths they opened to the later rain.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 29:24–25

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I smiled their way, they would not swerve;

If I lit up my face, they fulfilled (every word).

I would choose their path and sit at their head;

I’d be poised like the king in an army—

Wherever I’d lead them, they’d camp.

---

Footnote 29:24–25. For this sense, literally “to turn right,” see Isaiah 30:21 and compare Job 39:24.

Footnote 29:24–25. Literally, “they did not cause (the word) to fall down.” For the full biblical expression, see for example 1 Samuel 3:19.

Footnote 29:24–25. Reading *’obilem yaḥanu* for the received text’s *’abelim yenaḥem* “mourners he comforts,” which is entirely inapt. For a similar use of the verb *hobil*, see Jeremiah 31:8.
