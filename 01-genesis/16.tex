\Verse{13}
{
	\hJ{וַתִּקְרָא שֵׁם יְהֹוָה הַדֹּבֵר אֵלֶיהָ אַתָּה אֵל רֳאִי כִּי אָמְרָה הֲגַם הֲלֹם רָאִיתִי אַחֲרֵי רֹאִי׃}
}
{
	\eJ{
		And she called the name of YHWH who spoke to her “You are
		El-roi,” for she said, “Have I also seen after the one who
		sees me here?”
	}
}
{
	Now the text says she called the name of ``YHWH who spoke to her.''

	So I guess we figured out who the messenger was.
	
	She says ``You are El-roi'' which is like ``The God who sees.''

	Now she says a phrase that translators can't seem to agree on the meaning of.

	\aC{Here's some of their choices:
	
	\begin{enumerate}
	\item I have now seen the One who sees me.
	\item Have I also here seen Him who sees me?
	\item Truly here I have seen him who looks after me.
	\item Have I also here looked after him that seeth me?
	\item Have I even remained alive here after seeing Him?
	\item Here also I looked after him seeing me.
	\item Verily here have I seen the hinder parts of him that seeth me.
	\item Certainly, here I have seen the back of the one who sees me.
	\item Haue I not also here looked after him that seeth me?
	\item Haue I not also heare loked after hym that seeth mee?
	\item Of a suertye I haue sene the back partes of him that sawe me.
	\item I haue of a suertie sene here the backe parties of him that seith me.
	\item Profecto hic vidi posteriora videntis me.
	\end{enumerate}

	Or in other words:

	\begin{enumerate}
	\item I have seen X.
	\item X sees me. Have I seen X?
	\item I have seen X. And X looks after me.
	\item X sees me. Have I looked after X?
	\item Am I still alive after I saw X?
	\item I looked after X while X was seeing me.
	\item I definitely saw X's \eRJE{posterior}.
	\item Same as above but less formal. She saw the back.
	\item Same as above but from 1587.
	\item Same as above but from 1568.
	\item Same as above but from 1535.
	\item Same as above but from 1526.
	\item ``Indeed, here I saw the back of the one who saw me.''\footnote{We've now arrived at the version of the verse from the first book ever printed, the Gutenberg Bible, from around 1454 or 1455. I think that means we can be done with this verse.}
	\end{enumerate}

	Rather than offer a preferred interpretation, we'd just like to note that the Hebrew is \heb{אחרי}, which means ``after'' or ``behind,'' but to be fair the same term without the yodh \heb{אחר} means ``hind part,'' and so it's easy to see how that led to six hundred or so years of, well, whatever that was.

	Let's try to put all that behind us.}
