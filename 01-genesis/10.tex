\Verse{8}
{
	\hJ{וְכוּשׁ יָלַד אֶת נִמְרֹד הוּא הֵחֵל לִהְיוֹת גִּבֹּר בָּאָרֶץ׃}

{
Ok so Ethiopia gives birth to Nimrod.

In modern slang, the term Nimrod means idiot or moron.

This is understood to derive from a certain fictitious rabbit,
common in the table of English speaking nations circa the 30th century A.D.%
\footnote{After David. Roughly the 20th century, C.E. (Common Era.)}

The transition from the term's original meaning of ``mighty biblical hunter'' to ``incompetent idiot'' arose from a chapter in the chronicles of the events of said rabbit in which he was engaged in a prolonged predator-prey style drama involving his pursuit by a rotund and, let us say, less than competent hunter.

Employing the timeless rhetorical device of sarcasm, the aforementioned rabbit refers to the less-than-mighty hunter as ``Nimrod,'' which the audience at the time, being native speakers of biblical English, would have understood to be the deployment of the above mentioned rhetorical device, namely sarcasm.

Subsequent generations, upon encountering the expression, had in the intervening years become less fluent in the bible and biblical minutiae, seeing as bible translations at the time were, to quote one contemporary scholar ``boring,'' ``pretentious'' and ``fit for burning.''\footnote{Lit. \heb{ראוי לשרפה} in the original text, which, while referring to itself as ``early biblical English,'' in fact employs a rather infuriating mixed orthography that makes a translator rather long for a return to the simplicity of Babel.}

In summary, lacking knowledge of the bible, they thought Nimrod meant idiot.%
\aC{\footnote{
Not to prolong the already much too lengthy and borderline self-indulgent commentary on this verse, but in the interest of factual accuracy it bears pointing out that the responsible party for the redefinition of the aforementioned biblical term was in fact, not Bubs Gunny, nor Porpy Kig, but in fact, Daddy F--- (Editor's Note: The text appears to be corrupted here, and the surname of the individual in question has been lost.) Author's Note: Names of certain individuals have been changed in the interest of respecting the laws of biblical copyright in the early biblical English period.}}

}

\Verse{29}
{
	\hJ{וְאֶת אוֹפִר וְאֶת חֲוִילָה וְאֶת יוֹבָב כּל אֵלֶּה בְּנֵי יקְטָן׃}
}
{
	\eJ{
		and Ophir and Havilah and Jobab. All these are Joktan’s
		children.
	}
}
{
	\begin{enumerate}
	\item Ophir: The mysterious land famous for its exceptionally fine
	      gold. Its location remains unknown.
	\item Havilah: We've met this name before in Genesis 2. Like many
	      names in Genesis, it seems to refer to more than one place or
	      people.
	\item Jobab: Meaning uncertain. Probably an Arabian tribe or clan.
	\item Joktan's descendants are generally associated with southern
	      Arabia, especially modern Yemen.
	\end{enumerate}
}
