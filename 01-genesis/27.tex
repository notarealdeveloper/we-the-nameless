		Technical flaws in this approach are discussed in the footnote below.\footnote{
		Isaac's approach here relies on the well known fact that the first requirement
		for any Esau detection system is ``if not hairy, then not Esau.'' By modus
		tollens, this is equivalent to the contrapositive ``If Esau, then hairy.''
		However, as every Esau detection system engineer well knows, the inverse
		(If hairy then Esau) and converse (If not Esau, then not hairy) of Isaac's
		sentence do not follow! The first known Esau detection system, namely Isaac in Gen 27:21, famously failed to recognize this, due to (a) the existence of goats, and (b) the degradation of visual capacity in Gen 27:1, commonly known The Blinding of Isaac, which combined to form a security vulnerability that allowed an attacker to authenticate as Esau and exfiltrate the blessing.
