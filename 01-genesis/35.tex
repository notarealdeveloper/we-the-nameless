%	\footnote{
%		Isaac expired. Isaac’s life is markedly different from the lives of his
%		father Abraham and his son Jacob. They both spend time in the two great
%		centers of civilization at that time, Mesopotamia and Egypt, but Isaac
%		never leaves the land of the covenant. They both have multiple wives and
%		concubines, but Isaac has only Rebekah. They both have their names
%		changed, but Isaac does not (see the comment on Gen 17:19). They both
%		fight, but Isaac does not. There are fewer stories about Isaac, and he
%		is a fairly passive figure in them. Regarding the two most famous
%		stories of Isaac: in the story of his near-sacrifice he is acted upon by
%		his father, and in the story of Jacob’s appropriation of Esau’s blessing
%		he is acted upon by his son. Perhaps the explanation for his being
%		different is that he once lay on the altar as a sacrifice, and so his
%		life is consecrated in a singular way. Footnote 35:29. Esau and Jacob,
%		his sons. It is hard to miss the order here: Esau before Jacob. It is
%		not simply a matter of birth order, because Isaac is listed before
%		Ishmael in the corresponding passage (25:9). This seems rather to be a
%		sign of recognition, compassion, and compensation to Esau for his
%		displacement by Jacob.
