Notes.

Λ⁰𑀕⁰⋀. The word Logos, written in three of the world’s writing systems, or maybe four, depending on which group of humans we choose to blame for the little “o” guys between the peaks.

Λ. The capital letter lambda, equivalent to the letter L in the Greek writing system, the language in which the New Testament was originally written.

O. The letter O, as it appears in Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, and a variety of other writing systems going all the way back to Paleo-Hebrew and arguably earlier. Basically just the regular old O you love and know.

𑀕. The letter G in the Brahmi script, ancestor of the writing systems native to roughly 94% of India’s population. Brahmi itself ultimately derives from an early Semitic writing system related to Paleo-Hebrew. And although some scholars out there still dispute this claim, well, they’re wrong. Just look at the damn thing. It's on the internet. Also note that despite the striking similarities of many of the corresponding letters in both the Paleo Hebrew and Brahmi Scripts, occasionally someone turned one of the letters upside down. Just make a note of that and let’s keep moving.

O. Just O again. Nothing new here.

ㅅ. The letter S in Hangul, the brilliantly simple writing system native to the Korean peninsula. While its shape was originally explained as representing the teeth, we prefer the folk etymology that it derives it from the shape of the Chinese character 人, meaning “human.” Given the importance of folk etymologies in the biblical text—most prominently in the earliest strata of biblical prose conventionally attributed to the so-called J source in bible circles—we feel that this move, which is technically cheating, is an important part of Doing Bible.
