## II Samuel 7:19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

¹⁹ Yet even this, O Lord GOD, has seemed too little to You; for You have spoken of Your servant’s house also for the future. May that be the law for the people, O Lord GOD.

"You've promised so much, but even that seems too little for You. I mean we're talking about the future here. May your words become the law, Lord Gᴏᴅ."

Side Note: In a rare case of typographical theology, the "Lord" here is in normal font while "Gᴏᴅ" is in smallcaps.

From this, we can infer that:

1. David actually said Adonai, the literal Hebrew word for Lord.
2. David did not say Elohim after that, because Elohim doesn't render as Gᴏᴅ in smallcaps.
3. Therefore, David must have said "Adonai YHWH."
4. Which, given the conventions of English bible translation, would render (on first pass) as "Lord Lᴏʀᴅ."
5. But that's just silly.
6. Therefore, the translation above must have fixed the problem with a one-off decision to replace the smallcaps Lᴏʀᴅ with smallcaps Gᴏᴅ.
7. The O before Lord isn't there in the original. They just put that in for like, garnish.
8. You can check the Hebrew. That's what happened.

The bible is fun.
