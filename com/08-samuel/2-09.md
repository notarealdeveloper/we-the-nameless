## II Samuel 9:6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

⁶ and when Mephibosheth son of Jonathan son of Saul came to David, he flung himself on his face and prostrated himself. David said, “Mephibosheth!” and he replied, “At your service, sir.”

So Mephibosheth (Son of David's former best friend, also Grandson of Saul) comes to David (presumably with help) and he immediately falls on his face (from respect, not the legs) and David's like "Mephibosheth" and he replies (presumably still from the ground) "At your service, sir."

Also his name means "Mephi of Shame."

We don't know what a Mephi is.

In 1 Chronicles 8:34 and 9:40, he's called Meribaal, which means "Baal contends."

And since one of the readings of the folk etymology of Israel from Genesis 32:28 is "El contends," we've basically got:

Israel = The Canaanite highest god contends.

Meribaal = The Canaanite storm god contends.

Now of course El and Baal are also the generic terms for God and Lord respectively. 

This isn't anything deep going on in the text probably.

But it's a thing, and it's in the text.

Just make a note of that and let's keep moving.

## II Samuel 9:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

¹² Mephibosheth had a young son named Mica; and all the members of Ziba’s household worked for Mephibosheth.

Mephibosheth has a son named "Who's Like." 

(Possible Song of the Sea reference there. Like 800 years after this, the Macabees name themselves with the same reference.[^1][^2] It's a whole thing.)

Also Ziba's 15 sons and 20 slaves do their new job.

[^1]: The text here appears to be alluding to the traditional Jewish explanation that Maccabee (Hebrew: מכבים Makkabi) is an acronym for the Torah verse that was the battle-cry of the Maccabees, "Mi kamocha ba'elim YHWH", "Who is like you among the heavenly powers, oh God!"

[^2]: Translator's Note: The translator of this volume would just like to point out that the translation in the previous footnote was absolutely disgraceful, and should instead be properly rendered "Who is like you among the gods, YHWH?" but apparently some people are uncomfortable with the idea that polytheism was still at least kind of a thing in 1200 or so BC.
