\aC{The text repeatedly uses the term \heb{הַתְּקֹעִית֙} in this section, traditionally translated as "the Tekoite" or the woman "from Tekoa." The term may derive from the root \heb{תקע}, meaning the following:}

\Def{\heb{תָּקַע} (tāqaʿ)}{
    \begin{itemize}
        \item to sound or blow a horn, trumpet, or shofar
        \item to thrust or insert
        \item colloquially, to stuff (food)
        \item colloquially, to strike or slap someone
        \item colloquially, to throw in or inject a saying or phrase
        \item colloquially, to delay or hold up
        \item to jam
        \item slang, to stick or place someone somewhere
        \item vulgar slang, to fuck
    \end{itemize}
}

She bends over and she's like "Help, O king!"\fB{Lit. \chineseA{幫哦王}! in the original, a meaning "Help, O king!", or perhaps "Help me king!", if the middle character's mouth radical was a later scribal addition. Some commentators have noted that the first character, in modern Mandarin pinyin, would be transliterated "bāng" while the third character would be transliterated "wáng." The theological significance of this choice of words by the authors of the original text remains a matter of debate among scholars.}
