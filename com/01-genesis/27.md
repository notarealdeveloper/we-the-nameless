So Isaac's like "Let me feel you to see if you're really Esau."

Technical faws in this approach are discussed in the footnote below.\footnote{

Isaac's approach here relies on the well known fact that the first requirement for any Esau detection system is "if not hairy, then not Esau."

By modus tollens, this is equivalent to the contrapositive "If Esau, then hairy."

However, as every Esau detection system engineer well knows, the inverse (If hairy then Esau) and converse (If not Esau, then not hairy) of Isaac's sentence do not follow!

The first known Esau detection system, namely Isaac in Gen 27:21, famously failed to recognize this, due to (a) the existence of goats, and (b) the degradation of visual capacity in Gen 27:1, commonly known The Blinding of Isaac, which combined to form a security vulnerability that allowed an attacker to authenticate as Esau and exfiltrate the blessing.

}

Footnote 27:28. from. David Noel Freedman notes that there is play on words here,
as the blessings of the two sons hinge on the term min. In the blessing
given to Jacob he is promised access to and a rich share of the rain and
the dew, with min being used partitively In Esau’s blessing it is used
spatially to mean that Esau will be denied access to fertile lands and
the fruits of the soil. So what was a blessing for Jacob becomes in
effect a kind of curse for Esau, and the words are practically
identical.
