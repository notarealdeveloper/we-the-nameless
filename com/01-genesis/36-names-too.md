## Genesis 36:2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

² Esau had taken his wives from the daughters of Canaan: Adah, daughter of Elon, the Hittite, and Aholibamah, daughter of Anah, daughter of Zibeon, the Hivite,

| Name | Hebrew | Likely meaning / etymology |
|---|---|---|
| Adah | עָדָה (*ʿĀdâ*) | Probably “ornament/adornment”; from עדה, associated with adorning oneself. |
| Elon | אֵילוֹן (*ʾÊlôn*) | “Oak” or “terebinth”; related to אֵלוֹן. |
| Aholibamah | אָהֳלִיבָמָה (*ʾOholîbāmâ*) | Probably “my tent is a high place” / “tent of the high place”; etymology is somewhat uncertain. |
| Anah | עֲנָה (*ʿĂnâ*) | Uncertain; probably related to the root ענה, perhaps “answer/respond.” |
| Zibeon | צִבְעוֹן (*Ṣibʿôn*) | Probably “dyed/colored one” or “hyena”; etymology is uncertain. |

36:2–3. Adah, daughter of Elon … Aholibamah, daughter of Anah …
Basemath, daughter of Ishmael. These names are in conflict with the
names of Esau’s wives given earlier (26:34; 28:9). Rashi gives midrashic
explanations for this confusion, Ibn Ezra suggests that they each had
two names, and Ramban says that it is possible that Esau’s first two
wives died, he married two others, and he renamed his third wife. The
character and variety of their solutions indicate that they did not know
what the reason was for this confusion in the text. Critical scholars
have not solved it in terms of standard source divisions either. It may
be a combination of scribal and source reasons. It remains a classic
puzzle to be solved.

¹⁰ These are the names of Esau’s sons: Eliphaz, son of Adah, Esau’s wife; Reuel, son of Basemath, Esau’s wife.

## Genesis 36:14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

¹⁴ And these were the sons of Aholibamah, daughter of Anah, daughter of Zibeon, Esau’s wife: and she gave birth for Esau to Jeush and Jalam and Korah.

\aC{Aholibamah may be a Canaanite cultic compound, “my tent is the sanctuary,” with *bāmâ* understood as a sacred height or shrine. Anah may belong with a Northwest Semitic root for singing or answering in ritual response. Zibeon may preserve an old animal or clan-name. Jeush may be related to a Semitic root of strength or support. Jalam can be compared with Ugaritic *ʿlm*, “eternity, enduring time,” giving a sense such as “the enduring one.” Korah may be associated with a wider Semitic root for frost, ice, or cold.}

## Genesis 36:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

¹⁵ These are the chiefs of the children of Esau: the sons of Eliphaz, Esau’s firstborn: chief Teman, chief Omar, chief Zepho, chief Kenaz,

\aC{Teman may function as an Edomite territorial name, “the southern country.” Omar may be compared with Akkadian *amāru*, “to see, behold,” suggesting “seer” or “one who sees.” Zepho may reflect Northwest Semitic *ṣpy*, “to watch, observe,” and thus “watchman.” Kenaz may preserve an Edomite clan-name whose original lexical sense is no longer recoverable.}

## Genesis 36:17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

¹⁷ And these are the sons of Reuel, son of Esau: chief Nahath, chief Zerah, chief Shammah, chief Mizzah. These are the chiefs of Reuel in the land of Edom. These are the sons of Basemath, Esau’s wife.

\aC{Nahath may be connected with the widespread Semitic *nḫt* complex expressing strength or firmness, yielding “strong one.” Zerah fits common Northwest Semitic solar vocabulary and may mean “sunrise” or “radiance.” Shammah may preserve a theophoric or clan-name whose final element has been assimilated to familiar Canaanite speech. Mizzah may be related to a Semitic root concerned with pressing or extracting liquid.}
