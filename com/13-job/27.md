------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Job again took up his discourse and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

By the life of El, he has set aside my charges!

Shaddai has embittered my life!


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:3–4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

So long as there is life-breath within me,

And in my nostrils Eloah’s spirit,

I swear that my lips will speak nothing corrupt,

And my tongue will utter no deceit.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:5–7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

God forbid I declare you in the right—

Not till I expire!

I will not set aside my integrity.

I hold fast to my righteousness;

I will not let it go.

My heart has never been impudent (saying):

“May my enemy be like the wicked,

And my opponent like the corrupt!”

---

Footnote 27:5–7. For the full phrase, including the name of the deity, see 1 Samuel 24:6.

Footnote 27:5–7. My companions.

Footnote 27:5–7. The ultimate source of speech; see at 8:10.

Footnote 27:5–7. Characteristically, Job had never employed a curse, even against his enemies. Compare his concern that his children might curse the deity in their hearts in a lightheaded moment (1:5).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:8–10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For what hope will the impudent gain

If he turns to Eloah in prayer?

Will his outcry be heard by El

When he’s overcome by disaster?

Can he entreat Shaddai,

Or call on Eloah at will?

---

Footnote 27:8–10. Literally, “if he raises his spirit to Eloah.” Assuming an *aleph* has been dropped, I read *yissa’ le’loah;* see Psalm 25:1; Lamentations 3:41; and compare Job 22:26 (so Ball, *The Book of Job*). The received reading *yeishel* yields interpretations that do not comport with the context.

Footnote 27:8–10. Literally, “at any time.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I will instruct you in the powers of El;

What Shaddai has in hand I will not conceal.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Since you have yourselves all been witness,

How could you be spewing such nonsense:

---

Footnote 27:12. Literally, “have seen”; Job’s companions are aware of his situation.

Footnote 27:12. More literally, “breathing out such windy-speech.” For the verb and its usage, compare Jeremiah 23:16, where it is said of false prophets. The verb introduces direct discourse here—as Job quotes and paraphrases the kinds of things his friends have been saying.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

“This is the wicked man’s portion from El,

The allotment that villains receive from Shaddai:

---

Footnote 27:13. “Brigands” in 6:23.

Footnote 27:13. This verse is an almost verbatim quotation of Zophar in 20:29, a fact that supports the thesis that Job is here citing what he regards as the absurd contentions of his friends.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:14–15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Should he have many children—the sword is their fate;

And as for his offspring, they will want for food.

Those who survive him will be buried in a plague;

And their widows will not mourn them.

---

Footnote 27:14–15. The wicked.

Footnote 27:14–15. Literally, “to the sword”; compare Jeremiah 15:2.

Footnote 27:14–15. Compare Bildad in 18:16, 19; Zophar in 20:4–11, 21, 26; Eliphaz in 22:15–16, 20.

Footnote 27:14–15. For “death” in the sense of plague, see for example Exodus 10:17; Jeremiah 15:2.

Footnote 27:14–15. Compare Psalm 78:64.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:16–17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If he amasses silver like dirt,

And stores up garments like soil,

He will store up—but the righteous will wear;

And the silver—the innocent will receive.

---

Footnote 27:16–17. More literally, “receive as a portion,” echoing verse 13 above. For a similar sentiment, compare Zophar in 20:18–21.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Like a spider he builds his house;

Like the hut a watchman makes.

---

Footnote 27:18. Two letters have fallen out of the word *‘akabish* “spider,” leaving the word *‘ash* “moth”; the correct reading is supported by the ancient Greek and Syriac translations; and compare Saadia and R. Meyuhhas, among others. For the image of a flimsy spider’s web, see Bildad in 8:13–15; compare 18:14–15.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He lies down wealthy but it will not persist;

He will open his eyes—and be (wealthy) no more.

---

Footnote 27:19. Reading *yosip* “he adds” for *ye’asep* “he is gathered” with support of the Greek and Syriac translations. For the verb *hosip* spelled with *aleph*, see for example 1 Samuel 18:20.

Footnote 27:19. Compare Zophar in 20:7–9.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:20–21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Terror will wash him away like water;

A whirlwind at night will steal him away.

An east wind will lift him and blow,

Sweeping him far from his home.

---

Footnote 27:20–21. Compare Zophar in 20:28 and Eliphaz in 22:11. For the image, compare Hosea 5:10. The verb has the sense of “wash away” in Aramaic.

Footnote 27:20–21. Compare Bildad in 18:14.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He shoots at him without sparing (an arrow);

And he flees in flight from his hand.

---

Footnote 27:22. God. This verse alludes to Zophar’s image of the wicked fleeing the weapons of the deity in 20:24–25. Only in that context can the laconic language of the verse be understood (Kahana).

Footnote 27:22. For the context and usages, see Jeremiah 50:14.

Footnote 27:22. The wicked.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 27:23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

People clap their hands at him,

And hiss at him wherever they are.”

---

Footnote 27:23. Singular in the Hebrew: “one claps one’s hands …”

Footnote 27:23. Clapping and hissing are gestures a spectator makes when seeing the abject; see Lamentations 2:15. Job apparently alludes to Bildad’s description of people’s reactions to the wicked’s downfall in 18:20.
