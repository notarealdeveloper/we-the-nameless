------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Elihu and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:2–4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hear my words, O men of wisdom!

Give ear to me, O men of knowledge!

“For an ear tests words

As the palate tastes food.”

Let us examine the charges,

So together we can find what’s correct.

---

Footnote 34:2–4. For “taste” as a metaphor of reason, see the comment on 6:6. For *le’ekol* “to eat” read *lo ’okel* on the basis of 12:11, where the same traditional saying is quoted. So several ancient versions.

Footnote 34:2–4. In Aramaic synonymous with *baḥan* “test” in the preceding verse.

Footnote 34:2–4. Literally, “good”; but *tob* has the basic meaning of “sweet.” Thus “sweet taste” (verse 3) suggests “reasonable.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:5–6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For Job has said, “I’m in the right;

But El’s set aside my charges.

For the charges against me I’m pained;

Although I’ve not sinned, my ‘arrow’ is fatal.”

---

Footnote 34:5–6. Quoting Job in 27:2.

Footnote 34:5–6. Reading *’ek’ab* for *’akazzeb* “I lie, disappoint,” which makes no sense here. Compare Jeremiah 15:18.

Footnote 34:5–6. Divine affliction; see 6:4.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:7–9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

What man like Job

Drinks scorn like water;

And keeps company with workers of evil,

Traveling with wicked men?!

For he’s said: “A man does not benefit

When he pleases Elohim.”

---

Footnote 34:7–9. Elihu turns Job’s image in 6:4 against him. He is paraphrasing Eliphaz’s parody of the spirit in 15:16.

Footnote 34:7–9. Compare the scene described in Proverbs 1:10–19.

Footnote 34:7–9. Job has not said precisely this; but compare Malachi 3:14.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:10–11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

And so, men of mind,

Listen to me!

Wickedness—far be it from El!

Far be wrongdoing from Shaddai!

For he pays a person his (fair) wage;

Provides in line with one’s conduct.

---

Footnote 34:10–11. Literally, “heart”; compare 12:3.

Footnote 34:10–11. Elihu echoes or mimics Job’s “God forbid” in 27:5.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:12–13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

In truth, El does not do evil;

Shaddai does not corrupt justice.

Who gives him charge of the land?

Puts the whole earth in his hands?

---

Footnote 34:12–13. Elihu answers Bildad’s rhetorical question in 8:3.

Footnote 34:12–13. Literally, “Who has imposed (on him) the entire earth?”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:14–15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If he returns its spirit to him,

And gathers its breath to himself,

All that is flesh will expire,

And the human returns to the dust.

---

Footnote 34:14–15. Reading *yashib* for *yasim* “he places,” with the eastern Masoretic tradition. Compare Psalm 104:29. The word *libbo* “his heart” in the received text was apparently added secondarily to furnish an object to “he places.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If you’ve understanding, pray hear this!

Pray give ear to the gist of my words!

---

Footnote 34:16. The singular indicates Elihu is now addressing Job.

Footnote 34:16. The word “voice” (*qol*) can by metonymy indicate a message (for example Leviticus 5:1).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:17–20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Would a hater of justice bind (wounds)?

Would you condemn the Eminently Righteous—

Who would call a king worthless,

(Who would call) nobles wicked;

Who would show no favor to officers;

Nor treat magnates better than poor folk—

For they all are the work of his hands?

In an instant they can die,

In dead of night they can pass;

They expire like a moth;

They perish by no (human) hand.

---

Footnote 34:17–20. Compare 5:18.

Footnote 34:17–20. The deity. Compare God’s words in 40:8.

Footnote 34:17–20. Revocalizing *ha’amor* “calling” as *ha’omer* in line with some ancient versions. The deity gives equal justice to all.

Footnote 34:17–20. Literally, “recognize”; a rare usage of the *Pi‘el* form in this sense (so BDB).

Footnote 34:17–20. Perhaps an allusion to the tenth plague in Egypt (Exodus 12:29).

Footnote 34:17–20. The verse is by many modern accounts in disorder; I read the verb phrase “they pass” at the end of this line, not the next.

Footnote 34:17–20. Reading *yigwe‘u ‘im ‘ash* for *yego‘ashu ‘am* “the people are in turmoil,” which is inapt; it is the death of the powerful that is relevant. For the image, compare Psalm 39:12.

Footnote 34:17–20. Reading *yasuru* for *we-yasiru* “they remove.” The word *’abbir* “bull, potentate” is a dittography of *(lo)’ beyad* “not by hand.” The scribe forgot to write the *l*, realized his mistake, and recopied, without erasing the mistake (see above at 22:8).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:21–22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For his eyes are (trained) on the ways of a man;

And all of his steps he sees.

There is no darkness or deathly-shade

Where workers of evil can hide.

---

Footnote 34:21–22. Compare Job in 31:4. Job has found divine surveillance to be hostile; compare 14:16.

Footnote 34:21–22. Contra Job’s account in 24:14–17.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Thus a man should no more make a case,

To enter a lawsuit with El.

---

Footnote 34:23. For this expression, see at 23:6.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:24–27

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He smashes the eminent without limit;

And installs others in their stead.

He surely knows their activities;

He turns over the night and they’re crushed!

He strikes them down where the wicked stand,

In a place where all can see;

Because they strayed from behind him,

And none of his ways did they learn:

---

Footnote 34:24–27. The discourse picks up the argument of verses 21–22 above. Elihu alludes to the wording of Job, quoting the spirit, in 4:19. Compare the image used by the deity in 38:13–15.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:28–29

------------------------------------------------------------------------

To bring to him the poor man’s cry;

So he can hear the cry of the indigent.

When he calms (it) down,

Who would stir (it) up?

When he hides his face,

Who could perceive it?

He looks over people and nations;

---

Footnote 34:28–29. More literally, “quiets.”

Footnote 34:28–29. The verb *hirshia‘*, ordinarily “to act wickedly,” in Job has the sense of *hir‘ish* “to cause noise” (Ibn Ezra, Masnuth); see at 3:17.

Footnote 34:28–29. The line “over people and nation together” is a fragment. For *yaḥad* “together” I read, with Ehrlich, *yaḥaz* “he looks” (short form); compare the short form *’aḥaz* “I look” in 23:9.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:30

------------------------------------------------------------------------

So an impudent man does not reign—

Not one who entraps his people.

---

Footnote 34:30. In sin; compare for example Exodus 23:33; 34:12. Literally, “one of the traps of the people.” This verse continues the theme of verses 24–27.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:31–32

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Thus to Eloah you must say:

“I will bear (the yoke), I will not break (it).

What I cannot see myself, you must teach me.

If I have done an injustice, I will do so no more.”

---

Footnote 34:31–32. Slightly redividing the words (with some moderns): *’el ’eloah ’emor* for “to El be said,” which is also ungrammatical.

Footnote 34:31–32. The object of the verbs is apparently the “yoke” (*‘ol*) of the deity; see Lamentations 3:27 and Isaiah 10:27, respectively (so Hakham).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:33

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Should he reward you as you reckon,

Though you are “fed up”?

(Saying,) “You should choose, not I.

Whatever you think—speak!”

---

Footnote 34:33. Literally, “from what is with you”; for a similar usage, see 10:13.

Footnote 34:33. Elihu alludes to Job in 7:16.

Footnote 34:33. Compare Ehrlich, Hakham.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:34–35

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Men of mind will tell me,

Any wise man who hears me (will):

“Job speaks not from knowledge;

His words have no insight.”

---

Footnote 34:34–35. See note 10 above.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 34:36–37

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Would that Job be tested forever,

The way men of evil are answered!

For he keeps adding to his sin;

Among us his crime is unbounded;

And he multiplies words against El.

---

Footnote 34:36–37. Literally, “through answers against men of iniquity.”

Footnote 34:36–37. For s*apak* “be abundant,” see Isaiah 2:6.

Footnote 34:36–37. The verb (*yireb*) puns on the term for bringing legal charges (*rib*).
