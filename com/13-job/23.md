------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 23:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Job and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 23:2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My complaint is no less bitter today;

His hand weighs down on my moaning.

---

Footnote 23:2. See 7:11.

Footnote 23:2. “Hand of God” denotes a plague or calamity in ancient Semitic, and compare for example Exodus 9:3; 1 Samuel 5:9. The expression “the hand of the Lord weighed down upon” conveys an affliction of the enemy in 1 Samuel 5:6. Reading *yado* “his hand” for *yadi* “my hand” with the Greek and Syriac versions.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 23:3–5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If he would let me know how to find him,

I would come before his seat.

I would lay before him my lawsuit,

And fill my mouth with accusations.

I would know the words he would answer me,

And discern what he would say to me.

---

Footnote 23:3–5. Compare 13:18.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 23:6–7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Would he argue with me by force of might?

No, he would make his own case against me!

There the upright would have a hearing with him,

And I would truly get my day-in-court.

---

Footnote 23:6–7. What Job feared in 13:20–21.

Footnote 23:6–7. The verb *sim* “set” is an ellipsis of the Joban phrase “set (make) a legal case” in 5:8; this is confirmed by Elihu’s elaboration of the same ellipsis in 34:23.

Footnote 23:6–7. At the deity’s seat of justice (verse 3).

Footnote 23:6–7. A technical term for legal disputation in Job, related to “accusations” in verse 4 above.

Footnote 23:6–7. Comparing a similar Arabic expression (Kopf) and see 1 Samuel 15:29.

Footnote 23:6–7. Used of an animal snatching its prey in Isaiah 5:29.

Footnote 23:6–7. Reading *mishpati* “judgment, litigation” for *mi-shopti* “from my judge.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 23:8–9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But east I go, and he is not (there);

And west, but I do not discern him.

North, in his concealment I do not grasp (him);

He cloaks (himself) south, so I do not see (him).

---

Footnote 23:8–9. The verb *‘asah* in the sense of “cover” is an Arabism.

Footnote 23:8–9. The verb has a double sense: “see” (short form of *ḥazah*) and “grasp” (*’aḥaz*).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 23:10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yet he knows what path is mine;

Were he to test me, I’d emerge like (pure) gold.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 23:11–12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My foot has held fast to his course,

His path I have kept and not strayed.

The command of his lips I have never neglected,

In my bosom I’ve stored the words of his mouth.

---

Footnote 23:11–12. The same verb as “grasp” (see verse 9).

Footnote 23:11–12. Compare *mush* “relegate to oblivion” in Zechariah 3:9 and Akkadian *meshu* “to forget” (Held, “Studies”).

Footnote 23:11–12. Reading *beḥeqi* “in my bosom” for *meḥuqqi* “more than my allotment” with the Greek and Latin versions and in conformity with the similar expression in Psalm 119:11.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 23:13–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Once he’s chosen (a fate), who can reverse it?

Once his heart desires, he enacts it.

When he’s made complete my current lot,

He still has in hand much more of the same.

---

Footnote 23:13–14. Reading *baḥar* “he has chosen” for *be’eḥad* “in one” (*d* and *r* are often confused). Compare the pairing of verbs in Psalm 132:13.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 23:15–16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

That is why I’d be shaken in his presence:

I would apprehend—and be afraid of him.

For El has weakened my resolve;

And Shaddai has made me shaken.

---

Footnote 23:15–16. Job’s rejoinder to Eliphaz in 22:10.

Footnote 23:15–16. Literally, “softened my heart”—demoralized me.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 23:17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yet I am not devastated by gloom,

Nor has darkness covered my face.

---

Footnote 23:17. Literally, “by the face of gloom, darkness.”

Footnote 23:17. The language is somewhat unclear. For *mippanai* “from before me” in the second line read perhaps *panai* “my face” (with some versions).
