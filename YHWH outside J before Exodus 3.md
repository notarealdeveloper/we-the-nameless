## Footnotes to Add

### Genesis 14:22

Genesis 14 is basically its own thing. “El Elyon” occurs here in 14:18–22 and nowhere else in Genesis. “Creator/possessor of heaven and earth” is also not normal J/E/P wording. More importantly, Melchizedek says simply “El Elyon” in 14:18–20, while Abram says “YHWH, El Elyon” in 14:22. This looks like an independent El Elyon tradition later explicitly identified with YHWH.

### Genesis 20:18

Genesis 20 is very E-looking. God is “Elohim” in 20:3, 6, 11, 13, 17. God communicates in a dream in 20:3, 6; dreams pile up in E material: Gen 20:3, 6; 28:12; 31:10–11, 24; 37:5–10; 40:5–23; 41:1–32. Abraham is called a “prophet” in 20:7, the only occurrence of the word in Genesis. “Fear of Elohim” also shows up in E material in 20:11; 22:12; 42:18. Then 20:17 finishes the story with Abraham praying to Elohim and Elohim healing Abimelech, while 20:18 adds, “because YHWH had closed the wombs.” The switch to YHWH happens exactly in an explanatory sentence tacked onto an otherwise very E-looking ending.

### Genesis 21:33

Beer-sheba itself tells us little: J uses it too, e.g. Gen 26:23, 33; 28:10; 46:5. The interesting bit is “El Olam,” which occurs nowhere else in Genesis and looks like the same general kind of old “El + title” name as “El Elyon” in Gen 14. But “call on the name of YHWH” is very J-like: Gen 4:26; 12:8; 13:4; 26:25. So this verse looks mixed: the unusual “El Olam” tradition plus a formula J repeatedly uses for worship of YHWH. It could be E/older material with a J or RJE formula added.

### Genesis 22:11–16

The base story uses E's normal language: “Elohim” in 22:1, 8, 12, including “fear Elohim” in 22:12, compare E material in 20:11 and 42:18. Then suddenly: “messenger of YHWH” (22:11), “YHWH Yir'eh” (22:14), “messenger of YHWH” again (22:15), and “utterance of YHWH” (22:16). The seam is almost comically visible: the “messenger of YHWH” says, “now I know that you fear Elohim.” “Utterance of YHWH” is especially odd in Genesis but extremely common in later prophetic speech: Isa 1:24; 3:15; 14:22–23; Jer 1:8, 15; 2:3, 9; Amos 2:11, 16; 3:10, 13. This looks like an E story with a later YHWH layer still visibly wrapped around it.

### Genesis 28:21

This one may not be a contradiction at all. Dreams are heavily E-associated: Gen 20:3, 6; 28:12; 31:10–11, 24; 37:5–10; 40:5–23; 41:1–32. Standing stones also recur in E-linked Jacob material: Gen 28:18, 22; 31:45, 51–52; 35:14. And the actual sentence says, “If Elohim is with me ... then YHWH will be my God.” Jacob does not start by casually calling Elohim “YHWH”; YHWH appears in the result of the condition. E may therefore be deliberately saying: if the Elohim who appeared to Jacob actually protects him, Jacob will accept YHWH as his God.
