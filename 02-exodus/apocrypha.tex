Sadly for the curious layperson, the screenplay is a bit heavy of mouth and tongue.\footnote{That is, a foreign film.}

Namely, when the original Nameless text was originally discovered, we believed it to have been composed entirely in an odd dialect of German. The plot seemed to be a story in which YHWH is looking for Moses, and cannot find him, as can be seen here:

Since ``losing track of someone'' is a common theme in YHWH related stories (history or narrative) of all forms, the assumption that we'd understood the text went unchallenged for some time.

However, a breakthrough came recently when an anonymous individual calling himself ``Adam Ben Ben Ben Möses'' (sic?) made an anonymous post on a forum of Nameless Studies on the The Dynamic Read-Writable Free Encyclopedic Repository of the Modern State of Human Knowledge.

No claim is made as to the complete accuracy of the translation, but we believe it is roughly accurate in most details.

Whatever imperfections might remain, the text does appear to read as two completely different scenes in two different languages: a criterion now understood \mdash{} thanks to recent advances in Nameless Studies \mdash{} to be a requirement for all authentic texts written by ``the hand of god,'' or by the authentic sect of its nameless authors.

The biblically knowledgeable reader will likely recognize a similarity to Exodus 4's notoriously difficult ``Lodging Place'' scene, specifically where the text below reads ``Die Mösen'' and later in the same passage, ``Wen Möses die?''

The scholarly community is just coming to grips with the possibility that all ancient texts previously attributed to a god or gods appear to share the ``dual reading'' property.

This suggests that the above mentioned section of Exodus 4 may have simply been a result of the deity (or the author (or both)) making a series of crude plays on words, in multiple languages and language families, and that the original text may have been not far from the fragment below.

Just a final note: the text that follows is not appropriate for children, so please do make sure to put the kids to bed before reading any further. Thank you for reading, and we hope you find the text to be clarifying of whatever relationship you may have with the Lord, or perhaps clarifying of any of the topics discussed directly in the body of the text... if you're into that sort of thing.
