I Samuel 13:1	Saul was… years old when he became king, and he reigned over Israel two years.
I Samuel 13:2	Saul picked 3,000 Israelites, of whom 2,000 were with Saul in Michmas and in the hill country of Bethel, and 1,000 with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin; the rest of the troops he sent back to their homes.
I Samuel 13:3	Jonathan struck down the Philistine prefect in Geba; and the Philistines heard about it. Saul had the ram’s horn sounded throughout the land, saying, “Let the Hebrews hear.”
I Samuel 13:4	When all Israel heard that Saul had struck down the Philistine prefect, and that Israel had incurred the wrath of the Philistines, all the people rallied to Saul at Gilgal.
I Samuel 13:5	The Philistines, in turn, gathered to attack Israel: 30,000 chariots and 6,000 horsemen, and troops as numerous as the sands of the seashore. They marched up and encamped at Michmas, east of Beth-aven.
I Samuel 13:6	When the men of Israel saw that they were in trouble—for the troops were hard pressed—the people hid in caves, among thorns, among rocks, in tunnels, and in cisterns.
I Samuel 13:7	Some Hebrews crossed the Jordan, [to] the territory of Gad and Gilead. Saul was still at Gilgal, and the rest of the people rallied to him in alarm.d
I Samuel 13:8	He waited seven days, the time that Samuel [had set]. But when Samuel failed to come to Gilgal, and the people began to scatter,
I Samuel 13:9	Saul said, “Bring me the burnt offering and the sacrifice of well-being”; and he presented the burnt offering.
I Samuel 13:10	He had just finished presenting the burnt offering when Samuel arrived; and Saul went out to meet him and welcome him.
I Samuel 13:11	But Samuel said, “What have you done?” Saul replied, “I saw the people leaving me and scattering; you had not come at the appointed time, and the Philistines had gathered at Michmas.
I Samuel 13:12	I thought the Philistines would march down against me at Gilgal before I had entreated YHWH, so I forced myself to present the burnt offering.”
I Samuel 13:13	Samuel answered Saul, “You acted foolishly in not keeping the commandments that YHWH your God laid upon you! Otherwise YHWH would have established your dynasty over Israel forever.
I Samuel 13:14	But now your dynasty will not endure. YHWH will seek out a man after His own heart, and YHWH will appoint him ruler over His people, because you did not abide by what YHWH had commanded you.”
I Samuel 13:15	Samuel arose and went up from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin. Saul numbered the troops who remained with him—about 600 strong.
I Samuel 13:16	Saul and his son Jonathan, and the troops who remained with them, stayed in Geba of Benjamin, while the Philistines were encamped at Michmas.
I Samuel 13:17	The raiders came out of the Philistine camp in three columns: One column headed for the Ophrah road that leads to the district of Shual,
I Samuel 13:18	another column headed for the Beth-horon road, and the third column headed for the border road that overlooks the valley of Zeboim toward the desert.
I Samuel 13:19	No smith was to be found in all the land of Israel, for the Philistines were afraid that the Hebrews would make swords or spears.
I Samuel 13:20	So all the Israelites had to go down to the Philistines to have their plowshares, their mattocks, axes, and colters sharpened.
I Samuel 13:21	The charge for sharpening was a pim for plowshares, mattocks, three-pronged forks, and axes, and for setting the goads.
I Samuel 13:22	Thus on the day of the battle, no sword or spear was to be found in the possession of any of the troops with Saul and Jonathan; only Saul and Jonathan had them.
I Samuel 13:23	Now the Philistine garrison had marched out to the pass of Michmas.
