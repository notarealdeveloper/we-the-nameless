------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Call out now! Does anyone answer you?

To whom of the holy ones can you turn?

---

Footnote 5:1. 4:12–21 has been moved to precede 4:1; see above for the passage and for a brief justification of the move.

Footnote 5:1. Eliphaz mocks what to him is Job’s illusion that he has experienced a divine revelation.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For exasperation can kill the rogue,

And passion can bring death to the fool.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:3–4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I have myself seen a rogue striking roots,

And have observed the homestead of fools.

His sons are kept far from relief;

Oppressed at the city-gate, no one delivers them.

---

Footnote 5:3–4. Reading *peta’im* “fools” for *pit’om* “suddenly” (compare Proverbs 14:18) and finding behind *wa’eqqob* “I cursed” a synonym for “I have seen,” possibly *wa’abbit*.

Footnote 5:3–4. They are easily victimized in legal proceedings, which are held at the town gate. Compare Isaiah 59:11.

Footnote 5:3–4. Compare Proverbs 22:22.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

As for his harvest—famine will consume it;

And their substance—desiccation will remove it;

And drought will devour their wealth.

---

Footnote 5:5. Vocalizing *ra‘ab* for *ra‘eb* “the hungry.” The verse is extraordinarily difficult, and no translations are convincing.

Footnote 5:5. Reading *we’ulam* (see Psalm 73:4; so for example Tur-Sinai).

Footnote 5:5. Reconstructing a noun from the root *ts-n-m* “dried out” (Genesis 41:23).

Footnote 5:5. Reading *tsama’* “thirst, drought,” often found in conjunction with *ra‘ab* (Deuteronomy 28:48; Isaiah 5:13) for otherwise unknown *tsammim*.

Footnote 5:5. Reading *sapa’* (compare Hebrew *mispo’* “fodder” and Ugaritic *sapa’a* “consume”) for *sha’ap* “pant, aspire.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:6–7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For suffering does not stem from the dust,

Nor does travail grow from the ground.

But a human is born to travail,

As “sons of Resheph” fly up high.

---

Footnote 5:6–7. “Ground” (*’adama*) is of the same stem as “human” (*’adam*), which appears in the next verse.

Footnote 5:6–7. Or through revocalizing: *yolid* “gives birth (to travail)”; compare Job 15:34–35; Psalm 7:15.

Footnote 5:6–7. Arrows. Resheph is a Canaanite god of pestilence and is sometimes depicted with arrows, symbolizing his afflictions; he is called Lord of the Arrow. Compare Psalm 76:4; see 6:4 for an echo by Job. Others interpret “sons of flame, sparks,” comparing Song of Songs 8:6.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Rather I would seek out El,

Before Elohim would I lodge my complaint.

---

Footnote 5:8. Eliphaz resumes the thought of verse 1. He lays heavy emphasis on “El” (the name of God throughout Canaan, meaning “God”) through sound repetition: *’ulam* (“rather”) … *’el ’el* (“to El”) … *(we)’el ’elohim* (“and to Elohim”). The phrase “lodge a complaint,” literally, “place a word,” is an apparent calque from Babylonian, where it carries the connotation of lodging a legal complaint (Paul). Eliphaz may not intend to suggest that Job sue God, but Job, as it turns out, would seem to infer the idea.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:9–10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

(El) who performs great things too deep to probe,

Wondrous things, beyond number;

Who gives rain over the face of the earth,

And sends water over the face of the ranges.

---

Footnote 5:9–10. Eliphaz draws on clichés; compare for example Amos 4:13; 5:8; Psalms 136:4; 145:3.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:11–13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Placing the lowly up high,

So the stooped attain relief.

Who thwarts the plans of the treacherous,

So their hands can perform nothing clever.

Who catches the wise in their treachery,

So the perverse take their counsel in haste.

---

Footnote 5:11–13. In Biblical Hebrew *qoded* “stooped, lowly, depressed” seems to merge with *qoder* “dark, depressed” (Psalms 35:14; 38:7; 42:10; 43:2).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

In daytime they encounter darkness,

It’s like night when they grope at midday.

---

Footnote 5:14. Compare Job 12:25; Deuteronomy 28:29.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He has saved the swordless from the tormentor,

And the needy from the powerful.

---

Footnote 5:15. Reading *mippi ham* (compare Job 36:16: *mippi tsar* “from adversity”) for *mippihem* “from them.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Thus is there hope for the poor;

As (all) injustice is silenced.

---

Footnote 5:16. Literally, “and injustice shuts its mouth.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:17–18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Happy is the mortal whom Eloah reproves—

Do not reject Shaddai’s discipline.

For once he inflicts pain, he binds up;

Once he strikes, his own hands heal.

---

Footnote 5:17–18. Shaddai is another name for the deity; see the Note on (Not) Translating the Names of God. Eliphaz explains Job’s afflictions as only a warning from God, to keep him honest, as it were. For this conventional wisdom, compare Deuteronomy 8:5; Psalm 94:12; Proverbs 3:11–12.

Footnote 5:17–18. Compare Deuteronomy 32:39; Hosea 6:1.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

From six misfortunes he will deliver you,

In seven no harm will affect you.

---

Footnote 5:19. For the nuance of “affect,” see on 1:11. In numerical sequences of x, then x+1, in the Bible, it is the latter number that is definitive. Seven misfortunes will be enumerated.

## Job 5:20–23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

In famine he will ransom you from Death,

And in war, from the sword.

From the lashing of a slanderous-tongue you’ll be hidden;

And from oncoming disaster you will have no fear.

At a demon and at stones (at your feet) you will laugh;

And from wild animals you will have no fear.

You’ll be rather in league with the stones of the field,

And the beasts of the field will be at peace with you.

---

Footnote 5:20–23. Perhaps an allusion to the Canaanite god of death, Mot; compare 18:13–14.

Footnote 5:20–23. Both *shod* “disaster” and *kapan*, Aramaic for “famine,” have already been enumerated. Accordingly, revocalize them as *shed* “demon” and *kepin*, Aramaic for “stones”; see *kepim* in Job 30:6. “Stones” is recapitulated (in Hebrew) in the next verse. Compare Psalm 91 for the combined dangers of demons and stones that may cause one to stumble or bruise (verses 6, 12–13).

Footnote 5:20–23. Literally, “the animals of the earth.”

Footnote 5:20–23. Compare Hosea 2:20.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

You will find your abode is at peace;

You’ll take stock of your homestead with nothing amiss.

---

Footnote 5:24. Literally, “know.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:25–26

------------------------------------------------------------------------

You will find that your seed is numerous,

And your offshoots like the grass of the earth.

You will reach the grave like a ripened-stalk,

Like the rising of grain in its season.

---

Footnote 5:25–26. Literally, “know.”

Footnote 5:25–26. Compare *qelaḥ* in Rabbinic Hebrew and see Proverbs 11:30, where instead of *l-q-ḥ* read *q-l-ḥ* “stalk” (of life-breath) in parallelism with “tree of life.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 5:27

------------------------------------------------------------------------

You see, we have probed this—it’s true.

Pray listen, and know it yourself!
