## Job 1:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

A man there was in the Land of Uts—Job/Iyyob was his name;

and that man was whole (in heart) and straight (of path), and fearing of Elohim and turning from evil.

---

Footnote 1:1. There may have been a locale of this name in northwestern Arabia, but the Hebrew name connotes counsel, wisdom—a land of the wise; see 1 Kings 5:10, where the legendary King Solomon’s wisdom is compared with that of Qedem and Egypt. Uts is located in the southern Transjordan in Lamentations 4:21, where it is associated with Edom; and in Jeremiah 49:7 and Obadiah 8 Edom is noted for its wisdom.

Footnote 1:1. The name is attested in the fourteenth century BCE as that of a Transjordanian ruler, Ayyabu. The name (in Akkadian) means “Where is (God) the Father?” but also connotes “enemy.” Job will play on this latter meaning in 13:24.

Footnote 1:1. See especially 1 Kings 9:4 and compare “pure of heart” (restored) in Job 36:5. But there is also a connotation of pure of path (see Job 4:6; 22:3; compare Psalm 13:6). Ironically, Job’s illness will be represented by figures of disintegration, as physical wholeness will signify wellness; compare for example Psalm 38:4. Being whole in body ought to be the reward for someone who is whole in heart—a person of integrity.

Footnote 1:1. More than simply piety, “fearing God” denotes a fundamental commitment to decency; compare Genesis 20:11; Exodus 1:17. See the Note on (Not) Translating the Names of God.

## Job 1:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

There were born to him seven sons and three daughters.

His livestock were seven thousand small-cattle and three thousand camels and five hundred yoke of large-cattle and five hundred she-asses, and a very large servantry.

That man was greater (in wealth) than all the sons of Qedem.

---

Footnote 1:2–3. One cannot tell from the sequence of verbs whether Job’s material prosperity followed or preceded his pious conduct—whether he was rewarded for his piety or whether it was coincidental.

Footnote 1:2–3. The term “livestock” (*miqneh*) is associated with Israel’s patriarchs; see especially Genesis 46:32.

Footnote 1:2–3. Sheep and goats.

Footnote 1:2–3. A rare collective term that in context alludes to Genesis 26:13–14 and other verses evoking the wealth of the Hebrew patriarchs. Job is depicted as a patriarchal type.

Footnote 1:2–3. For *gadal* indicating wealth, see Genesis 24:35; 26:13; 1 Samuel 25:2; 2 Samuel 19:33.

Footnote 1:2–3. Qedem, “the East,” is identified with the area of Harran, the Hebrew patriarchs’ homeland, in Genesis 29:1–4; see also Numbers 23:7; but here it may refer to the entire Transjordan.

## Job 1:4–5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

His sons would go about making a drinking-feast at the house of each on his birthday.

They would send (messengers) and call for their three sisters, to eat and to drink with them.

When the days of a drinking-feast would come around, Job would send (messengers) to sanctify them;

And he would rise early in the morning and offer up offerings in the number of them all, for Job would say (to himself):

“Perhaps my children have sinned by ‘blessing’ (cursing) Elohim in their hearts.”

Thus would Job do all the days.

---

Footnote 1:4–5. Compare Pharaoh’s birthday celebration (Genesis 40:20) and see Job 3:1.

Footnote 1:4–5. Rising early evokes the Hebrew patriarchs; see for example Genesis 19:27; 20:8; 21:14; 22:3; 26:31; 28:18; 32:1.

Footnote 1:4–5. That is, ten offerings.

Footnote 1:4–5. “Blessing” is used as a euphemism for (God forbid) cursing God.

Footnote 1:4–5. Not (God forbid) out loud.

Footnote 1:4–5. On each of the children’s celebrations; or every year, all the time (see 1 Samuel 1:21; 2:19).

## Job 1:6–8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

On a (certain) day, the sons of Elohim came to station themselves around YHWH, and the Satan too came among them.

YHWH said to the Satan,

“Whence do you come?”

And the Satan answered YHWH:

“From roving the earth and from going all about it.”

YHWH said to the Satan:

“Have you set your mind on my servant Job,

that there is none like him on earth—a man whole (in heart) and straight (of path), fearing of Elohim and turning from evil?”

---

Footnote 1:6–8. For this usage, compare 1 Samuel 1:4.

Footnote 1:6–8. Once denoting the lesser deities (Genesis 6:1–4), the term has come to mean the members of the divine court, the angels (compare Job 38:7).

Footnote 1:6–8. The personal name of the God of Israel, traditionally rendered “the LORD.” See the introductory comment to this section. The verb “to station oneself on” connotes service.

Footnote 1:6–8. The Satan’s role is to accuse and prosecute people for their transgressions. The title is derived from a term meaning “obstruction” (Numbers 22:32) or “opposition” (1 Samuel 29:4). The Satan seeks to trip up human beings and then report on their missteps; but he is also the contrarian, who contradicts the opinions of God.

Footnote 1:6–8. The verb “rove” (*shut*) connotes spying (Zechariah 4:10) and is associated by wordplay with the look-alike term *Satan*.

Footnote 1:6–8. Literally, “your heart.”

## Job 1:9–11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Satan answered YHWH and said:

“Is it for nothing that Job is Elohim-fearing?

You have put a hedge around him and around his household and around all that is his.

You have blessed all that his hands do, so that his livestock have waxed abundantly in the land.

However—send forth your hand and affect all that is his,

I swear he will ‘bless’ (curse) you to your face!”

---

Footnote 1:9–11. Some render “possessions,” but when the term *miqneh* is not subjoined to another term it refers only to cattle.

Footnote 1:9–11. Compare Genesis 30:29–30.

Footnote 1:9–11. Hebrew *naga‘* has a strong connotation of affliction, plague.

Footnote 1:9–11. A positive oath formula: “If X is not so (then may disaster befall me).”

## Job 1:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

YHWH said to the Satan:

“Here: all that is his is in your hand.

Just do not send forth your hand against him.”

The Satan took leave of YHWH.

---

Footnote 1:12. Against him directly.

Footnote 1:12. Literally, “went out from before the face of YHWH,” from attending YHWH.

## Job 1:13–15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

On a (certain) day, his sons and his daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their brother, the firstborn,

when a messenger came to Job and said:

“The cows were plowing and the she-asses grazing nearby them,

when Sheba fell upon and took them,

and the attendants they struck down by sword.

Just I alone have escaped to tell you.”

---

Footnote 1:13–15. See above, verse 6.

Footnote 1:13–15. Job’s.

Footnote 1:13–15. The Hebrew term can also denote a divine messenger, an angel (for example in 4:18).

Footnote 1:13–15. A collective—the Sabeans; a south Arabian people whose famous queen paid a visit to King Solomon (1 Kings 10).

Footnote 1:13–15. Compare the fugitive who reported to Abra(ha)m in Genesis 14:13; and see 1 Kings 18:22.

## Job 1:16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

While this one is speaking, this (other) one comes and says:

“A fire of Elohim fell from the sky;

it burned the small-cattle and the attendants, and it consumed them.

Just I alone have escaped to tell you.”

---

Footnote 1:16. For the pattern, see 1 Kings 1:42.

Footnote 1:16. Compare the divine fire from the sky (not necessary lightning) in 2 Kings 1:12.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 1:17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

While this one is speaking, this (other) one comes and says:

“Chaldeans formed three columns, and they swept over the camels and took them;

and the attendants they struck down by sword.

Just I alone have escaped to tell you.”

---

Footnote 1:17. A tribal people from Mesopotamia, attacking from the north.

Footnote 1:17. Literally, “heads.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 1:18–19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

While this one is speaking, this (other) one comes and says:

“Your sons and your daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their brother, the firstborn,

When here: a great wind came from across the desert;

it affected the four corners of the house;

it fell on the young people and they died.

Just I alone have escaped to tell you.”

---

Footnote 1:18–19. See on 1:11 above.

Footnote 1:18–19. “Young people” is the same word as “attendants” in verses 14, 16, and 17.

## Job 1:20–21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Job arose; he tore his coat; he shaved his head;

he fell to the ground and prostrated himself.

He said: “Naked came I out of my mother’s womb,

and naked will I return there.

YHWH has given, and YHWH has taken.

May the name of YHWH be blessed.”

---

Footnote 1:20–21. It is unusual that Job, a non-Israelite, refers to the deity by his Israelite name YHWH. The lines may be formulaic, placed without alteration by the narrator in the mouth of Job.

## Job 1:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

In spite of all this, Job did not commit-a-sin—

he did not speak insult to Elohim.

---

Footnote 1:22. Compare Akkadian *tapiltu*, “unseemly speech.”
