------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Job and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Truly you are people-of-intelligence,

And with you wisdom will die.

I have a heart just like you.

I fall no lower than you.

And who doesn’t have such (sayings) as these?:

---

Footnote 12:2–3. There is likely a play on the Egyptian word for intelligence *‘am*, which is a regular Hebrew term for “people” (suggested to me by Stephen Geller).

Footnote 12:2–3. A mind; an apparent response to Zophar in 11:12.

Footnote 12:2–3. I am not inferior to you.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:4–6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

“One who laughs at calamity and disaster,

Calls out to Eloah and is answered—

The laugh of the wholly righteous—

He shows contempt for catastrophe,

At a time of devastation is at ease.

He is firm when the foot might stumble.

Tents are tranquil at a time of marauders,

Secure at a time of those who rile El—

At whatever the hand of Eloah has wrought.”

---

Footnote 12:4–6. This verse begins a series of platitudes, reminiscent of those of his companions. It is clear from the next line that a third-person subject is called for. For *seḥoq* read *soḥeq* in conformity with the participle that follows in the next line. For *le-re‘ehu* I read *le-ra‘ah* and for *’ehyeh* I read *howah* “disaster” (see Isaiah 47:11). (I am indebted to Ginsberg for most of the reconstruction of this passage.)

Footnote 12:4–6. Read *le-pid;* for *pid* “catastrophe,” see Job 30:24 and 31:29 as well as Proverbs 24:22.

Footnote 12:4–6. For the notoriously difficult *le‘ashtut* I read *le‘et sheyt*, “at the time of devastation”; for *shet* (written with *aleph*), see Lamentations 3:47 and compare the cognate *sho’ah* in Proverbs 3:25. For the spelling without *aleph*, compare the spelling of *se’et* “grandeur” without *aleph* in Job 41:17 and of *re’em* “wild ox” without *aleph* in Job 39:10. The resulting sense recalls the advice of Proverbs 1:33b.

Footnote 12:4–6. Compare Proverbs 25:19; for the phrase and the construction of the preceding line, compare Deuteronomy 32:35.

Footnote 12:4–6. Of the righteous.

Footnote 12:4–6. “At a time of” is mentally supplied from the preceding lines.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:7–8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Rather, ask the behemoth—and it will instruct you.

Or the fowl of the sky—and it will tell you.

Or converse with the earth—and it will instruct you;

And the fish of the sea will recount to you.

---

Footnote 12:7–8. A quasi-mythological beast, similar to the hippopotamus; see Job 40:14–24.

Footnote 12:7–8. A parody of Deuteronomy 32:7, and more immediately Bildad (8:8–10), in which the teaching of the elders is recommended.

Footnote 12:7–8. The animals of the earth are likely meant.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who does not know all these things—

“That the hand of YHWH has done this?”

---

Footnote 12:9. Job is making a direct quotation of Isaiah 41:20: “so that they will see and know and observe and learn that the hand of YHWH has done this, that the Holy One of Israel has created it”—otherwise Job would not be using the personal name of the Israelite God (see the introduction to this volume). The present passage seems to draw many images and expressions from Isaiah 41–45.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

“In whose hand is the breath of all life,

The spirit of all human flesh.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

You see, an ear examines words,

As a palate tastes food:

---

Footnote 12:11. “Taste” also connotes reasoning; see 6:6. From here through verse 24 is another string of mock wisdom sayings.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

In the elderly is wisdom,

And length of days is intelligence.

---

Footnote 12:12. Compare Eliphaz in 15:9–10.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

With him are wisdom and might;

His are good counsel and intelligence.

---

Footnote 12:13. God. There is no immediate antecedent because the sayings are excerpted from their sources.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He can destroy so it cannot be rebuilt;

He can close a man in so it can’t be reopened.

---

Footnote 12:14. Job turns Eliphaz’s traditional saying of 5:18 into a negative.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He can hold back the water so that (things) dry up;

Then he can release them so they convulse the earth.

---

Footnote 12:15. Job parodies Eliphaz’s beneficent image of divine rain-giving in 5:10; see also Isaiah 41:18; 42:15; 44:3, 27.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

With him are power and sage knowledge;

His are the mistaken and the misleader.

---

Footnote 12:16. Literally, “the one who causes others to be mistaken.” A very negative type, who trips up the blind (Deuteronomy 27:18) and corrupts the upright (Proverbs 28:10). Job may be parodying what Eliphaz says of God’s outsmarting the wise in 5:14–15.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:17–19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He causes counselors to go barefoot,

And judges he turns mad.

The girdles of kings he loosens,

And removes the strap on their loins.

He causes priests to go barefoot,

And the stalwarts he leads to ruin.

---

Footnote 12:17–19. A sign of desolation; see Micah 1:8 and compare Isaiah 20:2.

Footnote 12:17–19. Reading *mosar* (see Job 39:5; Jeremiah 2:2; Psalm 107:14) for *musar* “discipline, instruction.”

Footnote 12:17–19. Reading *wayyasar* for *wayye’sor* “and he girds,” which conveys the contrary sense. Compare Isaiah 45:1. Ironically “remove” puns on “gird.”

Footnote 12:17–19. Those doing combat would gird their loins; see Job 38:3; 40:7.

Footnote 12:17–19. For a similar usage of the verb, see Proverbs 21:12.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:20–21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He removes language from orators,

And takes sense away from elders.

He heaps contempt on leaders,

As the belt of (their) armor he slackens.

---

Footnote 12:20–21. Reading *na’manim* for *ne’emanim* “the faithful”; compare Jeremiah 23:31.

Footnote 12:20–21. Compare Psalm 107:40a.

Footnote 12:20–21. See Job 41:7.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He uncovers the depths of darkness,

And brings deep-shade into light.

---

Footnote 12:22. Compare Job 28:3, 11. Job would seem to be parodying Zophar in chapter 11.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He elevates nations, then disperses them;

He expands nations, then deports them.

---

Footnote 12:23. More precisely, leading them into exile or oblivion.

Footnote 12:23. The verb *hinḥah* nearly always connotes a supportive guidance. Here Job appears to be using it ironically—leading away into exile. Others, basing themselves on some of the ancient translations, read *wayyaniḥem* “he leaves them aside, abandons them.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 12:24–25

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He removes the heart from the landed folk’s chiefs,

Making them stray in a trackless waste.

They grope in darkness without light,

As he makes them stray like a drunkard.”

*Having demonstrated his familiarity with traditional wisdom, Job sums up, berates his companions for their insensitivity, and lays out his legal challenge to the deity—a challenge he had considered no more than a whimsy in chapter 9. Job now realizes he has nothing to lose by calling God to account, and he implores his companions to show no partiality as they listen to his case.*

---

Footnote 12:24–25. Mind, courage.

Footnote 12:24–25. Literally, “heads.”

Footnote 12:24–25. Identical to Psalm 107:40; compare Isaiah 42:16.

Footnote 12:24–25. Compare Deuteronomy 28:29.

Footnote 12:24–25. Compare Isaiah 19:14, where it is said of Egypt.
