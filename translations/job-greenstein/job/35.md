------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 35:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Elihu and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 35:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Is this why you planned your lawsuit:

You thought, “I am more just than God!”

When you asked, what’s the profit to you?

“How do I gain from not sinning?”

---

Footnote 35:2–3. Elihu is inferring that Job said this because Eliphaz appears to reject such a claim in 22:2–3; see the comment there.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 35:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I will put words back to you—

And your companions as well.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 35:5–7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Look up at the heavens and observe;

See how the sky is much higher than you!

If you’ve sinned, what can you do to him?

As your crimes multiply, what do you do for him?

If you are just, what do you give him?

What does he get from your hand?

---

Footnote 35:5–7. A quotation of Job in 7:20.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 35:8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When you’re wicked, it affects your fellow man;

And when you’re just, it affects another person.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 35:9–10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When the many oppress, people cry out;

When the many wield power, they call for aid.

They do not say, “Where is Eloah my Maker,

The Grantor of Strength in the night?”

---

Footnote 35:9–10. Literally, “On account of many oppressors.” For the form of this word, compare Jeremiah 22:3.

Footnote 35:9–10. More literally, “on account of the arm of many.”

Footnote 35:9–10. The Hebrew has the singular, referring back to the “man” and “person” of verse 8. The Qumran Aramaic and some other versions understand a plural here.

Footnote 35:9–10. A conventional prayer formula; see for example 2 Kings 2:14.

Footnote 35:9–10. Reading *zemarot* (singular: *zimra;* for example Exodus 15:2) for *zemirot* “songs.” Compare Psalm 68:36: “He grants power and might.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 35:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He more than wild beasts instructs us;

More than birds of the sky makes us wise.

---

Footnote 35:11. Elihu is overturning Job’s assertion in 12:8.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 35:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When they cry out, and he does not answer—

It’s because of the bad people’s pride.

---

Footnote 35:12. For the sense of “when,” analogous to Arabic *thamma*, compare Micah 3:4.

Footnote 35:12. Elihu explains that the deity’s seeming indifference to some sufferers is their fault—they are too proud to ask for God’s help; see verse 10. Compare Micah 3:4.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 35:13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For El will not hear insincerity;

Shaddai will not look at it.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 35:14–15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

All the more to your saying,

“You do not look at it!”

The matter’s before him,

And you must wait.

For he neither punishes in anger,

Nor greatly harms the sinner.

---

Footnote 35:14–15. At his case.

Footnote 35:14–15. Contra Job, who has said he cannot bear waiting for a hearing (13:15). For the unique form *teḥolel* read probably *titḥolel* “wait”; see Psalm 37:7.

Footnote 35:14–15. Vocalizing *poqed* in accord with the use of the negative particle *’ein* (for *’ayin* “nought”). For the sense, compare Psalm 89:33.

Footnote 35:14–15. Reading *yarea‘* for *yada‘* “know”; for the construal with the preposition *b*, see for example Psalm 74:3.

Footnote 35:14–15. Reading *beposhea‘* (compare the ancient versions) for *bappash*, which, though seemingly from the verb *push* “spread,” is completely anomalous. The entire verse is problematic.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 35:16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yet Job widens his mouth with wind;

He multiplies words without knowledge.

---

Footnote 35:16. For the negative connotation of this phrase, see for example Psalm 22:14.
