------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up speaks YHWH to Job from the windstorm, and he says:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who is this who obscures good counsel,

(Using) words without knowledge?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Bind up your loins like a man!

I will ask you—and you help me know!

---

Footnote 38:3. Preparation for combat.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:4–6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Where were you when I laid earth’s foundations?

Tell me—if you truly know wisdom!

Who set its dimensions? Do you know?

Who stretched the measuring line?

Into what were its foundations sunk?

Who laid its cornerstone?—


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When the stars of daybreak sang out,

And the sons of Elohim cheered;

---

Footnote 38:7. The angels; see 1:6.

Footnote 38:7. The verse echoes a couplet in a version of Deuteronomy 32:43 preserved in a Qumran scroll: “Sing of his people, all you heavens; pay homage to him, all you divine beings!”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:8–11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He hedged with double-doors Yamm,

As he was gushing out of the womb;

When I gave him cloud as a garment,

Raincloud as his swaddling clothes;

And imposed upon him my boundary,

Setting a bolt ’cross double-doors.

And I said, “This far you may come, but no more!

Here’s where I fix the surge of your waves!”

---

Footnote 38:8–11. The third-person unusually replaces the first-person discourse in this verse; but see 39:17. Some read *wa-mi sak* “Who hedged … ?”

Footnote 38:8–11. The Canaanite sea god; see 3:8; 7:12; 26:13.

Footnote 38:8–11. Reading *wa’eshmor* “I set as a guard,” in keeping with the image and language in 7:12.

Footnote 38:8–11. See 3:10. Note the irony: Job wished he could have been locked in the womb.

Footnote 38:8–11. Reading *u-po ’ashit* for *po’ yashit* “here (spelled unusually with *aleph*) he fixes.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:12–15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Have you ever in your days summoned daybreak,

Made known to the dawning its place?

Holding the earth by its corners,

So the wicked would from it be shaken?

The earth is upturned like the clay in a seal,

And the wicked stand there like a garment;

That the wicked be shed of their “light,”

And the upraised arm be broken.

---

Footnote 38:12–15. The earth under cover of darkness is here figured as a blanket, and the wicked are here figured as bugs that infest one’s bedding at night (Newsom).

Footnote 38:12–15. Literally, “it.”

Footnote 38:12–15. Literally, “a clay seal.” When a seal is removed from clay, an image appears.

Footnote 38:12–15. Literally, “they.”

Footnote 38:12–15. These lines are difficult, and this particular one is usually emended. For “garment” as an outer skin, see Job 41:5. In the daylight the wicked are visible, like one’s outer garment.

Footnote 38:12–15. Night is the wickeds’ day, the time they operate; see immediately below and 24:13–17.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:16–18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Have you ever reached the sources of Sea,

And walked on the bottom of Ocean?

Were you ever shown the gates of Death,

Or seen the gates of Deathly Shade?

Have you scanned the expanses of earth?

Tell—if you know all of this!

---

Footnote 38:16–18. Yamm (see verse 8).

Footnote 38:16–18. *Tehom*, the Deep (see 28:14).

Footnote 38:16–18. The realm of the dead; see 28:22; compare “the gates of Sheol” in Isaiah 38:10.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:19–21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

On what path dwells the light?

And the darkness—where is its place?

Can you take it to its domain?

Do you know the route to its home?

You must know, for you were born then;

Your number of days is so many!

---

Footnote 38:19–21. Returning to the point of verse 4, the deity speaks sarcastically.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:22–23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Have you reached the storerooms of snow?

Have you seen the storerooms of hail?—

I’ve been saving for a time of distress,

For a day of combat and battle.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

By what path is lightning dispensed,

Spreading east winds o’er the land?

---

Footnote 38:24. For the conjunction of lightning and wind, see Elihu’s descriptions in chapter 37.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:25–27

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who cleaves a downpour’s channel,

And a path for the thunderstorm;

To rain down on land without people,

On wilderness with no human in it;

Drenching utter wasteland,

And sprouting grassy growth?

---

Footnote 38:25–27. In contradiction to Eliphaz in 5:10 and Elihu in 36:27–28. For the negative connotation of such a wasteland, see Jeremiah 2:6.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:28–30

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Has the rain a progenitor?

Who begot the pools of dew?

From whose womb did ice emerge?

And frost from the sky—who gave it birth?

So water congeals like stone;

The surface of Ocean hardens.

---

Footnote 38:28–30. Compare Arabic *ma’jal* (Kaddari).

Footnote 38:28–30. From the root *ḥb’* “to congeal,” cognate to *ḥem’a* “curds,” not the common root *ḥb’* “to hide” (Dhorme).

Footnote 38:28–30. *Tehom*, the Deep.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:31–32

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Can you tie the bands of the Pleiades,

Or loosen the cords of Orion?

Can you bring the Mazarot out in its season,

Or lead the Hyades with its little ones?

---

Footnote 38:31–32. Compare 1 Samuel 15:32; derived via metathesis from *‘anad* “to chain.”

Footnote 38:31–32. For these two constellations, see 9:9. The stars of constellations are assumed to be held together by some kind of bond.

Footnote 38:31–32. Probably a variant of *mazal* “constellation.” Perhaps Canis Major and Minor (Halpern).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:33–35

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do you know the sky’s regularities?

Can you impose their regimen on earth?

Can you raise your voice toward a cloud,

And be covered by a downpour?

Can you send forth lightning bolts,

As they say to you, “Yes, my lord!”?

---

Footnote 38:33–35. Read *mishtaran* “their (for its) regimen,” referring to “regularities” (so Kahana).

Footnote 38:33–35. Literally, “here we are”; for this usage, see for example Genesis 22:1, 7, 11; compare Akkadian *anna/u* and postbiblical *hen* “yes.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:36

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who endowed the ibis bird with wisdom,

And gave understanding to the cock?

---

Footnote 38:36. There are several interpretations of these recipients of wisdom. Considering the contrast to the stupid bird in 39:17, I favor the sense of birds. The ibis is associated with the Egyptian god Thot (even if the first consonant in Hebrew is not the usual equivalent of the Egyptian; so Dhorme).

Footnote 38:36. So in rabbinic literature. These birds exhibit intelligence; the ibis finds food underground, and the cock discerns the dawn.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:37

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who can fill the heavens with wisdom,

And tip the waterskins of the sky?

---

Footnote 38:37. The verb *sipper* ordinarily means “to relate, inform,” but it may here be associated with the term *sipra* “bag, container” (see at 26:13; compare Kahana), and thus “to fill (a bag).”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:38

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Molding the dirt into solid,

As the clods are stuck together?

---

Footnote 38:38. This verse clearly belongs here. The copyist overlooked it and wrote it when he noticed, after verse 37 (compare Kahana).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 38:39–41

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do you hunt down prey for the lion,

And quell the hunger of beasts,

As they crouch in their lairs,

Or wait in the thicket to ambush?

Who provides rations to ravens,

When their children cry out to El,

As they wander without food?

*The deity questions Job concerning animals that are powerful and wild, while continuing to remind him of his inferiority.*

---

Footnote 38:39–41. Compare Psalm 10:9.
