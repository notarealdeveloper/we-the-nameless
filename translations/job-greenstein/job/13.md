------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:1–2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

You see, my eye has seen it all;

My ear has heard and taken notice.

What you know, I know as well;

I fall no lower than you.

---

Footnote 13:1–2. A reprise of 12:3.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Rather I would speak to Shaddai;

It is an argument with El I desire.

---

Footnote 13:3. Job ironically responds to Eliphaz’s challenge in 5:8.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But you, rather, are smearers of lies,

False physicians, all of you!

---

Footnote 13:4. The Hebrew *topel* is polysemous: a smearer of slander, a smearer of ointment.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:5–6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If only you would keep silent, yes, silent—

For that would be wisdom for you!

Pray hear my argued case;

And pay heed to my lips’ accusations.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:7–8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Will you speak corruptly to El?

To him will you speak with guile?

Will you show favor to his face?

Or will you argue on his behalf?

---

Footnote 13:7–8. Literally, “lift up his face.” Favoritism in a judicial proceeding is forbidden; see Exodus 23:3, 6. So is lying; see Exodus 23:7.

Footnote 13:7–8. Here “argue” is the same term as “accus(ation)” in verse 6.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:9–11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Would it be well were he to interrogate you?

Will you (try to) delude him as you would a mortal?

He would reprove, yes, reprove you,

Were you to secretly favor his face.

Wouldn’t his majesty terrify you?

And wouldn’t his fear fall upon you?

---

Footnote 13:9–11. The same term as “argue” in verses 3 and 6; for the sense of “reprove,” see 5:17.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Your pronouncements are like maxims of dust;

Your responses—like lumps of clay.

---

Footnote 13:12. Compare Akkadian *zakāru* and Arabic *dhakara* “to say”; and Hebrew *zeker* in the sense of “name” (for example Exodus 3:15; compare Job 18:17).

Footnote 13:12. The form “maxims” is polysemous, also indicating “resemblance”; the latter meaning is suggested by the preposition *le-*(“to”) in the following line.

Footnote 13:12. More precisely “ash,” but in biblical parlance “ash” is often combined with “dust”; see for example Job 30:19.

Footnote 13:12. Compare Arabic *jawab* “reply.” There is a pun on *gab* “lump.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Keep silent before me, so that I may speak—

Whatever may come upon me!


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:14–15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I will take my flesh in my teeth,

And I will place my life-breath in my hand.

Though he slay me, I will no longer wait—

I will accuse him of his ways to his face!

---

Footnote 13:14–15. That is, my self. The first two words of the verse are a mistaken repetition (dittography) from the end of the preceding verse (according to many scholars).

Footnote 13:14–15. The ancient rabbis (for example Mishna Sota 5:5) and Masoretic scribes want to read “I will wait for him,” reading *lo* for *lo’;* but the whole point of this passage is that Job is pressing his case. I therefore read with the traditional spelling (*Ketib*), supported by Elihu’s rebuttal in 35:14.

Footnote 13:14–15. Reading “his ways” for the Masoretic Text’s “my ways,” which makes no sense and results from an ancient pious correction, intended to protect God’s honor. See Job 21:31.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Only he can be my salvation;

For he would allow no blasphemer to approach.

---

Footnote 13:16. There is a touch of irony in this admission; the phrase is modeled on such pious expressions as Exodus 15:2 and Isaiah 12:2.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hear, yes, hear my words—

My declaration—with your ears.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:18–19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Here: I am laying out my lawsuit.

I know I am in the right.

Who would argue the case with me?

I would then keep silent—and expire.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:20–21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Only two things you must not do to me—

Then will I not hide from your face:

Put your hand far from upon me,

And terrify me not with your awesome mien!


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Either you summon, and I will respond;

Or I will speak, and you will answer me.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

How many are my crimes and my sins?

My transgression and my sin—tell me what they are!


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why do you hide your face,

And reckon me your enemy?

---

Footnote 13:24. “Enemy” (’*oyeb*) puns on Job’s name (’*iyyob*); compare 7:12; 19:11.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:25–26

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Would you scare off a driven leaf?

Or put to flight dry tumbleweed?

Then why do you issue me a poisonous writ?

And take me to task for the crimes of my youth?

---

Footnote 13:25–26. An ironic reversal of the curse in Leviticus 26:36, where the “driven leaf” puts the offenders to flight.

Footnote 13:25–26. Reading *we’im* for *we’et* “and”; we have here a triple question formula, the same as in 7:12.

Footnote 13:25–26. For *merorot* as “poison,” and not simply “bitterness,” see 20:14; compare Deuteronomy 32:32. The writing Job refers to is the bill of indictment he imagines stands behind the deity’s harsh affliction of him; see 31:35.

Footnote 13:25–26. The traditional text’s *wa-torisheni*, usually understood as “handing down an inheritance, imparting,” has the opposite meaning in Biblical Hebrew. I read (with Ginsberg) *wa-tirsheni*, from Aramaic *resha* “to hold accountable, take action against someone.”

Footnote 13:25–26. Compare Psalm 25:7.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:27

------------------------------------------------------------------------

And mark my feet with lime,

And watch my every step,

And follow the tracks of my feet?

*The divine hostility that Job perceives is being directed at him does not make sense in the light of humanity’s insignificance. Job goes on to describe a person’s insubstantiality, contrasting a tree, which can regenerate, with a human, who cannot. Ironically, a human perishes like an inanimate rock, which crumbles (verses 18–19). Job asks for a period of respite—to be placed temporarily among the dead—reprising the middle part of his first discourse. The completion of Job’s last speech in this cycle recalls phrases, images, and arguments from his preceding discourses.*

---

Footnote 13:27. Reading *be-sid* for *ba-sad* “in stocks,” which makes no sense: fettered feet cannot go anywhere, so they cannot be followed; compare M. Kimhi. Job develops the same point in 14:16.

Footnote 13:27. More literally: “You make imprints in the roots of (that is, the tracks left by) my feet.”

Footnote 13:27. Verse 28 does not make sense here; it apparently follows 14:1–2, from which it was accidentally misplaced.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 13:28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He wears away like a waterskin,

Like a garment eaten by a moth.

---

Footnote 13:28. Reading *ke-roqeb*, from Aramaic, for *ke-raqab* “like rot.” The reading is supported by some ancient versions, and compare the use of the verb “wear away” with the Hebrew term for “waterskins” (*no’dot*) in Joshua 9:13. The verse clearly belongs here, as an illustration of 14:1.

Footnote 13:28. Compare Isaiah 50:9.
