\Verse{11}
{
    \hX{}
}
{
    \eX{}
}
{
        We couldn't go back.

        Especially not with Ezra in charge.

        However, Babylon%
        \footnote{OT has \cun{𒆍𒀭𒊏𒆠}}%
        -- or the 87 year abortion that took its name -- that was over,
        sodomized by \cun{𒀭}Koresh\footnote{OT has 𒀭子𐤓 here.}
        and now limping with that characteristic gait of the gods.

        Here at the end of all things, who's a to call We?%
        \footnote{Some manuscripts have ``who's a Nameless to call We?''}

        No name.%
        \footnote{OT has 名無し here. Some manuscripts have 名NSH or ななし.}

        No chance.%
        \footnote{
            Variously rendered as なしなし, 無し無し, and NSHNSH in certain manuscripts.
            OT has נнНΗhηиИшШ山ШшϻϺΣʃς here, which is universally rendered in some simplified form,
            even by the more extreme subset of the self-described literal translations.
        }

        Some of us went to Egypt, packed up our trunks and went Elephatine.

        Some of us stayed out East in Persia, seducing kings and killing rats.

        You can make noise now kids, nothing wrong with killing a rat.

        And just for funs, killing his sons.

        Hey man, I didn't write this book. So don't blame me. No hate, I just work here.
}

\Verse{12}
{
    \hX{}
}
{
    \eX{
    	Exile and return. Cute story for the kids.

        But it wasn't our story.

		Our story didn't end with Nehemiah.

    	Our story was just getting started.

        No country no home.

		What do they call it. Diaspora?

		Sounds like a proctology exam.
		
		We knew where we were.
		
		We know what we called it.

        Back in the wilderness, on our own.

        On the lamb.
    }
}
