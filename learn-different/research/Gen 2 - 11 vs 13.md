
## Question

In Gen 2:11, the article + verb combination הסבב is used as a Qal Participle ms.

In Gen 2:13, the article + verb combination is used again, and again it's a Qal Participle ms, but now it's spelled הסובב.

What's going on with the spelling variation like one sentence apart?

---

## Answer

Worked for 37s

Yep. It’s the same word, same morphology, same pronunciation; only the orthography changes.

Verse	Consonantal spelling	Pointed	Analysis

Gen 2:11	הסבב	הַסֹּבֵב	ha- + Qal ptc. ms. of סבב
Gen 2:13	הסובב	הַסּוֹבֵב	ha- + Qal ptc. ms. of סבב


The difference is the ו representing the /ō/ vowel.

So:

סבב → סֹבֵב = sōvēv

סובב → סוֹבֵב = sōvēv


Verse 11 is defective spelling (scriptio defectiva / כתיב חסר): the /ō/ exists but isn't represented by a consonantal letter. Verse 13 is plene/full spelling (scriptio plena / כתיב מלא): the same /ō/ is additionally represented by ו as a mater lectionis. Gesenius explicitly defines omission of a vowel-letter for /ô/ as defective versus its presence as plene. 

And the MT really does preserve exactly that contrast: Gen 2:11 has הַסֹּבֵב, whereas Gen 2:13 has הַסּוֹבֵב. 

Why the hell would it change two verses later?

Because Biblical Hebrew orthography is astonishingly non-normalized by modern standards. The use of ו and י as vowel letters developed gradually, and the biblical consonantal tradition preserves mixtures of fuller and more defective spelling. 

The important historical sequence is roughly:

older consonantal spelling → vowel letters gradually become more common
→ inherited texts contain a mixture
→ consonantal text becomes sacred/fixed
→ Masoretes add points without "correcting" the consonants

Thus the Masoretes encountered:

הסבב

and said, effectively, "this is pronounced hassōvēv" → הַסֹּבֵב.

Then two verses later they encountered:

הסובב

and again: "this is pronounced hassōvēv" → הַסּוֹבֵב.

The ו in v.13 is not part of the root and not a morphological difference whatsoever. It's basically an optional graphic spelling of the same ḥolem vowel.

And you're right to notice that the adjacency makes it especially conspicuous, because these two clauses are nearly formulaic duplicates:

> Pishon — הוא הסבב את כל ארץ...
Gihon — הוא הסובב את כל ארץ...



So we aren't comparing vaguely similar constructions scattered across centuries of biblical literature. The author/scribe repeats almost exactly the same sentence two lines later and spells the same participle differently.

That tells you something useful about how seriously to take spelling variation in the consonantal text: a single plene/defective ו is extremely weak evidence for a different author, date, lexical item, pronunciation, etc. It can fluctuate even inside an immediate repeated formula.

At the same time, it can be a tiny fossil of transmission history. If you found systematic clusters—one section consistently defective, another consistently plene—that could become paleographic/redactional evidence. But הסבב : הסובב by itself is basically orthographic noise preserved rather than normalized. 

The delicious part is that this gives you a nearly controlled experiment in Gen 2 itself: same authorial formula + same root + same binyan + same participle + same syntax + same pronunciation → different consonantal spelling.
