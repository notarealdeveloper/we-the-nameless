

1. The “stupid / brutish” sense of בער

This comes from a different semantic branch of the same consonants:

בער (verb) → to burn / consume

בער (adj./noun) → brutish, animal-like, ignorant


Think:
👉 burn everything → no discrimination → animal-like → stupid


---

2. Clear examples (this meaning = “stupid / brutish”)

Psalms (strongest evidence)

Psalm 92 92:7

> אִישׁ־בַּעַר לֹא יֵדָע
“A brutish man does not know”



Psalm 49 49:11

> כַּבְּהֵמוֹת נִדְמוּ (parallel idea)
(paired conceptually with בער elsewhere—human ≈ beast)



Psalm 73 73:22

> וַאֲנִי בַעַר וְלֹא אֵדָע
“I was brutish and did not know”




👉 In Psalms, בער ≈ “like an animal”, not just “dumb,” but non-reflective, instinctive.


---

Jeremiah (important for your J/E/P polemic angle)

Book of Jeremiah 10:8

> וּבְאַחַת יִבְעֲרוּ וְיִכְסָלוּ
“They are altogether brutish and foolish”



Jeremiah 10:14

> נִבְעַר כָּל־אָדָם מִדָּעַת
“Every man is made brutish without knowledge”




👉 Here it’s explicitly anti-idolatry polemic language
→ ignorance = stupidity = animal-level cognition

3. What about the Torah (Pentateuch)?

Basically none in this sense.

In the Torah, בער always means:

🔥 burn (Exod 3, 35)

🌿 graze / consume (Exod 22)

🌆 evening (ערב root overlap—not same root historically, but same letters)


👉 Zero clear “stupid / brutish” uses in the Torah


---

4. Key linguistic insight (this is the interesting part)

You’re noticing something real:

Two conceptual clusters:

A. Physical

burn

consume

graze


B. Cognitive (later metaphor)

unthinking

animal-like

stupid



---

The bridge concept:

> בער = “to consume without distinction”



Which becomes:

> mind that consumes without discrimination → stupidity

5. Does this support your “באש = smelly / stupid” theory?

Careful—this is where you need to be precise.

Yes: בער can mean “brutish / stupid” later

No: It is not used that way in Exodus 3


In Exod 3:2–3, the usage is cleanly:

בֹּעֵר = burning

יִבְעַר = will burn


No ambiguity in context.
