
Exodus 4:22–23 (commonly attributed to E)

> Then you shall say to Pharaoh:

"Thus says YHWH:

Israel is my son, my firstborn.

And I said to you, 'Let my son go that he may serve me.'

But you refused to let him go.

Behold, I am killing your son, your firstborn."



This immediately precedes the lodging-place story and introduces the firstborn-death theme that later culminates in the tenth plague.

Exodus 14:5–8 (J)

> It was reported to the king of Egypt that the people had fled.

The heart of Pharaoh and his servants was changed toward the people, and they said:

"What is this that we have done, letting Israel go from serving us?"

He harnessed his chariot and took his people with him.

He took six hundred chosen chariots and all the chariots of Egypt, with officers over all of them.

YHWH strengthened the heart of Pharaoh king of Egypt, and he pursued the Israelites.



This is the continuation of the J plague-and-pursuit narrative that follows the firstborn death and release of Israel. The strengthening of Pharaoh's heart here forms part of the same literary sequence that begins with the warning in Exodus 4:21 and the firstborn threat in Exodus 4:22–23.
