## J vs either Job or Deutero Isaiah

Whenever either appears to reference J, even weakly, how do they do it? How does J do it?

| J Narrative | Job / Deutero-Isaiah Reference |
|------|------|
| Creation of humanity from dust. "YHWH Elohim formed the human from the dust of the ground." (Genesis 2:7) | "Remember that you have made me like clay... and will return me to dust." (Job 10:9); "The spirit of God has made me." (Job 33:4); "He gives breath to the people upon it." (Isaiah 42:5) |
| Sons of God appear before YHWH. (Genesis 6:1–4) | "The sons of God came to present themselves before YHWH." (Job 1:6; 2:1; 38:7) |
| Corruption before the Flood. (Genesis 6:5–13) | Job 22:15–17 explicitly references an ancient generation swept away by waters. |
| Flood. Fountains of the deep burst open. (Genesis 7:11) | Job 38:8–11; 38:16; 38:30; Isaiah 51:10. |
| Flood. God limits and reverses the waters. (Genesis 8:1–3) | Job 38:8–11; Isaiah 51:10. |
| Call of Abraham. Creator of all nations chooses one man. (Genesis 12:1–3) | Isaiah 41:8–10; 41:28; 51:2 explicitly invoke Abraham. |
| Promise that all nations are secondary to God's plan. (Genesis 12:3) | Isaiah 40:15–17; 45:22–23. |
| Abraham argues with YHWH over justice. (Genesis 18:22–33) | Entire structure of Job culminates in a human disputing with God about justice. |
| Tree of knowledge and human limitation. Humans seek divine knowledge. (Genesis 2–3) | Entire divine speeches (Job 38–41) can be read as a response to humanity's inability to possess divine wisdom. |
| "Where are you?" YHWH interrogates Adam. (Genesis 3:9–13) | "Where were you when I founded the earth?" (Job 38:4); long sequence of divine interrogation. |
| Human mortality imposed. "Dust you are, and to dust you shall return." (Genesis 3:19) | Job 10:9; 34:14–15; Isaiah 40:6–8. |
