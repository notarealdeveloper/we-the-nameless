## The Lodging Place

Hi Professor Friedman,

The "bridegroom of blood" scene is famously weird.

I've heard your explanation (Moses saying "kill me", pronoun ambiguity) and Propp's explanation (about דמים and all that), among others.

I'm curious what you think about the following explanation.

It's J's 10th plague.

And a bit of tragi-comic humor, in the typical J style.

The argument would be much too long and rambling as prose, so I'll format it as a few tables to keep it as brief as possible.

You can mostly ignore the Exodus 12 column. That's just there to document the parallels.

The main idea is expressed by the first two columns, some of which take things you've written and lectured about for granted (e.g., Moses was either Egyptian or Midianite, and likely didn't speak Hebrew, etc.)

Here's the idea.

In short:
1. Moses, as a foreigner with a foreign wife and a son named "foreigner,"
2. Is almost mistaken for one of the Egyptians subject to the 10th plague,
3. And just as our protagonists are about to fall victim to this tragic but very understandable mistake,
4. Zipporah cuts the covenant, circumcises her son, and saves the day,
5. Possibly with some J-style puns and/or broken Midianite dialect that may have contributed to the universally recognized strangeness of the terminology in this scene,
6. And the next time J shows up, they've already left Egypt. (Exo 13:21-22, Exo 14:5a)
7. So the story's in exactly the right place where it would have to be if it were "J's 10th plague."

That is, it's in the right place, provided we're willing to attribute Exodus 5:1-2 to someone other than J, which isn't a stretch, since it starts with "Moses and Aaron."

There's even a footnote in The First Great Writer (mistakenly known as Hidden Book in the Bible, due to publisher shenanigans) where it's acknowledged that Aaron appearing in J is anomalous:

> Note on Exod 19:24. The reference to Aaron’s coming up with Moses in this verse is strange because: (1) Aaron has not been introduced in this work up to this point. (2) Aaron does not in fact come up with Moses; Moses is told explicitly that no one is to come up with him (34:3). (3) Aaron is not mentioned in the story in this work at all outside of this verse. Aaron’s name does not come up after this until Judg 20:28, and then again in 1 Sam 12:6. I therefore take the reference to him here in Exod 19:24 to be a probable addition by the redactor (R).

So this theory's desire to attribute Exodus 5:1-2 to "non-J" would also fix a minor typo in the above book, where it says "Aaron has not been introduced in this work up to this point." 

Both The Bible with Sources Revealed and The Hidden Book in the Bible attribute Exodus 5:1 to J, and that verse mentions Aaron, so if we attribute Exodus 5:1-2 to non-J, we (1) bolster the case for items 6 and 7 above, and (2) fix a minor typo in HBB that attributes 5:1 to J while also saying in the note on Exod 19:24 that Aaron hasn't been introduced yet.

In other words, it sort of works.

Curious to hear your thoughts, and whether I've made any obvious major mistakes here.
