
P: I will be direct.

(papers placed on the table)

P: This version of your opening… cannot stand.

R: Let’s be specific.

P: I am being specific. “He crushed Rahab.” “He split the deep.” “He pierced the serpent.” This is not theology. This is imported chaos-myth.

E (mildly): It is older than Babylon.

P: That is not the defense you think it is.

---

J (leaning back): You asked for an account of beginnings.

P: I asked for an account fit for instruction. Fit for recitation. Fit for law.

J: And I gave you the oldest one we have.

---

P: No. You gave me a battle.

(beat)

P: Tell me plainly: what is creation to you?

---

J (after a pause): Creation… is a victory.

P: Over what?

J: Over chaos. Over the unformed. Over the thing that swallows everything if it is not restrained.

---

P: Then you misunderstand the assignment.

(coldly)

P: Creation is not victory. Creation is distinction.

---

E (quietly interested): Say that again.

P: Distinction. Separation. Ordering.
Light from darkness. Waters above from waters below. Sea from dry land. Clean from unclean. Holy from common.

(leans forward)

P: Creation is not the defeat of something. It is the definition of everything.

---

J: That is a very tidy world you’ve made.

P: It is a teachable world.

---

J (sits up now): Let me be clear.

I did not write a battle because I believe creation is a battle.

I wrote a battle because that is the first story anyone ever told about creation.

---

R: That’s an important distinction.

---

J: You think I am trying to describe what happened.

I am trying to describe what people know.

---

P: And you think that will suffice?

J: For now? Yes. For later? Even more so.

---

P: We are not writing for “now.”

We are writing something that must stand.

---

J (smiles slightly): Exactly.

So answer me this:

How do you propose we write a creation account that will still mean something in a thousand years? Two thousand?

None of us were there.

None of us know.

---

E: That is true.

---

J: But we do know something else.

We know how the world feels to those who inherit it.

We know how civilizations begin.

We know how power is established.

We know what it looks like when order is imposed on chaos.

---

P: That is politics.

J: That is history.

---

J (leaning forward now): And tell me—how different is that from what you call “creation”?

---

(silence)

---

J: Why do you think I put the Tigris and Euphrates in there?

For decoration?

---

P: You put them there because you cannot stop referencing Babylon.

---

J: I put them there because they are the only fixed points we have.

Empires rise around them. Stories gather around them. Every listener knows them.

---

E: They anchor the myth in memory.

---

J: Exactly.

You want something timeless? Then you don’t erase what people already know.

You transform it.

---

P: By preserving its errors?

J: By preserving its shape.

---

P: Its shape is violence.

---

J: Its shape is struggle.

And if you remove that, you are not writing something truer.

You are writing something… sterile.

---

(long pause)

---

P (quietly, but firm): Then I cannot attach my name to it.

---

R: That sounds like a threat.

---

P: It is a statement of responsibility.

If this text is to guide a people, it cannot begin with a god who conquers a monster like a king in a drunken epic.

---

E: You would resign?

---

P: I would withdraw.

And I would make it known why.

---

(silence)

---

R: Then we need a solution.

---

E (to J): Can you say the same thing… differently?

---

(J thinks for a long moment)

---

J: Yes.

---

P: Without the battle.

---

J: Without the battle on the surface.

---

P: No monsters.

---

J: No named monsters.

---

P: No dismemberment.

---

J: No dismemberment.

---

P: No chaoskampf.

---

J (after a beat): No explicit chaoskampf.

---

(P narrows his eyes, but says nothing)

---

R: And what replaces it?

---

J: Structure.

Sequence.

Speech instead of combat.

Separation instead of slaughter.

---

P: Now you are speaking sense.

---

J (softly): And underneath…

---

E: Underneath?

---

J: I keep the bones.

---

(P exhales sharply)

---

P: Of course you do.

---

R: So we proceed in two layers.

---

E: A surface that teaches.

---

J: And a depth that remembers.

---

P: And if the depth contradicts the surface?

---

J (smiles): Only to those who insist on reading both.

---

(long pause)

---

R: Then that is our compromise.

You rewrite the opening.

No battle. Ordered creation.

And the rest—

---

J: —I will make… more subtle.

---

P: You will make it acceptable.

---

J: I will make it survive.

---

(silence)
