Are there any ways of conjugating the verb דבר in Biblical Hebrew that turn it into דבורה, or something one letter away from it?

For example, דבור?

For example, דברה?

Yes — those are much closer to actual forms related to the root דבר.

דָּבוּר / דָּבוֹר (davur / dāvōr)

This can function as a passive participle/adjectival form from דבר in later or non-biblical patterns meaning roughly:

“spoken”

“said”

“uttered”


similar in idea to:

כָּתוּב (“written”) from כתב

שָׁמוּר (“guarded”) from שמר


Biblical Hebrew usually prefers other constructions for דבר, but forms like דבור become much more natural in later Hebrew.

And importantly:

דיבור (dibbur) = “speech, speaking”

מְדַבֵּר = “speaking”

דָּבוּר = “spoken”


all stay very close semantically.

So consonantally:

דבורה (“Deborah/bee”)

דבור (“spoken”)

דיבור (“speech”)


start clustering into a pretty suggestive sound-field.

דִּבְרָה / דָּבְרָה

Now this is even more directly verbal.

דִּבְּרָה (dibberāh)

This is basically:

> “she spoke”



from Piel perfect 3rd feminine singular of דבר.

The normal Masoretic spelling is usually:

דִּבְּרָה

though orthography can vary.

So yes:

דִּבְּרָה literally means “she spoke.”


That is extremely close visually and aurally to:

דְּבוֹרָה

especially in unpointed text:

דברה

דבורה


which differ only by the inserted ו.

And Biblical Hebrew spelling is often much looser than modern learners expect:

matres lectionis (like ו) fluctuate,

vowels are unwritten,

poetic spelling varies.


So from a literary-hearing perspective, the proximity is very real.

That makes the Deborah connection especially tempting because she is:

a prophetess,

a judge,

a singer of victory poetry,

a public speaker/oracle figure.


So:

דבורה = “bee”

but visually/hearing-adjacent to:

דברה (“she spoke”)

דבור (“spoken”)

דיבור (“speech”)



is exactly the sort of semantic clustering Biblical Hebrew authors loved exploiting.
