
> Section 30: The Machpelah Purchase (xxiii). Certain portions of this chapter appear to support the older view, which regards the narrative as part of the P document. It is a fact, moreover, that P refers to the Machpelah purchase more than once (xxv 9 f., xlix 29 f., l 13). Nevertheless, the opposing argument would seem to carry greater weight. The account is not only narrative in character, but is marked by a mock solemnity that is totally out of keeping with the sober manner of P. Besides, the repeated description of members of the local council as “those who came in at the gate of his city” (vss. 10, 18) has its idiomatic complement in the phrase “those who went out by the gate of his city,” which occurs twice in xxxiv (24), a narrative that stems from J.⁶ What this adds up to is that P appropriated and introduced the account in question because legal title to the Machpelah burial ground was considered vital by that source; but the secular overtones of the story did not suffer in the process. The end result was an excerpt from J in a framework by P, a unique blend in itself.
> 
> -The Anchor Bible Commentary on Genesis, E.A. Speiser. (pg xxxiv)

## Gen 23

And Abraham rose from before his dead and addressed the sons of Heth, saying:

“I find myself, as it were, a resident alien in your midst—present, yet not formally possessed of a duly recognized and legally constituted burial holding. I would therefore request—subject, of course, to your collective assent, properly witnessed, publicly acknowledged, and procedurally ratified—that you effect, facilitate, and bring about the conveyance to me of such a burial holding among you as may enable me to proceed with the burial of my dead from before me.”

And the sons of Heth responded to Abraham, saying:

“Hear us, my lord. You are, in every respect, a prince of God among us. In the choicest, most select, and most eminently suitable of our tombs—each fully compliant with customary expectations—you may bury your dead. There is not a man among us who will, in any capacity whatsoever, withhold, restrict, or otherwise impede your access to his tomb for the purposes of burial.”

And Abraham rose, and bowed—formally, deferentially—before the people of the land, the sons of Heth.

And he spoke with them, saying:

“If it is, indeed, your intention—collectively expressed and publicly affirmed—to see that I bury my dead from before me, then hear me, and undertake, if you will, to engage, consult, and intercede on my behalf with Ephron son of Zohar, that he may make available to me the cave of Machpelah—being his property, situated at the extremity of his field—on terms of full and proper compensation, that is to say, at the complete and unimpeachable silver valuation thereof; let him effect its transfer to me, in your presence, as a burial holding, duly established.”

Now Ephron was seated among the sons of Heth.

And Ephron the Hittite answered Abraham—in the hearing of the sons of Heth, before all who entered the gate of his city—saying:

“No, my lord—pray hear me. The field, I hereby give to you; and the cave that is therein, I likewise give to you. In the sight of the sons of my people, I give it to you. Proceed, therefore, with the burial of your dead.”

And Abraham bowed before the people of the land.

And he addressed Ephron in the hearing of the people of the land, saying:

“If you would but hear me—indeed, if you would be so good as to give due consideration—I will provide the silver corresponding to the field; accept it from me, that I may bury my dead there.”

And Ephron answered Abraham, saying:

“My lord, attend to me. A piece of land—its valuation being four hundred shekels of silver—what, in practical or relational terms, is that between you and me? Bury your dead.”

And Abraham listened to Ephron.

And Abraham weighed out for Ephron the silver which he had specified in the hearing of the sons of Heth:

four hundred shekels of silver, in full accordance with the prevailing commercial standard.

And thus the field of Ephron, which was in Machpelah, which was before Mamre—the field, and the cave that was therein, and every tree that was in the field, that was within all its surrounding boundaries—was established, confirmed, ratified, and rendered legally operative as a possession for Abraham,

in the sight of the sons of Heth, before all who entered the gate of his city.
