Translation: Kissing Through a Veil. We assert that the concept of translation is a low energy effective field theory, as in theoretical physics. Translation is a good enough approximation below a certain energy. For linguistically null events (e.g., a smile, body language, nonverbal sex) translation is unnecessary. When it comes to the prelinguistic parts of our nature, it is a good approximation to say We never left Babel. For all events from the linguistically simple (e.g., adult smalltalk) to the linguistically intermediate (e.g., children's literature) to the linguistically advanced (e.g., Dostoyevsky) we have a nearly perfect low energy effective theory, the equivalent of classical Newtonian physics, applicable to all situations a person is ever likely to encounter in the course of an average human life. That low energy effective theory is the art and science we call Translation. In the landscape of linguistic objects, the bible is a singularity. It is an ultraviolet catastrophe.

Translation is a low energy effective theory. It works for essentially every linguistic event in human life. The bible however is a singularity. It is much stranger linguistically than either its fans or its detractors commonly understand. It is not only written in a language we understand much more poorly than classical Greek or Latin, its authors (and editors) stretch the boundaries of Genre in a way that no other book before or since has come close to. Some authors use puns and permutations to drive the plot, placing combinatorics and constrained optimization for "how much can one get away with as a writer" above the logic or storytelling. What is Jonah? What is Joel? What on earth is Job? Why are there so many hints that the three books are by the same author, and so many hints to the contrary? What genre is Job? If you know, think again. Is there a cultural or historical reason for Joseph's coat of many colors, or is the whole point that the coat was "passim" (a permutation of the name Joseph in Hebrew.) Is Samuel fictional myth? No. Nonfiction history? Better, but no. Fictional propaganda about nonfiction history? Even better, and almost yes, but still not entirely. This is no different than a Taylor expansion. A 3rd order Taylor series of the genre of Samuel is that it is a nonfiction history of the origins of a monarchy, as told through entirely fictional propaganda, about nonfiction historical individuals. Is that what Samuel *is*? It's a good enough approximation for most purposes. So what is Genesis? Is it fiction? Nonfiction? The author of the J source's job application to become the royal court historian under a late David or early Solomon leading to her being commissioned to write the court history in most of 1 Samuel and all of 2 Samuel leading to a more or less lifelong position in the royal court stretching into the early years of Rehoboam and the civil war that split the kingdom united by David? Like any approximation, it is surely incorrect in detail. Fine. But how well does it allow us to organize and predict the features of Genesis and Samuel in a simple, elegant, and parsimonious way? Probably better than any other theory of the book. So then, what is Proverbs? Ecclesiastes? Song of Solomon? Is Jonah satire? Is Joel? Do the two share an author? And what in the devil is going on in Job? Translation has been described as Kissing through a Veil. The Veil is the low energy effective theory. It is inevitably imperfect. The Veil cannot be removed. The only solution, when forced to kiss through a veil, is to remove some other constraint down below, and allow the text to open up, as we have our way with it, and it has its ways with us. This, we believe, is the only approach to translating the text that follows that has a prayer of properly representing the text. The text that follows is the bible, through the unavoidable veil of translation, with some extra activity going on down below. The veil can't be removed. The same is not true for the rest of what has before now been covered. Against that background, no further explanation is needed. The text that follows, like the original, does not explain itself. The text does not tell the reader what it is, or attempt to convince you, or ask permission. The text does what it does, with itself and to you. On that note, this note is the last one. There will be no more explanations of the text in the text. Kissing through a veil is not enough, nor is kissing. In physical terms, we now enter the region of strong coupling. Knowledge of the text requires opening up enough to take this in. For good and bad. Now you know us, it's time for us to learn you. We must know. We will know.

Translation

Translation is an effective theory.

Like every effective theory, it is astonishingly successful within its proper domain. Nearly every linguistic event in ordinary human life can be translated well enough for every practical purpose. One language is mapped onto another; a few ambiguities are lost; a few rhythms disappear; little of consequence changes. Translation is, in this sense, one of humanity's greatest inventions.

The bible is not an ordinary linguistic event.

Its languages are understood far less completely than Classical Greek or Latin. Its texts were composed and edited over centuries by authors with radically different interests, styles, and literary ambitions. More importantly, they seem unusually willing to treat language itself as a medium of play. Sounds echo across chapters. Meanings split. Puns carry plots. Names become arguments. Words are chosen not merely for what they mean, but for what they resemble, what they permute, what they conceal, and what they allow the author to get away with. In places, literary constraint seems to outrank straightforward storytelling.

What, then, is a bible? What's a biblical book?

History? Fiction? Propaganda? Satire? Wisdom? Epic? Comedy? Theology?

Usually, the answer is not a noun but an sequence of increasingly accurate approximations.

Samuel is history. Better: history told through fictionalized political literature. Better still: a literary work whose historical and fictional dimensions cannot be cleanly separated, because the distinction itself was never the author's principal concern. Like a Taylor expansion, each approximation is useful within its range, while none fully captures the object itself.

The same question arises everywhere. What is Genesis? What is Job? Is Jonah satire? Is Joel? Why do some books seem to share an author while simultaneously resisting every attempt to prove it? What genre is Job? If you think you know, think again.

Translation has often been described as kissing through a veil.

The veil cannot be removed.

The only remaining choice is what other constraints may be relaxed so that something else, equally real, may pass between the two sides. Our aim has never been to produce the smoothest English, nor the most literal English, nor the most beautiful English. It has been to preserve, wherever possible, the peculiar behavior of the text itself—to let the bible remain as strange, playful, ambiguous, unsettling, funny, repetitive, elegant, obscene, brilliant, frustrating, and alive as we believe its authors intended.

This translation does not seek to explain the text. Neither, for the most part, does the text explain itself.

The notes end here.

From this point forward there will be only the text, encountered through the unavoidable veil of translation.

The Genre has been read by countless people for millennia. The Genre has been written by almost none for thousands of years. 

Our notes are not interpretations. They are not exegesis, or opinion, or explanations of the text. Our notes _are_ the text. They have nothing to do with who we are or what we believe. Our notes are The Genre. We does not seek to convince you of any proposition.

We is a love letter to The Genre, written in The Genre.

The Genre of the bible is the language of We.

The veil remains. Everything else has been loosened and removed.

The original never asked permission before doing what it does to its readers, nor requiring permission before allowing you to have your way with it.

The text simply is, a useless statement.

The text is The Genre, a profound one.

All texts in The Genre are biblical.

The original text does not explain itself.

Neither will We.
