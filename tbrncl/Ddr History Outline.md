- **DdH’s Persian-period frame**

- **Dude 1 — Persian decree + Temple Chronicle:** Cyrus permits the exiles to return, though most remain scattered among the nations.

- **Dude 2 — Returnee list:** The names of those who ascend are counted, family by family, as though restoration could be measured.
    
- **Dude 3 — Temple Chronicle:** The altar is rebuilt among ruins, and the old remember Solomon’s house and weep.
    
- **Dude 4 — Imperial correspondence:** Enemies accuse the builders before Persia; decrees and counter-decrees pass between governors.
    
- **Dude 5 — Haggai–Zechariah source:** Prophets rebuke those who panel their own houses while the sanctuary lies unfinished.
    
- **Dude 6 — Temple Chronicle:** The Second House is completed, smaller than the first yet filled with the hopes of the returned.
    
- **Dude 7 — Ezra Memoir:** Ezra comes from Babylon bearing Torah, genealogy, and authority from the Persian king.
    
- **Dude 8 — Ezra Memoir:** The community is purified by separating foreign wives, and DdH asks whether zeal for holiness has crushed the innocent.
    
- **Dude 9 — Nehemiah Memoir:** Nehemiah surveys Jerusalem by night and rebuilds its walls against mockery and threat.
    
- **Dude 10 — Builders’ register:** Priests, merchants, craftsmen, women, and provincial families repair the breaches beside one another.
    
- **Dude 11 — Nehemiah Memoir:** The wealthy enslave their poorer kin through debt, and Nehemiah compels restitution.
    
- **Dude 12 — Torah-reading tradition:** Ezra reads before the Water Gate; the people weep, feast, and bind themselves by covenant.
    
- **Dude 13 — Nehemiah Memoir:** Sabbath, marriage, Temple dues, and priestly boundaries are enforced with furious hands.
    
- **Dude 14 — DdH aside:** Thus Judah survived without a Davidic king, governed by Torah, priesthood, imperial permission, and memory.
    
- **Dude 15 — Apocalyptic supplement:** And some saw beasts rising from empires and awaited a kingdom not built by human hands.

- **Dude 1 — Hellenistic chronicle:** Alexander crosses the earth swiftly, and afterward his kingdom is divided among grasping successors.
    
- **Dude 2 — Judean archive:** Judea passes between the Ptolemies and Seleucids while Greek speech, schools, trade, and customs spread.
    
- **Dude 3 — Wisdom source:** Some Jews translate Torah into Greek, so that Israel’s God may be spoken of in Alexandria.
    
- **Dude 4 — Priestly court source:** High priests purchase office, Jerusalem’s aristocrats compete in Hellenism, and the sanctuary becomes a prize of factions.
    
- **Dude 5 — 2 Maccabees:** Antiochus IV plunders the Temple and suppresses ancestral practices.
    
- **Dude 6 — Martyr collection:** A mother and her sons choose torture and death rather than abandon the covenant.
    
- **Dude 7 — 1 Maccabees:** Mattathias rebels at Modein, and his son Judas takes command.
    
- **Dude 8 — Maccabean war annals:** Judas is called **Maccabee**, “the Hammer,” whether from a nickname, an acrostic, or a later explanation.
    
- **Dude 9 — Temple Dedication Chronicle:** The sanctuary is purified and its lamps rekindled; therefore the feast of Dedication is kept.
    
- **Dude 10 — Hasmonean annals:** Jonathan and Simon convert revolt into priestly government and national independence.
    
- **Dude 11 — DdH judgment:** The liberators take crowns for themselves, and the house raised against foreign kings begins imitating foreign kings.
    
- **Dude 12 — Sectarian source:** Pharisees, Sadducees, and Essenes dispute purity, resurrection, priesthood, oral teaching, and the interpretation of Torah.

- **Dude 13 — Qumran covenant source:** Priestly separatists withdraw into the wilderness and condemn Jerusalem’s rulers as wicked.

- **Dude 14 — Hasmonean annals:** Brothers contend for the throne and invite Rome to judge between them.

- **Dude 15 — DdH lament:** And Rome entered as an arbiter, but remained as master.

# III Dudetheyreontome

## Rome, Herod, and Josh

### Sources

- **Josephus**
    
- **Roman provincial records**
    
- **Herodian court traditions**
    
- **Sayings source**
    
- **Mark**
    
- **Early passion narrative**
    
- **Baptist traditions**
    
- **James traditions**
    
- **Acts traditions**
    
- **Jewish-Christian community memories**
    
- **Later Matthew and Luke expansions**
    
- **DdH’s brief Pauline notice**
    
- **Dude 1 — Roman annals:** Pompey enters the sanctuary, and Judea becomes subject to Rome.
    
- **Dude 2 — Herodian court history:** Herod, called Great because his buildings were great, gains the kingdom through Roman favor and blood.
    
- **Dude 3 — Building records:** Herod rebuilds the Temple in splendor, though priests measure every stone laid by an Idumean king.
    
- **Dude 4 — Court history:** Herod kills wife, sons, rivals, and friends; DdH observes that magnificence cannot cleanse fear.
    
- **Dude 5 — Baptist source:** John appears beside the Jordan, calling Israel to repentance outside the Temple establishment.
    
- **Dude 6 — Markan source:** **Josh, who was also called Jesus**, comes from Galilee proclaiming that God’s kingdom has drawn near.
    
- **Dude 7 — Sayings source:** Josh blesses the poor, warns the rich, eats with the impure, and teaches in riddles concerning seed, debt, banquets, and judgment.
    
- **Dude 8 — Miracle collection:** The sick are healed, demons flee, crowds gather, and opponents ask by whose authority these things are done.
    
- **Dude 9 — Markan source:** Josh rebukes both priestly power and the hunger of his followers for rank.
    
- **Dude 10 — Passion source:** Josh enters Jerusalem, disrupts the Temple courts, is seized under priestly accusation, and is crucified by Rome.
    
- **Dude 11 — Empty-tomb and appearance traditions:** Women find the tomb opened; disciples proclaim that God has raised Josh from death.
    
- **Dude 12 — Jerusalem-community source:** James, brother of Josh, leads those who follow the Way while remaining zealous for Torah.
    
- **Dude 13 — Acts source:** Peter and the Twelve proclaim Josh in Jerusalem; Greek-speaking believers carry the message outward.
    
- **Dude 14 — J-like summary of Paul:** And there arose Paul, formerly Saul, who wrote many words concerning faith, flesh, spirit, Adam, Abraham, and the nations; and he loosened the nations from circumcision, though many among the brothers contended with him.
    
- **Dude 15 — DdH aside:** The writings of Paul became greater among the churches than Paul could have foreseen, and interpreters built many houses from his sentences.
    
- **Dude 16 — Early church source:** Jews and gentiles share meals uneasily, dispute Torah and idols, and gradually become distinguishable communities.
    
- **Dude 17 — Roman annals:** Nero blames followers of Josh for Rome’s fire, and some are killed as spectacles.
    
- **Dude 18 — Judean war source:** Revolt erupts; factions consume Jerusalem from within while Rome surrounds it from without.
    
- **Dude 19 — Josephus:** Titus destroys the Second House in the year 70, and the priestly center is ended.

- **Dude 20 — DdH judgment:** Thus both the followers of the rabbis and the followers of Josh learned to live without the altar, each carrying selected ruins into a new house.

# IV Dudetheyreontome

## The Two Heirs of the Ruins

### Sources

- **Mishnah**
    
- **Tosefta**
    
- **Early rabbinic traditions**
    
- **Josephus**
    
- **Bar Kokhba letters**
    
- **Early church orders**

- **Apologists**
    
- **Catacomb inscriptions**
    
- **Gospel and apostolic collections**
    
- **Heresiologists**
    
- **DdH’s paired history**
    
- **Dude 1 — Rabbinic founding tradition:** Yohanan ben Zakkai and the sages rebuild Israel around study, prayer, deeds of mercy, and interpreted Torah.
    
- **Dude 2 — Christian house tradition:** Assemblies meet in homes, break bread, baptize converts, and remember Josh in prayers and meals.
    
- **Dude 3 — Symbol tradition:** Before the cross rules their monuments, Christians mark fish, anchor, shepherd, vine, dove, and Jonah.
    
- **Dude 4 — Canon traditions:** Communities copy gospels, letters, revelations, acts, and teachings, accepting some and rejecting others.
    
- **Dude 5 — Sectarian catalogue:** Ebionites, Marcionites, Valentinians, Montanists, and many others claim the authentic inheritance of Josh.
    
- **Dude 6 — Rabbinic legal source:** The Mishnah gathers generations of rulings, arguments, memories, and idealized Temple practice.
    
- **Dude 7 — Bar Kokhba archive:** Judea revolts again under Simon bar Kosiba, whom some call Bar Kokhba, the Son of the Star.
    
- **Dude 8 — Roman annals:** Hadrian crushes the revolt, devastates Judea, and refounds Jerusalem as Aelia Capitolina.
    
- **Dude 9 — DdH aside:** Jews are not expelled from every land, nor do all depart Judea; nevertheless, the dispersion deepens and Jerusalem becomes increasingly inaccessible.
    
- **Dude 10 — Martyr sources:** Christians killed by Roman authorities interpret endurance as victory and blood as testimony.
    
- **Dude 11 — Episcopal records:** Bishops grow from local overseers into guardians of doctrine, property, discipline, and succession.
    
- **Dude 12 — Rabbinic academy source:** Palestinian and Babylonian schools debate the Mishnah, and their discussions become Talmuds.
    
- **Dude 13 — DdH parallel:** As rabbis surround Torah with argument, bishops surround the apostolic writings with creeds and councils.
    
- **Dude 14 — Imperial persecution source:** Decius and Diocletian demand sacrifice; believers divide over those who comply and those who refuse.
    
- **Dude 15 — DdH transition:** Then an emperor saw a sign—or said that he had seen one—and the persecuted faith approached the throne.

# V Dudetheyreontome

## The Cross and the Eagle

### Sources

- **Imperial edicts**
    
- **Conciliar acts**
    
- **Bishops’ letters**
    
- **Creeds**
    
- **Monastic lives**
    
- **Anti-heretical catalogues**
    
- **Jewish communal records**
    
- **DdH’s imperial critique**
    
- **Dude 1 — Constantinian court source:** Constantine defeats his rivals beneath the sign of the Christian God and favors the churches.
    
- **Dude 2 — Imperial edict:** Christianity is tolerated, confiscated property restored, and bishops gain imperial patronage.
    
- **Dude 3 — DdH judgment:** The church escaped the sword and received the palace; but the sword also entered the church.
    
- **Dude 4 — Nicene acts:** Bishops gather at Nicaea to judge Arius and confess the Son as of one substance with the Father.
    
- **Dude 5 — Conciliar aftermath:** Emperors exile bishops, restore them, and exile them again while rival formulas contend.
    
- **Dude 6 — Athanasian tradition:** Alexandria’s bishop resists emperors and opponents, and later generations remember his side as orthodoxy.
    
- **Dude 7 — Imperial church source:** Theodosius establishes Nicene Christianity as the favored religion of the empire.
    
- **Dude 8 — DdH judgment:** Those once condemned as impious now possess authority to condemn others as impious.
    
- **Dude 9 — Monastic source:** Men and women flee cities for deserts, seeking an undivided life beyond wealth, marriage, office, and court.
    
- **Dude 10 — Jewish communal source:** Rabbinic academies flourish in Babylonia while Christian emperors increasingly restrict Jewish civic and religious life.
    
- **Dude 11 — Patriarchal annals:** Rome, Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch, and Jerusalem contend over honor and doctrine.
    
- **Dude 12 — Christological councils:** Bishops dispute how divinity and humanity meet in Josh; churches divide by formulas, languages, and imperial borders.
    
- **Dude 13 — Western chronicles:** Rome’s western empire falls, but its bishop remains and grows in prestige among successor kingdoms.
    
- **Dude 14 — Missionary lives:** Christianity spreads among Armenians, Ethiopians, Syrians, Goths, Franks, Irish, Slavs, and peoples beyond Rome.
    
- **Dude 15 — DdH aside:** Thus there was never one undivided Christianity except in the memory of those who wished to command it.

- **Sīra traditions**

- **Dude 1 — Arabian proclamation:** Muhammad of Mecca announces that the God of Abraham is one and that judgment is near.

- **Dude 3 — Arabian annals:** The tribes are united by treaty, warfare, devotion, and allegiance.

- **Dude 9 — Rabbinic academy source:** The Babylonian Talmud becomes the chief sea in which later Rabbinic Judaism sails.
    
- **Dude 10 — DdH transition:** And the children of Abraham disputed one another’s inheritance while borrowing one another’s words, sciences, stories, and laws.

# VII Dudetheyreontome

## Rabbinites, Karaites, and the Long Middle Age

### Sources

- **Geonic responsa**
    
- **Karaite polemics**
    
- **Rabbanite counter-polemics**
    
- **Geniza documents**
    
- **Medieval chronicles**
    
- **Crusade laments**
    
- **Church annals**
    
- **Papal registers**
    
- **Jewish travel accounts**
    
- **DdH’s diaspora framework**
    
- **Dude 1 — Geonic source:** The academies of Babylonia answer questions from scattered communities, binding the diaspora through responsa.
    
- **Dude 2 — Karaite source:** Anan and later scripturalists reject the binding authority claimed for rabbinic oral law.
    
- **Dude 3 — Rabbanite source:** Rabbinites defend Talmud, inherited interpretation, calendar, and communal custom.
    
- **Dude 4 — Polemical history:** Karaites and Rabbinites contend for communities, patronage, learning, and the right to define Israel.
    
- **Dude 5 — DdH correction:** The Rabbinites do not defeat the Karaites in one battle; over centuries Rabbinic institutions become dominant while Karaite communities endure.
    
- **Dude 6 — Masoretic source:** Scribes preserve pronunciation, accents, spellings, and marginal readings of the Hebrew scriptures.
    
- **Dude 7 — Saadian source:** Saadia Gaon confronts Karaites, translates scripture into Arabic, and joins Jewish law to philosophy.
    
- **Dude 8 — European church annals:** The bishop of Rome accumulates lands, courts, claims, and rivalries with kings and eastern patriarchs.
    
- **Dude 9 — Schism chronicles:** Rome and Constantinople exchange condemnations, later remembered as the division of Catholic and Orthodox churches.
    
- **Dude 10 — Crusade chronicle:** Armies bearing crosses march east and massacre Jews and Muslims, saying that violence serves the Holy One.
    
- **Dude 11 — Jewish lament:** Rhineland communities die under crusaders, and their poets place their grief beside the Binding of Isaac.
    
- **Dude 12 — Iberian source:** Jews prosper, translate, administer, trade, philosophize, and write poetry under alternating Muslim and Christian rulers.
    
- **Dude 13 — Maimonidean source:** Moses ben Maimon orders the law, guides the perplexed, and becomes both authority and controversy.
    
- **Dude 14 — Inquisition source:** Christian kingdoms demand conformity, investigate converts, and turn suspicion into an institution.
    
- **Dude 15 — Expulsion chronicle:** Spain expels professing Jews in 1492; refugees pass into Portugal, North Africa, Italy, the Netherlands, and the Ottoman lands.
    
- **Dude 16 — DdH judgment:** Every kingdom desired Jewish usefulness without Jewish security.

- **Dude 15 — Messianic source:** Sabbatai Zevi awakens hope across the diaspora, converts under Ottoman pressure, and leaves belief shattered or transformed.

### Sources

- **Palestinian oral histories**
    
- **Military records**
    
- **Arab state annals**
    
- **Refugee registers**
    
- **Ben-Gurion diaries**

- **United Nations records**
    
- **Peace agreements**
    
- **Human-rights reports**
    
- **DdH’s paired lament**
    
- **Dude 1 — Israeli Independence source:** David Ben-Gurion declares the State of Israel in May 1948.
    
- **Dude 2 — War annals:** Neighboring Arab armies enter; Israel survives and expands beyond the partition boundaries.
    
- **Dude 3 — Palestinian Nakba source:** Hundreds of thousands of Palestinians flee or are expelled, hundreds of communities are depopulated, and refugees are prevented from returning.
    
- **Dude 4 — DdH double account:** Israelis remember independence and deliverance; Palestinians remember catastrophe and dispossession.
    
- **Dude 5 — State-building annals:** Israel absorbs Holocaust survivors and Jews fleeing or expelled from Arab and Muslim-majority countries.
    
- **Dude 6 — Internal judgment:** Mizrahi immigrants encounter rescue, coercive assimilation, neglect, and discrimination within the new state.
    
- **Dude 7 — Palestinian dispersion source:** Refugees establish lives in Gaza, the West Bank, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, and farther abroad, preserving keys and village names.
    
- **Dude 8 — Ben-Gurion annals:** Ben-Gurion centralizes armed power, dissolves rival militias, establishes institutions, and dominates the state’s early decades.
    
- **Dude 9 — Suez source:** Israel, Britain, and France attack Egypt in 1956 and withdraw under international pressure.
    
- **Dude 10 — Six-Day War chronicle:** Israel defeats neighboring armies in 1967 and occupies the West Bank, East Jerusalem, Gaza, Sinai, and the Golan Heights.
    
- **Dude 11 — DdH judgment:** A people once stateless now rules another people without citizenship in the state governing much of their life.
    
- **Dude 12 — Settlement chronicle:** Civilian settlements spread through occupied territory under governments of several parties.
    
- **Dude 13 — Yom Kippur War source:** Egypt and Syria attack in 1973; surprise, loss, and eventual Israeli recovery transform regional politics.
