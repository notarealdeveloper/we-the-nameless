The Greek term (Euphrátēs) combined the prefix eu- (good) with the existing Phrat sound from Hebrew, Akkadian, and related languages, sometimes interpreted as "fruitful", in which case the meaning would be something like "good fruit."

The Akkadian Purattu has been perpetuated in Semitic languages and in other nearby languages of the time.

The Elamite, Akkadian, and possibly Sumerian forms are suggested to be from an unrecorded substrate language.

Buranun (Sumerian), the earliest known name, frequently appears in texts from the mid-third millennium BCE.

The origin is likely from proto-Sumerian \*burudu "copper" (Sumerian urudu), with an explanation that Euphrates was the river by which copper was transported, since Mesopotamia was the center of metallurgy during the bronze age.

In biblical Hebrew, the word for bronze is נחשת (NHSt) in its noun form, and נחוש (NHwS) as an adjective.

The word's origin is apparently from the passive participle of נחש (NHS) meaning divination, or from the red color of the throat of a serpent (H5175) when hissing.

So in summary Euphrates means something like Good Fruit but it's most likely from an older Sumerian term related to bronze which is נחש (NHS) which in Biblical Hebrew is spelled the same as snake נחש (NHS).
