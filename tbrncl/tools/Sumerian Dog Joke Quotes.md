
> _This bar joke from ancient Sumer has been making rounds on Twitter as non-sequitur humor. What does it actually mean?_

> _Sumerian taverns are also brothels. A double entendre occurs in line 74. “igi nu-mu-un-du₈” refers to an unopened eye; but “nu mu un su” means widow. The dog enters and says both “I don’t see anything” and “I don’t see anything but widows.” He pairs off with one (taka₄)._  
> _73. ur-gir₁₅-re e₂-dam-ce₃ in-kur₉-ma_  
> _74. nij₂ na-me igi nu-mu-un-du₈_  
> _75. ne-en jal₂ taka₄-en-e-ce_  
> _(cf. 6.2.3: UET 6/2 264)_

> _This askhistorians thread is pretty good. Basically, there are several theories about what this joke means. The older theory is that it is a sex joke, because I guess Sumerian taverns also had brothel functionality. Another theory is that there was a gesture that went along with the joke that isn’t captured. Finally, it might be a pun that the dog is just opening his eye, because the word for “to see” includes the word for “eye” (something like “to cast an eye”)._

> _There’s an ancient Sumerian joke that goes: “A dog walks into a bar and says, ‘I cannot see a thing. I’ll open this one.’” People have concluded the joke’s meaning is lost to how different Sumerian culture was. I have figured out the joke’s meaning._
