---

Short answer: almost nothing, with one important exception.


---

1. In the standard Epic of Gilgamesh (Akkadian version)

Utnapishtim (Uta-napišti) is:

a flood survivor

granted immortality


But:

> His father is not named.



Instead, what matters is:

the god Ea / Enki warns him directly


So in the Akkadian epic:

> lineage is basically irrelevant divine selection replaces genealogy




---

2. In the older Sumerian tradition (this is where it gets interesting)

In the Sumerian flood story:

the flood hero is Ziusudra (the earlier form of Utnapishtim)


And here we do get a father:

> Ziusudra = son of Ubara-Tutu


---

3. Sumerian King List

This is the key parallel source.

It lists:

Ubara-Tutu = last king before the flood

Then: “the flood swept over”


Ziusudra (Utnapishtim) is tied to this same figure.

👉 So:

> Utnapishtim’s father = Ubara-Tutu (in Sumerian tradition)




---

4. Who is Ubara-Tutu?

He’s:

the final antediluvian king

from Shuruppak (same city as the flood hero)


Meaning:

> Utnapishtim is the son of the last king of the pre-flood world




---

5. Why this matters (compare to Genesis)

Now look at the structural parallel:

Mesopotamia	Genesis

Ubara-Tutu (last king)	Lamech (last pre-flood father)
Utnapishtim (flood survivor)	Noah
divine warning (Ea)	divine warning (YHWH)


👉 Both traditions:

place the flood hero right at the end of a lineage

tie him to a significant father figure



---

6. Key difference

Mesopotamia	Genesis

royal line (king → son)	family line (father → son)
political legitimacy	moral / covenantal framing



---

7. Clean compressed version (your style)

> Akkadian: Utnapishtim has no father that matters.
Sumerian: He’s the son of the last king before the flood.



or sharper:

> Utnapishtim = heir to the old world.
Noah = reset of the new one.




---

8. One subtle connection you might like

Lamech (Genesis) = peak of violence

Ubara-Tutu (Sumerian) = peak of kingship


Both sit:

> immediately before the flood boundary

with

There are some very non-random correspondences there.
