
## Song of Lamech (Genesis 4:23–24)

Then Lamech spoke; his haughty bosom glowed,  
And thus before his wondering consorts showed:

"Adah and Zillah, hear your lord's command;  
Attend the words that fall from Lamech's hand.

Give ear, ye wives, while I my deeds proclaim,  
Nor think your husband fashioned out of shame.

A man I slew who dared my peace molest;  
A youth I struck who rose against my breast.

The wound he gave was slight, the vengeance great;  
Such is the portion rebels earn from fate.

Let weaker souls endure the threatening blow,  
Or nurse their wrongs and let their anger slow:

My hand is swift; my spirit knows no fear;  
The foe once marked shall find destruction near.

If Cain, though guilty, found such guarding care  
That sevenfold vengeance waited for him there,

Then far more dreadful shall my sentence prove,  
And Heaven itself my injured right approve.

Not seven alone shall answer for the deed,  
But seventy times and seven shall justice heed.

Let all who hear my name remember well:  
I strike but once, yet hosts shall for it fall."

## Song of the Sea (Exodus 15:1–18)

Then Moses sang, and Israel's ransomed throng  
To YHWH raised high the triumph of their song:

"To YHWH I sing, whose glory none withstand;  
The horse and rider fell beneath His hand.

My strength is YHWH, my song, my sure relief;  
He saved me from the hand of fear and grief.

This is my God—His praise shall fill my breath;  
My fathers' God, exalted after death.

YHWH loves the field where dreadful banners flame;  
YHWH is for ever His memorial name.

Pharaoh's proud cars and all his martial train  
He hurled and buried in the engulfing main.

His chosen chiefs beneath the waters lie;  
They sought the shore, but found no path to fly.

The deeps enclosed them in their dark domain;  
Like stones they plunged beneath the sounding plain.

Thy right hand, YHWH, in majesty renowned,  
Thy right hand dashed the haughty tyrant down.

In greatness of Thy glory, fierce and high,  
Thou cast'st Thy foes like stubble doomed to die.

Thy wrath went forth; before its scorching breath  
Their strength consumed and vanished into death.

At Thy nostrils' blast the flooding waters rose,  
And heaped like walls against Thy people's foes.

The rolling waves stood fixed on either side;  
The depths congealed beneath the parted tide.

The foe proclaimed: "I'll follow and o'ertake;  
Their spoil I'll seize, their trembling ranks I'll break.

My soul shall feast upon the conquered prey;  
My sword shall rule; my hand shall have the sway."

Thou blew'st Thy wind; the sea resumed its reign,  
And closed above them with its watery chain.

Like lead they sank beneath the burdening wave,  
And found within the deep a sudden grave.

Who among gods may with Thee, YHWH, compare?  
Who shines like Thee, so holy and so fair?

In fearful splendor seated far above,  
Working great wonders, terrible in love.

Thou stretchedst forth Thy hand; the earth below  
Received Thy foes and swallowed all the foe.

In steadfast love Thy rescued people came;  
Thou ledst the redeemed who trust Thy name.

By strength Thou guid'st them onward through the wild,  
To Thy holy dwelling keep Thy chosen child.

The nations hear, and all their hearts shall shake;  
The chiefs of Philistia fear and quake.

Then Edom's princes stand with pale dismay;  
The mighty men of Moab melt away.

The dwellers all in Canaan faint with fear;  
Their courage fails when Thy great deeds they hear.

Terror and dread upon their hearts shall fall;  
Thy mighty arm shall strike them still as wall.

Like lifeless stone they stand, bereft of breath,  
Awaiting YHWH, whose march ordains their death.

Till Thy own people safely pass the flood,  
The people purchased by Thy power and blood—

Not blood of offering laid on altar-stone,  
But bloodless victories by Thy strength alone.

Thou shalt bring them to Thy mountain's side,  
And plant them there secure in lasting pride.

The place Thy hands prepared for Thine abode,  
The sanctuary established, YHWH our Lord.

YHWH shall reign while endless ages flow,  
Above the heavens and all the worlds below."

## Song of Deborah (Judges 5)

Then Deborah and Barak raised the strain,  
And thus their triumph filled the rescued plain:

"For chiefs who led, for people who obeyed,  
Bless YHWH, by whom this mighty work was made.

Hear, kings! Attend, ye princes of the earth!  
While I proclaim my Sovereign's wondrous worth.

To YHWH I sing; His praise shall still employ  
My grateful tongue and all my sacred joy."

When Thou, O YHWH, from Seir's mountains came,  
And marched from Edom clothed in cloud and flame,

The trembling earth confessed Thy dreadful way;  
The heavens dissolved and wept the watery day.

The mountains melted at Thy presence there,  
Even Sinai bowed beneath the God of fear—

Before great YHWH, whose power all worlds obey,  
The God of Israel, glorious in array.

In Shamgar's days the traveled roads were bare;  
Men left the highways to the thief and snare.

Through winding paths the fearful wanderers crept;  
The sword prevailed, and every village wept.

Strength failed in Israel, courage fled away,  
Till I, Deborah, rose to lead the day.

A mother rose in Israel's dark distress,  
To save her children and their wrongs redress.

New gods they chose; then war was at the gate;  
No shield appeared to turn the hand of fate.

Among forty thousand scarcely one was found  
With spear or buckler fit for battle's ground.

My heart is with the rulers brave and true,  
Who freely came when duty called them through.

Bless YHWH, ye noble spirits, for the deed;  
The willing soul is Heaven's most valued meed.

Speak, ye who ride on asses white and fair;  
Ye judges, merchants, travelers everywhere.

Recount His works beside the watering-place,  
His righteous triumphs shown to Israel's race.

Then to the gates descended once again  
A people free, no longer bound in chain.

Awake, Deborah! Wake thy noblest lays!  
Arise and crown the Victor with thy praise!

Arise, O Barak! Lead thy captives on,  
Thou son of Abinoam, glory won!

Then marched the remnant at their God's command;  
The mighty followed to the battle's stand.

From Ephraim came they down with eager speed;  
Behind them Benjamin advanced to lead.

From Machir's line came counselors renowned;  
From Zebulun the marshal's staff was found.

The princes of Issachar joined the fight;  
With Deborah they stood for truth and right.

As Barak charged into the vale below,  
They rushed behind him on the routed foe.

Yet Reuben lingered by his quiet streams;  
Great thoughts he pondered, rich in idle dreams.

Why sat'st thou listening to the shepherd's call,  
While war's loud trumpet thundered over all?

Among Reuben's clans debate was long and grave;  
They weighed the cause, but none their brethren gave.

Gilead beyond the Jordan kept his seat;  
Dan lingered still among his ships' retreat.

Asher remained beside the sounding shore,  
And watched the waves break idly evermore.

But Zebulun and Naphtali disdained  
Their lives, and glory by their courage gained.

Upon the heights they dared the fatal field,  
Nor asked what safety flight or fear might yield.

The kings came forth; they fought by Taanach's side,  
Where Megiddo's waters through the valleys glide.

Yet silver gained they none, nor spoil of gold;  
Their hopes were broken, and their pride grew cold.

The stars themselves from heaven's high courses came,  
And fought against great Sisera's vaunted name.

Their ancient pathways in the skies they kept,  
Yet down upon his host destruction swept.

The Kishon swept them in its raging flood,  
The ancient torrent running dark with blood.

O march, my soul, in strength and triumph still,  
Nor let thy courage bend to fear or ill!

Then thundered loud the charging horse's hoof;  
The mighty fled, stripped bare of all reproof.

Their galloping steeds the trembling earth resound;  
Their broken squadrons scatter o'er the ground.

Curse Meroz, cried the Angel from above;  
Curse all her people for their want of love.

They came not forth when YHWH required the brave;  
They would not help the mighty hearts who gave.

Most blessed among women be the fame  
Of Jael, Heber's wife, exalted name!

Above the dwellers in the tent she stands,  
The crown of honor resting on her hands.

He asked for water; milk she kindly brought,  
In lordly bowl the choicest curds she sought.

Her left hand seized the nail prepared before;  
Her right the hammer warriors often bore.

She struck brave Sisera with a deadly blow,  
And laid the mighty captain groaning low.

She crushed his head; she pierced his temples through;  
The work was swift, the fatal stroke was true.

He bowed, he fell, he sank beneath her feet;  
He bowed, he fell, and owned a full defeat.

Beneath her feet the haughty warrior lay;  
He fell where first he stooped, and there he lay.

Meanwhile his mother watched with anxious eyes,  
And through the lattice sent her eager cries:

"Why stays his chariot on the distant road?  
Why comes he not beneath his triumph's load?

Why linger still the wheels that should appear?  
What cause delays the victor's glad career?"

Her wisest ladies answered to console,  
And she herself repeated to her soul:

"Surely they spoil and share the conquered land;  
The captives wait for every warrior's hand.

Rich robes embroidered meet the victor's claim;  
Bright garments wrought with needlework and flame.

Such spoil as this shall grace great Sisera's neck,  
And all his triumph with new splendor deck."

Thus perish all Thy foes, O YHWH of might!  
Confounded utterly before Thy sight.

But let Thy friends shine forth with growing blaze,  
Like morning's sun ascending through the haze.

So peace the land enjoyed for forty years,  
Released at last from battle's wounds and tears.

## Blessing of Jacob (Genesis 49)

Then Jacob called his sons around his bed,  
And thus the aged patriarch calmly said:

"Draw near, my sons, and hear your father's voice;  
Attend, and in his final words rejoice.

What shall befall you in the latter days,  
I now unfold in solemn, parting lays."

Reuben, my first-born, once my pride and might,  
The dawn of manhood and the elder's right;

In dignity supreme, in strength the same,  
Yet swift as waters, unstable as a flame.

Thou shalt not hold the honors of thy birth,  
Nor wear the foremost crown upon the earth;

For thou didst climb thy father's secret bed,  
And there profane the couch where once I tread.

Simeon and Levi, brethren fierce and bold,  
Their wrath more cruel than their swords of old.

O let my soul not join their dark design,  
Nor with their councils let my glory twine.

In anger once they slew the mighty man;  
In stubborn pride their lawless deeds began.

Cursed be their wrath, so violent and wild;  
Cursed be the rage no reason ever smiled.

Through Israel's tribes their portions shall be cast,  
Divided wide, their ancient power surpassed.

Judah, thy brethren shall thy praise proclaim;  
Before thy feet shall bow the sons of fame.

Thy hand shall press upon thy foeman's neck;  
Thy father's children shall thy power respect.

A lion's whelp art thou, advancing still;  
From slaughter rising to the royal hill.

He stoops, he couches, lordly in his den;  
Who dares provoke the king of beasts again?

The scepter shall not from fair Judah part,  
Nor ruler's staff depart from out his heart,

Till Shiloh come, whom nations shall obey,  
And peoples gather to his peaceful sway.

His foal he binds where fruitful vineyards climb;  
His ass's colt amid the choicest vine.

He washes garments in the grape's rich flood,  
And stains his robes with wine's abundant blood.

His eyes with wine are dark and shining bright;  
His teeth with milk outmatch the morning light.

Zebulun by the sea shall fix his seat,  
Where ships from distant coasts and kingdoms meet.

Toward Sidon's bounds his spreading borders run,  
Enriched by trade beneath the western sun.

Issachar, strong-boned ass, content to rest,  
Beneath the burden that he deems the best.

He saw the land was pleasant to behold,  
The fields abundant and the harvests gold.

He bowed his shoulder to the laboring yoke,  
And chose the tribute rather than provoke.

Dan shall his people judge with equal hand,  
Though but a single tribe within the land.

A serpent by the roadside shall he lie,  
An adder where the dusty pathways fly;

He strikes the horse's heels with sudden art,  
And casts the mounted rider from his cart.

For Thy salvation, YHWH, I waiting stand;  
My hope rests wholly in Thy mighty hand.

Gad shall be pressed by many a hostile band,  
Yet chase at last the spoilers from his land.

Asher's bread is rich, his table crowned with cheer;  
Fit fare for kings shall flourish year by year.

Naphtali is a hind let loose and free,  
Whose words flow fair as streams that seek the sea.

Joseph, fruitful bough beside the spring,  
Whose branches over every wall upspring.

The archers vexed him with malicious aim,  
And shot their shafts to wound his rising fame.

Yet firm remained his bow in strength and grace,  
Upheld by hands no earthly foes could chase—

By Jacob's Mighty One, whose power sustains,  
The Shepherd, Stone of Israel, who reigns.

By El of thy father, who shall help thee still;  
By Shaddai's blessings poured from heaven's hill;

Blessings above from skies that freely give,  
Blessings below where hidden fountains live;

Blessings of breast and womb in full increase,  
Crowning thy house with fruitfulness and peace.

Thy father's blessings rise beyond the height  
Of ancient hills and mountains crowned with light.

Upon the head of Joseph let them rest,  
Distinguished ever from his brethren blest.

Benjamin, ravening wolf at break of day,  
Devours the spoil and tears the prey away.

At evening still his conquering strength remains,  
And shares the plunder gathered from the plains.

Thus Jacob spoke, and thus his sons received  
The final words their aged sire believed.

To every tribe a fitting lot was given;  
His blessing sealed, his spirit turned to Heaven.

## Song of Moses (Deuteronomy 33)

This was the blessing Moses gave before  
He passed beyond the dark and silent shore.

The man of God, whose eye retained its flame,  
Bestowed his final gifts on Israel's name.

He said:

"From Sinai's height came forth the mighty YHWH;  
From Seir's hills His dawning splendors grew.

From Paran's mount He cast His rising light,  
And marched with myriads clothed in holy might.

Fire from His hand before His presence ran;  
His law descended then to guide His clan.

He loved His people; all the saints were near,  
Safe in His hand, upheld by Him from fear.

They sat before His feet to hear His word,  
And every heart received what it had heard.

Through Moses came the law that still shall stand,  
The treasured portion of the chosen land.

Then YHWH was King in Jeshurun's estate,  
When tribes assembled and the chiefs met state."

Let Reuben live, nor let his line be gone;  
Though few his numbers, still preserve his son.

Hear Judah's voice, O YHWH, when dangers call;  
Restore him safely to his people all.

Let strength abide within his warrior hand,  
And be his help against the hostile band.

Thy Urim and Thy Thummim, bright and pure,  
To faithful Levi shall remain secure.

At Massah's springs Thou provedst all his worth;  
At Meribah his zeal was tested forth.

He knew not father, brother, child, nor wife,  
When duty called him to the sacred strife.

Thy covenant he guarded from decline,  
And kept with jealous care each law of Thine.

Thy judgments shall to Jacob's sons be shown,  
And clouds of incense rise before Thy throne.

Whole offerings on Thine altar shall ascend,  
Their fragrant smoke to Heaven's high courts shall bend.

Bless, YHWH, his substance; prosper all his ways;  
Accept the labor that his hand displays.

Strike through the loins of all who dare oppose;  
Let none again presume to be his foes.

Beloved of YHWH, secure from fear shall rest;  
By Him protected and forever blessed.

Between His shoulders shall he dwell at ease,  
Sheltered beneath His everlasting peace.

Joseph be blessed by YHWH's abundant hand,  
Above the tribes that dwell throughout the land.

With treasures dropped from Heaven's refreshing dew,  
And riches hidden in the depths from view;

With fruits matured beneath the circling sun,  
And harvests born when changing moons have run;

With ancient mountains' wealth and ageless hills,  
And all the bounty earth's broad bosom fills;

With favor from the One who in the bush  
Appeared in fire amid the desert hush.

Let all these blessings crown fair Joseph's head,  
Distinguished prince among his brethren bred.

His firstborn bullock's glory shall be known;  
His horns like wild ox horns are widely thrown.

With these he drives the nations from his way,  
And thrusts earth's distant peoples far away.

Such Ephraim's thousands shall victorious stand;  
Such Manasseh's hosts possess the promised land.

Rejoice, Zebulun, venturing o'er the foam;  
Rejoice, Issachar, remaining still at home.

Together shall they call the tribes to pray,  
And offer righteous sacrifice each day.

They draw the treasures hidden in the seas,  
And gather wealth borne in by every breeze.

Blessed is Gad, whose borders wide extend;  
Like lion crouched he waits upon the bend.

The arm he tears, the crown he rends away;  
The foremost portion claimed he on the day.

With Israel's chiefs he marched in honor bright,  
And joined his brethren in the lawful fight.

Dan is a lion's whelp in youthful pride;  
From Bashan's forests shall he sudden stride.

Naphtali, rich in favor from above,  
Filled to the brim with YHWH's enduring love.

Possess the western sea, the southern plain;  
Let blessing crown thy harvests and thy grain.

Most blessed of sons let Asher ever be;  
Beloved among his brethren constantly.

Let him in oil his fruitful footsteps lave,  
And all the wealth his fertile valleys gave.

Let bars of iron and of brass defend;  
Let strength increase as all his years extend.

None is like Israel's God, enthroned on high,  
Who rides through Heaven and commands the sky.

The everlasting God thy dwelling place;  
Beneath, eternal arms uphold thy race.

He drives the foe before thy conquering tread;  
"Destroy!" He cries, and all their strength has fled.

Thus Israel dwells in safety and alone,  
Beneath the blessing of His sovereign throne.

A land of grain and wine before him lies;  
His heavens distill their dew from gracious skies.

Happy art thou, O Israel! Who can be  
Compared in glory and in strength with thee?

A people saved by YHWH's victorious hand,  
The shield and sword that guard thy chosen land.

Thy enemies shall bow before thy feet,  
And all their lofty places know defeat.

## Psalm 82

Elohim takes his stand as the the Elohim take seat.
Among the gods the highest calls the pantheon to meet.

"How long will you judge falsely and uphold the wicked's claim?
Judge the weak while you let corruption flourish without shame?

Defend the poor; the orphan's right maintain;  
Let not the weak appeal to you in vain.

Protect the lowly crushed by fraud and might;  
Deliver those deprived of lawful right.

Rescue the needy from oppression's hand;  
Let truth and judgment govern all the land."

But they know nothing and refuse to learn;  
In paths of darkness all their footsteps turn.

They wander blind wherever they are led;  
The light of wisdom from their councils fled.

The people walk in darkness
under all the wrongs they've dealt.
Every cornerstone is shaken,
all of earth's foundations melt.

"I had said you are gods,
and children of the Most High;  
I had crowned you all with light
Now like a human you will die.

And like the rulers you will fall;  
For it was I who made you great,
Now I will make you weak and small.

The divinity I gave you all, soon will turn to dust;  
For you did violence to my covenants, and you have broken all my trust.

No deathless state shall keep your name alive;  
No borrowed splendor past the tomb survive."

Arise the Elohim, and judge the earth once more;  
For you give inheritance to all the nations ever more.
