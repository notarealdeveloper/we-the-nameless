> When Enkidu heard glorious Shamash his angry heart grew quiet, he called back the curse and said, ‘Woman, I promise you another destiny.
> 
> The mouth which cursed you shall bless you! Kings, princes and nobles shall adore you. On your account a man though twelve miles off will clap his hand to his thigh and his hair will twitch.
> 
> For you he will undo his belt and open his treasure and you shall have your desire; lapis lazuli, gold and’ carnelian from the heap in the treasury.

## What’s going on in this scene

This is from the **Enkidu–Shamhat episode** in the _Epic of Gilgamesh_.

- Enkidu has just **cursed Shamhat** (the woman who seduced/civilized him), blaming her for pulling him out of the wild.
- Then the sun-god Shamash intervenes, basically saying: _without her, you’d still be an animal—this is your path to becoming human and great._
- Enkidu **reverses himself**:
    - He retracts the curse
    - He **blesses Shamhat instead**
    - He predicts her future prosperity, wealth, and social power

So the speech is a **curse → blessing reversal formula**, very typical of Mesopotamian literature.
