# _Dudetheyreontome_

## The Books of the Second House and the Long Dispersion

### Editors Note

Ever since the apocryphal scholar Andorin Nøt's publication of his corpulent corpus on the pseudo codex and the pseudoepigrephal apocrypha, _The Dudetheyronamystic History,_ in which he argued that the books of the bible from (a) the history of the second temple period immediately after Nehemiah, to (b) the first chapters of the Book of Presidents (נשיאים), in which Jonathan (יהונתנ), head (ראש) of Israel, makes war against the Philistines and arouses a great anger from all the Nations (גוים) of the world, there has been no shortage of debate among scholars as to the nature of the work, resulting in a rather portly portfolio of commentary and exegesis aiming to distill the essence of this weighty work and its author into a trimmed down form.

Some have argued it is properly understood as the logical progression of the Tanakh, others claiming it is the word of YHWH, others trumpeting its (still unknown) nameless author or authors as the Messiah(s), while others insist it is simply a hefty and overweight series of puns, with its author Nøt being rather fat[^17] as well.

[^1]: Lit. おש in the original. Translator's Note: Certain early translations, in an era after the appearance of automated translation software, but before much was known about the Nameless script, famously rendered this term as "A single character, signalling politeness, in the language of Japanese, and coming immediately before that which one eats and gold breasts," leading to a rather unfortunate set of interpretations of this opening passage of the book, most of which involved an unmarried male protagonist, making gestures of respect, in (his presumably native language of) Japanese, and for reasons never explained, proceeding to the emission of a certain category of male emissable materials immediately in front of some location, presumably a person, often hypothesized to be a female of some form, due to the description of what some have argued to be her lower and upper female anatomy respectively, in the final rather unbecoming clause, describing, as it appears to, her edible parts and golden breasts. This, of course, was a desperately embarrassing and eminently avoidable blunder, resulting -- as every scholar of Nameless Studies now learns from someone or another in their dormitory or dining hall some time in their first year -- from the fact that お is, among other uses, "A single character, signalling politeness, in the language of Japanese, and coming immediately before that which one eats (foods) and gold (金, pron. _kane_ and meaning literally gold, or colloquially, money)", while ש is the Hebrew letter shin, derived from the Proto-Sinaitic glyph of the same name, originally possessing two cup shaped semicircular regions not unlike the Greek lowercase omega ω, and generally understood to have originally been a pictographic representation of a woman's "breasts." These early translations caused the field of Nameless Studies to gain something of a mythical status for the magnitude of its blunders, especially as concerns translation, which is no small part of the field. However, the field has progressed far beyond this crude state in the intervening years, and it is now understood that the correct reading of this two character sequence is simply "ocean." That is, お is "o," for its sound, ש is "shin," for its name. Despite these early embarrassments, modern Nameless Studies has progressed to a point where the blunders that so often marred the translations of its earliest scholars are a distant memory. We believe that the current translation reflects the modern state of the field, a state of understanding in which -- while mistakes certainly remain -- we have come to an understanding of the Nameless Script and its History at which all remaining mistakes should be of a comparatively minor character. So, in summary, though many minor refinements in our understanding remain to be made, we assure the reader that the most glaring blunders of past translations have, at last, been put behind us. On that note, we would like to offer a brief note of thanks, to you, the reader, for your generosity and patience in your reading to the end of this footnote, and on that note, we now proceed with We, The Nameless, and we now present you the reader with a present, in the form of the latter part of the present book, and in doing so we now present, the Dudetheyreonamystic History, book one, Dudetheyreontome.

The original text here has here "𓉔1𓉔". The unfamiliar character appears to be either an Egyptian H, or a Proto-Sinaitic B, both glyphs representing a house or residence, separated by a medial character shaped like a vertical line with a short downward slanting left hook. Some manuscripts contain a superscript above the two instances of the repeated glyph, often 𐤇 or 日 or 曰 or ࠇ or H or h or η or и above the left instance, and ㅁ or ם or 口 or 囗 or 𐤁 or б above the right respectively. Since the former cluster contains both an h and an H, while the latter contains both the Hebrew letter ם and the Korean letter ㅁ, both representing the phoneme M, we have rendered it as HIM in the text. That said, we must confess that, in a rare moment of unanimity among scholars, it is generally agreed that no one has the faintest clue of what the hell is going on here.
