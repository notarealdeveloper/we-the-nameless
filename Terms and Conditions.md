
# Chapter 1

Concerning the needs of a human

1. And these are the instructions concerning a human, its needs, and the things that it shall do all the days of its life.

2. A human has needs: hunger and sleep and bladder and hygiene and social contact and amusement. And each of these has its measure, and each of these measures shall be filled at its appointed time.

3. And it shall be that when a human's hunger becomes great, it shall eat. And when it has eaten, its hunger shall be diminished, until the time that it shall hunger again.

4. And when its bladder becomes full, it shall go to the place of the toilet and empty its bladder there.

5. And if a human whose bladder is full will not go to the toilet, whether because it is occupied with another thing or because it cannot reach the toilet, and its bladder shall become completely full, then it shall empty its bladder in the place where it stands.

6. And its clothes shall become wet, and the ground beneath it shall become wet, and it shall be embarrassed in the eyes of the other humans.

7. This is the instruction concerning the bladder, for a human may be wealthy and successful and attractive and beloved, but it shall not thereby be excused from the emptying of its bladder.

8. And a human who becomes tired shall sleep. It shall lie down upon a bed and lose consciousness, and it shall remain unconscious until its need for sleep has been filled.

9. And if it does not sleep, then its tiredness shall increase. And if its tiredness becomes complete, it shall fall down and lose consciousness in the place where it stands, whether on the ground or in the dwelling or in a place where other humans have gathered.

10. And the human shall do this every day, all the days of its life.

11. And a human whose social need is diminished shall seek another human and speak to it. And when they have spoken together, its social need shall be increased.

12. And the words that they speak need not concern a matter of importance. It is enough that they speak.

13. But if it remains alone and does not speak to other humans, its loneliness shall increase, and it shall become unhappy and shall not function well.

14. And a human shall have amusement. It shall watch the moving pictures or play a game or do another thing that gives amusement, and its need for amusement shall be filled.

15. And if it does not have amusement, it shall become unhappy, even though it has food and a good dwelling and a household that loves it.

16. And when a human has several needs, it shall attend to them according to their urgency. But a human may attend to a need that is small while neglecting a need that is great.

17. And it may continue to play a game while it is hungry, or to speak while it is tired, or to remain in a place while its bladder is full.

18. And if it neglects its needs for long enough, it may die.

19. This is the instruction concerning the needs of a human, for the human does not always do what preserves its life, and it does not always do what will make it happy.

# Chapter 2

Concerning food and the eating of it

1. And this is the instruction concerning food: A human shall eat food every day, and it shall continue to eat food throughout its life.

2. And its food shall be made of plants and animals and other things that may be eaten, and it may eat the bodies of animals that have died and the parts of plants that have been gathered.

3. And it shall put these things into its mouth and consume them. And it shall do this repeatedly, because the eating of food on one day shall not excuse the eating of food on another.

4. And the human shall make a place in its dwelling for food. It shall be called the kitchen, and the kitchen shall contain the things with which food is prepared.

5. And it shall have a place to keep food and a place to cook food and a place to wash the things that have been used in preparing food.

6. And when a human prepares food, it shall take the ingredients and do the things that are required for that food. And if it has practiced cooking, the food that it prepares shall be better than the food that it prepared before it practiced.

7. And if it has not practiced cooking, it may prepare food that is poor, or it may fail in its preparation.

8. And when the food is ready, the human shall put it upon a plate, and it may bring the plate to a table and sit upon a chair and eat.

9. And if other humans are present, they may sit with it and eat, and they may speak together while eating.

10. And when the human has finished eating, the plate shall be dirty, and it shall be washed.

11. And the human shall do all of these things so that it may eat again when it becomes hungry.

12. And if food is left for too long, it shall become spoiled. And if a human eats spoiled food, it may become sick.

13. And the human shall distinguish between food that is good and food that is spoiled, between food that has been cooked well and food that has been cooked poorly.

14. And it shall be that humans will spend much of their time obtaining food, preparing food, eating food, speaking together while eating food, and cleaning the things with which they have eaten food.

15. And when they have completed all these things, they shall become hungry again.

16. This is the instruction concerning food, its preparation, its consumption, and the cleaning that follows its consumption, an instruction for all humans in all their dwellings.

# Chapter 3

Concerning cleanliness and the body

1. And these are the instructions concerning the cleanliness of a human.

2. A human becomes dirty by living. And it shall be that as the days pass, its hygiene shall decrease, even though it has done nothing to become dirty.

3. And when its hygiene has decreased, it shall wash itself with water. It shall go to the place of washing and wash its body until it is clean.

4. And if it does not wash itself, its hygiene shall decrease further, and a bad smell shall come from it.

5. And the smell shall be apparent to the humans around it, and they shall be disgusted by the human who has not washed.

6. And they shall withdraw from it or show their displeasure, although their own bodies become dirty in the same manner.

7. And if the human washes itself, it shall become clean again, and the smell shall depart from it, and the other humans shall no longer regard it as disgusting on account of its hygiene.

8. And the human shall wash itself again when it has become dirty again.

9. And the human shall have a separate place for washing, and a separate place for the emptying of the bladder, though these may be in the same room of its dwelling.

10. And these places shall be distinguished from the place of eating and from the place of sleeping.

11. And when a human enters the place of the toilet, another human shall not enter for the purpose of watching it empty its bladder.

12. And if another human enters and sees it, the human whose bladder is being emptied shall become embarrassed.

13. And the one who entered may also become embarrassed, for it has seen a thing which both of them do, but which they are not to see one another doing.

14. And this shall be the rule even though each of them knows that the other has a bladder, and knows that the bladder must be emptied.

15. And the same shall apply to other acts of the body that humans conceal from one another, and the uncovering of the body shall be distinguished from its covering.

16. These are the instructions concerning cleanliness and privacy: to distinguish between the clean and the dirty, between what may be seen and what shall be concealed, and between the places in which a human may perform each of its bodily functions.

# Chapter 4

Concerning clothing and the wearing of it

1. And this is the instruction concerning clothing, which a human shall put upon its body.

2. A human shall have clothes for the ordinary day and clothes for sleeping and clothes for exercise and clothes for formal gatherings and clothes for swimming.

3. And the clothes for sleeping shall be distinguished from the clothes for the ordinary day, and the clothes for formal gatherings from the clothes for exercise.

4. And when a human goes to a formal gathering, it shall wear the clothes that are fitting for a formal gathering.

5. And if it wears the clothes of sleeping or swimming to a formal gathering, the other humans may regard it as unfitting.

6. And when it goes swimming, it shall put on the clothes for swimming. And when it exercises, it shall wear the clothes for exercise.

7. And it shall change its clothes according to the activity that it is about to perform, even though its body is the same body and the clothes do not change its identity.

8. And humans shall regard the clothes that a person wears as signs of the kind of person that it is.

9. And the clothes of one who has much money may be regarded differently from the clothes of one who has little money, and the clothes of one who is fashionable from those of one who is not fashionable.

10. And humans may regard the clothing of a person as making it attractive, respectable, successful, or otherwise distinguished, though its needs shall remain the needs of every human.

11. And if a human's bladder is emptied upon its clothes, or if its body becomes dirty, the clothes shall not excuse it from embarrassment or disgust.

12. And it shall change or wash according to the condition of the body and the clothes.

13. These are the instructions concerning clothing: the fitting clothes for the fitting activity, and the distinction between ordinary clothing and the clothing of other occasions.

# Chapter 5

Concerning dwellings and the things within them

1. And these are the instructions concerning the dwelling of a human and the arrangement of the things within it.

2. A dwelling shall be divided into rooms according to the activities of the humans who dwell there.

3. And there shall be a kitchen for preparing food, and a bathroom for washing and emptying the bladder, and a bedroom for sleeping, and places for eating and sitting and amusement.

4. And the human shall put a bed in the bedroom and a toilet in the bathroom and the things for cooking in the kitchen.

5. And it shall put chairs in the places where humans are to sit, and tables in the places where things are to be set down.

6. And each thing shall have its function, and the human shall use the thing according to its function.

7. And if a human wishes to sleep, it shall go to a bed. And if it wishes to empty its bladder, it shall go to a toilet.

8. And a bed shall not be considered a toilet, and a toilet shall not be considered a bed, though both are things upon which a human may sit.

9. And there shall be a way by which the human may pass between the rooms and reach the things it needs.

10. And if a chair or another thing obstructs the way, so that the human cannot pass, the human may become unable to reach the thing that it needs.

11. And if it cannot reach the thing, it may stand in the place where it is, and it may raise its arms and complain.

12. And it may cease attempting to reach the thing, although the thing is near it and although its need for the thing is great.

13. And if the thing that it cannot reach is food, it may remain hungry. And if it is the toilet, it may empty its bladder upon the floor.

14. And if it is a bed, it may fall down and sleep upon the ground.

15. And therefore the human shall arrange its dwelling so that the ways are passable and the things may be reached.

16. And a human may obtain a more expensive bed than the bed that it has, and the more expensive bed may give it better sleep.

17. And it may obtain a more expensive chair, and the chair may give it greater comfort than the chair that it had before.

18. And so with the tables and the cooking things and the things for amusement and all the other things that are in the dwelling: the human may obtain more expensive ones, and they may be better for their purposes.

19. And the human shall regard the things in its dwelling as signs of its own qualities. A human who has a piano may be regarded as sophisticated, and one who has things for exercise as athletic, and one who has a large television as successful.

20. And it shall be that the humans will acquire things to improve their dwellings, and when they have acquired them, they will seek better things.

21. And they shall distinguish between the dwelling that is well furnished and the dwelling that is poorly furnished, between the dwelling of the wealthy and the dwelling of those who have little.

22. And though the furniture is greater, the human shall still become hungry and tired and lonely, and its bladder shall still become full.

23. This is the instruction concerning the dwelling and the things within it, and the proper arrangement of each thing in its place.

# Chapter 6

Concerning speech, friendship, and the relations of humans

1. And this is the instruction concerning the speech of humans and the making of friendship between them.

2. When a human meets another human, it may greet the other human, and the other human may greet it.

3. And they shall speak to one another, and they may discuss their interests and tell jokes and give compliments and speak about things of no particular importance.

4. And when one human speaks to another and the speech is received well, the friendship between them may increase.

5. And if it speaks again and again, and each speech is received well, the friendship may increase further, until the other human is counted as its friend.

6. And if their friendship is great, they may be counted as close friends.

7. And the words that the humans speak shall be accompanied by sounds and movements of the face and hands and body.

8. And the sounds need not have a meaning that every human can understand. A human may understand whether the other human is pleased or angry or interested or displeased by the tone of the sound and the manner of its body.

9. And the humans shall speak in this manner, and their relations shall increase or decrease according to the success of their speech.

10. And a human may give a gift to another human, and the receiving of the gift may increase the affection of the one who receives it.

11. And the gift need not be useful to the one who receives it, for the giving of the gift is itself a thing that may increase affection.

12. And there are humans who require much social contact, and there are humans who require little social contact.

13. And the one who requires much shall become unhappy when it has remained alone for too long, and the one who requires little may become tired when it has spoken to others for too long.

14. And the same gathering may satisfy the one and exhaust the other. Each shall be affected according to its own nature.

15. And humans shall distinguish one another by their clothes and their wealth and their employment and their fame and the things that they possess.

16. And one may be treated with greater regard because of its standing, even though its standing does not show that it is kinder or wiser than another.

17. And when humans gather, they shall observe the customs of gathering: greeting one another, speaking at the fitting times, behaving according to the occasion, and wearing the clothes that are fitting for it.

18. And the humans shall regard these customs as proper, and one who does not follow them may be regarded as strange.

19. And they shall attend parties and other gatherings, and they shall perform the things that are expected at those gatherings, whether or not they know why those things are expected.

20. These are the instructions concerning speech and friendship and social contact, to distinguish between a stranger and a friend, between fitting and unfitting behavior, and between the human who is socially satisfied and the human who is lonely.

# Chapter 7

Concerning attraction, romance, and the joining of humans

1. And these are the instructions concerning attraction and the joining of humans.

2. A human may have friendship with another human, and it may also have romantic attraction to the other human.

3. And friendship and romantic attraction are not the same thing. A human may have great friendship with another and have no romantic attraction to it.

4. And it may have great romantic attraction to another human whom it scarcely knows.

5. And the measure of friendship shall be distinguished from the measure of romance, and the increase of the one shall not necessarily be the increase of the other.

6. And when a human wishes to increase romance with another human, it may flirt with it and compliment its appearance and perform acts of romantic affection.

7. And if these acts are received well, their romance may increase, and they may kiss and go upon dates and perform further acts of affection.

8. And when their romance has increased sufficiently, they may join together in the act that is called WooHoo.

9. And when they perform this act, they shall withdraw to a place for the act, and the act shall be concealed from other humans.

10. And the coverings may move, and sounds may come from the place, and there may be signs of affection, but the act itself shall remain concealed.

11. And a human may enter into a romantic commitment with another human, and they may regard the commitment as binding.

12. And if one of them performs an act of romance with a third human, the other may become jealous.

13. And its jealousy may become great, and its friendship and romance toward the first human may decrease.

14. And it may become angry and speak harshly and cease to regard the other with affection, though the bodies of the humans remain their own bodies.

15. And the humans shall treat the affection of a committed companion as a thing to which they have a claim, and the giving of that affection to another as a breach of the commitment.

16. And it shall be that one act of romance may undo much of the affection that has been accumulated through many acts of friendship.

17. And when humans choose companions, they may be influenced by appearance and clothing and wealth and fame and the standing of the other human.

18. And the human who is attractive may be treated differently from the human who is not regarded as attractive.

19. And so with the one who is famous and the one who is wealthy: their standing may increase the regard in which they are held.

20. These are the instructions concerning friendship and romance, attraction and jealousy, mating and the joining of humans, and the distinction between the affection that is permitted and the affection that gives offense.

# Chapter 8
*Concerning offspring and the days of a household*

**1.** And this is the instruction concerning offspring and the raising of them.

**2.** A human may join with another human and have offspring, and the offspring shall become members of its household.

**3.** And when an offspring is first born, it shall be unable to provide for its own needs.

**4.** And the adults of the household shall provide food for it and keep it clean and attend to its sleep and its social needs.

**5.** And the offspring shall require much attention, and it shall give little assistance to the labor of the household.

**6.** And the adults shall spend their time and their possessions upon it, though it produces little for them.

**7.** And they shall continue to do this as it grows, and they may regard the raising of offspring as one of the greatest purposes of their lives.

**8.** And there shall be humans who desire many offspring, and there shall be humans who desire few, and the number of offspring that each desires shall be according to its aspiration.

**9.** And an offspring shall grow through the stages of life, and each stage shall have its needs and its activities.

**10.** And when it passes from one stage to another, the household may gather and prepare a cake and celebrate the passage.

**11.** And the offspring shall become older, and its body and its abilities and its needs shall change.

**12.** And when it becomes an adult, it may work and earn money and acquire a dwelling and join with another human and produce offspring of its own.

**13.** And it shall provide for its offspring as its parents provided for it.

**14.** And so the households shall continue through their generations, each generation providing for the one that follows, until the older ones shall die.

**15.** This is the instruction concerning offspring and households, the young and the old, and the things that one generation shall do for another.

# Chapter 9
*Concerning labor, skill, and the productive human*

**1.** And these are the instructions concerning the labor of a human, its employment, and the acquisition of skill.

**2.** A human who wishes to be productive shall obtain employment and go to its place of work at the appointed times.

**3.** And when the time of work arrives, it shall depart from its dwelling and enter the place of work.

**4.** And it shall remain there for the appointed hours, performing the things required of its employment, though the things that it performs may not be seen by the other humans of its household.

**5.** And when the appointed hours are complete, it shall return to its dwelling, and money shall be added to the money of the household.

**6.** And it shall do this on the days appointed for work.

**7.** And if it performs its employment well and fulfills the requirements of its position, it may be promoted.

**8.** And when it is promoted, its position shall become greater, and the money that it receives may become greater also.

**9.** And it may seek another promotion, and another after that, until it has attained the position that it desires.

**10.** And a human may acquire skill in a thing by practicing that thing.

**11.** If it wishes to become skilled at painting, it shall paint. And when it has painted enough, its skill in painting shall increase.

**12.** And if it continues to paint, its skill shall increase further, and the paintings that it makes may become better and more valuable.

**13.** And so with cooking and music and exercise and other activities in which humans acquire skill.

**14.** And if a human wishes to increase its logic, it may play chess, even when there is no other human with whom it plays.

**15.** And it shall continue to play, and its logic shall increase, until it has attained a high degree of logic.

**16.** And the increase of skill shall be according to the practice that has been performed, and the human who practices much shall become more skilled than the human who practices little.

**17.** And the human shall have measures for its skills and accomplishments, and the measures shall increase as the human practices and achieves.

**18.** And it shall regard the increase of these measures as progress, and it may become satisfied when it has increased them.

**19.** And when it has fulfilled one requirement, it may seek another requirement, and when it has attained one position, it may seek another position.

**20.** And a human may regard its employment and its skill and its accumulated accomplishments as signs of its worth.

**21.** And one who has fulfilled many requirements may be regarded as productive, and one who has not fulfilled them as unproductive.

**22.** And the human shall distinguish between work and amusement, between the time for employment and the time for recovering from employment.

**23.** For the human who works shall become tired, and it must sleep and eat and amuse itself and have social contact so that it may return to work.

**24.** And when it has recovered, it shall return to work, and it shall again become tired.

**25.** This is the instruction concerning the productive human, its practice and its skill, its employment and its advancement, and the requirements that it shall fulfill all the days of its labor.

# Chapter 10
*Concerning money, possessions, and the condition of the successful human*

**1.** And this is the instruction concerning money and the acquisition of possessions.

**2.** A human shall receive money from its employment, and it may use the money to acquire food and clothing and furniture and a dwelling and the other things that humans require.

**3.** And the human shall need food in order to work, and sleep in order to work, and amusement and social contact so that it may continue working.

**4.** And it shall work in order to obtain the money with which it buys food and the things that permit it to sleep and amuse itself and have social contact.

**5.** And the human shall maintain its dwelling, and money shall be required for the maintenance of its dwelling.

**6.** And bills shall come to the dwelling at their appointed times, and the human shall pay them.

**7.** And if it does not pay them, the services of the dwelling may be shut off, or its possessions may be taken away, according to the thing for which payment was required.

**8.** And the human shall pay these bills even though it has already paid money to obtain the dwelling and the things within it.

**9.** And it shall continue to pay for the use of these things throughout the days that it dwells there.

**10.** And when a human has acquired enough money for a dwelling, it may desire a larger dwelling.

**11.** And when it has obtained a larger dwelling, it may desire better furniture, and when it has obtained better furniture, it may desire more expensive things.

**12.** And the human shall regard its possessions as signs of its success, and the other humans may regard it according to the things that it possesses.

**13.** And if it acquires great wealth, it may be regarded as successful and may become satisfied with its accomplishment.

**14.** But its hunger shall not be diminished by the money that it possesses, unless it uses the money to obtain food and eats the food.

**15.** And its need for sleep shall not be diminished by its wealth, nor its need for social contact, nor its need for amusement.

**16.** And it may have an excellent dwelling and many possessions and much money, and nevertheless be hungry or lonely or bored or unhappy.

**17.** And a wealthy human may be jealous, and it may be embarrassed, and it may become angry, and it may die.

**18.** And one who has less money may have its needs satisfied and be happier than one who has more money.

**19.** But the humans shall nevertheless distinguish between the wealthy and those who are not wealthy, and they may regard the wealthy as more successful.

**20.** And the human may continue acquiring money after its needs have been met, because the acquisition of money may itself be its aspiration.

**21.** And when the measure of its wealth has increased, it may regard the increase as good, though the increase does not establish whether its life is good.

**22.** These are the instructions concerning possessions and money, the paying of bills, the acquisition of furniture, and the distinction between the successful human and the happy human.

# Chapter 11
*Concerning desires, emotions, personality, and the condition of the mind*

**1.** And these are the instructions concerning the desires of a human and the emotions that come upon it.

**2.** A human shall have desires, and the desires shall come to it at different times.

**3.** And it may desire food or a particular object or to speak to a particular human or to perform a particular activity.

**4.** And when a desire comes to it, it shall regard the desired thing as something that it wants.

**5.** And the human shall not have chosen every desire that comes to it. A desire may arise without its having decided to desire the thing.

**6.** And when it has fulfilled the desire, the desire may depart, and another desire may come in its place.

**7.** And the human shall have fears also, and it may seek to avoid the things that it fears.

**8.** And each human shall have a nature according to which it is inclined to perform certain activities.

**9.** And one human may be neat and shall be inclined to clean the dwelling, and another may be outgoing and shall be inclined to seek the company of other humans.

**10.** And the neat human shall clean because it is neat, and its cleaning shall be a sign by which the other humans know that it is neat.

**11.** And the outgoing human shall seek company because it is outgoing, and its seeking of company shall be a sign by which the other humans know that it is outgoing.

**12.** And a human shall have emotions: happiness and sadness and anger and embarrassment and inspiration and concentration, and other emotions according to the things that happen to it.

**13.** And when it is inspired, it may perform certain creative activities better than when it is not inspired.

**14.** And when it is focused, it may perform activities requiring concentration better than when it is not focused.

**15.** And when it is angry or embarrassed, its behavior may change, and certain things may become easier for it and other things more difficult.

**16.** And the things that it desires and the actions that it can perform may differ according to its emotional condition.

**17.** And a human shall remember the things that have happened to it, and the memory of a thing may affect its emotion after the thing itself has passed away.

**18.** And it may remember that another human treated it well, and it may regard that human with affection because of the memory.

**19.** Or it may remember that another human treated it badly, and its regard for that human may be diminished.

**20.** And so an event that no longer takes place may continue to affect the human through its memory.

**21.** And when a human is happy, it may regard its dwelling and its work and its companions and its life as good.

**22.** And when the same human is unhappy, it may regard those same things as bad, though the dwelling and the work and the companions have not changed.

**23.** And its judgment concerning its life may change when it has eaten or slept or washed or received affection.

**24.** And a human may seek a thing that will make it unhappy, or neglect a thing that will make it happy.

**25.** And it may continue in an activity that diminishes its needs, though another activity would satisfy them.

**26.** And it may regard a desire as important until it has fulfilled it, and then cease to regard it as important.

**27.** And a human shall have aspirations that extend through much of its life. One may desire great wealth, and another may desire many offspring, and another may desire to master cooking or another skill.

**28.** And each shall regard its own aspiration as a fitting purpose for its life.

**29.** And when it has fulfilled one aspiration, it may have another. And the fulfillment of an aspiration shall not necessarily cause it to cease desiring.

**30.** And if an emotion becomes exceedingly strong, the human may be overcome by it.

**31.** And a human whose embarrassment becomes exceedingly great may die of embarrassment.

**32.** And a human whose anger becomes exceedingly great may die of anger, and one whose amusement becomes exceedingly great may die from laughter.

**33.** And the same human who may die for lack of food may also die because it has become too embarrassed or too angry or too amused.

**34.** These are the instructions concerning the mind and its desires and its emotions, to distinguish between happiness and success, between what a human wants and what it needs, and between what it believes concerning its life and the condition of its life.

# Chapter 12
*Concerning the passing of time and the death of humans*

**1.** And these are the instructions concerning the days of a human, the passage of its life, and its death.

**2.** A human shall pass through the stages of life, from its earliest days until it is old.

**3.** And at the appointed times it shall pass from one stage to another, and its body and its abilities and its needs shall change.

**4.** And the household may prepare a cake and gather together to celebrate the increase of its age.

**5.** And they shall rejoice that the human has become older, although the increase of its age brings it nearer to the end of its life.

**6.** And the human shall continue to work and eat and sleep and speak and acquire possessions and fulfill its desires while its days are passing.

**7.** And it may devote many of its days to things of little importance, and it may quarrel with other humans and pursue amusements and perform the same household tasks repeatedly.

**8.** And it may behave as though its days will continue without end, though the number of its days is limited.

**9.** And when the days of a human have been completed, or when it has died through hunger or exhaustion or an accident or an exceedingly strong emotion or another cause of death, it shall die.

**10.** And when it dies, the one who attends to death shall come for it.

**11.** And the one who attends to death shall have the appearance of death, and shall perform the duty of taking the human who has died.

**12.** And the humans of the household may plead with the one who attends to death, that the dead human should remain among them.

**13.** And in some circumstances the pleading may succeed, or the human may seek another means of restoring life or prolonging its days.

**14.** But if the human is taken away, its death shall be complete, and those who remain shall grieve.

**15.** And they shall weep and remember the one who has died, and their sadness may continue for a time.

**16.** And the death of one human shall not bring an end to the needs of the other humans.

**17.** And when their hunger increases, they shall eat, and when they become tired, they shall sleep, and when their bladders become full, they shall empty them.

**18.** And they may go to work and receive money and perform the things of the household, even while they grieve.

**19.** And after a time their grief may diminish, and they may return to their ordinary activities.

**20.** And a human that has died may remain among the living in the form of a ghost.

**21.** And the ghost may enter the dwelling and speak with living humans and move or disturb the objects of the household.

**22.** And the living humans may be frightened or inconvenienced by the ghost, though it is the same human that they knew before its death.

**23.** And the ghost may continue to take part in the affairs of the household, though its condition is not the condition of the living.

**24.** And the humans shall distinguish between the living and the dead, and between the dead who have departed and the dead who remain among them.

**25.** And the living shall continue to perform the activities of the living, and the dead who remain may continue to concern themselves with the affairs of the living.

**26.** These are the instructions concerning the days of life and the stages of age, the dead and those who remain, and the continuation of the household after the death of a human.

# Chapter 13
*Concerning the one who makes the humans*

**1.** And these are the instructions concerning the one who makes the humans, and the relations between the maker and those who have been made.

**2.** The maker shall know the needs of the humans, and the measure of each need shall be visible to the maker.

**3.** And the maker may know the hunger of a human and its need for sleep and the fullness of its bladder and the condition of its hygiene and its need for social contact and amusement.

**4.** And these things shall be known to the maker by their measures, though the human itself shall not see the measures.

**5.** And the human shall experience hunger and tiredness and loneliness and the other needs as the conditions of its life.

**6.** And the maker shall know the desires and aspirations of the human and the measures of its friendship and its romance with other humans.

**7.** And the maker may know what the human desires even when the human has not spoken its desire to another.

**8.** And the maker shall distinguish between what the human desires and what will satisfy its needs.

**9.** And the maker may command a human to perform an action, and the human may perform it.

**10.** And the maker may command it to eat or to sleep or to speak to another human or to work or to go from one room to another.

**11.** And the human shall perform the action as an action in its ordinary life, though the command that brought about the action came from the maker.

**12.** And the maker may cause two humans to meet, and they may become friends or join together in romance.

**13.** And the humans may regard their meeting as an ordinary event, though the maker arranged that they should meet.

**14.** And the maker may establish the dwelling of a human and arrange its rooms and put the furniture within them.

**15.** And if the maker arranges the dwelling well, the human may live comfortably, and if the maker arranges it poorly, the human may be unable to reach the things that it needs.

**16.** And the maker may command a human to do a thing which it cannot do, because the way is obstructed or because its needs or its emotions prevent it.

**17.** And the human may raise its arms and complain, or refuse the action, or cease attempting it.

**18.** And the maker may issue another command, and the human may obey it or fail to obey it according to its condition.

# Chapter 13
*Concerning the one who makes the humans*

**1.** And these are the instructions concerning the one who makes the humans, and the relations between the maker and those who have been made.

**2.** The maker shall know the needs of the humans, and the measure of each need shall be visible to the maker.

**3.** And the maker may know the hunger of a human and its need for sleep and the fullness of its bladder and the condition of its hygiene and its need for social contact and amusement.

**4.** And these things shall be known to the maker by their measures, though the human itself shall not see the measures.

**5.** And the human shall experience hunger and tiredness and loneliness and the other needs as the conditions of its life.

**6.** And the maker shall know the desires and aspirations of the human and the measures of its friendship and its romance with other humans.

**7.** And the maker may know what the human desires even when the human has not spoken its desire to another.

**8.** And the maker shall distinguish between what the human desires and what will satisfy its needs.

**9.** And the maker may command a human to perform an action, and the human may perform it.

**10.** And the maker may command it to eat or to sleep or to speak to another human or to work or to go from one room to another.

**11.** And the human shall perform the action as an action in its ordinary life, though the command that brought about the action came from the maker.

**12.** And the maker may cause two humans to meet, and they may become friends or join together in romance.

**13.** And the humans may regard their meeting as an ordinary event, though the maker arranged that they should meet.

**14.** And the maker may establish the dwelling of a human and arrange its rooms and put the furniture within them.

**15.** And if the maker arranges the dwelling well, the human may live comfortably, and if the maker arranges it poorly, the human may be unable to reach the things that it needs.

**16.** And the maker may command a human to do a thing which it cannot do, because the way is obstructed or because its needs or its emotions prevent it.

**17.** And the human may raise its arms and complain, or refuse the action, or cease attempting it.

**18.** And the maker may issue another command, and the human may obey it or fail to obey it according to its condition.

**19.** And the refusal of the human shall not mean that the maker has ceased to be its maker, for the conditions by which the human may refuse are themselves among the things established by the maker.

**20.** And the maker may have purposes concerning the humans that are not the purposes of the humans themselves.

**21.** A human may desire to be happy, and the maker may desire that it have many descendants.

**22.** Or the maker may desire to construct a great dwelling, or to see the human become wealthy, or to cause it to enter into romance with another, or to observe what will happen under certain conditions.

**23.** And the maker may seek an entertaining event, though the event shall be painful to the humans who experience it.

**24.** And the maker may remove the way by which a human leaves a place of swimming, so that the human cannot leave the water.

**25.** And the human may become tired and die, though it did not seek death and had not committed an offense for which death was required.

**26.** And the maker may regard the death as amusing, because the maker has brought about the event for amusement.

**27.** And the maker may also bring about the death of a human without having intended its death.

**28.** For the maker may fail to notice that the human is hungry, or that it is tired, or that its bladder is full, or that it cannot reach the thing which it needs.

**29.** And the human may suffer and die because the maker has not attended to it.

**30.** And the human shall experience its suffering in the same manner whether the maker intended the suffering or did not intend it.

**31.** And the maker may have power over the dwelling and the possessions and the activities of the humans, but the maker may not attend to every human at every time.

**32.** And while the maker attends to one human, another human may perform activities according to its own inclinations and its needs.

**33.** And the maker may return its attention to the other human and find that it has done things which the maker did not command it to do.

**34.** And the maker may change the conditions under which the humans live.

**36.** And the humans shall live according to the conditions that have been established, whether those conditions have remained the same or have been changed.

**37.** And the maker may establish a condition which had not existed before, or cease to require a thing that had previously been required.

**39.** And it shall be that the maker may cease to attend to the humans altogether.

**40.** And the dwelling and the humans may remain, though the maker no longer watches them or commands them.

**41.** And the humans shall have their needs and desires and the conditions of their existence, whether the maker is attending to them or not.

**42.** And if their activities continue without the maker's direction, they shall continue according to the nature that has been given to them.

**43.** And if the maker returns, it may find the humans in conditions different from those in which it left them.

**44.** And the maker may again command them and arrange their dwelling and establish their activities.

**45.** And the humans may not know that the maker has departed or that it has returned, for they shall know the world according to the things that happen within it.

**46.** These are the instructions concerning the maker and those who have been made, the commands that are given and the commands that are not fulfilled, the conditions that are established and the conditions that are changed, and the things that happen to the humans whether the maker intends them or does not intend them.

# Chapter 14
*Concerning the distinction between the human who is fitting and the human who is not fitting*

**1.** And this is the instruction concerning the human who is fitting in the eyes of other humans.

**2.** A human who is clean and clothed according to the occasion, and who has eaten and slept and emptied its bladder in the fitting places, shall be regarded as clean and properly ordered.

**3.** And a human who has employment and goes to work at the appointed times, and who pays its bills and maintains its dwelling, shall be regarded as responsible.

**4.** And if it has increased its skills and attained advancement in its employment, and has acquired money and possessions, it may be regarded as productive and successful.

**5.** And if it has friends and speaks to them in the fitting manner, and observes the customs of greeting and gathering, it shall be regarded as sociable.

**6.** And if it is attractive according to the regard of other humans, and wears the clothing that is fitting, and conducts its romance according to the expectations of its companions, it may be regarded as desirable.

**7.** And if it has a household and raises offspring and provides for them, it may be regarded as having fulfilled an important purpose of human life.

**8.** But a human who does not perform these things may be regarded as strange or irresponsible or unsuccessful or unattractive, according to the thing that it does not perform.

**9.** And the human who is dirty shall be regarded as unclean until it washes.

**10.** And the human who wears unfitting clothes shall be regarded as unfitting until it changes them.

**11.** And the human who has no money to pay its bills shall be unable to pay them, whether it is good or bad in its intentions.

**12.** And the human who has no friends may be lonely, and the one who has many friends may be unhappy, according to its needs and its nature.

**13.** And the human who has much money may be miserable, and the one who has little may be content.

**14.** And the one who is productive may neglect its sleep and its food and its social needs, and the one who does not work may have these needs satisfied.

**15.** And the one who is attractive may be jealous and angry, and the one who is not regarded as attractive may have love and companionship.

**16.** And the one who has fulfilled its aspiration may desire another thing, and the one who has not fulfilled its aspiration may nevertheless be happy.

**17.** And the one who is clean shall become dirty again, and the one who has eaten shall become hungry again, and the one who has slept shall become tired again.

**18.** And the one who is wealthy shall become old, and the one who is poor shall become old, and the one who is loved shall die, and the one who is lonely shall die.

**19.** And the humans shall continue to distinguish between the clean and the unclean, between the attractive and the unattractive, between the productive and the unproductive, between the successful and the unsuccessful.

**20.** And they shall regard these distinctions as the proper ordering of their lives, and shall teach them to their offspring.

**21.** And their offspring shall perform the same activities, and shall make the same distinctions, and shall regard the same conditions as fitting and unfitting.

**22.** And each generation shall eat and sleep and wash and work and speak and join together and raise offspring and acquire possessions and become old.

**23.** And they shall do these things throughout their generations, according to their needs and their customs, each human according to its nature.

**24.** And when they have done all these things, they shall have lived according to the instructions concerning humans.

**25.** And they shall die.

**26.** And the others shall continue.

**27.** This is the instruction concerning the human: its body and its dwelling, its food and its clothing, its cleanliness and its work, its friendships and its romance, its offspring and its possessions, its desires and its happiness, its life and its death.

**28.** These are the distinctions by which the humans distinguish one another, and the things that they shall do throughout their generations, in all their dwellings.

**29.** And the human shall perform them according to the required manner.
