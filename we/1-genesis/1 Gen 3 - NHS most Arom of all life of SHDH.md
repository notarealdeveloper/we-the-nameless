
Arom. (ערום)
- Cunning, slick, intelligent.

SHDH. (שדה)
- Sadé. (שָׂדֶה). Field, wide space for crops.
- Shedá. (שֵׁדָה). Demon, she-devil. (masculine counterpart שֵׁד)

SHD. (שד)
- Shad. (שֵׁד). Breast.
- Shed. (שֵׁד). Demon, devil, ghost, phantom, spirit, shade.

El. (אל)
- Generic term meaning "god."
- Proper name for the highest god of the Canaanites.
- A term referring to any god, either in mono- or polytheistic contexts.

Shaddai. (שדי)
- Shaddáy. (שַׁדַּי)
  1. Meaning unknown. Possibly from שָׁד (shad, "breast") + י (-áy), indicating a connection between God and nourishment.
  2. Epithet for God, often translated as Almighty.
- Shedi. (שֵׁדִי). Demonic, mischievous, deceitful, malicious.

El Shaddai. (אל שדי)
- Epithet of Yahweh, usually translated as "God Almighty", though the exact meaning of "Shaddai" remains uncertain.

Arom. (עירם)
- Erom. (עֵירֹם). Defective spelling of Eróm (עירום).
- Eróm. (עירום / עֵירֹם). Naked, nudity.
   From âram, aw-ram' (עָרַם); a primitive root; properly, to be (or make) bare; but used only in the derivative sense (through the idea perhaps of smoothness) to be cunning (usually in a bad sense): — very, beware, take crafty (counsel), be prudent, deal subtilly.

Translator's Note:
- The transliterated terms "Arom" and "Erom" differ only in a letter which in the original Hebrew they share. (i.e., ערום and עירם respectively).
- In the presence of multiple sources employing multiple conflicting and undocumented transliterations, especially in a work known for its use of defective spellings and a heavy use of puns, in a source language with no vowel markers, well... you'll just have to do your best to figure out what the original Author or Authors might have been up to.
