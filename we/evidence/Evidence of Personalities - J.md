| Sodom and Gibeah Parallel | Genesis 19; Judges 19 | Mob violence, threatened guests, abused women, destruction of community. | J-style storytelling uses mirrored atrocities to critique society. |

| Satire of Empire | Babel; Egypt; Sodom | Large urban civilizations repeatedly humiliated or destroyed. | J deeply suspicious of imperial arrogance. |
| Satire of Kingship | Genesis patterns; Samuel echoes | Dynasties arise from sex scandals, lies, younger sons, and outsider women. | J undermines simplistic royal propaganda. |
| Davidic Ambivalence | Judah/Davidic themes | Judah elevated but also repeatedly disgraced. | J both legitimizes and critiques monarchy. |
| Solomon Resonance | Gihon theory; Eden imagery | Solomon’s court may frame J’s literary endpoint. | J likely tied to royal court culture while remaining ironic about it. |
