| Egyptian Names   | Exodus 2:1–10; Numbers 26:57–61; 1 Samuel 2:27–36   | Moses, Phinehas, Hophni, Hur, Merari, Mushi, Pashhur are commonly cited as Egyptian or Egyptian-looking names, and they cluster in Levite/priestly circles. | If only one Israelite group preserves Egyptian names, that group is the best candidate for having actually been in Egypt.      |
| Moses Levite     | Exodus 2:1                                          | “A man from the house of Levi went and took a daughter of Levi.” Moses is explicitly Levite from birth.                                                     | The central Exodus figure is specifically Levite, not generic Israelite.                                                       |
| No Land          | Deuteronomy 18:1–2; Numbers 18:20–24; Joshua 13:14  | Levites receive no normal tribal territory: “Yahweh is their inheritance.”                                                                                  | A landless tribe looks like an attached priestly group incorporated into Israel later.                                         |
| Attached Name    | Genesis 29:34                                       | Leah says: “Now my husband will become attached (ילוה) to me,” therefore his name was called Levi.                                                          | The tribal name itself may imply attachment or joining onto another group.                                                     |
| Missing Tribe    | Judges 5                                            | Levi is absent from the Song of Deborah’s tribal list.                                                                                                      | If Judges 5 preserves an early Israelite confederation, Levi may not yet have been part of it.                                 |
| No Israel        | Exodus 15                                           | The archaic Song of the Sea speaks of Yahweh redeeming “the people,” not clearly “Israel” in the poetic core itself.                                        | Could preserve memory of a smaller Exodus group before expansion into a national story.                                        |
| Yahweh Name      | Exodus 3:1–15; Exodus 6:2–3                         | Moses learns the divine name at Horeb/Midian; Yahweh says the patriarchs did not know that name.                                                            | Yahweh revelation is tied to Moses, Midian, Sinai, and Egypt-linked traditions rather than old Canaanite patriarchal religion. |
| Exodus Focus     | Exodus–Numbers; Deuteronomy                         | Levite/priestly materials dwell intensely on Exodus, Sinai, covenant, tabernacle, slavery, and wilderness law.                                              | The group preserving the densest Exodus memory may be the group that experienced it.                                           |
| Battle Tent      | Ramesses II battle camp comparisons                 | The tabernacle layout has been compared to Egyptian royal military tent structures.                                                                         | Egyptian-style sacred tent imagery appears specifically in Levite/priestly traditions.                                         |
| Sacred Bark      | Exodus 25–31; Numbers 10                            | The ark resembles Egyptian portable divine bark shrines carried on poles.                                                                                   | Egyptian cultic symbolism clusters around Levite ritual objects.                                                               |
| Circumcision     | Genesis 17; Exodus 4:24–26; Joshua 5:2–9            | Circumcision is emphasized in priestly covenant traditions and Moses narratives; Egypt also practiced circumcision.                                         | Egyptian-style body ritual enters Israel through Levite-associated material.                                                   |
| Levite Priests   | Deuteronomy 18:1–8                                  | “The Levitical priests, the whole tribe of Levi…”                                                                                                           | A whole priestly tribe attached to Israel is unusual and consistent with a migrant cultic class.                               |
| Anti-Baal        | Exodus 32; Numbers 25; Deuteronomy 13               | Levite/Yahwist traditions strongly attack calf worship, Baal worship, and local cults.                                                                      | A Yahweh group entering Canaan would naturally polemicize against existing Canaanite religion.                                 |
| Southern Yahweh  | Soleb inscription; Amarah-West inscriptions         | Egyptian inscriptions mention “Shasu of Yhw” in the southern wilderness region.                                                                             | Supports Yahweh originating among southern nomadic groups linked to Moses/Midian traditions.                                   |
| Small Exodus     | Highland settlement surveys; Iron I archaeology     | Archaeology does not support a mass migration of all Israel from Egypt, but a smaller migrating subgroup is plausible.                                      | A Levite-only Exodus explains how Egyptian traditions entered a mostly indigenous highland population.                         |
| Alien Ethics     | Exodus 22:21; Deuteronomy 10:19; Leviticus 19:33–34 | “You know the life of the stranger, because you were strangers in the land of Egypt.”                                                                       | A group with remembered oppression in Egypt would preserve unusually strong slave/alien ethics.                                |
| Egyptian Cluster | Comparative synthesis of Egyptian parallels         | Egyptian names, circumcision, ark symbolism, priestly tents, and Moses traditions all cluster around Levites.                                               | The clustering matters more than any single item alone.                                                                        |
