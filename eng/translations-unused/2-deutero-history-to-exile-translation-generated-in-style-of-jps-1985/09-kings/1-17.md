I Kings 17:1	Elijah the Tishbite, an inhabitant of Gilead, said to Ahab, “As the LORD lives, the God of Israel whom I serve, there will be no dew or rain except at my bidding.”
I Kings 17:2	The word of the LORD came to him:
I Kings 17:3	“Leave this place; turn eastward and go into hiding by the Wadi Cherith, which is east of the Jordan.
I Kings 17:4	You will drink from the wadi, and I have commanded the ravens to feed you there.”
I Kings 17:5	He proceeded to do as the LORD had bidden: he went, and he stayed by the Wadi Cherith, which is east of the Jordan.
I Kings 17:6	The ravens brought him bread and meat every morning and every evening, and he drank from the wadi.
I Kings 17:7	After some time the wadi dried up, because there was no rain in the land.
I Kings 17:8	And the word of the LORD came to him:
I Kings 17:9	“Go at once to Zarephath of Sidon, and stay there; I have designated a widow there to feed you.”
I Kings 17:10	So he went at once to Zarephath. When he came to the entrance of the town, a widow was there gathering wood. He called out to her, “Please bring me a little water in your pitcher, and let me drink.”
I Kings 17:11	As she went to fetch it, he called out to her, “Please bring along a piece of bread for me.”
I Kings 17:12	“As the LORD your God lives,” she replied, “I have nothing baked, nothing but a handful of flour in a jar and a little oil in a jug. I am just gathering a couple of sticks, so that I can go home and prepare it for me and my son; we shall eat it and then we shall die.”
I Kings 17:13	“Don’t be afraid,” said Elijah to her. “Go and do as you have said; but first make me a small cake from what you have there, and bring it out to me; then make some for yourself and your son.
I Kings 17:14	For thus said the LORD, the God of Israel: The jar of flour shall not give out and the jug of oil shall not fail until the day that the LORD sends rain upon the ground.”
I Kings 17:15	She went and did as Elijah had spoken, and she and he and her household had food for a long time.
I Kings 17:16	The jar of flour did not give out, nor did the jug of oil fail, just as the LORD had spoken through Elijah.
I Kings 17:17	After a while, the son of the mistress of the house fell sick, and his illness grew worse, until he had no breath left in him.
I Kings 17:18	She said to Elijah, “What harm have I done you, O man of God, that you should come here to recall my sin and cause the death of my son?”
I Kings 17:19	“Give me the boy,” he said to her; and taking him from her arms, he carried him to the upper chamber where he was staying, and laid him down on his own bed.
I Kings 17:20	He cried out to the LORD and said, “O LORD my God, will You bring calamity upon this widow whose guest I am, and let her son die?”
I Kings 17:21	Then he stretched out over the child three times, and cried out to the LORD, saying, “O LORD my God, let this child’s life return to his body!”
I Kings 17:22	The LORD heard Elijah’s plea; the child’s life returned to his body, and he revived.
I Kings 17:23	Elijah picked up the child and brought him down from the upper room into the main room, and gave him to his mother. “See,” said Elijah, “your son is alive.”
I Kings 17:24	And the woman answered Elijah, “Now I know that you are a man of God and that the word of the LORD is truly in your mouth.”
