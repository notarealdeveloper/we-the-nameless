------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My entire being despises my life.

I would prepare a complaint on my behalf,

I would speak in the bitterness of my being;


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I would say to Eloah: “Do not condemn me!

Let me know of what you accuse me!

---

Footnote 10:2. Job assumes that the deity holds him guilty of some transgressions.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Does it do you any good to do wrong?

To reject the effort of your hands?

While you shine favor on the schemes of the wicked?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:4–5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Have you eyes that are of flesh?

Do you see as a mortal sees?

Are your days like the days of a mortal?

Are your years like those of a man?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:6–7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When you go looking for my crime,

And investigate my sin,

You know very well I am not guilty—

But no one can rescue from your hand!

---

Footnote 10:6–7. Job typically converts a positive statement of God’s power from Deuteronomy 32:39 (“no one can rescue from my hand”) to a negative one.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:8–9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Your hands formed me and made me,

Put me together—then destroyed me!

Mind now, it is you who made me like clay,

And will return me to the dust!

---

Footnote 10:8–9. The verb in context also connotes “pain” (see 9:28), from the same root.

Footnote 10:8–9. Literally, “together and about.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:10–11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Have you not poured me out like milk,

And jelled me like cheese?

Clothed me in skin and flesh,

And woven me in bones and sinews?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

You have handled me with life and devotion,

And your providence has protected my spirit.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:13–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But certain things you’ve kept hidden in your heart;

I know this is what you think:

If I commit a sin, you’ll be watching me;

You’ll not declare me clean of my crime.

---

Footnote 10:13–14. Literally, “with you.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I am guilty—then woe be to me!

But even in the right, I could not hold up my head.

Be sated with (my) disgrace,

And regard my affliction!

---

Footnote 10:15. Or “being sated with disgrace, and experiencing affliction.” Compare the expression “to see (experience) affliction,” known from Lamentations 3:1 (and compare “we have seen evil/disaster” in Psalm 90:15). Job here inverts the conventional perspective by addressing the deity.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:16–17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

And if (my head) were to loom, you would hunt me down like a lion;

You would work wonders against me over and over!

You would renew your hostilities toward me,

As your anger grows great against me,

Arraying reinforcements against me.

---

Footnote 10:16–17. As in wreaking plagues (see Deuteronomy 28:59); compare *pela’im* “appalling acts” in Lamentation 1:9.

Footnote 10:16–17. Compare Arabic *‘adawa*.

Footnote 10:16–17. More literally, “replacements and army are with me.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:18–19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

So why did you take me out of the womb?

Would I had died, with no eye seeing me!

Would I had been as though I had not been;

Would I had been carried from womb to tomb.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 10:20–22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Very few are the days of my lifespan—

Look away from me, so I may have a respite.

Before I go and do not return,

To a land of darkness and deep-shade;

A land whose brightness is like pitch-black,

Deep-shade and disorder;

That shines like pitch-black.”

---

Footnote 10:20–22. For the written form (*Ketib*) *yḥdl*, which is read *wḥdl* in the Masoretic tradition, read *ḥeldi* “my lifespan,” which is found in parallelism with “my days” in Psalm 39:6; compare the ancient Greek and Syriac translations.

Footnote 10:20–22. Reading *she‘eh* for *yashit*, “he puts,” which does not suit the context. Job uses the same phrase in 7:19; and for the combination of *sha‘ah* (*Hiph‘il*) and *hiblig* “have a respite,” compare Psalm 39:14.

Footnote 10:20–22. The realm of the dead is depicted, as in chapter 3, as the reverse of the land of the living—a gloomy abode where daylight is darkness. So already Saadia Gaon; and see Ginsberg, “Unrecognized Allusion.”
