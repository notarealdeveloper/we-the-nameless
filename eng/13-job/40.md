------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke YHWH to Job and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Should one who censures charge Shaddai?

Should Eloah answer an accuser?

---

Footnote 40:2. The form *yissor* is a noun formed from *yisser* “to discipline” on the pattern of *gibbor* “brave one.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Job to YHWH and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:4–5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Lacking respect, how can I answer you?

My hand I place over my mouth.

I have spoken once and I will not repeat;

Twice—and I will (speak) no more.

---

Footnote 40:4–5. In view of the next line, read *’eshneh* (see 29:22; so several scholars) for *’e‘eneh* “I will (not) answer.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke YHWH to Job from the windstorm, and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Bind up your loins like a man!

I will ask you—and you help me know!


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Will you go so far as to breach my justice?

Accuse me of wrong so that you’re in the right?

---

Footnote 40:8. For the irony of God making this accusation, see Job 9:30–31.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:9–13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If you’ve an arm (as strong as) El’s,

And if your voice can thunder like his,

Bedeck yourself with pride and stature,

Clothe yourself with glory and grandeur!

Scatter your raging anger!

Look for the proud and bring him low;

Look for the proud and bend him down!

And crush the wicked where they stand!

Cover them all in the dust;

In the dust wrap their faces!

---

Footnote 40:9–13. Literally, “the place of covering.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Then I myself will praise you,

As your right hand brings you triumph.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Behold now Behemoth,

Which like you I created!

Like large cattle he eats grass.

---

Footnote 40:15. This line belongs to verse 15 in the received text.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:16–18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Behold now the strength in his loins!

The power in his belly’s muscles!

He drops his “tail” like a cedar (trunk);

The sinews of his testes intertwine.

His limbs are pipes of bronze,

His bones like an iron rod.

---

Footnote 40:16–18. Commonly derived from Arabic *khafada* “to lower.”

Footnote 40:16–18. A euphemism for his phallus, which is retracted when inactive.

Footnote 40:16–18. Aramaic.

Footnote 40:16–18. Aramaic.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:19–20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He is the first of El’s ways;

Only his Maker can prod him with a sword.

For the mountains bear him produce,

Where the wild animals play.

---

Footnote 40:19–20. An ironic allusion to Proverbs 8:22, where the “first of” the deity’s “ways” is wisdom!

Footnote 40:19–20. For the motif of bringing tribute to a king in this fashion, see Psalm 72:3.

Footnote 40:19–20. The term for “produce” (ordinarily *yebul*)—*bul*—denotes wild animals in Akkadian, suggesting a second meaning for the verse: “Mountain creatures raise (their voices in song) to him; all the wild animals jubilate there” (H. R. Cohen).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:21–22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He lies down under the thornbush,

Under cover of reeds and swamp.

Thornbushes shade him with a thicket;

River willows surround him.

---

Footnote 40:21–22. Literally, “the thornbushes form a thicket above him as shade.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If the river crashes (against him), he will not be alarmed.

He would feel secure were the Jordan to rush in his mouth!


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Can one take him as he eyes one?

Can his nose be pierced by barbs?

---

Footnote 40:24. Reading *bi-qimmosim* (see Isaiah 34:13) for *bi-moqeshim* “with traps,” which is inapt.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:25–26

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Can you pull the Leviathan with a fish-hook?

Can you bind his tongue with a rope?

Can you place a bulrush ’round its snout,

And pierce its jaw with a hook?

---

Footnote 40:25–26. A word known in Samaritan Aramaic; read perhaps *tishqa‘* for *tashqia‘* “press down.”

Footnote 40:25–26. Tie it closed; compare the Qumran Aramaic translation.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:27–28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Will he make many pleadings to you?

To you will he speak gently?

Will he make a pact with you?

Will he be your slave forever?

---

Footnote 40:27–28. Note the irony in contrasting Eliphaz’s promise in 5:23.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:29

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Can you toy with him like a bird?

Tie him up (to amuse) your maidens?

---

Footnote 40:29. Compare Psalm 104:26.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:30

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Will traders barter over him?

Will they divide him among merchants?

---

Footnote 40:30. A unique term, a person who “associates”; cognate *ebarūtu(m)* in Old Assyrian denotes an association of merchants.

Footnote 40:30. A regular meaning of the term “Canaanite.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 40:31–32

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Can you cover his hide with prongs,

And his head with a fish harpoon?

Just lay your hand upon him!—

That’s the last you will think of battle!

---

Footnote 40:31–32. The word understood from context to be some sort of harpoon echoes the word for “thornbush” in verses 21–22 above.
