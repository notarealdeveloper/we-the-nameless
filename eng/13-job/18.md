------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Bildad the Shuhite and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

“How long will you (men) put an end to discourse?

Once you understand, then we can speak.”

---

Footnote 18:2. Bildad begins by mocking the opening of Job’s last discourse and then goes on to charge him with being crazed (verse 4). That verse 2 is a paraphrase of Job is clear from the use of the plural—a clear sign that the companions are being addressed. The phrase (literally) “ends to words (*millin*)” echoes Job’s pseudo-quotation of the friends in 16:3, “end to words (*dibrei*) of wind.”

Footnote 18:2. Job had told his friends to be silent and just listen to him (13:13).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:3–4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why do you think of us as animals?

Why do we seem to you brutish?

He who tears himself apart in his anger—

For you will the earth be made desolate?

(For you) a mountain moved from its place?

---

Footnote 18:3–4. Reading the singular rather than plural suffix together with the ancient Greek translation.

Footnote 18:3–4. Reading *netammonu* (compare *timtem* in later Hebrew) for *nitminu*, which seems to have been taken by the Masorah as a deviant form of “we are tainted.”

Footnote 18:3–4. An insulting reference to Job, throwing Job’s use of the phrase “(God’s) anger raged” (16:9) back at him, but in a deconstructed form (separating the terms of the idiom and giving them a literal sense). Accusing Job of unrestrained anger pegs him as a fool (see 5:2).

Footnote 18:3–4. Literally, “abandoned”; compare the curse in Leviticus 26:43.

Footnote 18:3–4. An echo of Job in 14:18.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:5–6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The light of the wicked really does wane,

And the flame of his fire fails to glow;

The light goes dark in his habitation,

And his lamp goes out on him.

---

Footnote 18:5–6. An elaboration of the conventional wisdom of Proverbs 13:9. The extinction of the hearth fire can suggest the extinction of progeny; see 2 Samuel 14:7.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

His pernicious steps are shortened;

His schemes make him stumble.

---

Footnote 18:7. Ironically also “his strong steps.”

Footnote 18:7. The wicked’s schemes are thwarted—an echo of Job in 17:11, who complains that his plans have been frustrated.

Footnote 18:7. Reading *wa-takshilehu* for *wa-tashlikehu* “throws him down” on the basis of Proverbs 4:12, which is alluded to here.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:8–10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For he is caught by his feet in a net!

Wherever he walks there’s a mesh!

His heel is seized by a trap;

He is held fast by a snare.

A trip-cord is buried for him in the ground,

And a catch for him by the path.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Horrors terrify him all about;

Causing his legs to tremble.

---

Footnote 18:11. Bildad’s enumeration of disasters befalling the wicked recalls Eliphaz’s itemization of the seven disasters from which the righteous will be saved (5:19–22).

Footnote 18:11. Reading *we-hiplitsuhu* for *we-hepitsuhu* “they scattered him,” which is inapt in context. The phrase is drawn from Isaiah 21:4; compare Job 9:6; 21:6.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hunger exhausts his strength,

And disaster awaits his stumble!

---

Footnote 18:12. Reading *yekalleh ra‘ab* for *yehi ra‘eb* “let (his strength) be hungry”; see Genesis 41:30 where the verb *killah* is predicated of *ra‘ab* in the phrase “and the famine will destroy the land.”

Footnote 18:12. Compare Psalm 38:18. A different Hebrew term from that rendered “stumble” in verse 7 above.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Someone consumes the limbs of his skin,

Death’s Firstborn consumes his limbs.

---

Footnote 18:13. “Skin” is here a metonym for the body. The subject of the consumption is not specified here—it will be specified in the next clause. There may be a superstitious reluctance to name the demonic personality.

Footnote 18:13. “Death” is here personified—the Canaanite god Mot (Death), whose image as ravenously devouring the living is found in Habakkuk 2:5. The “firstborn” of Mot is apparently a name for a dreadful disease—like the skin disease from which Job suffers. In Biblical Hebrew “death” sometimes denotes a plague or disease (see Job 27:15).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He is cut off from his secure habitation;

And he’s led to the King of Horrors.

---

Footnote 18:14. The wicked.

Footnote 18:14. “His tent.” Contrast the proverbial fate of the righteous in 5:24 and 12:6.

Footnote 18:14. Literally, “it leads him”; the antecedent would seem to be “his schemes” in verse 7, which in Hebrew is a feminine singular like the verb in this clause (compare Ibn Ezra). The two verses are linked by the noun “step” there and its cognate verb “to lead to,” used here.

Footnote 18:14. Apparently another name for Death. The reference is to personified death or a plague demon, not necessarily the Canaanite god Mot, who is never called “king.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Lilith will dwell in his habitation;

Sulfur’ll be strewn over his estate.

---

Footnote 18:15. Reading *lilit* for *MBLYLW*, which is read confusedly, as one or two words, in different Masoretic traditions; the Greek reads “in his night.” A feminine singular subject is required, and the ancient female demon Lilith is found together with two other terms from our verse—“sulfur” and “estate”—in the curse against Edom in Isaiah 34 (see verses 9, 13, 14; compare Driver-Gray).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Below, his roots will dry out;

And above, his foliage will wither.

---

Footnote 18:16. That is, his lineage will be wiped out. Compare Amos 2:9. Bildad revisits kindred images in Job 8:11–13 and 15:32–34.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

His reputation disappears from the land,

And his good name is gone from the countryside.

---

Footnote 18:17. Job will complain of the damage his reputation suffered within his community in chapter 29.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He is driven from light into darkness,

Banished from th’inhabited world.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He has no son or scion among his people;

Nor any remnant where he resides.

---

Footnote 18:19. Compare Isaiah 14:22. Contrast Eliphaz’s forecast of progeny for Job in 5.25.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Westerners are appalled at his day-of-doom,

And Easterners are seized with alarm.

---

Footnote 18:20. For the correct understanding of the terms for “Westerners” and “Easterners,” see 23:8; also Joel 2:20; Zechariah 14:8.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 18:21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yes, these are the dwellings of the depraved,

The place of one who knows not El.

---

Footnote 18:21. Some interpreters understand this verse to be spoken by the Westerners and Easterners of verse 20, but this pithy conclusion is of a piece with Bildad’s prior one in 8:22; and compare the conclusion of Zophar’s discourse in 20:29.
