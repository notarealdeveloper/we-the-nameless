------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 33:1–4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But you, Job, pray hear my words;

To all that I speak give your ear!

You see I have opened my mouth;

My tongue speaks from inside my palate.

All that I say is (from) my upright heart,

The speech of my lips—honest discourse.

The spirit of El has created me;

The breath of Shaddai invigorates me.

---

Footnote 33:1–4. “Palate” is apparently a metonym for “mouth.”

Footnote 33:1–4. Literally, “The discourse of my lips they speak sincerely” (compare Kahana).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 33:5–7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If you are able—answer me!

Set yourself before me—make your case!

Before El I am just like you;

I too have been pinched from clay.

See, my awesome mien will not terrify you;

My pressure will not feel heavy to you.

---

Footnote 33:5–7. “To pinch off clay” is a figure for creating a human in the ancient Semitic world; compare 10:9.

Footnote 33:5–7. Elihu alludes to Job’s reasons for fearing a confrontation with the deity in 13:21. The term for “force, pressure” here puns on “your hand” there.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 33:8–11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yet you have said in my ears;

These words in your voice I have heard:

“Pure am I, without transgression;

I am innocent, I’ve done no crime!

But he finds pretexts against me;

He regards me as his enemy.

He’s marked my feet with lime;

Watching my every step.”

---

Footnote 33:8–11. Elihu paraphrases Zophar’s pseudo-quotation of Job in 11:4.

Footnote 33:8–11. God.

Footnote 33:8–11. Reading *to’anot* for *tenu’ot* “depredations” (with the ancient Syriac, Rashi, and others); compare for example Judges 14:4; 2 Kings 5:7.

Footnote 33:8–11. Quoting Job in 13:24; compare 19:11.

Footnote 33:8–11. Quoting Job in 13:27.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 33:12–13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Where you are not right I will answer you;

For Eloah is greater than mortals.

Why do you complain against him,

That he’ll answer no one’s charges?

---

Footnote 33:12–13. Literally, “will not answer his (a human’s) words (legal charges).”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 33:14–17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For one time El will speak (and be heard),

But a second time he will not see it.

In a dream, a vision in the night,

When slumber falls upon people,

When they’re lying down to sleep,

Then does he open people’s ears,

Frightening them with (his) discipline;

To turn one away from one’s misdeeds

And remove the pride from a man.

---

Footnote 33:14–17. A human.

Footnote 33:14–17. Will not realize it. The unrecognized revelation is in a nocturnal vision. Elihu turns Job’s complaint (4:12–21) into a positive.

Footnote 33:14–17. A quotation from the report (of Job) in 4:13.

Footnote 33:14–17. Literally, “when sleep falls upon the place-of-lying-down.”

Footnote 33:14–17. A biblical expression for revealing a secret.

Footnote 33:14–17. Reading *yeḥittem* for *yaḥtom* “he seals.”

Footnote 33:14–17. The means by which he will educate them. For the term see also 36:10 and compare 5:17 (Eliphaz).

Footnote 33:14–17. Literally, “cover.”

Footnote 33:14–17. Aramaic; compare Daniel 4:34.

## Job 33:18–22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He spares his life from the pit,

His vigor from crossing the channel.

For he’s reproved when he’s lying in pain,

And his limbs in a constant tremor.

His throat treats his bread as rotten;

His gullet most delectable food.

His flesh wastes away out of sight;

His never seen bones are laid bare.

His life-force approaches the pit,

And his vigor the locus of death.

---

Footnote 33:18–22. The grave.

Footnote 33:18–22. See Ezekiel 31:5; Nehemiah 3:15. Crossing the channel is apparently an image of dying, as in Mesopotamia (the River Hubur) and Greece (the River Styx). See also 36:12.

Footnote 33:18–22. For *rob* “shivers, tremor,” see at 4:3.

Footnote 33:18–22. His gullet treats the most desirable food as rotten.

Footnote 33:18–22. Compare 19:20.

Footnote 33:18–22. One should probably vocalize *memotim* as in Jeremiah 16:4 and Ezekiel 28:8 (Held, “Pits and Pitfalls”).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 33:23–24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But if he has a guardian-angel,

Just one champion out of a thousand,

To declare a person upright,

He will appeal (to El), saying:

“I will redeem him from entering the pit;

I have found the ransom funds.”

---

Footnote 33:23–24. An “intercessor.”

Footnote 33:23–24. The angel.

Footnote 33:23–24. Reading *’epdehu* for the peculiar form *peda‘ehu* “redeem him!” with an *ayin* instead of an *aleph*. For the sequence and verb form, see Psalm 49:8.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 33:25–26

------------------------------------------------------------------------

His flesh then grows plumper than a child’s;

It returns to the days of his youth.

He’ll entreat Eloah, who’ll accept him;

Show him in gladness his face;

Return the mortal his rightful status.

---

Footnote 33:25–26. The *r* at the head of the verb is an apparent dittograph from the preceding word.

Footnote 33:25–26. For the sense of “gladness,” see the parallel to *simḥa* in 8:21.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 33:27–28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He’ll return then to the men and announce:

“I have sinned! I’ve accused the Upright of crime;

And it was not worth my while.

He’s redeemed me from entering the pit;

So my life can bask in the light.”

---

Footnote 33:27–28. Reading *yashub* for *yashor*, an anomalous form of “he’ll sing”—a likely contamination from *yashar* “upright” in the next line. For the usage, compare Hosea 5:15.

Footnote 33:27–28. To Job, these are his three companions, referred to above as the three “men” (32:1, 5).

Footnote 33:27–28. An epithet of the deity; see Deuteronomy 32:4; Psalms 25:8; 92:16; compare Psalm 119:137. The same term used to indicate Job’s character: “straight (of path)” (1:1, 8; 2:3).

Footnote 33:27–28. Reading the *Ketib* (written traditional text) here and in the following line.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 33:29–30

------------------------------------------------------------------------

These things El performs

Two or three times for a man:

Returning his life from the pit,

To bask in the light of the living.

---

Footnote 33:29–30. In the course of a life.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 33:31–33

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pay heed now, Job, hear me!

Keep silent now and I will speak!

If you have words, then answer me!

Speak, for I want to acquit you!

But if you have none, then hear me!

Keep silent and I’ll teach you wisdom!

---

Footnote 33:31–33. Elihu parodies Job’s address to his companions in 13:13.
