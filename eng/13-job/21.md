------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Job and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Listen oh listen to my words,

And let them be your (words of) consolation!

Bear with me while I speak;

Then after I speak, you can blather.

---

Footnote 21:2–3. Reading *ta‘legu* (adding the plural suffix), a metathesis of *tal‘igu* “you mock,” which ordinarily takes the preposition *le*-with object; see above at 11:3.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Is this complaint of mine with a human?

Should I not lose patience over an evil?

---

Footnote 21:4. Literally, “should my spirit not be short,” an idiom.

Footnote 21:4. Reading *meroa‘* “on account of evil” for *maddua‘* “why?,” which cannot follow the interrogative *’im*. The letters *d* and *r* are nearly identical.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Face me and be still,

And set your hands over your mouths!

---

Footnote 21:5. So the Aramaic translation. The verb *sh-m-m* connotes desolation; it puns on “set, put” (*simu*) in the next line.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

When I call (the evil) to mind, I am shaken;

My flesh has been seized by trembling.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why do the wicked live on and live well,

Grow old and gain in power and wealth?

---

Footnote 21:7. Thriving, prospering is a nuance of “live” known in Old Aramaic and compare Psalms 22:27; 69:33.

Footnote 21:7. And strong. The nuance of “old” is particularly found in Aramaic.

Footnote 21:7. Hebrew *ḥayil* denotes “wealth” in Zophar’s speech (20:15, 18, 21) but always connotes “strength” as well.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:8–9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Their seed is steadfastly before them,

Their offspring ever before their eyes.

Their homesteads are at peace without dread,

With no rod of Eloah’s (anger) over them.

---

Footnote 21:8–9. Children.

Footnote 21:8–9. The phrase *‘immam* “with them,” which seems redundant in the first line of this couplet, should probably be read as part of the second line (Kahana); compare Isaiah 65:23.

Footnote 21:8–9. Compare Eliphaz’s assurance to Job in 5:24.

Footnote 21:8–9. Job counters Zophar’s assertions in 20:23–25.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

His bull never fails to impregnate,

His cow calves without any losses.

---

Footnote 21:10. The wicked now in the singular.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

They let their young run rampant like sheep,

And their children prance around.

---

Footnote 21:11. The wicked.

Footnote 21:11. “Young running rampant” is the same term as “hooligans” in 19:18.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

They raise (their voices) with hand-drum and lyre,

And take pleasure at the sound of the flute.

---

Footnote 21:12. An expression for singing in Isaiah 42:11.

Footnote 21:12. Reading *be-* for *ke-* with ancient translations and some Masoretic manuscripts.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:13–16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

They end their days in bounty,

And descend to Sheol at ease,

Though they say to El: “Turn away from us!

We are not interested in knowing your ways.

Why should we serve Shaddai?

What do we gain by imploring him?

Not in his hands is our bounty,

The scheme of the wicked is far from him.”

---

Footnote 21:13–16. Reading the *Qere* (tradition of reading) rather than the *Ketib* (written version) with Saadia Gaon and others; compare 36:11. The expression *billa* “wear out” of the *Ketib* has a negative connotation.

Footnote 21:13–16. Contrast Zophar’s claim in 20:21.

Footnote 21:13–16. Vocalizing *yeḥatu* from the regular Aramaic verb for descending; it is generally understood this way.

Footnote 21:13–16. Vocalizing *beroga‘* instead of the received *berega‘* “in a moment”—a punning reply to Zophar in 20:5. Compare Deuteronomy 28:65; Isaiah 28:12; Jeremiah 6:16.

Footnote 21:13–16. The opposite of what the righteous will desire; see Isaiah 58:2, which is quoted here almost verbatim.

Footnote 21:13–16. Compare Jeremiah 7:16.

Footnote 21:13–16. Reading *beyadaw* for *beyadam* “in their hands.” (For the reading of this verse I am indebted to Alexander Rofé.)

Footnote 21:13–16. Reading *tubenu* for *tubam* “their bounty” (the letters *nw* when written adjacent to each other look like final *m;* see at 8:8).

Footnote 21:13–16. Reading *mennu* for *menni* “from me”—beyond the deity’s notice. Compare Eliphaz’s rejoinder in 22:18. The received text does not make sense as an observation of Job’s.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:17–18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

How often does the lamp of the wicked wane?

And their ruin overcome them?

(How often) does he dispense disaster in his anger?

(How often) are they like straw in the wind,

And like chaff that a wind sweeps away?

---

Footnote 21:17–18. A direct refutation of Bildad in 18:5—and Proverbs 13:9.

Footnote 21:17–18. A denial of conventional wisdom, as in Psalm 1:4.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:19–21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

(How often) does Eloah store up his misfortune for his sons,

Pay him (thus) his retribution—and he knows it?

(How often) do his eyes witness his downfall,

Does he drink from Shaddai’s wrathful venom?

For what interest has he in his household after him—

When the number of his months has been halved?

---

Footnote 21:19–21. The wicked, now in the singular.

Footnote 21:19–21. Reading *pid* “catastrophe” (see Job 12:5 and elsewhere) for otherwise unknown *kid;* compare Rashi.

Footnote 21:19–21. For the expression and image, see for example Isaiah 51:17.

Footnote 21:19–21. When he is nearing death.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Can one teach awareness to El,

When he is judging from the heights?

---

Footnote 21:22. “Knowledge” of what transpires among humans; see Eliphaz’s paraphrase of this verse in 22:13.

Footnote 21:22. Too high in the heavens to be aware of earthly doings; see 22:13–14 and Job in 3:23.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:23–24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

This one dies wholly hale,

Completely at ease and at rest.

His buckets are full of milk,

The marrow of his bones refreshed.

---

Footnote 21:23–24. The wicked. Job reasserts his contention in 9:22.

Footnote 21:23–24. In Job health, like integrity, is figured by wholeness.

Footnote 21:23–24. Possibly a metaphor for testes full of semen (Gordis, *Book of Job*).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:25–26

------------------------------------------------------------------------

While this one dies in a bitter spirit,

Never having eaten sweet bounty.

They lie in the dust together,

And maggots cover them both.

---

Footnote 21:25–26. The righteous.

Footnote 21:25–26. Compare Isaiah 14:11.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:27–28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

See, I know what you are thinking,

The arguments you contemplate against me.

You will say, “Where’s the house of the beneficent?

And where is the home the wicked dwell in?”

---

Footnote 21:27–28. Reading *tahmosu* “you imagine” (known from Syriac, with many commentators) for *taḥmosu* “you do violence.”

Footnote 21:27–28. Literally, “the tent.”

Footnote 21:27–28. That is, you will claim there is a difference in the lot of the good and the bad.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:29–30

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Have you not questioned wayfarers?

Their indications you can hardly deny:

That on the day of disaster the wicked is spared,

On the day of fury they will be delivered.

---

Footnote 21:29–30. Compare Deuteronomy 32:27.

Footnote 21:29–30. Compare Isaiah 55:12.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:31

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who will confront (the wicked) with his ways?

For what he has done, who will pay him his due?

---

Footnote 21:31. Literally, “tell to his face his ways.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:32–33

------------------------------------------------------------------------

(Who will tell him) he’ll be delivered to the grave,

That a tomb will keep watch over him?

That the clods of the stream will be his comfort;

That he will draw after him every man,

As countless ones (went) before him?

---

Footnote 21:32–33. Reading *‘alaw* “over him” (Ball).

Footnote 21:32–33. Literally, “will be sweet for him.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 21:34

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why do you console me with windy breath?

Why do your responses remain futile?

---

Footnote 21:34. Reading a plural verb (*nish’aru*).

Footnote 21:34. Reading *‘amal* for *ma‘al* “betrayal”; see the similar usage in 16:2.
