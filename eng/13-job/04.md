------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Eliphaz the Teimanite and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

To hold back a word from you—(even) you could not!

To refrain from words—who could?

---

Footnote 4:2. Vocalizing *hanasa* (for *hanissa*, “has someone tried?”) and parsing *hanas* as an older Aramaic form of Hebrew *’anas*, “to withhold, restrain”; see Esther 1:8; Daniel 4:6.

Footnote 4:2. Using Aramaic *millin*, the equivalent of Hebrew *dabar* in the preceding line.

Footnote 4:2. For *la’a* “cannot” and *yakol* “can” as antonyms, compare Exodus 7:18 and 21.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:3–4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

It is you who have fortified the trembling,

And limp arms you have strengthened;

The stumbling would your words raise up,

And buckling knees would you stiffen.

---

Footnote 4:3–4. For *yisser* in the sense of “fortify,” see Hosea 7:15. For *rabbim* “many” I read *rabim* “trembling”; see the note on 4:14 above and the introduction to this volume. These two unusual verbal usages produce a secondary meaning: “You have instructed many.”

Footnote 4:3–4. The language is drawn from Isaiah 35:3.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But now that (calamity) has come to you, you cannot (bear it);

It touches you yourself, and you are shaken.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hasn’t your reverence been your backbone,

And your integrity your source-of-strength?

---

Footnote 4:6. Literally, “the wholeness of your ways.” See the comment on “whole (in heart)” at 1:1.

Footnote 4:6. For this understanding of the syntax, see already the reading of Saadia Gaon and the Tosafist commentary to Babylonian Talmud *Baba Metzia* 58b.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Think: what innocent ever disappeared?

And where have the upright been destroyed?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Whenever I have seen plowers of suffering,

And sowers of travail—it is they who reap it.

---

Footnote 4:8. The word-pair *’awen-‘amal* can denote either “iniquity” or “suffering.” Job uses *‘amal* in 3:10 and 20 in the latter sense, and that is apparently Eliphaz’s intention through-out his speech; see also Psalm 90:10 and probably Numbers 23:21. However, especially in view of the fact that a similar image is used of cultivating wrongdoing in Proverbs 11:18; 14:22; and 22:8, Job—and the reader—may be hearing in Eliphaz’s words the implication that he has sinned, even though Eliphaz does not express that perception in his first discourse (compare Fullerton).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

By the breath of Eloah they disappear,

By the wind of his nostrils they come to an end.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:10–11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The lion growls and the beast roars;

But the teeth of the predators (finally) crack.

The lion perishes for lack of prey,

And the lion whelps are eradicated.

---

Footnote 4:10–11. Eliphaz’s use of the lion metaphor may have been prompted by Job’s use of “growls” in 3:24. The lion is a typical image of the wicked; see Psalms 7:3; 17:12; 22:14; and others. The poet employs five different terms for “lion” in this passage, a feat that cannot be duplicated in English.

Footnote 4:10–11. The use of the same verb as in verse 9 links the lion and its fate to the wicked that it metaphorically represents.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:12–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yet to me did a word come in stealth,

And my ear grasped a hint of it;

In shudders from visions in the night,

When slumber falls upon people.

Fear overcame me, and trembling;

As shivers set my bones to shaking.

---

Footnote 4:12–14. Job spells out the apprehension to which he alluded in 3:25; compare 4:14, which resumes 3:25.

Footnote 4:12–14. In 7:13–14 Job will blame God for giving him nightmares.

Footnote 4:12–14. For *rob* “shivers” compare Akkadian *rūbu* and see Job 33:19 and 4:3 (with a slight revocalization). For the expression “bones shaking”—reading *hirḥip* for *hipḥid*—see Jeremiah 23:9 (Ginsberg, “Job the Patient”). In Hebrew script of all periods, *d* and *r* look alike.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:15–16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a spirit passed across my face;

It set the hair of my flesh on end.

It stood still, but I could not discern its demeanor,

(Nor) the form in front of my eyes.

A moaning and voice did I hear:

---

Footnote 4:15–16. An “angel” from the divine assembly, described in Job 1:6; 2:1; and elsewhere; see further 15:8.

Footnote 4:15–16. Compare Job’s language in 9:11 to his language here and in verse 15 above.

Footnote 4:15–16. “A moaning and a voice” echoes the phrase often rendered “still (small) voice” in the revelation to Elijah (1 Kings 19:12).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:17–19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

“Can a mortal be righteous before Eloah?

Can a man be pure before his Maker?

If in his servants he puts no trust,

And in his angels he finds fault,

Then all the more those who dwell in clay houses,

Whose foundation is in the dust.

They are quashed before twilight;

---

Footnote 4:17–19. The preposition *min* can also be understood as “more than,” but so ironic a sense does not comport with the present context. For the usage of *min* found here, see Psalm 18:22, where the sense “more than” is impossible.

Footnote 4:17–19. A borrowing from Arabic (Weber).

Footnote 4:17–19. The expression *’ap* is short for *’ap ki* (as in the parallel passage 15:16); for the formula, “If …, then all the more so …,” see Deuteronomy 31:27; Proverbs 11:31.

Footnote 4:17–19. A metaphor for the body.

Footnote 4:17–19. Humans were created out of the dust of the earth; Genesis 2:7; see also Job 10:9; 32:6.

Footnote 4:17–19. Compare Arabic *‘asā* “evening” (Ginsberg, “Job the Patient”) and Hebrew *‘ashash* “grow dark” in Psalm 6:8.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

From day-break till evening they are crushed;

When it is not even nightfall they forever disappear.

---

Footnote 4:20. The participle of *sim* with prefixed *mem* is a ghost-word in pre-Mishnaic Hebrew. Revocalize *mashayim*, comparing Arabic *masā’* “evening,” Late Hebrew *’emesh* “last evening.”

Footnote 4:20. This phrase together with “Let the day disappear” (3:3) frames Job’s first discourse.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 4:21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Their tent-pin is pulled up on them;

They die without (ever finding) knowledge.”

---

Footnote 4:21. Reading *yetedam* for *yitram* “their tent-cord” on the basis of Isaiah 33:20 (Luzzatto and others). The letters *d* and *r* look alike.

Footnote 4:21. Life is figured here as a tent one puts up and takes down.

Footnote 4:21. For the idea, compare Proverbs 5:23; 10:22.
