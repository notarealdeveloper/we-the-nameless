------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But now they laugh at me—

Young men years younger than I,

Whose fathers I so detested,

I would not place them with my herding dogs!


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:2–4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

What need have I of their arms’ strength?

Because of them the harvest is lost!

Who in times of want and severe famine

Abscond to the desert—

To the dark, the bleak, and the barren.

Who pluck the mallow bush flower,

And the root of the broom-bush for food.

---

Footnote 30:2–4. See the comment at 5:26.

Footnote 30:2–4. For this usage, see Genesis 31:42.

Footnote 30:2–4. More literally, “Who pluck the mallow on the bush.”

Footnote 30:2–4. These are eaten by Bedouin in desperate times (Bailey, 53–54). The verses Isaiah 47:14 and Psalm 120:4 suggest the root was burned to keep warm, in which case vocalize *leḥummam* “to warm themselves” instead of *laḥmam* “their food.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

As they’re driven from town,

People holler as at thieves.

---

Footnote 30:5. Compare *goy* in Zephaniah 2:14, synonymous with “land” or “field.” In Phoenician the term indicates a community.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:6–8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

To dwell in the clefts of streams,

In the hollows of ground and stones.

They bray among the shrubs,

They huddle at the thorn-bush.

Sons of fools and nobodies,

They’re estranged from the land.

---

Footnote 30:6–8. Referring back to “they abscond” in verse 3.

Footnote 30:6–8. That is, in caves.

Footnote 30:6–8. Reading *nikkeru* for *nikke’u* “they were smitten” or “brought low,” neither of which provides the generally anticipated sense of “expelled.” For *nikker* “to alienate, transfer, remove,” see 1 Samuel 23:7 and the Akkadian cognate.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:9–10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But now I’m the butt of their jingles,

I’ve become to them a byword.

They abhor me and distance themselves;

They spare no spit from my face.

---

Footnote 30:9–10. Referring back to the disrespectful youths above.

Footnote 30:9–10. Compare Job 17:6; Psalm 69:12–13.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:11–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

They loosen my reins and my bridle;

And throw the bit off their mouths;

They break the lead off their noses;

They rise up and let loose their legs,

And mount against me paths of destruction;

They’ve wrecked my road, increasing my ruin,

And none can restrain them.

They advance like a wide burst of water,

They overrun the barren tract.

---

Footnote 30:11–14. The rope by which I would restrain them; reading *yitri* with the tradition. The youths are likened to a once-domesticated animal now turned wild.

Footnote 30:11–14. For the verb “and they abused me” I read “my bridle” in view of Arabic *‘inan* with that meaning. Recall that the poet loves synonyms.

Footnote 30:11–14. Reading *mi-pihem* “from their mouths” for *mi-panai* “from my face”; compare the Syriac.

Footnote 30:11–14. More literally, “My nose ring is broken off them.” For the very perplexing Hebrew I read (after Ginsberg), redividing but barely changing the letters: *‘aleimo napor ḥaḥi*. The Hebrew as it stands means: “On the right a punk arise (plural).”

Footnote 30:11–14. Reading *regel yeshalleḥu* for “my leg they released.”

Footnote 30:11–14. A pseudo-Aramaic form of Hebrew *natats*.

Footnote 30:11–14. A pseudo-Aramaic form of Hebrew *‘atsar*. The Arabic cognate has the sense of reining in a horse (Kister).

Footnote 30:11–14. For the image, compare 2 Samuel 5:20.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Terror’s been turned against me;

Blowing my stature away like the wind;

My prestige dissipates like a cloud.

---

Footnote 30:15. Literally, “chasing.”

Footnote 30:15. Derived from *shoa‘* “noble” (Isaiah 32:5).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:16–18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

And now my spirit is emptied;

Days of affliction take hold of me;

At night it gnaws on my limbs,

So my sinews cannot rest.

It takes all my strength to change clothing;

It constrains like the neck of my tunic.

---

Footnote 30:16–18. More literally, “poured out.”

Footnote 30:16–18. The affliction.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

It’s thrown me down to the clay,

Making me seem like dust and ashes.

---

Footnote 30:19. “Dust and ashes” is a figure for abjection; see Genesis 18:27 and compare Job 42:6.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:20–22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I cry out to you, but you do not answer;

I stand, and you just look at me.

You have turned cruel against me;

With your brute power you obstruct me.

You lift me to be carried off by the wind,

And you sweep me away in a tempest.

---

Footnote 30:20–22. Literally, “the power of your hand.”

Footnote 30:20–22. The verb “obstruct” is related to the name of the Satan (see comment on 1:6).

Footnote 30:20–22. More literally, “you lift me to the wind and mount me upon it”; compare Psalm 18:11.

Footnote 30:20–22. For this usage, compare Nahum 2:7; Psalm 65:11.

Footnote 30:20–22. Reading the received text’s *t-sh-w-h* as *teshu’ah* “tumult” as in Job 39:7; compare *sho’* “surge” in Psalm 89:10.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I know you’ll return me to Death,

To the meeting house of all who’ve lived.

*Job’s oath of innocence begins here, intermittently.*

---

Footnote 30:23. See at 28:22.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:24–25

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If a poor man would extend me his hand,

If in time of disaster he cried out to me,

I swear that I wept for the wretched,

My spirit was pained by the needy.

---

Footnote 30:24–25. Reading *’im ’eilai ‘ani* “If to me a poor-man” for *’ak lo’ be‘i* “But not in a ruin-mound,” which makes no sense (compare Dhorme and others). “Poor man” puns on “affliction” in verses 16 and 27.

Footnote 30:24–25. Reading … *’eilai shiwa‘* “to me he cried out” for *lahen shua‘* “to/for them (feminine) noble,” which makes no sense.

Footnote 30:24–25. Literally, “the hard of day.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:26–28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For I hoped for good, but there came bad;

I expected light, but there came darkness,

My insides roil and can’t be still;

I’ve been greeted by days of affliction.

I walk in gloom without a sun;

In the assembly I stand and cry out.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 30:29–31

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I’ve been brother to the jackal,

And companion to the ostrich;

My skin has blackened off me,

And my body is charred from the heat.

My harp’s been replaced by wailing,

And my flute—by the voice of keeners.

---

Footnote 30:29–31. Literally, “daughters of the desert.”

Footnote 30:29–31. Literally, “has become.”

Footnote 30:29–31. Recall Job’s having become the “butt of jingles” (30:9 above).
