------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:1–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

No one’s fierce enough to engage him.

Who could take a stand before him?

One’s hope (of doing so) is dashed—

At the mere sight of him one’s knocked down!

Who has ever confronted him and survived?

Of all that’s under heaven—he is mine.

---

Footnote 41:1–3. The verb *‘ur* is sometimes used of wielding weapons (see for example 2 Samuel 23:18).

Footnote 41:1–3. Reading *le-panaw* for *le-panay* “before me” (God). Verse 1 belongs syntactically and semantically following verse 2 (so for example Dhorme). The copyist apparently skipped verse 1 by mistake and then copied it following verse 2.

Footnote 41:1–3. For the verb, see Psalm 37:24.

Footnote 41:1–3. Literally, “has remained whole.” Reading with others on the basis of the Greek: *hiqdimennu … wa-yishlam* for *hiqdimani … wa’ashallem* “(Who) has confronted/greeted me and I have compensated.” Compare 9:4 (in regard to the deity).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I cannot keep silent about him,

The fact of his incomparable valor.

---

Footnote 41:4. Reading *geburato* for *geburot* “mighty acts” and *’ein ‘erek* “nothing to compare” for *ḥin ‘erko* “the favor (?) of his worth (or arrangement or military array).”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who can discover what’s under his garb?

Who can penetrate his double armor?

---

Footnote 41:5. Literally, “on the inside (*penim*) of his garment,” under his scaly skin.

Footnote 41:5. Reading *siryono* (for example Jeremiah 51:3) for *risno* “his bridle” with the ancient translations.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Who can pry open the doors of his face?

All about his teeth is terror!

---

Footnote 41:6. His jaws.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:7–9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

His back is tiers of shields,

Locked like a tight seal.

One reaches up to the other—

No wind could come between them.

Each is stuck onto its brother;

They consolidate and cannot be parted.

---

Footnote 41:7–9. Reading *gewoh* for *ga’awah* “pride” with the ancient Greek. For the same confusion in the opposite direction, see 22:29.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:10–13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

His sneeze makes a shining light;

His eyes (flash) like glimmers of dawn.

Torches proceed from his mouth;

Sparks of fire are emitted.

From his nostrils issues smoke,

As from a cauldron fanned and boiling.

His breathing ignites coals,

As a flame issues from his mouth.

---

Footnote 41:10–13. The verb indicates that the singular, not plural, is intended*—‘atishato*.

Footnote 41:10–13. The Aramaic equivalent of Hebrew *sir* (see Jeremiah 1:13).

Footnote 41:10–13. Reading *’ogem* “burning” (so in Arabic) without the final *nun*, which is apparently copied from the beginning of the next word. Compare the description of the deity in Psalm 18:9.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:14–16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

In his neck inheres strength;

Power runs ahead of him.

The folds of his flesh stick together;

Forming a solid, it does not slip.

His heart is as solid as rock,

As solid as a bottom millstone.

---

Footnote 41:14–16. Read not *da’abah* “anxiety” but *dob’ah* “power” (Deuteronomy 33:25; and Ugaritic).

Footnote 41:14–16. Reading *taruts* for *taduts* “skips” (a unique Hebrew word drawn from later Aramaic) with the Aramaic version from Qumran.

Footnote 41:14–16. Compare the image in 1 Kings 18:46.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Even gods live in fear of his majesty;

They’re in terror of the ruin he wreaks.

---

Footnote 41:17. Spelled here without *aleph;* compare 13:11.

Footnote 41:17. Reading *yeḥattu* for *yitḥatte’u* “they are cleansed, purified”—compare the usage with *shibbaron* “shattering (disaster)” in Jeremiah 17:18.

Footnote 41:17. Reading *shebaraw* “*his* ruinous acts” for *shebarim* “ruinous acts.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:18–21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

One who engages him—(his) sword will fail,

(Likewise) spear, dagger, and dart.

Iron he reckons as hay,

And bronze as rotted wood.

No son of a bow will put him to flight;

Sling stones seem to him straw.

Like straw is reckoned a spear;

He scorns the sound of the javelin.

---

Footnote 41:18–21. Literally, “it cannot stand.”

Footnote 41:18–21. The latter two terms have cognates in Arabic.

Footnote 41:18–21. An arrow; compare “sons of the quiver” in Lamentations 3:13.

Footnote 41:18–21. The word “straw” (*qash*) puns on “bow” (*qeshet*).

Footnote 41:18–21. Reading the singular.

Footnote 41:18–21. The unique Hebrew word *totaḥ* has been interpreted variously according to several etymologies, none of which is borne out. In view of the parallel *kidon* “spear, javelin,” we should probably read *romaḥ*, a well-attested synonym for “spear, javelin” (compare for example Ball).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Beneath him are sharp-edged shards;

He beds down on spikes over clay.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:23–24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He roils the deep like a cauldron;

He treats the sea like a mixing pot.

His wake shines brightly behind him;

The ocean looks to be white-haired!

---

Footnote 41:23–24. Ordinarily “an ointment-pot” (BDB). Leviathan’s thrashing stirs the water like the mixture in a mortar (compare for example Rashbam). Compare the image in Ezekiel 32:2.

Footnote 41:23–24. Reading *yeḥasheb* “it is reckoned” for *yaḥshob* “he reckons” (Dillmann).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 41:25–26

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He has no match on earth,

Who is made as fearless as he.

All that is haughty he’s got in his view;

Over beasts of all kinds he is king.

---

Footnote 41:25–26. The passive participle ‘*asu* for ‘*asuy*, if not an error, is archaic (Gordis, *Book of Job*).
