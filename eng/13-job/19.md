------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Job and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

How long will you make me anguished,

Oppressing me with words!

Ten times you try to refute me,

You have no shame as you demean me.

---

Footnote 19:2–3. Many times; compare Numbers 14:22.

Footnote 19:2–3. Compare Job 11:3.

Footnote 19:2–3. For “demean,” compare Arabic *hqr* (so already M. Kimhi).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For even if I’ve been at fault,

The fault resides within me.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:5–6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

But if you keep speaking to me arrogantly,

Reproaching me on account of my disgrace,

Know at least it’s Eloah who’s abused me,

Surrounding me with his siege-works.

---

Footnote 19:5–6. The verb *higdil* “to do greatly” is here an ellipsis of “to do greatly with the mouth”; compare Ezekiel 35:13.

Footnote 19:5–6. Compare Job 16:13–14 above; and verses 11–12 below.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Though I scream “I’ve been wronged!” I receive no response;

(Though) I make an outcry, there is no justice.

---

Footnote 19:7. Compare Jeremiah 20:8 and Lamentations 3:8.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He has fenced in my way so I cannot pass through;

He has laid darkness over my footpaths.

---

Footnote 19:8. Compare Hosea 2:8 and especially Lamentations 3:7, 9. Job ironically inverts the sense of God’s hedging Job about as a figure of protection (1:10).

Footnote 19:8. Contrast Job 22:28.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He has stripped me of my (clothes of) honor,

Removed the crown from my head.

---

Footnote 19:9. Compare for example Ezekiel 16:39.

Footnote 19:9. Compare for example Jeremiah 13:18.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He has broken me down and I’m going (to die).

He has uprooted my hope like a tree.

---

Footnote 19:10. Like a house; compare for example Psalm 52:7. Job may be alluding to the destruction of his estate and the house in which his children were killed in particular (1:19).

Footnote 19:10. Compare for example Psalm 39:14; for the full expression, see for example Genesis 25:32.

Footnote 19:10. For the sentiment, compare Job 17:15. Job ironically twists the image in 14:7–9.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:11–12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He has inflamed his anger against me,

And reckoned me one of his enemies.

His troops come at me at once,

They clear an attack-road against me,

And they camp surrounding my tent.

---

Footnote 19:11–12. Compare 13:24; but there Job uses a term for “enemy” that puns on his name; here he uses a different term (*tsar*) that puns on the polysemous verb *zur* in the following four verses.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:13–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My relatives he’s kept distant from me,

And my friends have withdrawn from me.

My close ones have stopped (coming near),

And my familiar ones have rejected me.

---

Footnote 19:13–14. Literally, “my brothers.”

Footnote 19:13–14. Literally, “those who know me (well).”

Footnote 19:13–14. Hebrew *zaru*.

Footnote 19:13–14. For the theme of alienating people from the sufferer, see Psalm 31:12; 38:23; 88:9, 19; and see further Job 29.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:15–16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Men of my household and women-who-serve-me

Imagine me a stranger—

In their eyes I am a foreigner.

I call to my man-servant but he does not respond—

Though I beg-him-for-mercy with my very-own mouth!

---

Footnote 19:15–16. Literally, “(males) who dwell in my house.”

Footnote 19:15–16. Hebrew *zar*.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My breath is foul to my wife,

And my odor to my siblings.

---

Footnote 19:17. Hebrew *zara*. A different verb from the better-known term for “stranger”; see for example Numbers 11:20; Psalm 78:30.

Footnote 19:17. Read *tsaḥanati* (see Joel 2:20) for *ḥannoti* “my pleading” (?).

Footnote 19:17. Literally, “sons/children of my womb”—the womb in which he gestated; see Job 3:10 and the comment there. See also the introduction to this volume. This verse is strongly akin to Psalm 69:9, where “my brothers” are also called “sons of my mother.” Job replaces the conventional phrase with his idiosyncratic one.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Even young-hooligans detest me;

When I arise (to speak), they speak against me.

---

Footnote 19:18. See Job 30:1; the same term is used of criminals in 16:11.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:19

------------------------------------------------------------------------

All members of my circle abhor me;

Those I befriended have turned on me.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My bone sticks to my skin and my flesh,

I’m cemented at the skin of my teeth.

---

Footnote 19:20. Unable to eat in his abject state, Job’s bones protrude from his emaciated body; compare Psalm 102:6.

Footnote 19:20. Compare later Aramaic *mlt*. Others tend to render: “I escape by the skin of my teeth,” which doesn’t mean anything. Job claims that the skin surrounding his teeth (his palate and lower jaw) is bound together so that he cannot speak. This is a conventional complaint of the pious sufferer; compare the Babylonian “Let Me Praise the Lord of Wisdom” (in Oshima). The claim is, of course, hyperbole; Job is not actually prevented from speaking.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Have compassion, compassion, you my friends!

For the hand of Eloah has afflicted me.

---

Footnote 19:21. Compare 1 Samuel 6:9; Ruth 1:13. The same term rendered “affected” in 1:11, 19 and 2:5.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why do you like El persecute me?

Why can’t you get your fill of my flesh?

---

Footnote 19:22. To eat someone’s flesh is an ancient Semitic idiom for defaming him; see Psalm 27:2; Daniel 3:8 (where the Aramaic phrase has a cognate in Babylonian).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:23–24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If only my words could be written!

If only be engraved in a record!

With a stylus of iron or lead,

Let them be hewn in rock as a witness!

---

Footnote 19:23–24. The term “record” is *seper*—any written document; there may be a pun on Babylonian *siparru* “copper, brass”—producing a double entendre: would that my words were engraved with a brass tool, or in brass/copper. The same pun may be found in Isaiah 30:8, on which the Job verse may be drawing.

Footnote 19:23–24. Reading *la‘ed* for *la‘ad* “forever.” The latter reading makes sense, but the former gives more weight to the forensic framework of Job’s arguments. Job wants to present his case, not to memorialize his words. The image of an inscription engraved on a high stone ledge may be modeled on the famous Behistun inscription of King Darius of Persia from the late sixth century BCE (compare Habel). The incised signs were filled with lead.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:25

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For I know that my redeemer lives;

The respondent will testify on earth.

---

Footnote 19:25. God; compare 16:19 and see below. The “redeemer” in this context is one who protects the wronged; see Proverbs 23:10–11. For the sense, see also Isaiah 50:8.

Footnote 19:25. Literally, “the latter (second party),” the deity who is being sued by Job. The usage is explained by Proverbs 18:17: the “first” litigant begins a suit, and the next (“his colleague”) comes to cross-examine him. “Latter” (*’aḥaron*) here is the counterpart of the “first (litigant)” (*rishon)* there.

Footnote 19:25. Literally, “will rise (to testify)”; see Deuteronomy 19:15.

Footnote 19:25. Literally, “dust”; for “dust” in the sense of “the earth,” see for example Job 41:25.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:26–27

------------------------------------------------------------------------

From behind my skin I look out,

While in the flesh I’ll see Eloah.

Something I myself will view—

What my eyes, not a stranger’s, will see.

My innards wear away within my breast.

---

Footnote 19:26–27. This clause is one of the most perplexing in the book. Literally it reads: “And afterwards, my skin (masculine) they flayed this (feminine).” My attempt at reconstruction is based on the assumption that, on account of the parallelism of “skin” and “flesh” (for example Job 7:5; compare 10:11; 19:20), the first clause will convey something similar to the second, which is fairly clear. The awkward combination of “they flayed this” (*nikkepu zo’t*) is emended to *nishkapti* “I look out.” Hebraists should note that the *Niph‘al* conjugation of this verb often has an active meaning; see Judges 5:28; 2 Samuel 6:16; and Proverbs 7:6—where we find exactly the form that is restored here.

Footnote 19:26–27. That is, I pine for this; compare 16:20.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 19:28–29

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If you are thinking, “for what shall we persecute him,

What root cause can we find in him?”

You had better fear the sword—

For rage is a crime of the sword–

You had better beware of demons!

---

Footnote 19:28–29. Literally, “say (to yourselves).”

Footnote 19:28–29. Literally, “root of the matter” (or legal case).

Footnote 19:28–29. The received text has *bi* “in me” rather than *bo* “in him”; but only the latter reading makes sense, and it is in fact attested in several Masoretic manuscripts.

Footnote 19:28–29. The passion that impels you to persecute an innocent man. The clause is unclear; but the idea that those who transgress with the sword will be chased by the sword is found for example in Leviticus 26:25. An alternate interpretation of the same words is: “For crimes by the sword are venom.”

Footnote 19:28–29. Literally, “know.”

Footnote 19:28–29. The word as traditionally read, *shaddun*, has no meaning. I read the word the way it is spelled, *shedin* “demons” (with the Aramaic plural so common in Job; compare Luzzatto). Both the “sword” and the “demon” are among the seven frightening calamities enumerated by Eliphaz in 5:20–22. Job would seem to be responding to Bildad (18:13–15), who foresees various demons afflicting the wicked. There is also a pun on *shod* “catastrophe,” which is also among the seven calamities enumerated by Eliphaz.
