------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My spirit has been bruised,

My days are on the wane,

My grave is ready.

---

Footnote 17:1. For the motif of the grave being prepared for the pious sufferer in anticipation of his death, see the Syrian (Ugarit) version of the Babylonian “Let Me Praise the Lord of Wisdom” (in Y. Cohen).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:2

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I attest that mockery is my lot,

And the bitterer it gets, the more my eyes cannot (bear it).

---

Footnote 17:2. Reading *til’enah ‘einay* for *talan ‘eini* “my eye spends the night.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pray, you be my guarantor!

Who else will strike a hand (in pledge) for me?

---

Footnote 17:3. God.

Footnote 17:3. For the gesture and language, see Proverbs 17:18 (Freedman).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

You have hidden reason from their hearts.

For this you will not be exalted!

---

Footnote 17:4. More literally, “You have hidden their (the mockers’) hearts from (being able to) reason.”

Footnote 17:4. Reading *teromam* with several scholars. Although the reference is not clear, the context suggests that it is Job who will not be exalting the deity, who has allowed his companions and others to mock him.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

One invites over friends to share (a meal),

While the eyes of one’s children languish.

---

Footnote 17:5. This would appear to be a gnomic saying: the companions dispense wisdom freely to others, like Job, but they keep none for themselves.

Footnote 17:5. From hunger.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

He has set me up as a popular taunt;

I have become like spit in the face.

---

Footnote 17:6. In the light of 30:9–10, *topet* derives from Aramaic for “spittle.” Job has become an object of disdain.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My eye has gone dim from anguish,

And my limbs wear away like a shadow.

---

Footnote 17:7. Reading *kalim* for *kullam* “all of them” (similarly Ehrlich); compare the use of *kalah* “wear away” in 19:27 and 33:21.

Footnote 17:7. Compare 7:9, where the verb *kalah* is used of a cloud.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:8–9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Even the upright rejoice over this—

And the innocent exults along with the impudent,

As the righteous holds fast to his ways,

And the pure of hands becomes even bolder.

---

Footnote 17:8–9. Reading *yismeḥu* with the addition of only the *ḥeth*. We have the same pair of poetic synonyms (*samaḥ* and *hit‘orer*) in 31:29.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

And yet, may you all return, pray come,

Though I won’t find among you even one who is wise!

---

Footnote 17:10. Reading *kullekem* “all of you” for *kullam* “all of them,” seeing that the verb is second-person plural.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:11

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My days have passed,

My plans have been rent asunder,

As have the wishes of my heart.

---

Footnote 17:11. The noun is derived from the rare verb *’arash* “desire” (see Psalm 21:3). For the form *morash*, compare *mosar* “binding” from *’asar* (Job 39:5 and see the comment at 12:18).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

They replace night with day;

(To them) light is closer than darkness.

---

Footnote 17:12. All those who see Job’s afflictions and are glad that the seemingly wicked is being punished.

Footnote 17:12. Job feels that people who, unlike him in his present condition, prefer light to darkness have come to see his suffering as a positive—deserved affliction from God.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:13–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I measure for my home in Sheol,

Arrange my bedding in the Dark,

Call out to the Pit—“You are my father!”

To the worm—“You are my mother and sister!”

---

Footnote 17:13–14. Compare “to measure with a line” in the sense of building in 38:5 (Yellin). The verb for measuring with a line is cognate to the word for “hope” in verse 15 below, producing a brilliant irony.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Where then would be my hope?

My hope—who will have seen it?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 17:16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Down to Sheol it will go—

Resting way down in the ground.

---

Footnote 17:16. More literally, “All the way down to the dust of resting.” The word *yaḥad* sometimes means just “altogether” in the sense of “entirely,” as in 10:8.
