------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 32:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

These three men ceased responding to Job since in his own eyes he was right and just.

---

Footnote 32:1. Compare Proverbs 16:1.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 32:2–5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Elihu son of Berak-El the Buzite, of the family of Ram, became angry at Job;

he became angry (at him) for justifying himself before Elohim.

And at his three companions he became angry for their not finding a (proper) response;

they made Elohim (appear to) be in the wrong.

So Elihu waited out Job’s words, since they were much older than he in years.

When Elihu saw there was no (proper) response in the mouths of these three men, he became angry.

---

Footnote 32:2–5. The name means “He (or YHWH—the name of Israel’s God) is God”; unlike the names of the three older companions, this name appears to be Israelite (for example 1 Samuel 1:1).

Footnote 32:2–5. The name means “El (or God) Blesses”; the name is not otherwise known in the Bible.

Footnote 32:2–5. Buz is the name of Uts’s brother in Genesis 22:21 and a place in Edom/North Arabia in Jeremiah 25:23.

Footnote 32:2–5. Ram is an Israelite name (Ruth 4:19 and elsewhere), although some from ancient to modern times identify it with Aram.

Footnote 32:2–5. The received Hebrew text has “Job,” not “Elohim,” here; but rabbinic tradition (for example Midrash Tanḥuma; compare Rashi, Ibn Ezra, and others) identifies this as a late scribal correction intended to save the deity’s honor.

Footnote 32:2–5. The Hebrew here is clumsy.

Footnote 32:2–5. The three companions—Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar.

Footnote 32:2–5. Literally, “days.”

## Job 32:6–10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Elihu son of Berak-El the Buzite and he said:

Young am I in years

Whereas you are elderly.

That’s why I was too timid and afraid

To express my discourse to you.

I thought, (the many of) days should speak,

The many in years should make wisdom known.

There is however a spirit within mortals;

Shaddai’s breath gives them understanding.

It is not the many (of years) who get wisdom,

Nor the old who understand custom.

That’s why I say: “Listen to me!

I too will express my discourse.”

---

Footnote 32:6–10. Literally, “days.”

Footnote 32:6–10. Elihu is here addressing the companions, not Job.

Footnote 32:6–10. The term *dea‘/ de‘ah* (and sometimes *da‘at*) is derived from the verb *da‘a* “to speak” (see Proverbs 24:14), not *yada‘* “to know,” as is commonly thought. The phrase “to express discourse” is unique to Elihu.

Footnote 32:6–10. “Make known” echoes “my discourse.” Elihu may be invoking a traditional saying (Fontaine).

Footnote 32:6–10. The term *mishpat*, most often “judgment, justice,” can also denote “regimen, custom, manner” (for example Genesis 40:13; 2 Samuel 27:11; 2 Kings 17:33).

## Job 32:11–12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I have been abiding your speeches,

Giving ear to your explanations—

While you examined (Job’s) words.

I have observed your discourse,

And I find: not one of you proves Job wrong;

Not one answers his assertions.

---

Footnote 32:11–12. “Your understandings.”

Footnote 32:11–12. See Proverbs 18:17: “The righteous may be first in his lawsuit, but then comes his counterpart and cross-examines him.”

Footnote 32:11–12. Reading *we-de‘ikem* for *we-‘adekem* “at you.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 32:13–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Perhaps you’ll say, “We’ve found a wise course:

El, not a man, will defeat him.”

But had he put his case to me,

I would not respond with your assertions.

---

Footnote 32:13–14. Literally, “drive him off.”

Footnote 32:13–14. Reading *lu’* “if, would” for the received *lo’* “not” (so Masnuth and some moderns). Compare the comment on 9:33.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 32:15–16

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Confounded, they spoke up no more;

They were at a loss for words.

I abided till they were no more speaking;

They stopped and responded no more.

---

Footnote 32:15–16. For this usage, see Isaiah 37:27.

Footnote 32:15–16. The three older companions. In this verse and the next Elihu is addressing an audience, as in theater.

Footnote 32:15–16. Literally, “words moved away from them” or “they moved words away from themselves.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 32:17–20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I will have my own part spoken;

I too will express my discourse.

For I am filled with words;

Bloated by the wind in my belly.

My belly, you see, is like unopened wine;

And like a new wineskin will burst.

I must speak to enjoy relief;

I must open my lips and speak up.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 32:21–22

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let me show favor to no one;

Nor defer to a gentleman’s title.

If I’d favor a man for his title,

Then my creator would carry me off.

---

Footnote 32:21–22. Contrary to the older companions, according to Job (13:8).

Footnote 32:21–22. For this usage, see Isaiah 44:5; 45:4.

Footnote 32:21–22. The vocalization *lu’* for *lo’* “not” is based on the formula “if … then …” (*lu … kim‘at*); see Psalm 94:17.

Footnote 32:21–22. More literally, “If would favor whom I would title.”
