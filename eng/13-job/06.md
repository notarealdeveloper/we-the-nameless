------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Up spoke Job and he said:


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:2–3

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Were my anguish weighed, yes, weighed,

And my vexation borne with it on scales,

It would be heavier than the sand at the sea.

That is why my speech is a garble (to you).

---

Footnote 6:2–3. “Garble” renders a unique noun derived from “stammer” (Obadiah 1:16).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Shaddai’s arrows are in me,

and my life-spirit drinks up their venom.

Eloah’s terrors are arrayed against me.

---

Footnote 6:4. There is early Canaanite (Ugaritic) evidence to the effect that Shaddai was a god of the field (Hebrew *sadeh*) who hunts, making the association with arrows very apt. Here he is depicted like the Canaanite god Resheph; see 5:7 with the comment there.

Footnote 6:4. Because both “life-spirit” and “venom” are feminine, like the verb, a reverse of subject and object is plausible: “Their venom saps my life-spirit.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Does a wild ass bray (when it’s) in the meadow?

Does an ox low (when it’s) at its feed?

---

Footnote 6:5. I only speak out because I am in anguish.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:6–7

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Can the insipid be eaten without salt?

Is there any taste in the juice of chubeza?

My gullet refuses to touch it;

To me it’s like sickening food.

---

Footnote 6:6–7. A Middle Eastern herb, lacking in taste. The Hebrew word (read *ḥalamit*) is known from ancient Syria (Alalakh). The term for “taste” is polysemous, denoting “reason” as well; see also 12:11 and 34:3. Job is telling Eliphaz that his argument is lacking in reason and that he will have none of it.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:8–9

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Would someone grant what I ask?

Would Eloah grant what I hope for?

That Eloah would comply and crush me!

Release his hand and cleave me!


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Yet would I have this one consolation,

When I’d writhe and recoil as he’d show no mercy:

That I did not conceal the words of the holy one.

---

Footnote 6:10. Not the deity but the spirit that appeared in 4:12–21; “holy ones” in Job refers to the angels (see 5:1; 15:15).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:11–12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

What strength have I that I should wait?

What span (of life) that I should show patience?

Is my strength the strength of stone?

Is my flesh made of bronze?

---

Footnote 6:11–12. Like Leviathan (41:16).

Footnote 6:11–12. Like Behemoth (40:18).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:13

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Alas, there is no more power in me;

And my wits are driven out of me.

---

Footnote 6:13. For *ha’im* in this sense, see Numbers 17:28.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why is one who turns from evil put to shame?

And one who fears Shaddai accursed?

---

Footnote 6:14. Reading *lama sar me-ra‘ yeḥussad* (Ginsberg, “Studies”) for the impossible traditional text. For the verb, compare Proverbs 25:10 (*ḥissed* “shames”).

Footnote 6:14. Reading *ye‘uzzar* for *ya‘azor* “he helps”; compare Akkadian *ezēru* and Phoenician *’zr*. Note how Job unwittingly evokes his characterization by the narrator and the deity in 1:1, 8; 2:3.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:15–17

------------------------------------------------------------------------

My brothers have betrayed me like a wadi,

Like the bed that the wadis stream through.

They turn dark from ice,

And snow piles up on them.

In the season they are scorched, they are devastated;

In the seasonal heat, they dissolve from their place.

---

Footnote 6:15–17. Taking *yit‘alem* as a pseudo-Aramaized form of *yit‘arem*.

Footnote 6:15–17. Compare Job 24:19.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:18–20

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Caravans turn a twisting route,

They go into the desert and are lost.

Caravans from Tema look out (for the wadis),

Convoys from Sheba hold out for them.

They balk for having relied (on them),

They arrive at (the spot) and are dismayed.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Thus have you now become naught (to me);

You see a terrifying sight, and you are seized with fear.

---

Footnote 6:21. Job’s language echoes that of Eliphaz in 4:5, suggesting that here, as elsewhere in the discourse, Job is responding to Eliphaz.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:22–23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Have I ever asked you, “Give of yours for me”?

Or “From your means pay out for me”?

Or “Rescue me from an assailant”?

Or “Ransom me from brigands”?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:24

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Enlighten me and I’ll silence myself;

Just inform me where I’ve done (you) wrong!


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:25–26

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Why do my honest words provoke (you)?

Why do they prompt you to reproach?

Do you regard (my) words as mere wind?

This desperate man’s speech as mere wind?

---

Footnote 6:25–26. “Straight” in speech. The term “straight (of path)” (see 1:2) is used of the “heart,” the seat of speech, in 33:3.

Footnote 6:25–26. A different Old Greek reading shows the traditional text was unclear. For *ha-le-hokaḥ* “as reproach,” I read *ha-le-ruaḥ*.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:27

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Would you cast lots even over an orphan?

Would you barter even over your friend?

---

Footnote 6:27. Compare Psalm 22:19. The lots are cast to determine who benefits.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

So now please oblige and face me;

I swear I won’t lie to your faces.

---

Footnote 6:28. The verb “to lie” alludes to the wadi that has run dry of verses 15–20; the term for “wadi” in the verse being parodied, Jeremiah 15:18, is of the same root, “to disappoint.” Job’s connotation is: I would not fail you the way you have failed me.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 6:29–30

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Turn back now—there will be no corruption!

Turn back—I hold to what’s right!

Is there corruption on my tongue?

Does my palate not discern false speech?

*Job responds to Eliphaz’s argument that if Job would only be patient he would find that his lot would return to its former goodness (God wounds, then heals; 5:18). Job addresses not only his companions; he begins to confront God. Attention from the deity is what the righteous typically desire. Job now regards divine attention as malevolent.*

---

Footnote 6:29–30. See Psalm 5:10, where *hawwa* is in parallel with “no truthfulness.”
