------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:1

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I made a pact with my eyes,

Never to look at a maiden.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:2–4

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For what is the lot from Eloah on high,

And what the portion from Shaddai above?

It’s disaster for the villain,

Misfortune for wrongdoers.

He sees the paths (I take),

And counts my every step.

---

Footnote 31:2–4. Vocalize *nokar* for *neker*, as in Obadiah 1:12.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

I’ve never accompanied falsity;

My foot’s never rushed toward deceit.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:6

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let him weigh me on scales of justice,

Let Eloah know how whole I am.

---

Footnote 31:6. More literally, “my wholeness,” my integrity; see on 1:2.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:7–8

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If my step has ever strayed from the path;

If my heart has ever followed my eyes;

If any blemish has clung to my hands;

Then may I sow (my field) and another man eat (it);

And may my crops be uprooted!

---

Footnote 31:7–8. Reading with the Eastern Masoretic tradition; and compare Daniel 1:4. A blemish spoils the wholeness of the body, metaphorically the integrity of the person; compare Isaiah 1:6.

Footnote 31:7–8. For the literal use of the term, figuratively “progeny,” see for example Isaiah 34:1; 42:5.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:9–10

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If my heart has been lured to (a married) woman;

If I’ve ever lain in wait at my neighbor’s door;

Then may my wife grind for another man;

And may other men crouch over her.

---

Footnote 31:9–10. In order to fornicate with his wife; compare Proverbs 7:6–22.

Footnote 31:9–10. An apparent sexual innuendo (so the Talmud); see the following line.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:11–12

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For that is an unseemly act;

It is even a criminal offense.

It’s a fire that consumes all the way to Abaddon;

One that would burn all my produce.

---

Footnote 31:11–12. The term is associated with fornication, for example in Leviticus 18:17; 20:14.

Footnote 31:11–12. The fornication; compare Proverbs 6:27–29.

Footnote 31:11–12. See at 28:23.

Footnote 31:11–12. Reading *tisrop* for *tesharesh* “it would uproot” (contaminated by 31:8).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:13–14

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I’ve ever dismissed my man-servant’s claim,

Or my woman-servant’s, in disputing with me—

What would I do should El rise (to judge)?

If he’d call me to account—how could I answer him?

---

Footnote 31:13–14. Compare 19:25.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:15

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Did my maker not make him in the belly like me?

Did he not fashion him the same in the womb?


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:16–18

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I’ve ever thwarted a poor man’s desires;

Or the eyes of a widow left pining;

Or ate my loaf by myself,

So an orphan could not eat of it—

For since I was a boy I have reared him like a father;

And her I have guided since (I left) my mother’s womb—

---

Footnote 31:16–18. Job counters the charges Eliphaz imagined in 22:7, 9.

Footnote 31:16–18. Literally, “he (the orphan) has grown up with me as his father.”

Footnote 31:16–18. The widow.

Footnote 31:16–18. Hyperbole.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:19–21

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I ever saw a vagabond with nothing to wear;

Or the needy with nothing to cover him;

If his loins did not bless me,

As he warmed in the fleece of my lambs;

If I ever raised my hand to the fatherless,

For I sighted support in the town-gate;

---

Footnote 31:19–21. The town gate was the site of official transactions; see above at 5:2. Job never abused the powerless, even though he could get away with it.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:22–23

------------------------------------------------------------------------

May the shoulder-blade fall from my shoulder,

And my arm break off at the socket!

For disaster from El is dread to me;

I cannot withstand his grandeur.

---

Footnote 31:22–23. One should probably read ’*akil* for *’ukal;* compare Jeremiah 10:10. For the sentiment, compare Job 13:11.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:24–25

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I ever made gold my reliance,

And called pure gold “my security!”

If I ever rejoiced in the size of my wealth,

That my hand had attained so much;

---

Footnote 31:24–25. Job responds to Eliphaz’s accusation in 22:24–25.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:26–28

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I ever looked at the light as it shone,

Or the moon as it moved so nobly;

If my heart was secretly lured,

So my mouth kissed my hand—

That too is a criminal offense,

For denying the deity above (them).

---

Footnote 31:26–28. Apparently the light of the stars; the language alludes to Isaiah 13:10.

Footnote 31:26–28. If I engaged in pagan worship.

Footnote 31:26–28. A gesture of adoration; see 1 Kings 19:18 and probably Genesis 41:40.

Footnote 31:26–28. Literally, “El.”

Footnote 31:26–28. So for example Ibn Ezra.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:29–30

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I ever rejoiced at my enemy’s ruin,

And exulted when evil befell him;

If I ever allowed my palate to sin,

And invoked a curse on his life;

---

Footnote 31:29–30. Compare Job in 27:6–7.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:31

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If the men of my town ever said,

“If we could only get our fill of his flesh!”

---

Footnote 31:31. More literally, “my tent.”

Footnote 31:31. See on 19:22. The if-clauses of the oaths are not completed by then-clauses in this section. It is understood that Job invokes a curse upon himself for any transgression he had actually committed.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:32

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The stranger never spent a night outdoors;

I would open my doors to the wayfarer.

---

Footnote 31:32. One should probably revocalize *’oreaḥ* (see Jeremiah 14:8).


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:33–34

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If I ever like Adam covered my sins,

Burying my crimes in my bosom—

Intimidated by the town mob,

Frightened by the contempt of the clans,

Halting, not leaving the house.

---

Footnote 31:33–34. So the Aramaic translation; Kahana. Compare Adam’s attempts to suppress his culpability in Genesis 3:9–12.

Footnote 31:33–34. Literally, “The contempt of the clans frightens me.”

Footnote 31:33–34. Literally, “And I halt and do not go out the entrance.”


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:35–37

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If only I had a hearing-judge!

Here is my mark—let Shaddai respond!

And let my rival write his indictment!

I swear I would carry it on my shoulder;

I would wear it like a crown.

I would declare the number of my steps;

And like a prince would engage him.

---

Footnote 31:35–37. See Judges 11:10.

Footnote 31:35–37. That is, give a complete accounting.

Footnote 31:35–37. “Like a prince” puns on “I would declare.” More literally, “I would approach him” (in litigation). A double entendre: “I would do battle like a commander.” This belligerent tone may be what provokes the belligerent response of the deity in 38:1–3.


------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Job 31:38–40

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If ever my land has complained of me,

If ever its furrows cried out;

If ever I ate its yield without payment,

And caused its owner chagrin;

Let thorns grow instead of wheat,

And stinkweed instead of barley!

Completed are the words of Job.

---

Footnote 31:38–40. Literally, “without silver.”

Footnote 31:38–40. See at 11:20.
