I Kings 22:1	There was a lull of three years, with no war between Aram and Israel.
I Kings 22:2	In the third year, King Jehoshaphat of Judah came to visit the king of Israel.
I Kings 22:3	The king of Israel said to his courtiers, “You know that Ramoth-gilead belongs to us, and yet we do nothing to recover it from the hands of the king of Aram.”
I Kings 22:4	And he said to Jehoshaphat, “Will you come with me to battle at Ramoth-gilead?” Jehoshaphat answered the king of Israel, “I will do what you do; my troops shall be your troops, my horses shall be your horses.”
I Kings 22:5	But Jehoshaphat said further to the king of Israel, “Please, first inquire of YHWH.”
I Kings 22:6	So the king of Israel gathered the prophets, about four hundred men, and asked them, “Shall I march upon Ramoth-gilead for battle, or shall I not?” “March,” they said, “and YHWH will deliver [it] into Your Majesty’s hands.”
I Kings 22:7	Then Jehoshaphat asked, “Isn’t there another prophet of YHWH here through whom we can inquire?”
I Kings 22:8	And the king of Israel answered Jehoshaphat, “There is one more man through whom we can inquire of YHWH; but I hate him, because he never prophesies anything good for me, but only misfortune—Micaiah son of Imlah.” But King Jehoshaphat said, “Don’t say that, Your Majesty.”
I Kings 22:9	So the king of Israel summoned an officer and said, “Bring Micaiah son of Imlah at once.”
I Kings 22:10	The king of Israel and King Jehoshaphat of Judah were seated on their thrones, arrayed in their robes, on the threshing floor at the entrance of the gate of Samaria; and all the prophets were prophesying before them.
I Kings 22:11	Zedekiah son of Chenaanah had provided himself with iron horns; and he said, “Thus said YHWH: With these you shall gore the Arameans till you make an end of them.”
I Kings 22:12	And all the other prophets were prophesying similarly, “March upon Ramoth-gilead and triumph! YHWH will deliver it into Your Majesty’s hands.”
I Kings 22:13	The messenger who had gone to summon Micaiah said to him: “Look, the words of the prophets are with one accord favorable to the king. Let your word be like that of the rest of them; speak a favorable word.”
I Kings 22:14	“As YHWH lives,” Micaiah answered, “I will speak only what YHWH tells me.”
I Kings 22:15	When he came before the king, the king said to him, “Micaiah, shall we march upon Ramoth-gilead for battle, or shall we not?” He answered him, “March and triumph! YHWH will deliver [it] into Your Majesty’s hands.”
I Kings 22:16	The king said to him, “How many times must I adjure you to tell me nothing but the truth in the name of YHWH?”
I Kings 22:17	Then he said, “I saw all Israel scattered over the hills like sheep without a shepherd; and YHWH said, ‘These have no master; let everyone return to his home in safety.’”
I Kings 22:18	“Didn’t I tell you,” said the king of Israel to Jehoshaphat, “that he would not prophesy good fortune for me, but only misfortune?”
I Kings 22:19	But [Micaiah] said, “I call upon you to hear the word of YHWH! I saw YHWH seated upon His throne, with all the host of heaven standing in attendance to the right and to the left of Him.
I Kings 22:20	YHWH asked, ‘Who will entice Ahab so that he will march and fall at Ramoth-gilead?’ Then one said thus and another said thus,
I Kings 22:21	until a certain spirit came forward and stood before YHWH and said, ‘I will entice him.’ ‘How?’ YHWH asked him.
I Kings 22:22	And he replied, ‘I will go out and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.’ Then He said, ‘You will entice and you will prevail. Go out and do it.’
I Kings 22:23	So YHWH has put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these prophets of yours; for YHWH has decreed disaster upon you.”
I Kings 22:24	Thereupon Zedekiah son of Chenaanah stepped up and struck Micaiah on the cheek, and demanded, “Which way did the spirit of YHWH pass from me to speak with you?”
I Kings 22:25	And Micaiah replied, “You’ll find out on the day when you try to hide in the innermost room.”
I Kings 22:26	Then the king of Israel said, “Take Micaiah and turn him over to Amon, the city’s governor, and to Prince Joash,
I Kings 22:27	and say, ‘The king’s orders are: Put this fellow in prison, and let his fare be scant bread and scant water until I come home safe.’”
I Kings 22:28	To which Micaiah retorted, “If you ever come home safe, YHWH has not spoken through me.” He said further, “Listen, all you peoples!”
I Kings 22:29	So the king of Israel and King Jehoshaphat of Judah marched upon Ramoth-gilead.
I Kings 22:30	The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “Disguise yourself and go into the battle; but you, wear your robes.” So the king of Israel went into the battle disguised.
I Kings 22:31	Now the king of Aram had instructed his thirty-two chariot officers: “Don’t attack anyone, small or great, except the king of Israel.”
I Kings 22:32	So when the chariot officers saw Jehoshaphat, whom they took for the king of Israel, they turned upon him to attack him, and Jehoshaphat cried out.
I Kings 22:33	And when the chariot officers became aware that he was not the king of Israel, they turned back from pursuing him.
I Kings 22:34	Then a man drew his bow at random and he hit the king of Israel between the plates of the armor; and he said to his charioteer, “Turn the horses around and get me behind the lines; I’m wounded.”
I Kings 22:35	The battle raged all day long, and the king remained propped up in the chariot facing Aram; the blood from the wound ran down into the hollow of the chariot, and at dusk he died.
I Kings 22:36	As the sun was going down, a shout went through the army: “Every man to his own town! Every man to his own district.”
I Kings 22:37	So the king died and was brought to Samaria. They buried the king in Samaria,
I Kings 22:38	and they flushed out the chariot at the pool of Samaria. Thus the dogs lapped up his blood and the whores bathed [in it], in accordance with the word that YHWH had spoken.
I Kings 22:39	The other events of Ahab’s reign, and all his actions—the ivory palace that he built and all the towns that he fortified—are all recorded in the Annals of the Kings of Israel.
I Kings 22:40	Ahab slept with his fathers, and his son Ahaziah succeeded him as king.
I Kings 22:41	Jehoshaphat son of Asa had become king of Judah in the fourth year of King Ahab of Israel.
I Kings 22:42	Jehoshaphat was thirty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned in Jerusalem for twenty-five years. His mother’s name was Azubah daughter of Shilhi.
I Kings 22:43	He followed closely the course of his father Asa and did not deviate from it, doing what was pleasing to YHWH.
I Kings 22:44	However, the shrines did not cease to function; the people still sacrificed and offered at the shrines.
I Kings 22:45	And further, Jehoshaphat submitted to the king of Israel.
I Kings 22:46	As for the other events of Jehoshaphat’s reign and the valor he displayed in battle, they are recorded in the Annals of the Kings of Judah.
I Kings 22:47	(He also stamped out the remaining male prostitutes who had survived in the land from the time of his father Asa.)
I Kings 22:48	There was no king in Edom; a viceroy acted as king.
I Kings 22:49	Jehoshaphat constructed Tarshish ships to sail to Ophir for gold. But he did not sail because the ships were wrecked at Ezion-geber.
I Kings 22:50	Then Ahaziah son of Ahab proposed to Jehoshaphat, “Let my servants sail on the ships with your servants”; but Jehoshaphat would not agree.
I Kings 22:51	Jehoshaphat slept with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the city of his father David, and his son Jehoram succeeded him as king.
I Kings 22:52	[Meanwhile,] Ahaziah son of Ahab had become king of Israel, in Samaria, in the seventeenth year of King Jehoshaphat of Judah; he reigned over Israel two years.
I Kings 22:53	He did what was displeasing to YHWH, following in the footsteps of his father and his mother, and in those of Jeroboam son of Nebat who had caused Israel to sin.
I Kings 22:54	He worshiped Baal and bowed down to him; he vexed YHWH, the God of Israel, just as his father had done.
