I Samuel 17:1	The Philistines assembled their forces for battle; they massed at Socoh of Judah, and encamped at Ephes-dammim, between Socoh and Azekah.
I Samuel 17:2	Saul and the men of Israel massed and encamped in the valley of Elah. They drew up their line of battle against the Philistines,
I Samuel 17:3	with the Philistines stationed on one hill and Israel stationed on the opposite hill; the ravine was between them.
I Samuel 17:4	A champion of the Philistine forces stepped forward; his name was Goliath of Gath, and he was six cubits and a span tall.
I Samuel 17:5	He had a bronze helmet on his head, and wore a breastplate of scale armor, a bronze breastplate weighing five thousand shekels.
I Samuel 17:6	He had bronze greaves on his legs, and a bronze javelin [slung] from his shoulders.
I Samuel 17:7	The shaft of his spear was like a weaver’s bar, and the iron head of his spear weighed six hundred shekels; and the shield-bearer marched in front of him.
I Samuel 17:8	He stopped and called out to the ranks of Israel and he said to them, “Why should you come out to engage in battle? I am the Philistine [champion], and you are Saul’s servants. Choose one of your men and let him come down against me.
I Samuel 17:9	If he bests me in combat and kills me, we will become your slaves; but if I best him and kill him, you shall be our slaves and serve us.”
I Samuel 17:10	And the Philistine ended, “I herewith defy the ranks of Israel. Get me a man and let’s fight it out!”
I Samuel 17:11	When Saul and all Israel heard these words of the Philistine, they were dismayed and terror-stricken.
I Samuel 17:12	David was the son of a certain Ephrathite of Bethlehem in Judah whose name was Jesse. He had eight sons, and in the days of Saul the man was already old, advanced in years.
I Samuel 17:13	The three oldest sons of Jesse had left and gone with Saul to the war. The names of his three sons who had gone to the war were Eliab the first-born, the next Abinadab, and the third Shammah;
I Samuel 17:14	and David was the youngest. The three oldest had followed Saul,
I Samuel 17:15	and David would go back and forth from attending on Saul to shepherd his father’s flock at Bethlehem.
I Samuel 17:16	The Philistine stepped forward morning and evening and took his stand for forty days.
I Samuel 17:17	Jesse said to his son David, “Take an ephah of this parched corn and these ten loaves of bread for your brothers, and carry them quickly to your brothers in camp.
I Samuel 17:18	Take these ten cheeses to the captain of their thousand. Find out how your brothers are and bring some token from them.”
I Samuel 17:19	Saul and the brothers and all the men of Israel were in the valley of Elah, in the war against the Philistines.
I Samuel 17:20	Early next morning, David left someone in charge of the flock, took [the provisions], and set out, as his father Jesse had instructed him. He reached the barricade as the army was going out to the battle lines shouting the war cry.
I Samuel 17:21	Israel and the Philistines drew up their battle lines opposite each other.
I Samuel 17:22	David left his baggage with the man in charge of the baggage and ran toward the battle line and went to greet his brothers.
I Samuel 17:23	While he was talking to them, the champion, whose name was Goliath, the Philistine of Gath, stepped forward from the Philistine ranks and spoke the same words as before; and David heard him.
I Samuel 17:24	When the men of Israel saw the man, they fled in terror.
I Samuel 17:25	And the men of Israel were saying [among themselves], “Do you see that man coming out? He comes out to defy Israel! The man who kills him will be rewarded by the king with great riches; he will also give him his daughter in marriage and grant exemption to his father’s house in Israel.”
I Samuel 17:26	David asked the men standing near him, “What will be done for the man who kills that Philistine and removes the disgrace from Israel? Who is that uncircumcised Philistine that he dares defy the ranks of the living God?”
I Samuel 17:27	The troops told him in the same words what would be done for the man who killed him.
I Samuel 17:28	When Eliab, his oldest brother, heard him speaking to the men, Eliab became angry with David and said, “Why did you come down here, and with whom did you leave those few sheep in the wilderness? I know your impudence and your impertinence: you came down to watch the fighting!”
I Samuel 17:29	But David replied, “What have I done now? I was only asking!”
I Samuel 17:30	And he turned away from him toward someone else; he asked the same question, and the troops gave him the same answer as before.
I Samuel 17:31	The things David said were overheard and were reported to Saul, who had him brought over.
I Samuel 17:32	David said to Saul, “Let no man’s courage fail him. Your servant will go and fight that Philistine!”
I Samuel 17:33	But Saul said to David, “You cannot go to that Philistine and fight him; you are only a boy, and he has been a warrior from his youth!”
I Samuel 17:34	David replied to Saul, “Your servant has been tending his father’s sheep, and if a lion or a bear came and carried off an animal from the flock,
I Samuel 17:35	I would go after it and fight it and rescue it from its mouth. And if it attacked me, I would seize it by the beard and strike it down and kill it.
I Samuel 17:36	Your servant has killed both lion and bear; and that uncircumcised Philistine shall end up like one of them, for he has defied the ranks of the living God.
I Samuel 17:37	YHWH,” David went on, “who saved me from lion and bear will also save me from that Philistine.” “Then go,” Saul said to David, “and may YHWH be with you!”
I Samuel 17:38	Saul clothed David in his own garment; he placed a bronze helmet on his head and fastened a breastplate on him.
I Samuel 17:39	David girded his sword over his garment. Then he tried to walk; but he was not used to it. And David said to Saul, “I cannot walk in these, for I am not used to them.” So David took them off.
I Samuel 17:40	He took his stick, picked a few smooth stones from the wadi, put them in the pocket of his shepherd’s bag and, sling in hand, he went toward the Philistine.
I Samuel 17:41	The Philistine, meanwhile, was coming closer to David, preceded by his shield-bearer.
I Samuel 17:42	When the Philistine caught sight of David, he scorned him, for he was but a boy, ruddy and handsome.
I Samuel 17:43	And the Philistine called out to David, “Am I a dog that you come against me with sticks?” The Philistine cursed David by his gods;
I Samuel 17:44	and the Philistine said to David, “Come here, and I will give your flesh to the birds of the sky and the beasts of the field.”
I Samuel 17:45	David replied to the Philistine, “You come against me with sword and spear and javelin; but I come against you in the name of YHWH of Hosts, the God of the ranks of Israel, whom you have defied.
I Samuel 17:46	This very day YHWH will deliver you into my hands. I will kill you and cut off your head; and I will give the carcasses of the Philistine camp to the birds of the sky and the beasts of the earth. All the earth shall know that there is a God in Israel.
I Samuel 17:47	And this whole assembly shall know that YHWH can give victory without sword or spear. For the battle is YHWH’s, and He will deliver you into our hands.”
I Samuel 17:48	When the Philistine began to advance toward him again, David quickly ran up to the battle line to face the Philistine.
I Samuel 17:49	David put his hand into the bag; he took out a stone and slung it. It struck the Philistine in the forehead; the stone sank into his forehead, and he fell face down on the ground.
I Samuel 17:50	Thus David bested the Philistine with sling and stone; he struck him down and killed him. David had no sword;
I Samuel 17:51	so David ran up and stood over the Philistine, grasped his sword and pulled it from its sheath; and with it he dispatched him and cut off his head. When the Philistines saw that their warrior was dead, they ran.
I Samuel 17:52	The men of Israel and Judah rose up with a war cry and they pursued the Philistines all the way to Gai and up to the gates of Ekron; the Philistines fell mortally wounded along the road to Shaarim up to Gath and Ekron.
I Samuel 17:53	Then the Israelites returned from chasing the Philistines and looted their camp.
I Samuel 17:54	David took the head of the Philistine and brought it to Jerusalem; and he put his weapons in his own tent.
I Samuel 17:55	When Saul saw David going out to assault the Philistine, he asked his army commander Abner, “Whose son is that boy, Abner?” And Abner replied, “By your life, Your Majesty, I do not know.”
I Samuel 17:56	“Then find out whose son that young fellow is,” the king ordered.
I Samuel 17:57	So when David returned after killing the Philistine, Abner took him and brought him to Saul, with the head of the Philistine still in his hand.
I Samuel 17:58	Saul said to him, “Whose son are you, my boy?” And David answered, “The son of your servant Jesse the Bethlehemite.”
