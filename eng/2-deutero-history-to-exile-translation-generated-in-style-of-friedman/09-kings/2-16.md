II Kings 16:1	In the seventeenth year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Ahaz son of King Jotham of Judah became king.
II Kings 16:2	Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did not do what was pleasing to YHWH his God, as his ancestor David had done,
II Kings 16:3	but followed the ways of the kings of Israel. He even consigned his son to the fire, in the abhorrent fashion of the nations which YHWH had dispossessed before the Israelites.
II Kings 16:4	He sacrificed and made offerings at the shrines, on the hills, and under every leafy tree.
II Kings 16:5	Then King Rezin of Aram and King Pekah son of Remaliah of Israel advanced on Jerusalem for battle. They besieged Ahaz, but could not overcome [him].
II Kings 16:6	At that time King Rezin of Aram recovered Elath for Aram; he drove out the Judites from Elath, and Edomites came to Elath and settled there, as is still the case.
II Kings 16:7	Ahaz sent messengers to King Tiglath-pileser of Assyria to say, “I am your servant and your son; come and deliver me from the hands of the king of Aram and from the hands of the king of Israel, who are attacking me.”
II Kings 16:8	Ahaz took the gold and silver that were on hand in the House of YHWH and in the treasuries of the royal palace and sent them as a gift to the king of Assyria.
II Kings 16:9	The king of Assyria responded to his request; the king of Assyria marched against Damascus and captured it. He deported its inhabitants to Kir and put Rezin to death.
II Kings 16:10	When King Ahaz went to Damascus to greet King Tiglath-pileser of Assyria, he saw the altar in Damascus. King Ahaz sent the priest Uriah a sketch of the altar and a detailed plan of its construction.
II Kings 16:11	The priest Uriah did just as King Ahaz had instructed him from Damascus; the priest Uriah built the altar before King Ahaz returned from Damascus.
II Kings 16:12	When the king returned from Damascus, and when the king saw the altar, the king drew near the altar, ascended it,
II Kings 16:13	and offered his burnt offering and meal offering; he poured his libation, and he dashed the blood of his offering of well-being against the altar.
II Kings 16:14	As for the bronze altar which had been before YHWH, he moved it from its place in front of the Temple, between the [new] altar and the House of YHWH, and placed it on the north side of the [new] altar.
II Kings 16:15	And King Ahaz commanded the priest Uriah: “On the great altar you shall offer the morning burnt offering and the evening meal offering and the king’s burnt offering and his meal offering, with the burnt offerings of all the people of the land, their meal offerings and their libations. And against it you shall dash the blood of all the burnt offerings and all the blood of the sacrifices. And I will decide about the bronze altar.”
II Kings 16:16	Uriah did just as King Ahaz commanded.
II Kings 16:17	King Ahaz cut off the insets—the laver stands—and removed the lavers from them. He also removed the tank from the bronze oxen that supported it and set it on a stone pavement—
II Kings 16:18	on account of the king of Assyria. He also extended to the House of YHWH the sabbath passage that had been built in the palace and the king’s outer entrance.
II Kings 16:19	The other events of Ahaz’s reign, and his actions, are recorded in the Annals of the Kings of Judah.
II Kings 16:20	Ahaz slept with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the City of David; his son Hezekiah succeeded him as king.
